# Jiaoshi Yilin Zhu — Jiao's Forest of Changes, Annotated (焦氏易林注) — Juan 1
Source: 焦氏易林注 _ 焦氏易林注卷一 - 中國哲學書電子化計劃.html
Status: complete — 64 sections (session 1, both halves). This is the opening of the commentary to Jiao Yanshou's Forest of Changes: 64 quatrains of omen verse (林詞) on the 64 hexagrams of this book's own sequence, each followed by the annotator's trigram-image reading. The annotator's modern variant-collation notes (introduced by ○) are retained in ZH/PY but omitted from EN; they are not the received classical text being glossed but the editor's apparatus. Conventions for this book: hexagram names romanized (Qian, Kun, Tun…) with characters; the eight trigrams by their standard images (乾 heaven, 坤 earth, 震 thunder, 坎 water, 艮 mountain, 巽 wind, 離 fire, 兌 lake); 林詞 → "the Forest verse"; 互 → "the nuclear trigram"; 伏 → "the hidden trigram"; 通 → "the opposing hexagram"; 元本/宋本/汲古 refer to editions (not translated).

## Section 1 (乾)

**ZH:** 乾。道陟石阪，胡言連謇。譯喑且聾，莫使道通。請謁不行，求事無功。乾為道、為陟、為山，故曰石阪。為言，在西北，故曰胡言。連謇，口吃也。震為鳴，坎為耳，艮為道、為請求，今為純乾，乾三子具不見，故曰暗、曰聾、曰道不通、曰請求無功，蓋三子各分乾一爻也。又林詞所以不吉者，以卦為純陽，陽遇陽則窒故也。此易之根本大義，自此義不明而易多誤解。○石，汲古作多，謇作蹇，喑訛瘠。依宋元本。
**PY:** qián。 dào zhì shí bǎn， hú yán lián jiǎn。 yì yīn qiě lóng， mò shǐ dào tōng。 qǐng yè bù xíng， qiú shì wú gōng。 qián wèi dào、 wèi zhì、 wèi shān， gù yuē shí bǎn。 wèi yán， zài xī běi， gù yuē hú yán。 lián jiǎn， kǒu chī yě。 zhèn wèi míng， kǎn wèi ěr， gěn wèi dào、 wèi qǐng qiú， jīn wèi chún qián， qián sān zi jù bù jiàn， gù yuē àn、 yuē lóng、 yuē dào bù tōng、 yuē qǐng qiú wú gōng， gài sān zi gè fēn qián yī yáo yě。 yòu lín cí suǒ yǐ bù jí zhě， yǐ guà wèi chún yáng， yáng yù yáng zé zhì gù yě。 cǐ yì zhī gēn běn dà yì， zì cǐ yì bù míng ér yì duō wù jiě。○ shí， jí gǔ zuò duō， jiǎn zuò jiǎn， yīn é jí。 yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Qian (乾). The road climbs a stony slope; foreign speech stammers and halts. The interpreter is dumb and deaf; none can make the way go through. Petition and audience do not proceed; seeking affairs brings no merit. Qian is the road, is climbing, is mountain — hence "stony slope." It is speech, lying in the northwest — hence "foreign speech." 連謇 means stammering. Zhen is crying out; Kan is the ear; Gen is the road and petition. Now the hexagram is pure Qian, and Qian's three sons are all absent — hence "dumb," "deaf," "the way does not go through," and "petition without merit," for the three sons each take one line of Qian. And the reason the Forest verse is inauspicious is that the hexagram is pure yang: when yang meets yang, there is blockage. This is the root and great meaning of the Changes; since this meaning has gone dark, the Changes has been much misunderstood.

## Section 2 (坤)

**ZH:** 坤。招殃來螫，害我邦國。病傷手足，不得安息。乾變陰故曰招。日來坤陰為災殃、為毒螫、為害、為邦國。艮手震足，今純坤艮震毀，故曰病傷手足。坤逆行故曰來，《系辭》「來者，伸也。」即謂坤逆行。○殃，元本作禍，依宋本、汲古。傷，宋元本作在，依汲古。
**PY:** kūn。 zhāo yāng lái shì， hài wǒ bāng guó。 bìng shāng shǒu zú， bù dé ān xī。 qián biàn yīn gù yuē zhāo。 rì lái kūn yīn wèi zāi yāng、 wèi dú shì、 wèi hài、 wèi bāng guó。 gěn shǒu zhèn zú， jīn chún kūn gěn zhèn huǐ， gù yuē bìng shāng shǒu zú。 kūn nì xíng gù yuē lái，《 xì cí》「 lái zhě， shēn yě。」 jí wèi kūn nì xíng。○ yāng， yuán běn zuò huò， yī sòng běn、 jí gǔ。 shāng， sòng yuán běn zuò zài， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Kun (坤). Beckoning calamity comes the sting; it harms my state and land. Sick and wounded in hand and foot, I find no rest or peace. Qian changed into yin, hence "beckoning." "Comes" — Kun's yin is calamity, is poison sting, is harm, is state and land. Gen is the hand; Zhen is the foot. Now it is pure Kun, and Gen and Zhen are destroyed — hence "sick and wounded in hand and foot." Kun moves backward, hence "comes": the Appended Statements say "the coming is the stretching forth" — that is, Kun's backward motion.

## Section 3 (屯)

**ZH:** 屯。陽孤亢極，多所恨惑。車傾蓋亡，身常憂惶。乃得其願，雌雄相從。坎為孤，一陽居五，民皆歸初，故曰孤亢。坎為憂疑，故曰恨惑、曰憂惶。艮為蓋，震為車。坎破，故傾亡。坤為身，初陽四陰、二陰五陽皆有應與，故曰雌雄相從。○凡六子易林皆有孤、獨、鰥、寡象，人皆知巽寡，餘失傳。不下贅。
**PY:** tún。 yáng gū kàng jí， duō suǒ hèn huò。 chē qīng gài wáng， shēn cháng yōu huáng。 nǎi dé qí yuàn， cí xióng xiāng cóng。 kǎn wèi gū， yī yáng jū wǔ， mín jiē guī chū， gù yuē gū kàng。 kǎn wèi yōu yí， gù yuē hèn huò、 yuē yōu huáng。 gěn wèi gài， zhèn wèi chē。 kǎn pò， gù qīng wáng。 kūn wèi shēn， chū yáng sì yīn、 èr yīn wǔ yáng jiē yǒu yīng yǔ， gù yuē cí xióng xiāng cóng。○ fán liù zi yì lín jiē yǒu gū、 dú、 guān、 guǎ xiàng， rén jiē zhī xùn guǎ， yú shī chuán。 bù xià zhuì。
**EN:** Tun (屯). Yang stands solitary, high at its utmost; much it grieves and doubts. The cart overturns, its canopy lost; the body is ever anxious and afraid. Then it gets its wish: female and male follow one another. Kan is solitary: one yang dwells in the fifth place, and the people all return to the first — hence "solitary, high." Kan is worry and doubt — hence "grieves and doubts" and "anxious and afraid." Gen is the canopy; Zhen is the cart. Kan is broken — hence "overturns," "lost." Kun is the body; the first yang with the fourth yin, and the second yin with the fifth yang, each has its answering pair — hence "female and male follow one another."

## Section 4 (蒙)

**ZH:** 蒙。鴶鵴鳲鳩，專一無尤。君子是則，長受嘉福。艮為鳥，故曰鴶鵴鳲鳩。艮止，故專。一說鳲鳩義與毛同。艮為君子，坎為法則，震為嘉福。○艮，鳥象，失傳。鴶，音戞。鴶鵴鳴鳩，皆布穀也。鴶，各本多訛鵠，依宋元本。
**PY:** méng。 jiá jú shī jiū， zhuān yī wú yóu。 jūn zǐ shì zé， zhǎng shòu jiā fú。 gěn wèi niǎo， gù yuē jiá jú shī jiū。 gěn zhǐ， gù zhuān。 yī shuō shī jiū yì yǔ máo tóng。 gěn wèi jūn zǐ， kǎn wèi fǎ zé， zhèn wèi jiā fú。○ gěn， niǎo xiàng， shī chuán。 jiá， yīn jiá。 jiá jú míng jiū， jiē bù gǔ yě。 jiá， gè běn duō é hú， yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Meng (蒙). The jiaju and the cuckoo are single-hearted and without fault. The gentleman takes them as his rule and long receives excellent fortune. Gen is the bird — hence "the jiaju and the cuckoo." Gen is stillness — hence "single-hearted." One reading takes the cuckoo's meaning as the same as in the Odes. Gen is the gentleman; Kan is rule and model; Zhen is excellent fortune. [uncertain: the identity of 鴶鵴 (jiaju); the annotator glosses both birds as cuckoo-kinds (布穀), and the verse's point — the cuckoo's proverbial constancy — follows the Odes tradition.]

## Section 5 (需)

**ZH:** 需。目瞤足動，喜如其願，舉家蒙寵。互離為目。瞤，亦動也。乾為喜、為寵。坎為官室、為家。《西京雜記》「目瞤得酒食，故曰喜。」足，疑為指訛，《左傳》「食指動」是也，伏艮象。○目，汲古訛日，從宋元本。
**PY:** xū。 mù rún zú dòng， xǐ rú qí yuàn， jǔ jiā méng chǒng。 hù lí wèi mù。 rún， yì dòng yě。 qián wèi xǐ、 wèi chǒng。 kǎn wèi guān shì、 wèi jiā。《 xī jīng zá jì》「 mù rún dé jiǔ shí， gù yuē xǐ。」 zú， yí wèi zhǐ é，《 zuǒ chuán》「 shí zhǐ dòng」 shì yě， fú gěn xiàng。○ mù， jí gǔ é rì， cóng sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Xu (需). The eye twitches, the foot stirs: joy comes as he wished, and the whole house is graced with favor. The nuclear Li is the eye; 瞤 is likewise movement. Qian is joy, is favor. Kan is office and chamber, is house. The Xijing Miscellany says: "a twitching eye means wine and food" — hence "joy." "Foot" is suspected to be an error for "finger," as in the Zuo Tradition's "his food-finger moved" — the hidden Gen image.

## Section 6 (訟)

**ZH:** 訟。龍馬上山，絕無水泉。喉焦唇乾，舌不能言。乾為龍馬、為山，在上，坎在下而中為火、為日，故水泉絕。兌為喉舌、為唇，乃兌覆而與火鄰，故焦渴不能言。
**PY:** sòng。 lóng mǎ shàng shān， jué wú shuǐ quán。 hóu jiāo chún qián， shé bù néng yán。 qián wèi lóng mǎ、 wèi shān， zài shàng， kǎn zài xià ér zhōng wèi huǒ、 wèi rì， gù shuǐ quán jué。 duì wèi hóu shé、 wèi chún， nǎi duì fù ér yǔ huǒ lín， gù jiāo kě bù néng yán。
**EN:** Song (訟). The dragon-horse climbs the mountain: no spring, no water at all. Throat scorched, lips dried, the tongue cannot speak. Qian is dragon-horse, is mountain, above; Kan is below, and its midst becomes fire and sun — hence the springs run dry. Dui is throat and tongue, is lips; now Dui is inverted and neighbors fire — hence scorched with thirst, unable to speak.

## Section 7 (師)

**ZH:** 師。倉盈庾億，宜種黍稷。年豐歲熟，民人安息。坤為腹、為囊，倉庾象也。坤眾故曰盈、曰億、曰豐熟。震為黍稷，坤為歲年、為民人、為安息。後比之升、坤之恆詞同，亦皆以坤為倉庾。《詩小雅楚茨》云「我黍與與，我稷翼翼；我倉既盈，我庾維億。」又《甫田》云「曾孫之稼，如茨如梁；曾孫之庾，如坁如京。乃求千斯倉，乃求萬斯箱；黍稷稻粱，農夫之慶。」○年豐歲熟，元刊作國家富有；安作蕃，依宋本、汲古。
**PY:** shī。 cāng yíng yǔ yì， yí zhǒng shǔ jì。 nián fēng suì shú， mín rén ān xī。 kūn wèi fù、 wèi náng， cāng yǔ xiàng yě。 kūn zhòng gù yuē yíng、 yuē yì、 yuē fēng shú。 zhèn wèi shǔ jì， kūn wèi suì nián、 wèi mín rén、 wèi ān xī。 hòu bǐ zhī shēng、 kūn zhī héng cí tóng， yì jiē yǐ kūn wèi cāng yǔ。《 shī xiǎo yǎ chǔ cí》 yún「 wǒ shǔ yǔ yǔ， wǒ jì yì yì； wǒ cāng jì yíng， wǒ yǔ wéi yì。」 yòu《 fǔ tián》 yún「 céng sūn zhī jià， rú cí rú liáng； céng sūn zhī yǔ， rú zhǐ rú jīng。 nǎi qiú qiān sī cāng， nǎi qiú wàn sī xiāng； shǔ jì dào liáng， nóng fū zhī qìng。」○ nián fēng suì shú， yuán kān zuò guó jiā fù yǒu； ān zuò fān， yī sòng běn、 jí gǔ。
**EN:** Shi (師). The granary brims, the storehouse is full; it is fitting to sow the millets. The year is rich, the harvest ripe; the people rest in peace. Kun is belly, is sack — the granary and storehouse image. Kun is the multitude — hence "brims," "full," "rich and ripe." Zhen is the millets; Kun is year and harvest, is people, is rest and peace. Later, in Bi at Sheng and in Kun at Heng, the verse is the same, likewise taking Kun as granary and storehouse. The Odes, Xiaoya, "Chuci" says: "Our millets are thick and tall; our granaries are filled, our storehouses are numbered in the millions." And in "Futian": "The grain of the grandson is like thatch, like beams; his storehouses are like banks, like mounds. So he seeks a thousand granaries, he seeks ten thousand carts — millets, rice, and sorghum: the husbandman's joy."

## Section 8 (比)

**ZH:** 比。中夜犬吠，盜在牆外。神明佑助，消散皆去。坤、坎皆為夜。坎為中，故曰中夜。艮為犬，伏兌口，故曰犬吠。坎為盜，艮為牆，坎在外故曰在牆外。伏乾為神，艮為明，坤消故曰消散。○犬，元刊作狗，依汲古。
**PY:** bǐ。 zhōng yè quǎn fèi， dào zài qiáng wài。 shén míng yòu zhù， xiāo sàn jiē qù。 kūn、 kǎn jiē wèi yè。 kǎn wèi zhōng， gù yuē zhōng yè。 gěn wèi quǎn， fú duì kǒu， gù yuē quǎn fèi。 kǎn wèi dào， gěn wèi qiáng， kǎn zài wài gù yuē zài qiáng wài。 fú qián wèi shén， gěn wèi míng， kūn xiāo gù yuē xiāo sàn。○ quǎn， yuán kān zuò gǒu， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Bi (比). At midnight the dog barks: a thief is outside the wall. The spirits give aid and help; melting away, all depart. Kun and Kan are both night; Kan is the middle — hence "midnight." Gen is the dog; hidden Dui is the mouth — hence "the dog barks." Kan is the thief; Gen is the wall; Kan is outside — hence "outside the wall." Hidden Qian is spirit; Gen is brightness; Kun is dissolving — hence "melting away."

## Section 9 (小畜)

**ZH:** 小畜。據斗運樞，順天無憂，與樂並居。通豫。艮為星，數七，故曰據斗。坎為樞，故曰運樞。乾順行故曰順天。坎伏故無憂。震為樂，艮為居。○汲古多「所行造德」四字，依宋元本。
**PY:** xiǎo chù。 jù dòu yùn shū， shùn tiān wú yōu， yǔ lè bìng jū。 tōng yù。 gěn wèi xīng， shù qī， gù yuē jù dòu。 kǎn wèi shū， gù yuē yùn shū。 qián shùn xíng gù yuē shùn tiān。 kǎn fú gù wú yōu。 zhèn wèi lè， gěn wèi jū。○ jí gǔ duō「 suǒ xíng zào dé」 sì zì， yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Xiaochu (小畜). Grasping the Dipper, turning the hinge: following Heaven, without worry, dwelling together with joy. Opposing hexagram: Yu. Gen is the star; its number is seven — hence "grasping the Dipper." Kan is the hinge — hence "turning the hinge." Qian moves in the forward course — hence "following Heaven." Kan is hidden — hence "without worry." Zhen is joy; Gen is dwelling.

## Section 10 (履)

**ZH:** 履。空拳握手，倒地更起。富饒豐衍，快樂無已。通謙。艮為拳、為手，坤為地，為下。震起，故曰倒地更起。坤為富饒，震為快樂，全用伏象，易卦爻詞每如此也，易林無創例。○倒，汲古作委，依宋元本。
**PY:** lǚ。 kōng quán wò shǒu， dǎo dì gèng qǐ。 fù ráo fēng yǎn， kuài lè wú yǐ。 tōng qiān。 gěn wèi quán、 wèi shǒu， kūn wèi dì， wèi xià。 zhèn qǐ， gù yuē dǎo dì gèng qǐ。 kūn wèi fù ráo， zhèn wèi kuài lè， quán yòng fú xiàng， yì guà yáo cí měi rú cǐ yě， yì lín wú chuàng lì。○ dào， jí gǔ zuò wěi， yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Lü (履). Empty fist grasping the hand; fallen to the ground, he rises again. Riches and bounty swell and spread; gladness and joy without end. Opposing hexagram: Qian. Gen is fist, is hand; Kun is earth, is below. Zhen is rising — hence "fallen to the ground, he rises again." Kun is riches and bounty; Zhen is gladness and joy. This uses the hidden images throughout — the line-texts of the Changes are always thus; the Forest of Changes creates no new precedents.

## Section 11 (泰)

**ZH:** 泰。不風不雨，白日皎皎。宜出驅馳，通利大道。巽伏故不風，半坎故不雨。震為白，乾為日，震為出、為馳驅、為大塗、為通利。○利，汲古作理，從宋本。
**PY:** tài。 bù fēng bù yǔ， bái rì jiǎo jiǎo。 yí chū qū chí， tōng lì dà dào。 xùn fú gù bù fēng， bàn kǎn gù bù yǔ。 zhèn wèi bái， qián wèi rì， zhèn wèi chū、 wèi chí qū、 wèi dà tú、 wèi tōng lì。○ lì， jí gǔ zuò lǐ， cóng sòng běn。
**EN:** Tai (泰). Neither wind nor rain; the white sun shining bright. Fitting to go out and gallop, to pass freely on the great road. Xun is hidden — hence no wind; it is only half of Kan — hence no rain. Zhen is white; Qian is the sun; Zhen is going out, is galloping, is the great road, is free passage.

## Section 12 (否)

**ZH:** 否。載日晶光，驂駕六龍。祿命徹天，封為燕王。坤為載，乾為日，在上故曰載日。伏震為龍、為駕，乾數六，故曰驂駕六龍。乾為祿，巽為命，乾在上，故曰徹天，伏震為王，兌為燕，故曰燕王。○載，元本作戴，依宋本、汲古。
**PY:** fǒu。 zài rì jīng guāng， cān jià liù lóng。 lù mìng chè tiān， fēng wèi yàn wáng。 kūn wèi zài， qián wèi rì， zài shàng gù yuē zài rì。 fú zhèn wèi lóng、 wèi jià， qián shù liù， gù yuē cān jià liù lóng。 qián wèi lù， xùn wèi mìng， qián zài shàng， gù yuē chè tiān， fú zhèn wèi wáng， duì wèi yàn， gù yuē yàn wáng。○ zài， yuán běn zuò dài， yī sòng běn、 jí gǔ。
**EN:** Fou (否). Bearing the sun's crystal light, he drives a team of six dragons. His lot and mandate pierce the sky; he is enfeoffed as King of Yan. Kun is bearing; Qian is the sun, above — hence "bearing the sun." Hidden Zhen is dragon, is driving; Qian's number is six — hence "a team of six dragons." Qian is lot; Xun is mandate; Qian is above — hence "pierces the sky." Hidden Zhen is king; Dui is Yan — hence "King of Yan."

## Section 13 (同人)

**ZH:** 同人。子號索哺，母行求食。反見空巢，訾我長息。通師。震為子、為號、為哺、為請。請，求也。故曰索哺、曰求食。震為行，坤為母，艮為巢，坤虛，故曰空巢。震為長息，坎上下兩兌口相背，兌為口舌，故曰訾。○巢，依宋元本，汲古作窠。
**PY:** tóng rén。 zi hào suǒ bǔ， mǔ xíng qiú shí。 fǎn jiàn kōng cháo， zī wǒ zhǎng xī。 tōng shī。 zhèn wèi zi、 wèi hào、 wèi bǔ、 wèi qǐng。 qǐng， qiú yě。 gù yuē suǒ bǔ、 yuē qiú shí。 zhèn wèi xíng， kūn wèi mǔ， gěn wèi cháo， kūn xū， gù yuē kōng cháo。 zhèn wèi zhǎng xī， kǎn shàng xià liǎng duì kǒu xiāng bèi， duì wèi kǒu shé， gù yuē zī。○ cháo， yī sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ zuò kē。
**EN:** Tongren (同人). The child cries, begging to be fed; the mother goes out to seek food. Returning, she sees an empty nest and curses me for growing up. Opposing hexagram: Shi. Zhen is the child, is crying, is being fed, is asking — asking is seeking — hence "begging to be fed" and "to seek food." Zhen is going; Kun is the mother; Gen is the nest; Kun is empty — hence "empty nest." Zhen is growing up; Kan above and below has two Dui mouths facing away from each other; Dui is mouth and tongue — hence "curses."

## Section 14 (大有)

**ZH:** 大有。上帝之生，福佑日成。修德行惠，樂且安寧。乾為上帝、為福、為德惠。通比，比樂坤安。本象、伏象兼取。○第四句元刊作樂安且寧，依汲古。
**PY:** dà yǒu。 shàng dì zhī shēng， fú yòu rì chéng。 xiū dé xíng huì， lè qiě ān níng。 qián wèi shàng dì、 wèi fú、 wèi dé huì。 tōng bǐ， bǐ lè kūn ān。 běn xiàng、 fú xiàng jiān qǔ。○ dì sì jù yuán kān zuò lè ān qiě níng， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Dayou (大有). Born of the Lord on High, blessed and daily brought to fullness. Cultivating virtue, practicing kindness, joyful and at peace. Qian is the Lord on High, is blessing, is virtue and kindness. Opposing hexagram: Bi — Bi is joy, Kun is peace. It takes the primary and the hidden images together.

## Section 15 (謙)

**ZH:** 謙。山險難登，澗中多石。車馳轊擊，載重傷軸。擔負差躓，跌踒右足。艮為山、為石，坎為澗，坎險，故難登。震為車，坎多眚，故轊擊傷軸。坎為轊、為軸，艮為何，故曰擔負。坎蹇，故差躓、故跌踒。差，同蹉也。震為足，艮伏兌，故曰右足。○擔，依汲古。元刊作儋，義同。跌踒，汲古作踠跌，依宋元本。
**PY:** qiān。 shān xiǎn nán dēng， jiàn zhōng duō shí。 chē chí wèi jī， zài zhòng shāng zhóu。 dān fù chà zhì， diē wō yòu zú。 gěn wèi shān、 wèi shí， kǎn wèi jiàn， kǎn xiǎn， gù nán dēng。 zhèn wèi chē， kǎn duō shěng， gù wèi jī shāng zhóu。 kǎn wèi wèi、 wèi zhóu， gěn wèi hé， gù yuē dān fù。 kǎn jiǎn， gù chà zhì、 gù diē wō。 chà， tóng cuō yě。 zhèn wèi zú， gěn fú duì， gù yuē yòu zú。○ dān， yī jí gǔ。 yuán kān zuò dān， yì tóng。 diē wō， jí gǔ zuò wǎn diē， yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Qian (謙). The mountain is steep and hard to climb; the ravine is full of stones. The cart gallops, the axle-caps strike; heavy laden, it breaks its axle. Bearing his burden he slips and stumbles; falling, he wrenches his right foot. Gen is mountain, is stone; Kan is ravine; Kan is peril — hence "hard to climb." Zhen is the cart; Kan has much misfortune — hence "the axle-caps strike, it breaks its axle." Kan is axle-cap, is axle; Gen is to carry on the back — hence "bearing his burden." Kan is lameness — hence "slips and stumbles," hence "falling, wrenches." 差 is the same as 蹉. Zhen is the foot; Gen's hidden trigram is Dui — hence "the right foot."

## Section 16 (豫)

**ZH:** 豫。禹鑿龍門，通利水源。東注滄海，民得安存。震為帝，故曰禹。艮手，故曰鑿。坤為門，震為龍，故曰龍門。坎水坤水，故曰水源，震為通利、為東，坤為滄海、為民、為安。
**PY:** yù。 yǔ záo lóng mén， tōng lì shuǐ yuán。 dōng zhù cāng hǎi， mín dé ān cún。 zhèn wèi dì， gù yuē yǔ。 gěn shǒu， gù yuē záo。 kūn wèi mén， zhèn wèi lóng， gù yuē lóng mén。 kǎn shuǐ kūn shuǐ， gù yuē shuǐ yuán， zhèn wèi tōng lì、 wèi dōng， kūn wèi cāng hǎi、 wèi mín、 wèi ān。
**EN:** Yu (豫). Yu cuts the Dragon Gate, opening the water's source. It flows east into the grey sea, and the people find safety. Zhen is the sovereign — hence Yu. Gen is the hand — hence "cuts." Kun is the gate; Zhen is the dragon — hence "Dragon Gate." Kan is water; Kun is water — hence "the water's source." Zhen is opening, is east; Kun is the grey sea, is the people, is safety.

## Section 17 (隨)

**ZH:** 隨。乘龍上天，兩蛇為輔。踊躍雲中，游觀滄海。民樂安處。震為龍，艮為天，故曰乘龍上天。巽蛇，兌卦數二，故曰兩蛇。輔，附也。互大坎為雲，坎在上，震起，故曰踊躍雲中。兌為澤，故曰海，艮為觀、為安。○民樂安處，元刊作安樂長處，依汲古。
**PY:** suí。 chéng lóng shàng tiān， liǎng shé wèi fǔ。 yǒng yuè yún zhōng， yóu guān cāng hǎi。 mín lè ān chù。 zhèn wèi lóng， gěn wèi tiān， gù yuē chéng lóng shàng tiān。 xùn shé， duì guà shù èr， gù yuē liǎng shé。 fǔ， fù yě。 hù dà kǎn wèi yún， kǎn zài shàng， zhèn qǐ， gù yuē yǒng yuè yún zhōng。 duì wèi zé， gù yuē hǎi， gěn wèi guān、 wèi ān。○ mín lè ān chù， yuán kān zuò ān lè zhǎng chù， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Sui (隨). Riding the dragon he mounts the sky; two serpents attend him. Leaping and soaring amid the clouds, he roams and beholds the grey sea. The people are happy in their peaceful places. Zhen is the dragon; Gen is heaven — hence "riding the dragon he mounts the sky." Xun is the serpent; the Dui trigram's number is two — hence "two serpents." 輔 means attend. The nuclear great Kan is clouds; Kan is above and Zhen rises — hence "leaping and soaring amid the clouds." Dui is the lake — hence "the sea"; Gen is beholding, is peace.

## Section 18 (蠱)

**ZH:** 蠱。彭祖九子，據德不殆。南山松柏，長受嘉福。艮為壽，故曰彭祖。彭祖名篯，壽八百歲，即《論語》所謂老彭也。震為子，數九，故曰九子。艮為據，震為德，艮山，震南，故曰南山。巽為松柏，震為嘉福。○德，元刊作得。
**PY:** gǔ。 péng zǔ jiǔ zi， jù dé bù dài。 nán shān sōng bǎi， zhǎng shòu jiā fú。 gěn wèi shòu， gù yuē péng zǔ。 péng zǔ míng jiān， shòu bā bǎi suì， jí《 lùn yǔ》 suǒ wèi lǎo péng yě。 zhèn wèi zi， shù jiǔ， gù yuē jiǔ zi。 gěn wèi jù， zhèn wèi dé， gěn shān， zhèn nán， gù yuē nán shān。 xùn wèi sōng bǎi， zhèn wèi jiā fú。○ dé， yuán kān zuò dé。
**EN:** Gu (蠱). Pengzu (彭祖) has nine sons; grounded in virtue, he is not endangered. The pines and cypresses of the southern mountain long receive excellent fortune. Gen is longevity — hence Pengzu. Pengzu's name is Jian (篯), eight hundred years old — the Old Peng of the Analects. Zhen is sons; its number is nine — hence "nine sons." Gen is grounded; Zhen is virtue. Gen is mountain; Zhen is south — hence "southern mountain." Xun is pines and cypresses; Zhen is excellent fortune.

## Section 19 (臨)

**ZH:** 臨。南山昊天，刺政閔身。疾悲無辜，皆憎為仇。通遁。艮為南山。乾盈已正當夏日，故曰昊天。本象。坤為政、為身、為悲、為憎，故刺政憫身。艮為背也。南山昊天，皆小雅篇名，皆刺幽王而悲憫身世也。刺政，承南山而言，謂「赫赫師尹，不平謂何」也。閔身，承昊天言，謂若此無罪，熏胥以鋪也。○閔，宋、元刊、汲古皆作關，依局本。
**PY:** lín。 nán shān hào tiān， cì zhèng mǐn shēn。 jí bēi wú gū， jiē zēng wèi chóu。 tōng dùn。 gěn wèi nán shān。 qián yíng yǐ zhèng dāng xià rì， gù yuē hào tiān。 běn xiàng。 kūn wèi zhèng、 wèi shēn、 wèi bēi、 wèi zēng， gù cì zhèng mǐn shēn。 gěn wèi bèi yě。 nán shān hào tiān， jiē xiǎo yǎ piān míng， jiē cì yōu wáng ér bēi mǐn shēn shì yě。 cì zhèng， chéng nán shān ér yán， wèi「 hè hè shī yǐn， bù píng wèi hé」 yě。 mǐn shēn， chéng hào tiān yán， wèi ruò cǐ wú zuì， xūn xū yǐ pù yě。○ mǐn， sòng、 yuán kān、 jí gǔ jiē zuò guān， yī jú běn。
**EN:** Lin (臨). "Southern Mountain" and "Haotian" — satire of government, pity for one's person. Pained grief for the innocent; all are hated as enemies. Opposing hexagram: Dun. Gen is the southern mountain. Qian at its fullness corresponds to the summer sun — hence "haotian" (the vast sky). This uses the primary images. Kun is government, is person, is grief, is hatred — hence "satire of government, pity for one's person." Gen is the back. "Southern Mountain" and "Haotian" are both Odes titles from the Xiaoya; both satirize King You and grieve for one's lot. "Satire of government" follows "Southern Mountain," meaning its line "So mighty, the Marshal Yin — why no justice?" "Pity for one's person" follows "Haotian," meaning "such as these, though innocent, are consumed and punished together."

## Section 20 (觀)

**ZH:** 觀。江河淮海，天之奧府。眾利所聚，可以饒有。樂我君子，百福是受。坤為江河淮海，艮為府，坤伏乾，故曰天府。坤為眾、為利、為聚、為富饒。艮為君子，巽伏震為樂，坤為我也。○元本下多百福是受四字，茲從之宋本，汲古無。
**PY:** guān。 jiāng hé huái hǎi， tiān zhī ào fǔ。 zhòng lì suǒ jù， kě yǐ ráo yǒu。 lè wǒ jūn zǐ， bǎi fú shì shòu。 kūn wèi jiāng hé huái hǎi， gěn wèi fǔ， kūn fú qián， gù yuē tiān fǔ。 kūn wèi zhòng、 wèi lì、 wèi jù、 wèi fù ráo。 gěn wèi jūn zǐ， xùn fù zhèn wèi lè， kūn wèi wǒ yě。○ yuán běn xià duō bǎi fú shì shòu sì zì， zī cóng zhī sòng běn， jí gǔ wú。
**EN:** Guan (觀). The rivers — the Yangzi, the Huai, the sea — are Heaven's secret storehouse. Where every profit gathers, one may be rich and have. Happy is my gentleman: a hundred fortunes are his to receive. Kun is the rivers, the Yangzi, the Huai, the sea; Gen is the storehouse; Kun hides Qian — hence "Heaven's storehouse." Kun is the many, is profit, is gathering, is riches. Gen is the gentleman; hidden Xun and Zhen are joy; Kun is "my."

## Section 21 (噬嗑)

**ZH:** 噬嗑。堅冰黃鳥，終日悲號。不見白粒，但見藜蒿。數驚鷙鳥，為我心憂。坎為堅冰，震為黃，艮為鳥、為終。離為日，故曰終日。坎為悲，震為號、為粒，坎隱故不見。震為藜蒿、為驚。艮為黔啄、為鷙鳥。坎為心憂。《詩小雅》「黃鳥黃鳥，無集於榖，此邦之人，不我肯榖。言旋言歸，複我邦族。」林似說此詩。○第二句各本多作常哀悲愁。白作甘，依解之夬校丁晏云「楚策『俯啄白粒』是也。」
**PY:** shì kē。 jiān bīng huáng niǎo， zhōng rì bēi hào。 bù jiàn bái lì， dàn jiàn lí hāo。 shù jīng zhì niǎo， wèi wǒ xīn yōu。 kǎn wèi jiān bīng， zhèn wèi huáng， gěn wèi niǎo、 wèi zhōng。 lí wèi rì， gù yuē zhōng rì。 kǎn wèi bēi， zhèn wèi hào、 wèi lì， kǎn yǐn gù bù jiàn。 zhèn wèi lí hāo、 wèi jīng。 gěn wèi qián zhuó、 wèi zhì niǎo。 kǎn wèi xīn yōu。《 shī xiǎo yǎ》「 huáng niǎo huáng niǎo， wú jí yú gǔ， cǐ bāng zhī rén， bù wǒ kěn gǔ。 yán xuán yán guī， fù wǒ bāng zú。」 lín sì shuō cǐ shī。○ dì èr jù gè běn duō zuò cháng āi bēi chóu。 bái zuò gān， yī jiě zhī guài jiào dīng yàn yún「 chǔ cè『 fǔ zhuó bái lì』 shì yě。」
**EN:** Shike (噬嗑). On hard ice the yellow bird cries in grief all day. It sees no white grain, only weeds and wormwood. Time and again the fierce birds startle it — this is the sorrow of my heart. Kan is hard ice; Zhen is yellow; Gen is bird, is whole; Li is sun — hence "all day." Kan is grief; Zhen is crying, is grain; Kan is hidden — hence "sees not." Zhen is weeds and wormwood, is startling. Gen is the dark-beaked, the fierce birds; Kan is heart's sorrow. The Odes, Xiaoya, has: "Yellow bird, yellow bird, do not settle on the grain. The people of this land will not be good to me. I turn and return, back to my homeland." The Forest seems to speak of this ode. [uncertain: 黔啄 ("dark-beaked") is an unusual bird-image gloss; the verse's sense — a bird harassed by birds of prey — is kept.]

## Section 22 (賁)

**ZH:** 賁。室如懸磬，既危且殆。早見之士，依山谷處。艮為室，震為磬，上卦震覆，故曰懸磬。坎險故危殆。震為士、為旦，故曰早見之士。離為見也。艮為山谷，艮止故曰依。○谷處，元本作處穀，依宋本、汲古。
**PY:** bì。 shì rú xuán qìng， jì wēi qiě dài。 zǎo jiàn zhī shì， yī shān gǔ chù。 gěn wèi shì， zhèn wèi qìng， shàng guà zhèn fù， gù yuē xuán qìng。 kǎn xiǎn gù wēi dài。 zhèn wèi shì、 wèi dàn， gù yuē zǎo jiàn zhī shì。 lí wèi jiàn yě。 gěn wèi shān gǔ， gěn zhǐ gù yuē yī。○ gǔ chù， yuán běn zuò chù gǔ， yī sòng běn、 jí gǔ。
**EN:** Bi (賁). His house is like a hanging chime-stone — perilous and near to ruin. A scholar who sees early lodges in the mountain valley. Gen is the house; Zhen is the chime-stone; the upper trigram Zhen is inverted — hence "hanging chime." Kan is peril — hence "perilous and near to ruin." Zhen is the scholar, is dawn — hence "a scholar who sees early." Li is seeing. Gen is the mountain valley; Gen is stillness — hence "lodges."

## Section 23 (剝)

**ZH:** 剝。大禹戒路，蚩尤除道。周匝萬里，不危不殆。見其所使，無所不在。伏乾為王，故曰大禹。艮為路，伏兌為口，故曰大禹戒路，言警備也。坤為惡，故曰蚩尤。艮為道、為手，故曰除道。除，治也。反震為周，坤為萬里，坤安艮止，故不危殆。艮為僮僕，故曰所使，卦為大艮，故無不在。○戒，宋元本作式，依汲古。
**PY:** bō。 dà yǔ jiè lù， chī yóu chú dào。 zhōu zā wàn lǐ， bù wēi bù dài。 jiàn qí suǒ shǐ， wú suǒ bù zài。 fú qián wèi wáng， gù yuē dà yǔ。 gěn wèi lù， fú duì wèi kǒu， gù yuē dà yǔ jiè lù， yán jǐng bèi yě。 kūn wèi è， gù yuē chī yóu。 gěn wèi dào、 wèi shǒu， gù yuē chú dào。 chú， zhì yě。 fǎn zhèn wèi zhōu， kūn wèi wàn lǐ， kūn ān gěn zhǐ， gù bù wēi dài。 gěn wèi tóng pú， gù yuē suǒ shǐ， guà wèi dà gěn， gù wú bù zài。○ jiè， sòng yuán běn zuò shì， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Bo (剝). Great Yu keeps the road under watch; Chiyou (蚩尤) clears the way. Round and round ten thousand li, no peril, no ruin. Behold his servants — there is nowhere they are not. Hidden Qian is king — hence Great Yu. Gen is the road; hidden Dui is the mouth — hence "Great Yu keeps the road under watch," meaning guarded vigilance. Kun is evil — hence Chiyou. Gen is the way, is the hand — hence "clears the way"; 除 means to put in order. Inverted Zhen is round-about; Kun is ten thousand li; Kun is safe and Gen still — hence "no peril, no ruin." Gen is servant-lads — hence "his servants"; the hexagram is a great Gen — hence "nowhere they are not."

## Section 24 (複)

**ZH:** 複。三人為旅，俱歸北海。入門上堂，拜謁王母，飲勞我酒。震為人，數三，故曰三人，坤為海、為北，震為反，故曰俱歸北海。坤為門戶，震往，故曰入、曰上。坤為母，震為王，故曰王母。坤為我、為漿，故亦為酒，震為言，故曰勞。○王母，汲古作主母，依宋元本。上，元刊作升，依宋本、汲古。
**PY:** fù。 sān rén wèi lǚ， jù guī běi hǎi。 rù mén shàng táng， bài yè wáng mǔ， yǐn láo wǒ jiǔ。 zhèn wèi rén， shù sān， gù yuē sān rén， kūn wèi hǎi、 wèi běi， zhèn wèi fǎn， gù yuē jù guī běi hǎi。 kūn wèi mén hù， zhèn wǎng， gù yuē rù、 yuē shàng。 kūn wèi mǔ， zhèn wèi wáng， gù yuē wáng mǔ。 kūn wèi wǒ、 wèi jiāng， gù yì wèi jiǔ， zhèn wèi yán， gù yuē láo。○ wáng mǔ， jí gǔ zuò zhǔ mǔ， yī sòng yuán běn。 shàng， yuán kān zuò shēng， yī sòng běn、 jí gǔ。
**EN:** Fu (複). Three men make a company and together return to the Northern Sea. Entering the gate and mounting the hall, they pay audience to the Queen Mother, who pours wine to welcome me. Zhen is men; its number is three — hence "three men." Kun is the sea, is north; Zhen is returning — hence "together return to the Northern Sea." Kun is gate and door; Zhen is going — hence "entering," "mounting." Kun is mother; Zhen is king — hence "Queen Mother." Kun is me, is a drink, so also wine; Zhen is words — hence "welcome."

## Section 25 (無妄)

**ZH:** 無妄。傳言相誤，非乾徑路。鳴鼓逐狐，不知跡處。震為言，艮敗言，故曰誤，艮為徑路、為求。非乾徑路，言非所求之徑路也。震為鼓、為鳴，艮為狐。震為逐、為跡。巽為伏，故無跡。○乾，俗本皆誤於。
**PY:** wú wàng。 chuán yán xiāng wù， fēi qián jìng lù。 míng gǔ zhú hú， bù zhī jī chù。 zhèn wèi yán， gěn bài yán， gù yuē wù， gěn wèi jìng lù、 wèi qiú。 fēi qián jìng lù， yán fēi suǒ qiú zhī jìng lù yě。 zhèn wèi gǔ、 wèi míng， gěn wèi hú。 zhèn wèi zhú、 wèi jī。 xùn wèi fú， gù wú jī。○ qián， sú běn jiē wù yú。
**EN:** Wuwang (無妄). A message carried wrong mistook one another — not Qian's path. They sound the drum and chase the fox, not knowing where its tracks lie. Zhen is speech; Gen is spoiled speech — hence "mistook." Gen is path, is seeking: "not Qian's path" means not the path they sought. Zhen is drum, is sounding; Gen is fox; Zhen is chasing, is tracks. Xun is hiding — hence no tracks.

## Section 26 (大畜)

**ZH:** 大畜。三羊爭雌，相逐奔馳。終日不食，精氣勞疲。兌為羊，震數三，故曰三羊。兌為雌，艮為手，正反兩艮相對，故曰爭雌。震為馳逐，乾為日，兌為食，艮為終，艮止，故終日不食。乾為精氣，爭故勞疲。○羊，汲古作年，雌作妻，依宋元本。
**PY:** dà chù。 sān yáng zhēng cí， xiāng zhú bēn chí。 zhōng rì bù shí， jīng qì láo pí。 duì wèi yáng， zhèn shù sān， gù yuē sān yáng。 duì wèi cí， gěn wèi shǒu， zhèng fǎn liǎng gěn xiāng duì， gù yuē zhēng cí。 zhèn wèi chí zhú， qián wèi rì， duì wèi shí， gěn wèi zhōng， gěn zhǐ， gù zhōng rì bù shí。 qián wèi jīng qì， zhēng gù láo pí。○ yáng， jí gǔ zuò nián， cí zuò qī， yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Dachu (大畜). Three rams fight for the female, chasing one another at a gallop. All day they do not eat; their vital spirit is wearied out. Dui is the ram; Zhen's number is three — hence "three rams." Dui is the female; Gen is the hand; upright and inverted Gen face each other — hence "fight for the female." Zhen is galloping; Qian is the day; Dui is eating; Gen is whole, Gen is still — hence "all day they do not eat." Qian is vital spirit; from fighting comes weariness.

## Section 27 (頤)

**ZH:** 頤。純服素裳，載主以興。德義茂生，天下歸仁。坤為黑、為裳，震為白，故曰純服素裳。震為主、為載、為興，故曰載主以興。坤為義，震為德、為生，故曰德義茂生。坤為天下，震為歸、為仁，故曰天下歸仁。史記武王載木主，觀兵孟津，八百諸侯來歸，故必素裳。○素，各本皆作黃，載主作戴上，茂作既，均非。茂依比之益校。
**PY:** yí。 chún fú sù shang， zài zhǔ yǐ xìng。 dé yì mào shēng， tiān xià guī rén。 kūn wèi hēi、 wèi shang， zhèn wèi bái， gù yuē chún fú sù shang。 zhèn wèi zhǔ、 wèi zài、 wèi xìng， gù yuē zài zhǔ yǐ xìng。 kūn wèi yì， zhèn wèi dé、 wèi shēng， gù yuē dé yì mào shēng。 kūn wèi tiān xià， zhèn wèi guī、 wèi rén， gù yuē tiān xià guī rén。 shǐ jì wǔ wáng zài mù zhǔ， guān bīng mèng jīn， bā bǎi zhū hóu lái guī， gù bì sù shang。○ sù， gè běn jiē zuò huáng， zài zhǔ zuò dài shàng， mào zuò jì， jūn fēi。 mào yī bǐ zhī yì jiào。
**EN:** Yi (頤). In pure white and undyed skirts, bearing the tablet, he arises. Virtue and rightness flourish; all under heaven returns to humaneness. Kun is black, is skirts; Zhen is white — hence "pure white and undyed skirts." Zhen is the tablet, is bearing, is arising — hence "bearing the tablet, he arises." Kun is rightness; Zhen is virtue, is growth — hence "virtue and rightness flourish." Kun is the world; Zhen is returning, is humaneness — hence "the world returns to humaneness." The Records say King Wu, bearing the wooden tablet, reviewed the troops at Mengjin and eight hundred lords came to him — so the skirts must be undyed.

## Section 28 (大過)

**ZH:** 大過。桀跖並處，人民勞苦。擁兵荷糧，戰於齊魯。伏坤為惡，故曰桀。巽為盜，故曰跖。坤為人民、為憂、為勞，故曰勞苦。伏艮為兵、為荷，震為糧，故曰擁兵荷糧。巽齊兌魯，正覆艮相背，艮手，故曰戰。○勞，依元刊。汲古作愁。
**PY:** dà guò。 jié zhí bìng chù， rén mín láo kǔ。 yōng bīng hé liáng， zhàn yú qí lǔ。 fú kūn wèi è， gù yuē jié。 xùn wèi dào， gù yuē zhí。 kūn wèi rén mín、 wèi yōu、 wèi láo， gù yuē láo kǔ。 fú gěn wèi bīng、 wèi hé， zhèn wèi liáng， gù yuē yōng bīng hé liáng。 xùn qí duì lǔ， zhèng fù gěn xiāng bèi， gěn shǒu， gù yuē zhàn。○ láo， yī yuán kān。 jí gǔ zuò chóu。
**EN:** Daguo (大過). Jie and Robber Zhi abide together; the people toil and suffer. Bearing arms, carrying grain, they war in Qi and Lu. Hidden Kun is evil — hence Jie. Xun is the thief — hence Zhi. Kun is the people, is care, is toil — hence "toil and suffer." Hidden Gen is arms, is bearing; Zhen is grain — hence "bearing arms, carrying grain." Xun is Qi, Dui is Lu; upright and inverted Gen face away from each other; Gen is the hand — hence "they war."

## Section 29 (坎)

**ZH:** 坎。黃鳥採菉，既嫁不答。念我父兄，思複邦國。黃鳥、採菉皆小雅篇名，皆傷怨曠，故曰既嫁不答。震為黃、為菉，艮為鳥、為採，故曰黃鳥採菉。震為嫁、為言，艮止，故曰不答。坎為思念，震為父兄、為複，艮為邦國。○採菉，自明以後本皆誤為來集，茲依宋元本。
**PY:** kǎn。 huáng niǎo cǎi lù， jì jià bù dá。 niàn wǒ fù xiōng， sī fù bāng guó。 huáng niǎo、 cǎi lù jiē xiǎo yǎ piān míng， jiē shāng yuàn kuàng， gù yuē jì jià bù dá。 zhèn wèi huáng、 wèi lù， gěn wèi niǎo、 wèi cǎi， gù yuē huáng niǎo cǎi lù。 zhèn wèi jià、 wèi yán， gěn zhǐ， gù yuē bù dá。 kǎn wèi sī niàn， zhèn wèi fù xiōng、 wèi fù， gěn wèi bāng guó。○ cǎi lù， zì míng yǐ hòu běn jiē wù wèi lái jí， zī yī sòng yuán běn。
**EN:** Kan (坎). "Yellow Bird," "Gathering Green" — married off, and he does not answer. I think of my father and brothers; I long to return to my homeland. "Yellow Bird" and "Gathering Green" are both Odes titles from the Xiaoya; both grieve at abandonment — hence "married off, and he does not answer." Zhen is yellow, is green; Gen is bird, is gathering — hence the titles. Zhen is marrying off, is speaking; Gen is stillness — hence "does not answer." Kan is longing; Zhen is father and brothers, is returning; Gen is homeland.

## Section 30 (離)

**ZH:** 離。胎生孚乳，長息成就。充滿帝室，家國昌富。通坎。互震為胎、為生，坎為孚，艮為乳，故曰胎生孚乳。震為長息，艮為成，故曰成就。震為帝、為昌，艮為室、為家國，全取旁通象。
**PY:** lí。 tāi shēng fú rǔ， zhǎng xī chéng jiù。 chōng mǎn dì shì， jiā guó chāng fù。 tōng kǎn。 hù zhèn wèi tāi、 wèi shēng， kǎn wèi fú， gěn wèi rǔ， gù yuē tāi shēng fú rǔ。 zhèn wèi zhǎng xī， gěn wèi chéng， gù yuē chéng jiù。 zhèn wèi dì、 wèi chāng， gěn wèi shì、 wèi jiā guó， quán qǔ páng tōng xiàng。
**EN:** Li (離). Born in the womb, nurtured at the breast, growing to maturity. Filling the imperial chambers, the house and state flourish. Opposing hexagram: Kan. The nuclear Zhen is the womb, is birth; Kan is brooding; Gen is the breast — hence "born in the womb, nurtured at the breast." Zhen is growing; Gen is completion — hence "growing to maturity." Zhen is the sovereign, is flourishing; Gen is the chamber, is house and state — all taken from the opposing hexagram's images.

## Section 31 (咸)

**ZH:** 咸。三人求橘，反得丹穴。女清以富，黃金百鎰。互乾為人，艮數三，故曰三人。艮為求，乾為橘，故曰三人求橘。兌為穴，乾為赤，故曰丹穴。巽為女，承乾故富，故為金。伏坤為黃，故曰黃金。乾為百。顧千里曰「清者，人名。《貨殖傳》所謂巴寡婦清也。清家得丹穴。」○丹穴，依宋元本，汲古作大慄。清，依顧校。諸本皆作貴，惟汲古作青，可証顧說是也。
**PY:** xián。 sān rén qiú jú， fǎn dé dān xué。 nǚ qīng yǐ fù， huáng jīn bǎi yì。 hù qián wèi rén， gěn shù sān， gù yuē sān rén。 gěn wèi qiú， qián wèi jú， gù yuē sān rén qiú jú。 duì wèi xué， qián wèi chì， gù yuē dān xué。 xùn wèi nǚ， chéng qián gù fù， gù wèi jīn。 fú kūn wèi huáng， gù yuē huáng jīn。 qián wèi bǎi。 gù qiān lǐ yuē「 qīng zhě， rén míng。《 huò zhí chuán》 suǒ wèi bā guǎ fù qīng yě。 qīng jiā dé dān xué。」○ dān xué， yī sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ zuò dà lì。 qīng， yī gù jiào。 zhū běn jiē zuò guì， wéi jí gǔ zuò qīng， kě zhèng gù shuō shì yě。
**EN:** Xian (咸). Three men went seeking oranges and instead found the cinnabar cave. The widow Qing grew rich on it: a hundred yi of yellow gold. The nuclear Qian is men; Gen's number is three — hence "three men." Gen is seeking; Qian is oranges — hence "three men went seeking oranges." Dui is the cave; Qian is red — hence "the cinnabar cave." Xun is the woman; supported by Qian she is rich, and so is gold. Hidden Kun is yellow — hence "yellow gold." Qian is a hundred. Gu Qianli (顧千里) says: "Qing is a person's name — the widow Qing of Ba, in the 'Huozhi zhuan' (貨殖傳), whose family obtained the cinnabar cave."

## Section 32 (恆)

**ZH:** 恆。東山西嶽，會合俱食。百家送從，以成恩福。通益。互艮為山嶽。震東，本卦。兌為西，故曰東山西嶽。坤為會合，震為食，正覆震，故曰俱食。艮為家，震為百、為從、為恩福，艮為成也。
**PY:** héng。 dōng shān xī yuè， huì hé jù shí。 bǎi jiā sòng cóng， yǐ chéng ēn fú。 tōng yì。 hù gěn wèi shān yuè。 zhèn dōng， běn guà。 duì wèi xī， gù yuē dōng shān xī yuè。 kūn wèi huì hé， zhèn wèi shí， zhèng fù zhèn， gù yuē jù shí。 gěn wèi jiā， zhèn wèi bǎi、 wèi cóng、 wèi ēn fú， gěn wèi chéng yě。
**EN:** Heng (恆). The eastern mountain and the western peak meet and eat together. A hundred households escort the bride, and grace and fortune are made. Opposing hexagram: Yi. The nuclear Gen is mountain and peak. Zhen is east — the primary trigram; Dui is west — hence "the eastern mountain and the western peak." Kun is meeting; Zhen is eating; Zhen inverted — hence "eat together." Gen is household; Zhen is a hundred, is escorting, is grace and fortune; Gen is completion.

## Section 33 (遯)

**ZH:** 遯。眵雞無距，與鵲交鬬。翅折目盲，為鳩所傷。巽為雞。《說文》：「目眥傷曰眵」，眥，眼角也。艮離目不全，故曰眵雞。震伏，故無距。艮為鵲、為鬬，震為羽翰。震覆，故翅折。離為目，目眥下裂，故為目盲。艮為鳥、為鳩，巽羸故傷。○眵，依汲古，宋元本作弱，非。
**PY:** dùn。 chī jī wú jù， yǔ què jiāo dòu。 chì zhé mù máng， wèi jiū suǒ shāng。 xùn wèi jī。《 shuō wén》：「 mù zì shāng yuē chī」， zì， yǎn jiǎo yě。 gěn lí mù bù quán， gù yuē chī jī。 zhèn fú， gù wú jù。 gěn wèi què、 wèi dòu， zhèn wèi yǔ hàn。 zhèn fù， gù chì zhé。 lí wèi mù， mù zì xià liè， gù wèi mù máng。 gěn wèi niǎo、 wèi jiū， xùn léi gù shāng。○ chī， yī jí gǔ， sòng yuán běn zuò ruò， fēi。
**EN:** Dun (遯). The eye-hurt chicken, without spurs, fights a magpie. Wing broken, eye blinded, it is wounded by the dove. Xun is the chicken. The Shuowen says: "an injury at the eye's corner is 眵 (chi)"; 眥 is the eye's corner. Gen and Li leave the eye incomplete — hence "eye-hurt chicken." Zhen is hidden — hence "without spurs." Gen is the magpie, is fighting; Zhen is the pinion; Zhen inverted — hence "wing broken." Li is the eye; the eye's corner is torn below — hence "eye blinded." Gen is bird, is dove; Xun is weak — hence "wounded."

## Section 34 (大壯)

**ZH:** 大壯。卻大牆壞，蠧眾木折。狼虎為政，天降罪罰。高弒望夷，胡亥以斃。通觀。巽為卻、為壞、為牆，故曰卻大牆壞。巽為蟲、為木，坤眾，故曰蠧眾，風隕故木折。艮為狼虎，坤為政，故曰狼虎為政。乾為天，坤為罪罰，故曰天降罪罰。艮為宦寺，坤為弒，艮為觀，坤為夷，故曰高弒望夷，言趙高弒胡亥於望夷宮也。坤為胡，居亥，又為斃也。雖人名、地名，無不從象生，本之《易》也。
**PY:** dà zhuàng。 què dà qiáng huài， dù zhòng mù zhé。 láng hǔ wèi zhèng， tiān jiàng zuì fá。 gāo shì wàng yí， hú hài yǐ bì。 tōng guān。 xùn wèi què、 wèi huài、 wèi qiáng， gù yuē què dà qiáng huài。 xùn wèi chóng、 wèi mù， kūn zhòng， gù yuē dù zhòng， fēng yǔn gù mù zhé。 gěn wèi láng hǔ， kūn wèi zhèng， gù yuē láo hǔ wèi zhèng。 qián wèi tiān， kūn wèi zuì fá， gù yuē tiān jiàng zuì fá。 gěn wèi huàn sì， kūn wèi shì， gěn wèi guān， kūn wèi yí， gù yuē gāo shì wàng yí， yán zhào gāo shì hú hài yú wàng yí gōng yě。 kūn wèi hú， jū hài， yòu wèi bì yě。 suī rén míng、 dì míng， wú bù cóng xiàng shēng， běn zhī《 yì》 yě。
**EN:** Dazhuang (大壯). A great wall falls back and breaks; woodworms swarm, the timbers snap. Wolf and tiger hold the government; Heaven sends down crime and punishment. Gao slays at Wangyi Palace — Hu Hai is killed. Opposing hexagram: Guan. Xun is falling back, is breaking, is wall — hence "a great wall falls back and breaks." Xun is worm, is wood; Kun is a swarm — hence "woodworms swarm"; wind falls on them — hence "the timbers snap." Gen is wolf and tiger; Kun is government — hence "wolf and tiger hold the government." Qian is Heaven; Kun is crime and punishment — hence "Heaven sends down crime and punishment." Gen is the eunuch-palace officer; Kun is slaying; Gen is watching; Kun is Yi — hence "Gao slays at Wangyi," meaning Zhao Gao (趙高) slew Hu Hai (胡亥) at Wangyi Palace. Kun is Hu, residing in Hai, and is also perishing. Though there are personal names and place names, all arise from the images — as is rooted in the Changes.

## Section 35 (晉)

**ZH:** 晉。三癡俱走，迷路失道。惑不知歸，反入患口。坤為癡，爻數三，故曰三癡。互艮為道路，坤迷，故曰迷路失道。坎為惑，震為歸，陽居四不反初，故曰不知歸。坎為患，四五形兌，故曰患口。○惑，元刊作或，依汲古。
**PY:** jìn。 sān chī jù zǒu， mí lù shī dào。 huò bù zhī guī， fǎn rù huàn kǒu。 kūn wèi chī， yáo shù sān， gù yuē sān chī。 hù gěn wèi dào lù， kūn mí， gù yuē mí lù shī dào。 kǎn wèi huò， zhèn wèi guī， yáng jū sì bù fǎn chū， gù yuē bù zhī guī。 kǎn wèi huàn， sì wǔ xíng duì， gù yuē huàn kǒu。○ huò， yuán kān zuò huò， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Jin (晉). Three fools run together, lose the road and lose the way. Confused, not knowing how to return, they fall instead into calamity's mouth. Kun is foolishness; the lines number three — hence "three fools." The nuclear Gen is road and way; Kun is lost — hence "lose the road and lose the way." Kan is confusion; Zhen is returning; the yang in the fourth place does not return to the first — hence "not knowing how to return." Kan is calamity; lines four and five form Dui — hence "calamity's mouth."

## Section 36 (明夷)

**ZH:** 明夷。弓矢俱張，把彈折弦。丸發不至，道遇害患。互坎為弓、為矢，震為張，故曰弓矢俱張。坎為彈，伏巽為弦，坎折，故曰把彈折弦。坎為丸，震為發，坤閉，故不至。震為道塗，坤為患害，故曰道遇患害。
**PY:** míng yí。 gōng shǐ jù zhāng， bǎ dàn zhé xián。 wán fā bù zhì， dào yù hài huàn。 hù kǎn wèi gōng、 wèi shǐ， zhèn wèi zhāng， gù yuē gōng shǐ jù zhāng。 kǎn wèi dàn， fú xùn wèi xián， kǎn zhé， gù yuē bǎ dàn zhé xián。 kǎn wèi wán， zhèn wèi fā， kūn bì， gù bù zhì。 zhèn wèi dào tú， kūn wèi huàn hài， gù yuē dào yù huàn hài。
**EN:** Mingyi (明夷). Bow and arrows both drawn — grasping the pellet, he breaks the string. The pellet flies but does not arrive; on the road he meets calamity. The nuclear Kan is bow, is arrow; Zhen is drawing — hence "bow and arrows both drawn." Kan is the pellet; hidden Xun is the string; Kan is breaking — hence "grasping the pellet, he breaks the string." Kan is the pellet-ball; Zhen is flying; Kun is closed — hence "does not arrive." Zhen is the road; Kun is calamity and harm — hence "on the road he meets calamity."

## Section 37 (家人)

**ZH:** 家人。三女求夫，伺候山隅，不見複關，長思憂嘆。卦二離、一巽，皆女象，坎為夫，故曰三女求夫。艮為關，卦有兩半艮形，故曰複關；坎伏，故不見。不見複關，詩衛風語也。艮止，故曰伺候。艮山，故曰山隅。坎為憂思，故曰長思憂嘆。○汲古二三句倒置，依元本。元本憂嘆作嘆憂，失韻，依汲古。
**PY:** jiā rén。 sān nǚ qiú fū， cì hòu shān yú， bù jiàn fù guān， zhǎng sī yōu tàn。 guà èr lí、 yī xùn， jiē nǚ xiàng， kǎn wèi fū， gù yuē sān nǚ qiú fū。 gěn wèi guān， guà yǒu liǎng bàn gěn xíng， gù yuē fù guān； kǎn fú， gù bù jiàn。 bù jiàn fù guān， shī wèi fēng yǔ yě。 gěn zhǐ， gù yuē cì hòu。 gěn shān， gù yuē shān yú。 kǎn wèi yōu sī， gù yuē zhǎng sī yōu tàn。○ jí gǔ èr sān jù dào zhì， yī yuán běn。 yuán běn yōu tàn zuò tàn yōu， shī yùn， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Jiaren (家人). Three women seek a husband, waiting upon the mountain's corner; they cannot see the Double Pass, and long they sigh in sorrow. The hexagram has two Li and one Xun — all images of women; Kan is the husband — hence "three women seek a husband." Gen is the pass; the hexagram has two half-Gen forms — hence "Double Pass"; Kan is hidden — hence "cannot see." "Cannot see the Double Pass" is a phrase of the Weifeng in the Odes. ## Section 43 (夬)

The remaining images are mostly unclear. ## Section 44 (姤)

## Section 45 (萃)

**EN:** Cui (萃). Weak and inferior, feeble of strength, timid and afraid — like the hedgehog meeting the magpie, not daring to resist or fight. Kun is soft — hence "weak and inferior." The commentary to the Records' "Tortoise and Yarrow" chapter says: "The hedgehog can master the tiger, but meeting the magpie it rolls onto its back."

## Section 47 (困)

**ZH:** 困。噂噂所言，莫如我垣。歡喜堅固，可以長安。《詩》：「噂沓背憎「箋云「噂噂，對語，背則相憎。」困三至上，正反兩兌口相背，故易曰有言不信，其伏象則正反兩震言相對，故曰噂噂所言。巽為垣墉，坎陷，故莫如我垣。兌悅而伏艮，故曰歡喜堅固，曰長安。○如，俗本誤加。如者，往也，宋本、汲古本均不誤，元本作知。
**PY:** kùn。 zǔn zǔn suǒ yán， mò rú wǒ yuán。 huān xǐ jiān gù， kě yǐ zhǎng ān。《 shī》：「 zǔn dá bèi zēng「 jiān yún「 zǔn zǔn， duì yǔ， bèi zé xiāng zēng。」 kùn sān zhì shàng， zhèng fǎn liǎng duì kǒu xiāng bèi， gù yì yuē yǒu yán bù xìn， qí fú xiàng zé zhèng fǎn liǎng zhèn yán xiāng duì， gù yuē zǔn zǔn suǒ yán。 xùn wèi yuán yōng， kǎn xiàn， gù mò rú wǒ yuán。 duì yuè ér fú gěn， gù yuē huān xǐ jiān gù， yuē zhǎng ān。○ rú， sú běn wù jiā。 rú zhě， wǎng yě， sòng běn、 jí gǔ běn jūn bù wù， yuán běn zuò zhī。
**EN:** Kun (困). The chattering of their words — none like my wall. Joyful and firm, it can long keep peace. Xun is wall and rampart; Kan is sinking — hence "none like my wall." Dui is joy with hidden Gen — hence "joyful and firm," "can long keep peace."

## Section 48 (井)

**PY:** jǐng。 yuán míng qí shān， biē yīng yōu yuān。 nán nǚ gòu jīng， wàn wù huà shēng。 wén wáng yǐ chéng， wèi kāi zhōu tíng。 tōng shì kē。 hù gěn wèi yuán biē、 wèi shān， xià zhèn liǎng qí， gù yuē qí shān。 ér chū zhì sì zhèng fǎn gěn zhèn， zhèn wèi míng， gù shàng míng xià yīng， yǔ《 zhōng fú》 jiǔ èr「 míng hè zài yīn， qí zǐ hé zhī」 yòng xiàng tóng yě。 kǎn wèi yōu， kǎn nán lí nǚ， ér kǎn wèi jīng， gù yuē nán nǚ gòu jīng， zhèn wèi wàn wù、 wèi shēng、 wèi wáng。 shàng lí wèi wén， gù yuē wén wáng yǐ chéng。 gěn wèi chéng yě， gěn yòu wèi tíng。 zhèn wèi zhōu、 wèi kāi， gù yuē wèi kāi zhōu tíng。○ yuán， yī jí gǔ， sòng yuán běn zuò yuè。 yōu yuān， yī sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ zuò shān yuān。

## Section 49 (革)

**ZH:** 革。玄黃虺隤，行者勞罷。役夫憔悴，逾時不歸。通蒙。互震為玄黃。《詩毛傳》「玄黃虺隤，病也。」蒙坎為病、為罷勞，震為行，故曰行者勞罷。坎為夫、為勞卦，故曰役夫憔悴。艮為時，在外，故逾時不歸。
**PY:** gé。 xuán huáng huī tuí， xíng zhě láo bà。 yì fū qiáo cuì， yú shí bù guī。 tōng méng。 hù zhèn wèi xuán huáng。《 shī máo chuán》「 xuán huáng huī tuí， bìng yě。」 méng kǎn wèi bìng、 wèi bà láo， zhèn wèi xíng， gù yuē xíng zhě láo bà。 kǎn wèi fū、 wèi láo guà， gù yuē yì fū qiáo cuì。 gěn wèi shí， zài wài， gù yú shí bù guī。
**EN:** Ge (革). Dusky yellow, weary and worn — the traveler toils and tires. The laborer is haggard and worn; past his time, he does not return. Opposing hexagram: Meng. The nuclear Zhen is dusky yellow. The Mao commentary to the Odes: "dusky yellow, weary and worn, is sickness." Meng's Kan is sickness, is weary toil; Zhen is traveling — hence "the traveler toils and tires." Kan is the laborer, is the trigram of toil — hence "the laborer is haggard and worn." Gen is time, and is outside — hence "past his time, he does not return."

## Section 50 (鼎)

**ZH:** 鼎。弱足刖跟，不利出門。市賈無利，折亡為患。下巽，震伏不見，故曰弱足刖跟，故不利出門，鼎初六「鼎，顛趾」即如是取象。三五伏艮，艮為門。巽為市賈、為利，兌折，故不利、故折亡，大坎為患。○利，汲古訛私，無，從元本，宋本作不。
**PY:** dǐng。 ruò zú yuè gēn， bù lì chū mén。 shì jiǎ wú lì， zhé wáng wèi huàn。 xià xùn， zhèn fú bù jiàn， gù yuē ruò zú yuè gēn， gù bù lì chū mén， dǐng chū liù「 dǐng， diān zhǐ」 jí rú shì qǔ xiàng。 sān wǔ fú gěn， gěn wèi mén。 xùn wèi shì jiǎ、 wèi lì， duì zhé， gù bù lì、 gù zhé wáng， dà kǎn wèi huàn。○ lì， jí gǔ é sī， wú， cóng yuán běn， sòng běn zuò bù。
**EN:** Ding (鼎). Weak of foot, heel cut off — no profit in going out the gate. In market trade there is no gain; ruin and loss are the calamity. Below is Xun; Zhen is hidden and unseen — hence "weak of foot, heel cut off," hence "no profit in going out the gate." Ding's first line, "the cauldron's upturned toes," takes the image in just this way. Lines three and five have hidden Gen; Gen is the gate. Xun is market trade, is gain; Dui is ruin — hence "no gain," hence "ruin and loss." Great Kan is the calamity.

## Section 51 (震)

**ZH:** 震。懸貆素餐，居非其官。失輿剝廬，休坐徙居。懸貆素餐，《詩伐檀》語也，互艮為貆，伏巽繩，故懸貆，震為食、為白，故曰素餐。艮為居、為官，艮覆，故居非其官。震為輿，坎失，故曰失輿。艮為廬，艮覆，故剝廬，「君子得輿，小人剝廬」，《剝》上九爻詞也。艮為坐、為居，艮覆，故休坐徙居。○官，依元本，宋本作安。徙，依元本，宋本作徒。餐，從宋本，元本訛饕。
**PY:** zhèn。 xuán huán sù cān， jū fēi qí guān。 shī yú bō lú， xiū zuò xǐ jū。 xuán huán sù cān，《 shī fá tán》 yǔ yě， hù gěn wèi huán， fú xùn shéng， gù xuán huán， zhèn wèi shí、 wèi bái， gù yuē sù cān。 gěn wèi jū、 wèi guān， gěn fù， gù jū fēi qí guān。 zhèn wèi yú， kǎn shī， gù yuē shī yú。 gěn wèi lú， gěn fù， gù bō lú，「 jūn zǐ dé yú， xiǎo rén bō lú」，《 bō》 shàng jiǔ yáo cí yě。 gěn wèi zuò、 wèi jū， gěn fù， gù xiū zuò xǐ jū。○ guān， yī yuán běn， sòng běn zuò ān。 xǐ， yī yuán běn， sòng běn zuò tú。 cān， cóng sòng běn， yuán běn é tāo。
**EN:** Zhen (震). Hanging the badger, eating unearned fare — dwelling where his office is not. Losing his carriage, stripped of his hut, he sits idle and moves his dwelling. "Hanging the badger, eating unearned fare" is the language of the Odes' "Cutting Sandalwood." The nuclear Gen is the badger; hidden Xun is the rope — hence "hanging the badger"; Zhen is food, is white — hence "unearned fare." Gen is dwelling, is office; Gen inverted — hence "dwelling where his office is not." Zhen is the carriage; Kan is losing — hence "losing his carriage." Gen is the hut; Gen inverted — hence "stripped of his hut": "the gentleman gets a carriage; the small man is stripped of his hut" — Bo's top line. Gen is sitting, is dwelling; Gen inverted — hence "sits idle and moves his dwelling."

## Section 52 (艮)

**ZH:** 艮。民怯城惡，奸人所伏。寇賊大至，入我郛郭，妻子俘獲。《左傳》：「成九年楚伐莒，莒城惡，眾潰」。艮為城，坎為民、為怯、為陷，故曰民怯城惡。坎為伏、為奸，互震為人，故曰奸人所伏。坎為寇賊，艮為郛郭，伏巽為入，故曰入我郛郭。震為子，伏兌為妻，艮止，故曰妻子俘獲。○城，依宋元本，汲古訛伐。郛、俘，依宋元本，汲古作邦、系。
**PY:** gěn。 mín qiè chéng è， jiān rén suǒ fú。 kòu zéi dà zhì， rù wǒ fú guō， qī zǐ fú huò。《 zuǒ chuán》：「 chéng jiǔ nián chǔ fá jǔ， jǔ chéng è， zhòng kuì」。 gěn wèi chéng， kǎn wèi mín、 wèi qiè、 wèi xiàn， gù yuē mín qiè chéng è。 kǎn wèi fú、 wèi jiān， hù zhèn wèi rén， gù yuē jiān rén suǒ fú。 kǎn wèi kòu zéi， gěn wèi fú guō， fú xùn wèi rù， gù yuē rù wǒ fú guō。 zhèn wèi zǐ， fú duì wèi qī， gěn zhǐ， gù yuē qī zǐ fú huò。○ chéng， yī sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ é fá。 fú、 fú， yī sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ zuò bāng、 xì。
**EN:** Gen (艮). The people afraid, the wall unsound — the lurking-place of traitors. When the brigands arrive in force and enter my outer wall, wife and children are taken captive. The Zuo Tradition has: "in Cheng's ninth year Chu attacked Ju; Ju's wall was unsound, and the host broke." Gen is the wall; Kan is the people, is fear, is ruin — hence "the people afraid, the wall unsound." Kan is lurking, is traitors; the nuclear Zhen is men — hence "the lurking-place of traitors." Kan is brigands; Gen is the outer wall; hidden Xun is entering — hence "enter my outer wall." Zhen is son; hidden Dui is wife; Gen is stillness — hence "wife and children are taken captive."

## Section 53 (漸)

**ZH:** 漸。陽低頭，陰仰首，水為災，傷我寶，進不利，難生子。坎為首，坎伏故低，坎陽卦而在下，離陰卦在坎上，故曰陰仰首。坎為水災，震為玉、為寶，震覆，故傷。震為行、為子、為生，震覆，故不利、故難。○寶，從汲古，宋元作足。難生子，依宋元本，汲古作生其子，與《易》「婦孕不育」旨背。
**PY:** jiàn。 yáng dī tóu， yīn yǎng shǒu， shuǐ wèi zāi， shāng wǒ bǎo， jìn bù lì， nán shēng zi。 kǎn wèi shǒu， kǎn fú gù dī， kǎn yáng guà ér zài xià， lí yīn guà zài kǎn shàng， gù yuē yīn yǎng shǒu。 kǎn wèi shuǐ zāi， zhèn wèi yù、 wèi bǎo， zhèn fù， gù shāng。 zhèn wèi xíng、 wèi zǐ、 wèi shēng， zhèn fù， gù bù lì、 gù nán。○ bǎo， cóng jí gǔ， sòng yuán zuò zú。 nán shēng zi， yī sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ zuò shēng qí zǐ， yǔ《 yì》「 fù yùn bù yù」 zhǐ bèi。
**EN:** Jian (漸). The yang bows its head; the yin lifts its head. Water makes calamity; it harms my treasure. Advancing is unprofitable; hard to bear a child. Kan is the head; Kan is hidden — hence "bows"; Kan is a yang trigram yet lies below, and Li, a yin trigram, lies above Kan — hence "the yin lifts its head." Kan is water calamity; Zhen is jade, is treasure; Zhen inverted — hence "harms." Zhen is advancing, is son, is bearing; Zhen inverted — hence "unprofitable," hence "hard."

## Section 54 (歸妹)

**ZH:** 歸妹。背北相憎，心意不同，如火與金。兌伏艮，艮為背，坎為北、為憎、為心意，離為火，伏艮為金。《詩十月》篇「噂沓背憎。」○宋元本作艮辭，金讀略如京，與憎同協。
**PY:** guī mèi。 bèi běi xiāng zēng， xīn yì bù tóng， rú huǒ yǔ jīn。 duì fú gěn， gěn wèi bèi， kǎn wèi běi、 wèi zēng、 wèi xīn yì， lí wèi huǒ， fú gěn wèi jīn。《 shī shí yuè》 piān「 zǔn dá bèi zēng。」○ sòng yuán běn zuò gěn cí， jīn dú lüè rú jīng， yǔ zēng tóng xié。
**EN:** Guimei (歸妹). Back to the north they hate each other; hearts and minds do not agree — like fire and metal. Dui's hidden trigram is Gen; Gen is the back; Kan is the north, is hatred, is heart and mind; Li is fire; hidden Gen is metal. The Odes' "Tenth Month" has: "chattering, hating behind the back."

## Section 55 (豐)

**ZH:** 豐。太微帝室，黃帝所值。藩屏周衛，不可得入，常安無患。離為星，故曰太微、曰帝室。帝室者，帝座，即紫垣也。震為帝，伏坎為宮室，故曰帝室。震為黃，故曰黃帝。黃帝，軒轅星也。震為叢木、為屏藩、為周、為衛，巽入巽伏，故不可入。○周，從宋元本，汲古作固，於卦象無著。值，從汲古，宋元皆作宜，不協。
**PY:** fēng。 tài wēi dì shì， huáng dì suǒ zhí。 fān píng zhōu wèi， bù kě dé rù， cháng ān wú huàn。 lí wèi xīng， gù yuē tài wēi、 yuē dì shì。 dì shì zhě， dì zuò， jí zǐ yuán yě。 zhèn wèi dì， fú kǎn wèi gōng shì， gù yuē dì shì。 zhèn wèi huáng， gù yuē huáng dì。 huáng dì， xuān yuán xīng yě。 zhèn wèi cóng mù、 wèi píng fān、 wèi zhōu、 wèi wèi， xùn rù xùn fú， gù bù kě rù。○ zhōu， cóng sòng yuán běn， jí gǔ zuò gù， yú guà xiàng wú zhuó。 zhí， cóng jí gǔ， sòng yuán jiē zuò yí， bù xié。
**EN:** Feng (豐). The Taiwei, the imperial chamber, where the Yellow Emperor sits. Fenced, screened, surrounded, and guarded — none can enter in, ever at peace and without harm. Li is the star — hence "Taiwei," hence "the imperial chamber." The imperial chamber is the imperial seat, that is, the Purple Enclosure. Zhen is the sovereign; hidden Kan is palace and chamber — hence "the imperial chamber." Zhen is yellow — hence "the Yellow Emperor"; the Yellow Emperor is the Xuanyuan star. Zhen is thicket-wood, is screening and fencing, is surrounding, is guarding; Xun enters and Xun is hidden — hence "none can enter in."

## Section 56 (旅)

**ZH:** 旅。繭慄犧牲，敬享鬼神。神嗜飲食，受福多孫。互巽，故曰繭，艮為果，故曰慄。離為牛，故曰犧牲。《禮》「祭天地之牛角繭慄」，言其色似之也，故曰繭慄犧牲。伏坎為鬼，震為神、為飲食，故曰神嗜飲食。艮為孫，坎眾，故多孫。
**PY:** lǚ。 jiǎn lì xī shēng， jìng xiǎng guǐ shén。 shén shì yǐn shí， shòu fú duō sūn。 hù xùn， gù yuē jiǎn， gěn wèi guǒ， gù yuē lì。 lí wèi niú， gù yuē xī shēng。《 lǐ》「 jì tiān dì zhī niú jiǎo jiǎn lì」， yán qí sè sì zhī yě， gù yuē jiǎn lì xī shēng。 fú kǎn wèi guǐ， zhèn wèi shén、 wèi yǐn shí， gù yuē shén shì yǐn shí。 gěn wèi sūn， kǎn zhòng， gù duō sūn。
**EN:** Lü (旅). With cocoon-chestnut victims he reverently offers to the ghosts and spirits. The spirits savor the food and drink, and he receives fortune in many sons. The nuclear Xun — hence "cocoon"; Gen is the chestnut — hence "chestnut." Li is the ox — hence "victims." The Ritual says: "the horns of the sacrificial ox of heaven and earth are cocoon-chestnut" — meaning their color resembles them — hence "cocoon-chestnut victims." Hidden Kan is the ghosts; Zhen is the spirits, is food and drink — hence "the spirits savor the food and drink." Gen is sons; Kan is many — hence "many sons."

## Section 57 (巽)

**ZH:** 巽。出門逢惡，為患為怨。更相擊刺，傷我手端。通震。互艮，故曰出門，坎為惡、為患、為怨，初至四反正艮，艮手，故更相擊刺，坎折，故傷手。○患，宋本作禍。此全用震象，故互坎患。
**PY:** xùn。 chū mén féng è， wèi huàn wèi yuàn。 gèng xiāng jī cì， shāng wǒ shǒu duān。 tōng zhèn。 hù gěn， gù yuē chū mén， kǎn wèi è、 wèi huàn、 wèi yuàn， chū zhì sì fǎn zhèng gěn， gěn shǒu， gù gèng xiāng jī cì， kǎn zhé， gù shāng shǒu。○ huàn， sòng běn zuò huò。 cǐ quán yòng zhèn xiàng， gù hù kǎn huàn。
**EN:** Xun (巽). Going out the gate, meeting evil — calamity and resentment. Striking and stabbing one another, they wound my hand. Opposing hexagram: Zhen. The nuclear Gen — hence "going out the gate"; Kan is evil, is calamity, is resentment; from line one to line four, inverted and upright Gen; Gen is the hand — hence "striking and stabbing one another"; Kan is ruin — hence "wound my hand."

## Section 58 (兌)

**ZH:** 兌。鷁飛中退，舉事不遂，宋人亂潰。《左傳》：「六鷁退飛，風也。」為宋襄公敗徵，故曰宋人亂潰。兌伏艮，艮為鷁，巽為退，互震為舉、為人，艮為宋。《說文》「架木為屋曰宋」，艮形似之，故林以艮為宋。○遂，宋本、汲古作進，依元本，韻葉象亦合。
**PY:** duì。 yì fēi zhōng tuì， jǔ shì bù suí， sòng rén luàn kuì。《 zuǒ chuán》：「 liù yì tuì fēi， fēng yě。」 wèi sòng xiāng gōng bài zhēng， gù yuē sòng rén luàn kuì。 duì fú gěn， gěn wèi yì， xùn wèi tuì， hù zhèn wèi jǔ、 wèi rén， gěn wèi sòng。《 shuō wén》「 jià mù wèi wū yuē sòng」， gěn xíng sì zhī， gù lín yǐ gěn wèi sòng。○ suí， sòng běn、 jí gǔ zuò jìn， yī yuán běn， yùn yè xiàng yì hé。
**EN:** Dui (兌). The fish-hawks fly and fall back; the undertaking does not succeed; the men of Song are routed. The Zuo Tradition has: "six fish-hawks flew backward — that was the wind," taken as the omen of Duke Xiang of Song's defeat — hence "the men of Song are routed." Dui's hidden trigram is Gen; Gen is the fish-hawk; Xun is falling back; the nuclear Zhen is undertaking, is men; Gen is Song. The Shuowen says: "a house framed of wood is called 宋 (Song)" — the Gen form resembles it — so the Forest takes Gen as Song.

## Section 59 (渙)

**ZH:** 渙。跛踦相隨，日暮牛罷。陵遲後旅，失利亡雌。坎蹇，故曰跛踦，巽順故曰相隨。艮為日，坎為暮，故曰日暮，艮為牛，坎勞，故曰牛疲。巽為陵遲，震為後，故曰陵遲後旅。巽為利、為雌，坎失，故曰失利亡雌。
**PY:** huàn。 bǒ yǐ xiāng suí， rì mù niú bà。 líng chí hòu lǚ， shī lì wáng cí。 kǎn jiǎn， gù yuē bǒ yǐ， xùn shùn gù yuē xiāng suí。 gěn wèi rì， kǎn wèi mù， gù yuē rì mù， gěn wèi niú， kǎn láo， gù yuē niú pí。 xùn wèi líng chí， zhèn wèi hòu， gù yuē líng chí hòu lǚ。 xùn wèi lì、 wèi cí， kǎn shī， gù yuē shī lì wáng cí。
**EN:** Huan (渙). Limping and hobbling they follow one another; at sunset the ox is spent. Straggling late in the rear of the company, he loses the gain and the female. Kan is lameness — hence "limping and hobbling"; Xun is following — hence "they follow one another." Gen is the sun; Kan is sunset — hence "at sunset"; Gen is the ox; Kan is toil — hence "the ox is spent." Xun is straggling; Zhen is the rear — hence "straggling late in the rear of the company." Xun is gain, is the female; Kan is losing — hence "loses the gain and the female."

## Section 60 (節)

**ZH:** 節。龍角博顙，位至公卿。世祿久長，起動安寧。震為龍，艮為角，震為顙，龍角猶日角。博顙，大顙也，皆貴相。艮為位，震為公，坎為祿，艮為久長，故曰世祿久長。震為起、為動，艮為安，故曰起動安寧。○顙，宋元本作預，非，依汲古。
**PY:** jié。 lóng jiǎo bó sǎng， wèi zhì gōng qīng。 shì lù jiǔ zhǎng， qǐ dòng ān níng。 zhèn wèi lóng， gěn wèi jiǎo， zhèn wèi sǎng， lóng jiǎo yóu rì jiǎo。 bó sǎng， dà sǎng yě， jiē guì xiàng。 gěn wèi wèi， zhèn wèi gōng， kǎn wèi lù， gěn wèi jiǔ zhǎng， gù yuē shì lù jiǔ zhǎng。 zhèn wèi qǐ、 wèi dòng， gěn wèi ān， gù yuē qǐ dòng ān níng。○ sǎng， sòng yuán běn zuò yù， fēi， yī jí gǔ。
**EN:** Jie (節). Dragon-horned, broad of brow, he rises in rank to duke and minister. His hereditary lot is long-enduring; rising, moving, he is at peace. Zhen is the dragon; Gen is the horn; Zhen is the brow — "dragon-horned" is like "sun-horned"; "broad of brow" is a large brow: both are marks of nobility. Gen is rank; Zhen is duke; Kan is lot; Gen is long-enduring — hence "his hereditary lot is long-enduring." Zhen is rising, is moving; Gen is peace — hence "rising, moving, he is at peace."

## Section 61 (中孚)

**ZH:** 中孚。舜升大禹，石夷之野。徵詣王庭，拜治水土。震為帝，故曰舜禹。艮為石、為野。按：《洛書靈准聽》曰「禹出石夷，掘地代，懷玉斗。」石夷不見於他書，而《蜀志秦宓傳》云「禹生石紐」，又譙周《蜀本紀》「禹本汶山廣柔縣人，生於石紐」，又《吳越春秋》「鯀娶女嬉，吞薏苡有感，剖脅而產高密，家於西羌，地曰石紐。」又《地理通釋》引皇甫謐曰「孟子稱禹生於石紐，西夷人也。」茲曰石夷，不知於石紐是否為一地，不能明也。震為王，艮為庭，震往，故曰徵詣王庭。艮手為拜，伏大坎，坎為水、為土，故曰拜治水土。此以坎為土，與邵子同。○拜，從元本，宋本作刊。
**PY:** zhōng fú。 shùn shēng dà yǔ， shí yí zhī yě。 zhēng yì wáng tíng， bài zhì shuǐ tǔ。 zhèn wèi dì， gù yuē shùn yǔ。 gěn wèi shí、 wèi yě。 àn：《 luò shū líng zhǔn tīng》 yuē「 yǔ chū shí yí， jué dì dài， huái yù dǒu。」 shí yí bù jiàn yú tā shū， ér《 shǔ zhì qín mì chuán》 yún「 yǔ shēng shí niǔ」， yòu qiáo zhōu《 shǔ běn jì》「 yǔ běn wèn shān guǎng róu xiàn rén， shēng yú shí niǔ」， yòu《 wú yuè chūn qiū》「 gǔn qǔ nǚ xī， tūn yì yǐ yǒu gǎn， pōu xié ér chǎn gāo mì， jiā yú xī qiāng， dì yuē shí niǔ。」 yòu《 dì lǐ tōng shì》 yǐn huáng fǔ mì yuē「 mèng zǐ chēng yǔ shēng yú shí niǔ， xī yí rén yě。」 zī yuē shí yí， bù zhī yú shí niǔ shì fǒu wèi yī dì， bù néng míng yě。 zhèn wèi wáng， gěn wèi tíng， zhèn wǎng， gù yuē zhēng yì wáng tíng。 gěn shǒu wèi bài， fú dà kǎn， kǎn wèi shuǐ、 wèi tǔ， gù yuē bài zhì shuǐ tǔ。 cǐ yǐ kǎn wèi tǔ， yǔ shào zǐ tóng。○ bài， cóng yuán běn， sòng běn zuò kān。
**EN:** Zhongfu (中孚). Shun raised up Great Yu from the wilds of Shiyi. Summoned to the king's court, he bowed and was charged with the waters and the lands. Zhen is the sovereign — hence Shun and Yu. Gen is stone, is wilds. Note: the Luoshu Lingzhun Ting says: "Yu came from Shiyi; digging the earth in his turn, he carried the jade dipper." Shiyi is not seen in other books; but the Shu Zhi, "Qin Mi zhuan," says "Yu was born at Shiniu"; Qiao Zhou's Shu Benji says "Yu, of Wenshan Guangrou county, was born at Shiniu"; and the Wuyue Chunqiu says "Gun married Nüxi; she swallowed job's tears, was moved, and from her opened side bore Gaomi; their home was in Western Qiang, at a place called Shiniu"; and the Dili Tongshi quotes Huangfu Mi: "Mencius says Yu was born at Shiniu, a man of the Western Yi." Whether this Shiyi and Shiniu are one place cannot be made clear. Zhen is king; Gen is court; Zhen is going — hence "summoned to the king's court." Gen's hand is bowing; the hidden great Kan is water, is earth — hence "bowed and was charged with the waters and the lands." This takes Kan as earth, in agreement with Shaozi.

## Section 62 (小過)

**ZH:** 小過。從風放火，荻芝俱死。三害集房，叔子中傷。巽為風，艮為火，巽艮連，故曰從風放火。巽為草莽、為荻芝，中爻互大過，大過死，故曰荻芝俱死。艮為房，數三，坎為害，在中爻，故曰三害集房。艮為叔子，兌毀，故中傷，以艮為火，邵子所本，三害暴虐頗也。見《左傳昭十四年》注。
**PY:** xiǎo guò。 cóng fēng fàng huǒ， dí zhī jù sǐ。 sān hài jí fáng， shū zi zhōng shāng。 xùn wèi fēng， gěn wèi huǒ， xùn gěn lián， gù yuē cóng fēng fàng huǒ。 xùn wèi cǎo mǎng、 wèi dí zhī， zhōng yáo hù dà guò， dà guò sǐ， gù yuē dí zhī jù sǐ。 gěn wèi fáng， shù sān， kǎn wèi hài， zài zhōng yáo， gù yuē sān hài jí fáng。 gěn wèi shū zi， duì huǐ， gù zhōng shāng， yǐ gěn wèi huǒ， shào zǐ suǒ běn， sān hài bào nüè pō yě。 jiàn《 zuǒ chuán zhāo shí sì nián》 zhù。
**EN:** Xiaoguo (小過). Following the wind he sets a fire; rushes and zhi-plants all die. Three harms gather in the house; the uncle's son is wounded at the center. Xun is wind; Gen is fire; Xun and Gen joined — hence "following the wind he sets a fire." Xun is grass and scrub, is rushes and zhi-plants; the middle lines form great Guo; great Guo is death — hence "rushes and zhi-plants all die." Gen is the house; its number is three; Kan is harm, in the middle lines — hence "three harms gather in the house." Gen is the uncle's son; Dui is destruction — hence "wounded at the center." Taking Gen as fire is what Shaozi rests on; the three harms are violence, cruelty, and perversion — see the note to Zuo Tradition, Zhao 14.

## Section 63 (既濟)

**ZH:** 既濟。楩生荊山，命制輸班。袍衣剝脫，夏熱冬寒。飢餓枯槁，眾人莫憐。卦有三震形，故曰楩、曰荊，又有半艮，故曰山，又有半巽，巽為命、為工、為輸班。坎為制，故命制輸班。震為襦，故曰袍衣，三震上下往複，如剝脫然，言上六之初即成未濟也，坎冬寒，離夏熱。離虛為飢餓、為枯槁，坎為眾，震為笑樂，故曰莫憐。○眾人莫憐，宋本作莫人莫憐，宋本作莫人莫憐，元本作莫人震憐，皆不適，故從汲古。楩，宋元、汲古皆作梗，依局本。
**PY:** jì jì。 pián shēng jīng shān， mìng zhì shū bān。 páo yī bō tuō， xià rè dōng hán。 jī è kū gǎo， zhòng rén mò lián。 guà yǒu sān zhèn xíng， gù yuē pián、 yuē jīng， yòu yǒu bàn gěn， gù yuē shān， yòu yǒu bàn xùn， xùn wèi mìng、 wèi gōng、 wèi shū bān。 kǎn wèi zhì， gù mìng zhì shū bān。 zhèn wèi rú， gù yuē páo yī， sān zhèn shàng xià wǎng fù， rú bō tuō rán， yán shàng liù zhī chū jí chéng wèi jì yě， kǎn dōng hán， lí xià rè。 lí xū wèi jī è、 wèi kū gǎo， kǎn wèi zhòng， zhèn wèi xiào lè， gù yuē mò lián。○ zhòng rén mò lián， sòng běn zuò mò rén mò lián， sòng běn zuò mò rén mò lián， yuán běn zuò mò rén zhèn lián， jiē bù shì， gù cóng jí gǔ。 pián， sòng yuán、 jí gǔ jiē zuò gěng， yī jú běn。
**EN:** Jiji (既濟). The pian-tree grows on Jing mountain; his lot is ruled by Luban (輸班). His robe is stripped from him; in summer he burns, in winter he freezes. Hungry, starving, withered and gaunt — of the crowd, none pities him. The hexagram has three Zhen forms — hence "pian," hence "Jing"; it also has a half-Gen — hence "mountain"; and a half-Xun: Xun is lot, is craftsman, is Luban. Kan is ruling — hence "his lot is ruled by Luban." Zhen is the jacket — hence "his robe"; the three Zhens pass up and down, back and forth, as though stripped off — meaning that from the top line to the first it becomes Weiji. Kan is winter cold; Li is summer heat. Li's emptiness is hunger and starving, is withered and gaunt; Kan is the crowd; Zhen is laughing and joy — hence "none pities him." [uncertain: 命制輸班 ("his lot is ruled by Luban") — Luban is the legendary master craftsman; the verse's image logic (craftsman-lot stripping the maker's robe) is elliptical, and the annotator's trigram chain is kept as the best available thread.]

## Section 64 (未濟)

**ZH:** 未濟。長面大鼻，來解己憂。遺吾福子，惠我嘉喜。艮為鼻、為面，三艮形故曰長面大鼻。坎為憂，震為解、為福、為子、為嘉喜，此與上卦皆用半象，本之《易》也。○宋元本福子下多「與我惠妻」四字。
**PY:** wèi jì。 zhǎng miàn dà bí， lái jiě jǐ yōu。 yí wú fú zi， huì wǒ jiā xǐ。 gěn wèi bí、 wèi miàn， sān gěn xíng gù yuē zhǎng miàn dà bí。 kǎn wèi yōu， zhèn wèi jiě、 wèi fú、 wèi zǐ、 wèi jiā xǐ， cǐ yǔ shàng guà jiē yòng bàn xiàng， běn zhī《 yì》 yě。○ sòng yuán běn fú zi xià duō「 yǔ wǒ huì qī」 sì zì。
**EN:** Weiji (未濟). Long of face and big of nose, he comes and frees me from my cares. He leaves me a fortunate son, and favors me with excellent joy. Gen is nose, is face; there are three Gen forms — hence "long of face and big of nose." Kan is cares; Zhen is freeing, is fortune, is son, is excellent joy. This and the preceding hexagram both use the half-images, as is rooted in the Changes.
