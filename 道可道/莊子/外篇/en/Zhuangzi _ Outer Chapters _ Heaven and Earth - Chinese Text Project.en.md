# Heaven and Earth (天地)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ Heaven and Earth - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (18 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 天地雖大，其化均也；萬物雖多，其治一也；人卒雖眾，其主君也。君原於德而成於天，故曰：玄古之君天下，無為也，天德而已矣。以道觀言而天下之君正，以道觀分而君臣之義明，以道觀能而天下之官治，以道汎觀而萬物之應備。故通於天地者，德也；行於萬物者，道也；上治人者，事也；能有所藝者，技也。技兼於事，事兼於義，義兼於德，德兼於道，道兼於天。故曰：「古之畜天下者，無欲而天下足，無為而萬物化，淵靜而百姓定。」記曰：「通於一而萬事畢，無心得而鬼神服。」
**PY:** tiān dì suī dà， qí huà jūn yě； wàn wù suī duō， qí zhì yī yě； rén zú suī zhòng， qí zhǔ jūn yě。 jūn yuán yú dé ér chéng yú tiān， gù yuē： xuán gǔ zhī jūn tiān xià， wú wèi yě， tiān dé ér yǐ yǐ。 yǐ dào guān yán ér tiān xià zhī jūn zhèng， yǐ dào guān fēn ér jūn chén zhī yì míng， yǐ dào guān néng ér tiān xià zhī guān zhì， yǐ dào fàn guān ér wàn wù zhī yīng bèi。 gù tōng yú tiān dì zhě， dé yě； xíng yú wàn wù zhě， dào yě； shàng zhì rén zhě， shì yě； néng yǒu suǒ yì zhě， jì yě。 jì jiān yú shì， shì jiān yú yì， yì jiān yú dé， dé jiān yú dào， dào jiān yú tiān。 gù yuē：「 gǔ zhī chù tiān xià zhě， wú yù ér tiān xià zú， wú wèi ér wàn wù huà， yuān jìng ér bǎi xìng dìng。」 jì yuē：「 tōng yú yī ér wàn shì bì， wú xīn dé ér guǐ shén fú。」

**EN:** Great as heaven and earth are, their transforming power is impartial; many as the ten thousand things are, their order is one; numerous as the people are, their ruler is the lord. The lord takes his origin from virtue and is completed by Heaven. Hence it is said: the lords of the world in dark antiquity practiced non-action — Heaven's virtue, and nothing more. Look at speech through the Way, and the lords of the world are upright; look at stations through the Way, and the duty of lord and minister is clear; look at abilities through the Way, and the officers of the world are well ordered; look broadly through the Way, and the responses of the ten thousand things are all provided. So what penetrates heaven and earth is virtue; what courses through the ten thousand things is the Way; what orders men from above is affairs; what brings abilities to art is skill. Skill is encompassed by affairs; affairs by rightness; rightness by virtue; virtue by the Way; the Way by Heaven. Hence it is said: "Those of old who shepherded the world had no desires, and the world was sufficed; practiced non-action, and the ten thousand things were transformed; were deep and still, and the hundred clans were settled." The Record says: "Penetrate the One and the ten thousand affairs are finished; attain without mind and ghosts and spirits submit."

## Section 2

**ZH:** 夫子曰：「夫道，覆載萬物者也，洋洋乎大哉！君子不可以不刳心焉。無為為之之謂天，無為言之之謂德，愛人利物之謂仁，不同同之之謂大，行不崖異之謂寬，有萬不同之謂富。故執德之謂紀，德成之謂立，循於道之謂備，不以物挫志之謂完。君子明於此十者，則韜乎其事心之大也，沛乎其為萬物逝也。若然者，藏金於山，藏珠於淵；不利貨財，不近貴富；不樂壽，不哀夭；不榮通，不醜窮；壽夭俱忘，窮通不足言矣。不拘一世之利以為己私分，不以王天下為己處顯。顯則明，萬物一府，死生同狀。」
**PY:** fū zǐ yuē：「 fū dào， fù zài wàn wù zhě yě， yáng yáng hū dà zāi！ jūn zǐ bù kě yǐ bù kū xīn yān。 wú wèi wèi zhī zhī wèi tiān， wú wèi yán zhī zhī wèi dé， ài rén lì wù zhī wèi rén， bù tóng tóng zhī zhī wèi dà， xíng bù yá yì zhī wèi kuān， yǒu wàn bù tóng zhī wèi fù。 gù zhí dé zhī wèi jì， dé chéng zhī wèi lì， xún yú dào zhī wèi bèi， bù yǐ wù cuò zhì zhī wèi wán。 jūn zǐ míng yú cǐ shí zhě， zé tāo hū qí shì xīn zhī dà yě， pèi hū qí wèi wàn wù shì yě。 ruò rán zhě， cáng jīn yú shān， cáng zhū yú yuān； bù lì huò cái， bù jìn guì fù； bù lè shòu， bù āi yāo； bù róng tōng， bù chǒu qióng； shòu yāo jù wàng， qióng tōng bù zú yán yǐ。 bù jū yī shì zhī lì yǐ wèi jǐ sī fēn， bù yǐ wàng tiān xià wèi jǐ chù xiǎn。 xiǎn zé míng， wàn wù yī fǔ， sǐ shēng tóng zhuàng。」

**EN:** The Master said: "The Way is that which covers and bears up the ten thousand things — how vast, how great! The gentleman cannot do otherwise than hollow out his heart for it. To act without acting is called Heaven; to speak without speaking is called virtue; to love men and benefit things is called humaneness; to make one of what is not the same is called greatness; to move without overhanging singularities is called breadth; to hold the ten thousand differences is called riches. So to hold fast to virtue is called the guiding thread; to complete virtue is called standing; to follow the Way is called being furnished; not to let things crush the will is called being whole. When the gentleman is clear about these ten, then how great is the heart he sheathes; how torrential is his going forth with the ten thousand things! Being such, he leaves the gold hidden in the mountain, leaves the pearls hidden in the deep; he does not profit by goods and wealth, does not draw near to honor and riches; he does not rejoice in long life, does not grieve at early death; he does not prize success, does not scorn failure — long life and early death both forgotten, failure and success not worth the speaking of. He does not seize upon the profits of one generation to make them his private portion; he does not take kingship over the world as his personal place of eminence. Eminence belongs to clarity — when the ten thousand things are one storehouse, and death and life have the same shape."

## Section 3

**ZH:** 夫子曰：「夫道，淵乎其居也，漻乎其清也。金石不得，無以鳴。故金石有聲，不考不鳴。萬物孰能定之！夫王德之人，素逝而恥通於事，立之本原而知通於神。故其德廣，其心之出，有物採之。故形非道不生，生非德不明。存形窮生，立德明道，非王德者邪！蕩蕩乎！忽然出，勃然動，而萬物從之乎！此謂王德之人。視乎冥冥，聽乎無聲。冥冥之中，獨見曉焉；無聲之中，獨聞和焉。故深之又深，而能物焉；神之又神，而能精焉。故其與萬物接也，至無而供其求，時騁而要其宿，大小、長短、修遠。」
**PY:** fū zǐ yuē：「 fū dào， yuān hū qí jū yě， liáo hū qí qīng yě。 jīn shí bù dé， wú yǐ míng。 gù jīn shí yǒu shēng， bù kǎo bù míng。 wàn wù shú néng dìng zhī！ fū wáng dé zhī rén， sù shì ér chǐ tōng yú shì， lì zhī běn yuán ér zhī tōng yú shén。 gù qí dé guǎng， qí xīn zhī chū， yǒu wù cǎi zhī。 gù xíng fēi dào bù shēng， shēng fēi dé bù míng。 cún xíng qióng shēng， lì dé míng dào， fēi wáng dé zhě xié！ dàng dàng hū！ hū rán chū， bó rán dòng， ér wàn wù cóng zhī hū！ cǐ wèi wáng dé zhī rén。 shì hū míng míng， tīng hū wú shēng。 míng míng zhī zhōng， dú jiàn xiǎo yān； wú shēng zhī zhōng， dú wén hé yān。 gù shēn zhī yòu shēn， ér néng wù yān； shén zhī yòu shén， ér néng jīng yān。 gù qí yǔ wàn wù jiē yě， zhì wú ér gōng qí qiú， shí chěng ér yào qí sù， dà xiǎo、 zhǎng duǎn、 xiū yuǎn。」

**EN:** The Master said: "The Way — how deep is its dwelling, how pure and still its clarity! Without it, metal and stone have no means to sound. So though metal and stone have a voice, unstruck they do not sound. Who can determine this of the ten thousand things! The man of kingly virtue goes forth in plainness and is ashamed to be versed in affairs; he stands at the root and source, and his knowledge penetrates to the spirit. Therefore his virtue is broad; when his heart goes forth, things come to draw upon it. So without the Way the form would not be born; without virtue the life would not be bright. To preserve the form and live life out, to establish virtue and make the Way clear — is this not the man of kingly virtue! How vast and sweeping! Sudden he emerges, abrupt he moves — and the ten thousand things follow him! This is called the man of kingly virtue. He sees into the dark; he hears into the soundless. In the midst of the dark, he alone sees the dawn; in the midst of the soundless, he alone hears the harmony. So in the deepest of the deep, he can bring forth things; in the most spirit-like of the spirit, he can bring forth the essences. In his contacts with the ten thousand things, he is the utmost nothing that supplies their wants; he gallops with the times and gathers them to their lodgings — great and small, long and short, far and near."

## Section 4

**ZH:** 黃帝遊乎赤水之北，登乎崑崙之丘而南望，還歸，遺其玄珠，使知索之而不得，使離朱索之而不得，使喫詬索之而不得也。乃使象罔，象罔得之。黃帝曰：「異哉！象罔乃可以得之乎？」
**PY:** huáng dì yóu hū chì shuǐ zhī běi， dēng hū kūn lún zhī qiū ér nán wàng， hái guī， yí qí xuán zhū， shǐ zhī suǒ zhī ér bù dé， shǐ lí zhū suǒ zhī ér bù dé， shǐ chī gòu suǒ zhī ér bù dé yě。 nǎi shǐ xiàng wǎng， xiàng wǎng dé zhī。 huáng dì yuē：「 yì zāi！ xiàng wǎng nǎi kě yǐ dé zhī hū？」

**EN:** The Yellow Emperor roamed north of the Red River; he climbed the hills of Kunlun (崑崙) and gazed to the south. On his way home he lost his dark pearl. He sent Knowledge (知) to search for it — without success; he sent Li Zhu (離朱, Keen-Sight) to search for it — without success; he sent Wrangling Debate (喫詬) to search for it — without success. Then he sent Imageless (象罔) — and Imageless found it. The Yellow Emperor said: "How strange! That it should be Imageless who could find it!"

## Section 5

**ZH:** 堯之師曰許由，許由之師曰齧缺，齧缺之師曰王倪，王倪之師曰被衣。堯問於許由曰：「齧缺可以配天乎？吾藉王倪以要之。」許由曰：「殆哉圾乎天下！齧缺之為人也，聰明叡知，給數以敏，其性過人，而又乃以人受天。彼審乎禁過，而不知過之所由生。與之配天乎？彼且乘人而無天，方且本身而異形，方且尊知而火馳，方且為緒使，方且為物絯，方且四顧而物應，方且應眾宜，方且與物化而未始有恒。夫何足以配天乎？雖然，有族有祖，可以為眾父，而不可以為眾父父。治亂之率也，北面之禍也，南面之賊也。」
**PY:** yáo zhī shī yuē xǔ yóu， xǔ yóu zhī shī yuē niè quē， niè quē zhī shī yuē wáng ní， wáng ní zhī shī yuē bèi yī。 yáo wèn yú xǔ yóu yuē：「 niè quē kě yǐ pèi tiān hū？ wú jí wáng ní yǐ yào zhī。」 xǔ yóu yuē：「 dài zāi jī hū tiān xià！ niè quē zhī wèi rén yě， cōng míng ruì zhī， gěi shù yǐ mǐn， qí xìng guò rén， ér yòu nǎi yǐ rén shòu tiān。 bǐ shěn hū jìn guò， ér bù zhī guò zhī suǒ yóu shēng。 yǔ zhī pèi tiān hū？ bǐ qiě chéng rén ér wú tiān， fāng qiě běn shēn ér yì xíng， fāng qiě zūn zhī ér huǒ chí， fāng qiě wèi xù shǐ， fāng qiě wèi wù gāi， fāng qiě sì gù ér wù yīng， fāng qiě yīng zhòng yí， fāng qiě yǔ wù huà ér wèi shǐ yǒu héng。 fū hé zú yǐ pèi tiān hū？ suī rán， yǒu zú yǒu zǔ， kě yǐ wèi zhòng fù， ér bù kě yǐ wèi zhòng fù fù。 zhì luàn zhī lǜ yě， běi miàn zhī huò yě， nán miàn zhī zéi yě。」

**EN:** Yao's teacher was Xu You; Xu You's teacher was Nie Que; Nie Que's teacher was Wang Ni; Wang Ni's teacher was Piyi (被衣). Yao asked Xu You: "Could Nie Que be a match for Heaven? I could use Wang Ni to summon him." Xu You said: "Perilous! The world would be in danger! Nie Que as a man is keen of hearing and sight, wise and knowing, swift and nimble in response; his native gifts surpass other men's, and yet he would take the human and make it serve the heavenly. He is shrewd at forbidding error, but he does not know whence error arises. Give him Heaven to match? He will ride upon the human and lose the heavenly. Now he makes his own person the root and treats other forms as alien; now he exalts knowledge and gallops like wildfire; now he is driven along by thread-ends; now he is netted fast by things; now he glances all around answering to things; now he responds to every fitting demand; now he shifts with things, never holding any constancy. What could make him fit to match Heaven? Still, there are families and there are ancestors: he could serve as father of the multitudes, but not as the father of the multitudes' father. To govern is the leader into disorder — a calamity for the ministers facing north, a brigand for the ruler facing south."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 堯觀乎華。華封人曰：「嘻！聖人！請祝聖人：使聖人壽。」堯曰：「辭。」「使聖人富」。堯曰：「辭。」「使聖人多男子」。堯曰：「辭。」封人曰：「壽、富、多男子，人之所欲也。女獨不欲，何邪？」堯曰：「多男子則多懼，富則多事，壽則多辱。是三者，非所以養德也，故辭。」封人曰：「始也我以女為聖人邪，今然君子也。天生萬民，必授之職，多男子而授之職，則何懼之有！富而使人分之，則何事之有！夫聖人鶉居而鷇食，鳥行而無彰；天下有道則與物皆昌，天下無道則修德就閒；千歲厭世，去而上僊，乘彼白雲，至於帝鄉。三患莫至，身常無殃，則何辱之有！」封人去之，堯隨之，曰：「請問。」封人曰：「退已！」
**PY:** yáo guān hū huá。 huá fēng rén yuē：「 xī！ shèng rén！ qǐng zhù shèng rén： shǐ shèng rén shòu。」 yáo yuē：「 cí。」「 shǐ shèng rén fù」。 yáo yuē：「 cí。」「 shǐ shèng rén duō nán zi」。 yáo yuē：「 cí。」 fēng rén yuē：「 shòu、 fù、 duō nán zi， rén zhī suǒ yù yě。 nǚ dú bù yù， hé xié？」 yáo yuē：「 duō nán zi zé duō jù， fù zé duō shì， shòu zé duō rǔ。 shì sān zhě， fēi suǒ yǐ yǎng dé yě， gù cí。」 fēng rén yuē：「 shǐ yě wǒ yǐ nǚ wèi shèng rén xié， jīn rán jūn zǐ yě。 tiān shēng wàn mín， bì shòu zhī zhí， duō nán zi ér shòu zhī zhí， zé hé jù zhī yǒu！ fù ér shǐ rén fēn zhī， zé hé shì zhī yǒu！ fū shèng rén chún jū ér kòu shí， niǎo xíng ér wú zhāng； tiān xià yǒu dào zé yǔ wù jiē chāng， tiān xià wú dào zé xiū dé jiù xián； qiān suì yàn shì， qù ér shàng xiān， chéng bǐ bái yún， zhì yú dì xiāng。 sān huàn mò zhì， shēn cháng wú yāng， zé hé rǔ zhī yǒu！」 fēng rén qù zhī， yáo suí zhī， yuē：「 qǐng wèn。」 fēng rén yuē：「 tuì yǐ！」

**EN:** Yao was making a tour at Hua (華). The border guard of Hua said: "Ah — a sage! I beg leave to wish the sage well. May the sage live long!" Yao said: "I decline." "May the sage be rich!" Yao said: "I decline." "May the sage have many sons!" Yao said: "I decline." The guard said: "Long life, riches, many sons — these are what all men desire. You alone do not want them. Why?" Yao said: "Many sons mean many fears; riches mean many troubles; long life means many humiliations. These three are of no use in nourishing virtue — so I decline." The guard said: "At first I took you for a sage; now I see you are merely a gentleman. When Heaven gives birth to the myriad people, it is sure to give them offices. With many sons, you give them offices — what is there to fear! With riches, you let others share them — what troubles are there! The sage dwells like a quail and is fed like a fledgling; he travels like a bird and leaves no mark. When the world has the Way, he flourishes along with all things; when the world is without the Way, he cultivates his virtue and retires to ease. After a thousand years, weary of the world, he departs and ascends as an immortal; he rides those white clouds to the land of the Lord. The three calamities never reach him; his person is ever free of misfortune — what humiliation is there!" The guard turned and left. Yao followed after him, saying: "May I ask—" The guard said: "Be gone!"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 堯治天下，伯成子高立為諸侯。堯授舜，舜授禹，伯成子高辭為諸侯而耕。禹往見之，則耕在野。禹趨就下風，立而問焉，曰：「昔堯治天下，吾子立為諸侯；堯授舜，舜授予，而吾子辭為諸侯而耕。敢問其故何也？」子高曰：「昔堯治天下，不賞而民勸，不罰而民畏。今子賞罰而民且不仁，德自此衰，刑自此立，後世之亂自此始矣。夫子闔行邪？無落吾事！」俋俋乎耕而不顧。
**PY:** yáo zhì tiān xià， bó chéng zi gāo lì wèi zhū hóu。 yáo shòu shùn， shùn shòu yǔ， bó chéng zi gāo cí wèi zhū hóu ér gēng。 yǔ wǎng jiàn zhī， zé gēng zài yě。 yǔ qū jiù xià fēng， lì ér wèn yān， yuē：「 xī yáo zhì tiān xià， wú zǐ lì wèi zhū hóu； yáo shòu shùn， shùn shòu yǔ， ér wú zǐ cí wèi zhū hóu ér gēng。 gǎn wèn qí gù hé yě？」 zi gāo yuē：「 xī yáo zhì tiān xià， bù shǎng ér mín quàn， bù fá ér mín wèi。 jīn zi shǎng fá ér mín qiě bù rén， dé zì cǐ shuāi， xíng zì cǐ lì， hòu shì zhī luàn zì cǐ shǐ yǐ。 fū zǐ hé xíng xié？ wú luò wú shì！」 yì yì hū gēng ér bù gù。

**EN:** When Yao governed the world, Bocheng Zigao (伯成子高) was enfeoffed as a lord. Yao yielded to Shun; Shun yielded to Yu — and Bocheng Zigao resigned his lordship and took to ploughing. Yu went to see him and found him ploughing in the fields. Yu hurried to the downwind spot, stood, and asked: "Of old, when Yao governed the world, you, sir, stood as a lord. Yao yielded to Shun and Shun yielded to me — and you, sir, resigned your lordship to plough. May I ask the reason?" Zigao said: "Of old, when Yao governed the world, he made no rewards yet the people were encouraged; he made no punishments yet the people were in awe. Now you make rewards and punishments, and the people are unkind even so. Virtue declines from this point; punishments are established from this point; the disorder of later ages begins from this point. Will you not be gone, sir? Do not interrupt my work!" And steadily he ploughed on without looking back.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 泰初有無，無有無名，一之所起，有一而未形。物得以生，謂之德；未形者有分，且然無間，謂之命；留動而生物，物成生理，謂之形；形體保神，各有儀則，謂之性。性修反德，德至同於初。同乃虛，虛乃大。合喙鳴，喙鳴合，與天地為合。其合緡緡，若愚若昏，是謂玄德，同乎大順。
**PY:** tài chū yǒu wú， wú yǒu wú míng， yī zhī suǒ qǐ， yǒu yī ér wèi xíng。 wù dé yǐ shēng， wèi zhī dé； wèi xíng zhě yǒu fēn， qiě rán wú jiān， wèi zhī mìng； liú dòng ér shēng wù， wù chéng shēng lǐ， wèi zhī xíng； xíng tǐ bǎo shén， gè yǒu yí zé， wèi zhī xìng。 xìng xiū fǎn dé， dé zhì tóng yú chū。 tóng nǎi xū， xū nǎi dà。 hé huì míng， huì míng hé， yǔ tiān dì wèi hé。 qí hé mín mín， ruò yú ruò hūn， shì wèi xuán dé， tóng hū dà shùn。

**EN:** In the Grand Beginning there was nothing — no being, no name. From it arose the One; there was the One, but not yet any form. That by which things came to life is called virtue; the unformed having its divisions, yet the course unbroken — this is called destiny; the stillness moving and giving birth to things, things coming to completion and producing their pattern — this is called form; the bodily frame guarding the spirit, each with its own rule and measure — this is called nature. When nature is cultivated, it returns to virtue; virtue at its height is the same as at the beginning. Being the same, there is emptiness; being empty, there is greatness. Then the beak joins and sounds; the beak sounds and joins — one is joined with heaven and earth. The joining is dim and seamless, as though foolish, as though darkened. This is called dark virtue, the same as the Grand Compliance.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 夫子問於老聃曰：「有人治道若相放，可不可，然不然。辯者有言曰：『離堅白若縣宇。』若是，則可謂聖人乎？」老聃曰：「是胥易技係，勞形怵心者也。執留之狗成思，猿狙之便自山林來。丘！予告若，而所不能聞與而所不能言。凡有首、有趾、無心、無耳者眾，有形者與無形無狀而皆存者盡無。其動，止也；其死，生也；其廢，起也。此又非其所以也。有治在人，忘乎物，忘乎天，其名為忘己。忘己之人，是之謂入於天。」
**PY:** fū zǐ wèn yú lǎo dān yuē：「 yǒu rén zhì dào ruò xiāng fàng， kě bù kě， rán bù rán。 biàn zhě yǒu yán yuē：『 lí jiān bái ruò xiàn yǔ。』 ruò shì， zé kě wèi shèng rén hū？」 lǎo dān yuē：「 shì xū yì jì xì， láo xíng chù xīn zhě yě。 zhí liú zhī gǒu chéng sī， yuán jū zhī biàn zì shān lín lái。 qiū！ yǔ gào ruò， ér suǒ bù néng wén yǔ ér suǒ bù néng yán。 fán yǒu shǒu、 yǒu zhǐ、 wú xīn、 wú ěr zhě zhòng， yǒu xíng zhě yǔ wú xíng wú zhuàng ér jiē cún zhě jǐn wú。 qí dòng， zhǐ yě； qí sǐ， shēng yě； qí fèi， qǐ yě。 cǐ yòu fēi qí suǒ yǐ yě。 yǒu zhì zài rén， wàng hū wù， wàng hū tiān， qí míng wèi wàng jǐ。 wàng jǐ zhī rén， shì zhī wèi rù yú tiān。」

**EN:** The Master (Confucius) asked Lao Dan: "Here is a man who works at the Way as though chasing after it — he admissibles the inadmissible, so-s the not-so. The disputers have a saying: 'Separate the hard and white as distinctly as the eaves hang from the roof.' If he can do that, may he be called a sage?" Lao Dan said: "He is a clerk trading his skills for bondage, wearing out his body and fretting his mind. A dog that catches the fox earns his fretfulness; a gibbon that is nimble summons men down from the mountain forests. Qiu! Let me tell you what you cannot hear and cannot say. Those that have heads and feet but no mind and no ears are many; but that the formed should coexist with the formless and shapeless — of that there are none at all. [uncertain: 有形者與無形無狀而皆存者盡無 — highly compressed; rendered "that what has form should abide together with the formless and shapeless — of such there are none."] Its motion is a stopping; its death is a life; its decline is a rise. And these too are not of its own doing. Where there is ordering, it lies in man: forget things, forget Heaven — its name is forgetting oneself. One who forgets himself may be said to have entered into Heaven."

## Section 10

**ZH:** 將閭葂見季徹曰：「魯君謂葂也曰：『請受教。』辭不獲命，既已告矣，未知中否，請嘗薦之。吾謂魯君曰：『必服恭儉，拔出公忠之屬，而無阿私，民孰敢不輯！』」季徹局局然笑曰：「若夫子之言，於帝王之德，猶螳蜋之怒臂以當車軼，則必不勝任矣。且若是，則其自為處危，其觀臺多物，將往投跡者眾。」將閭葂覤覤然驚曰：「葂也汒若於夫子之所言矣。雖然，願先生之言其風也。」季徹曰：「大聖之治天下也，搖蕩民心，使之成教易俗，舉滅其賊心而皆進其獨志，若性之自為，而民不知其所由然。若然者，豈兄堯、舜之教民，溟滓然弟之哉？欲同乎德而心居矣。」
**PY:** jiāng lǘ miǎn jiàn jì chè yuē：「 lǔ jūn wèi miǎn yě yuē：『 qǐng shòu jiào。』 cí bù huò mìng， jì yǐ gào yǐ， wèi zhī zhōng fǒu， qǐng cháng jiàn zhī。 wú wèi lǔ jūn yuē：『 bì fú gōng jiǎn， bá chū gōng zhōng zhī shǔ， ér wú ā sī， mín shú gǎn bù jí！』」 jì chè jú jú rán xiào yuē：「 ruò fú zi zhī yán， yú dì wáng zhī dé， yóu táng láng zhī nù bì yǐ dāng chē yì， zé bì bù shèng rèn yǐ。 qiě ruò shì， zé qí zì wèi chù wēi， qí guān tái duō wù， jiāng wǎng tóu jī zhě zhòng。」 jiāng lǘ miǎn xì xì rán jīng yuē：「 miǎn yě máng ruò yú fū zǐ zhī suǒ yán yǐ。 suī rán， yuàn xiān shēng zhī yán qí fēng yě。」 jì chè yuē：「 dà shèng zhī zhì tiān xià yě， yáo dàng mín xīn， shǐ zhī chéng jiào yì sú， jǔ miè qí zéi xīn ér jiē jìn qí dú zhì， ruò xìng zhī zì wèi， ér mín bù zhī qí suǒ yóu rán。 ruò rán zhě， qǐ xiōng yáo、 shùn zhī jiào mín， míng zǐ rán dì zhī zāi？ yù tóng hū dé ér xīn jū yǐ。」

**EN:** Jianglü Mian (將閭葂) went to see Ji Che (季徹) and said: "The lord of Lu said to me, 'Instruct me, I pray.' I declined but could not obtain release; so I instructed him, though I do not know whether I hit the mark. Let me try to repeat it to you. I said to the lord of Lu: 'You must practice reverence and frugality; select and promote the public-spirited and loyal, without favoritism or partiality — then who among the people would dare not be harmonious!'" Ji Che laughed sputteringly: "Words like yours, measured against the virtue of true kings, are like the mantis rearing its angry arm against a cartwheel — they cannot possibly be up to the task. And if he did as you say, he would set himself in a perilous place; his terrace-towers would draw swarms of things, and crowds would come hurrying to leave their footprints there." Jianglü Mian started in alarm: "I am dazed by what you have said, sir. Still, I beg you to speak of the general bent." Ji Che said: "When the great sage governs the world, he sways and swings the hearts of the people, bringing them to complete their instruction and change their customs; he wipes out all their thieving hearts and advances each one's solitary will, as though their nature did it of itself — and the people do not know how it came about. Being thus, would he put the teachings of Yao and Shun above the people as an elder brother, and in dim bewilderment follow them as a younger? He desires them to be one in virtue, and his heart is at rest."

## Section 11

**ZH:** 子貢南遊於楚，反於晉，過漢陰，見一丈人方將為圃畦，鑿隧而入井，抱甕而出灌，搰搰然用力甚多而見功寡。子貢曰：「有械於此，一日浸百畦，用力甚寡而見功多，夫子不欲乎？」為圃者卬而視之曰：「奈何？」曰：「鑿木為機，後重前輕，挈水若抽，數如泆湯，其名為槔。」為圃者忿然作色而笑曰：「吾聞之吾師：『有機械者必有機事，有機事者必有機心。』機心存於胸中，則純白不備；純白不備，則神生不定；神生不定者，道之所不載也。吾非不知，羞而不為也。」子貢瞞然慙，俯而不對。
**PY:** zi gòng nán yóu yú chǔ， fǎn yú jìn， guò hàn yīn， jiàn yī zhàng rén fāng jiāng wèi pǔ qí， záo suì ér rù jǐng， bào wèng ér chū guàn， hú hú rán yòng lì shèn duō ér jiàn gōng guǎ。 zi gòng yuē：「 yǒu xiè yú cǐ， yī rì jìn bǎi qí， yòng lì shèn guǎ ér jiàn gōng duō， fū zǐ bù yù hū？」 wèi pǔ zhě áng ér shì zhī yuē：「 nài hé？」 yuē：「 záo mù wèi jī， hòu zhòng qián qīng， qiè shuǐ ruò chōu， shù rú yì tāng， qí míng wèi gāo。」 wèi pǔ zhě fèn rán zuò sè ér xiào yuē：「 wú wén zhī wú shī：『 yǒu jī xiè zhě bì yǒu jī shì， yǒu jī shì zhě bì yǒu jī xīn。』 jī xīn cún yú xiōng zhōng， zé chún bái bù bèi； chún bái bù bèi， zé shén shēng bù dìng； shén shēng bù dìng zhě， dào zhī suǒ bù zài yě。 wú fēi bù zhī， xiū ér bù wèi yě。」 zi gòng mán rán cán， fǔ ér bù duì。

**EN:** Zigong, roaming south in Chu, was returning through Jin, and passing south of the Han River he saw an old man at work in a garden plot. He had dug a tunnel down to the well, and was coming up with a jar in his arms to water the field — laboring mightily, with little to show for it. Zigong said: "There is a device for this that can water a hundred plots in a day, with very little labor and much result. Would you not like it, sir?" The gardener raised his head and looked at him: "How does it work?" Zigong said: "You carve wood into a machine, heavy behind and light in front; it draws up water as though plucking it, fast and free like boiling water overflowing. It is called a well-sweep." The gardener flushed with anger and then laughed: "I have heard from my teacher: 'Where there are machines, there are sure to be machine affairs; where there are machine affairs, there is sure to be a machine heart.' When a machine heart lodges in the breast, pure whiteness is not complete; when pure whiteness is not complete, the spirit-life is unsettled; and one whose spirit-life is unsettled is not borne up by the Way. It is not that I do not know of your device — I am ashamed to use it." Zigong, abashed and deflated, looked down and made no reply.

## Section 12

**ZH:** 有間，為圃者曰：「子奚為者邪？」曰：「孔丘之徒也。」為圃者曰：「子非夫博學以擬聖，於于以蓋眾，獨弦哀歌以賣名聲於天下者乎？汝方將忘汝神氣，墮汝形骸，而庶幾乎！而身之不能治，而何暇治天下乎？子往矣，無乏吾事！
**PY:** yǒu jiān， wèi pǔ zhě yuē：「 zi xī wèi zhě xié？」 yuē：「 kǒng qiū zhī tú yě。」 wèi pǔ zhě yuē：「 zi fēi fū bó xué yǐ nǐ shèng， yú yú yǐ gài zhòng， dú xián āi gē yǐ mài míng shēng yú tiān xià zhě hū？ rǔ fāng jiāng wàng rǔ shén qì， duò rǔ xíng hái， ér shù jǐ hū！ ér shēn zhī bù néng zhì， ér hé xiá zhì tiān xià hū？ zi wǎng yǐ， wú fá wú shì！

**EN:** After a while the gardener said: "What do you do?" Zigong said: "I am a follower of Kong Qiu (Confucius)." The gardener said: "Are you not one of those who study broadly to imitate the sages, who puff themselves up to overtop the crowd, who twang their lone strings and wail their sad songs to sell their names throughout the world? You would do better to forget your spirit and breath, cast off your bodily frame — and then you might come near it! You cannot even order your own person — what leisure have you to order the world? Be on your way, sir, and do not delay my work!"

## Section 13

**ZH:** 子貢卑陬失色，頊頊然不自得，行三十里而後愈。其弟子曰：「向之人何為者邪？夫子何故見之變容失色，終日不自反邪？」曰：「始以為天下一人耳，不知復有夫人也。吾聞之夫子：『事求可、功求成、用力少、見功多者，聖人之道。』今徒不然。執道者德全，德全者形全，形全者神全。神全者，聖人之道也。託生與民並行，而不知其所之，汒乎淳備哉！功利、機巧，必忘夫人之心。若夫人者，非其志不之，非其心不為。雖以天下譽之，得其所謂，謷然不顧；以天下非之，失其所謂，儻然不受。天下之非譽，無益損焉，是謂全德之人哉！我之謂風波之民。」反於魯，以告孔子。孔子曰：「彼假修渾沌氏之術者也：識其一，不知其二；治其內，而不治其外。夫明白入素，無為復朴，體性抱神，以遊世俗之間者，汝將固驚邪？且渾沌氏之術，予與汝何足以識之哉！」
**PY:** zi gòng bēi zōu shī sè， xū xū rán bù zì dé， xíng sān shí lǐ ér hòu yù。 qí dì zǐ yuē：「 xiàng zhī rén hé wèi zhě xié？ fū zǐ hé gù jiàn zhī biàn róng shī sè， zhōng rì bù zì fǎn xié？」 yuē：「 shǐ yǐ wèi tiān xià yī rén ěr， bù zhī fù yǒu fū rén yě。 wú wén zhī fū zǐ：『 shì qiú kě、 gōng qiú chéng、 yòng lì shǎo、 jiàn gōng duō zhě， shèng rén zhī dào。』 jīn tú bù rán。 zhí dào zhě dé quán， dé quán zhě xíng quán， xíng quán zhě shén quán。 shén quán zhě， shèng rén zhī dào yě。 tuō shēng yǔ mín bìng xíng， ér bù zhī qí suǒ zhī， máng hū chún bèi zāi！ gōng lì、 jī qiǎo， bì wàng fū rén zhī xīn。 ruò fú rén zhě， fēi qí zhì bù zhī， fēi qí xīn bù wèi。 suī yǐ tiān xià yù zhī， dé qí suǒ wèi， áo rán bù gù； yǐ tiān xià fēi zhī， shī qí suǒ wèi， tǎng rán bù shòu。 tiān xià zhī fēi yù， wú yì sǔn yān， shì wèi quán dé zhī rén zāi！ wǒ zhī wèi fēng bō zhī mín。」 fǎn yú lǔ， yǐ gào kǒng zi。 kǒng zi yuē：「 bǐ jiǎ xiū hún dùn shì zhī shù zhě yě： shí qí yī， bù zhī qí èr； zhì qí nèi， ér bù zhì qí wài。 fū míng bái rù sù， wú wèi fù pǔ， tǐ xìng bào shén， yǐ yóu shì sú zhī jiān zhě， rǔ jiāng gù jīng xié？ qiě hún dùn shì zhī shù， yǔ yǔ rǔ hé zú yǐ shí zhī zāi！」

**EN:** Zigong slunk away crestfallen, his color gone, out of sorts — and only after walking thirty li did he recover. His disciples said: "Who was that man just now? Why did you, Master, change countenance and lose color at the sight of him, and fail to recover yourself all day?" He said: "I had thought there was only one man under heaven; I did not know there was also this man. I heard from the Master: 'In affairs seek the feasible; in work seek completion; little labor, much result — this is the Way of the sage.' Now here is a man who thinks otherwise. One who holds the Way keeps his virtue whole; whose virtue is whole keeps his form whole; whose form is whole keeps his spirit whole. Wholeness of spirit — that is the Way of the sage. Such a one lodges his life among the people and walks beside them, not knowing where he goes — vast and vague, complete in purity! Work, profit, contrivance, skill — such a man is sure to forget them in his heart. A man like this does not go where his will is not, does not do what is not in his heart. Though the whole world praised him and he got what they ascribed to him, he would pay it no mind, lofty and unmoved; though the whole world condemned him and he lost what they ascribed to him, he would not take it in, carefree and indifferent. The world's blame and praise can neither add to him nor take away — this is called a man of whole virtue. As for me, I am a man of wind and waves." Returning to Lu, he reported this to Confucius. Confucius said: "He is one who affects the arts of the school of Hundun (渾沌氏): he knows the One but not the second thing; he orders what is within but does not order what is without. But one whose clarity enters into plainness, who in non-action returns to the uncarved block, who embodies his nature and embraces his spirit while roaming in the midst of the vulgar world — would you not surely be astonished at him? Besides, as for the arts of the school of Hundun — what could you and I ever know of them!"

## Section 14

**ZH:** 諄芒將東之大壑，適遇苑風於東海之濱。苑風曰：「子將奚之？」曰：「將之大壑。」曰：「奚為焉？」曰：「夫大壑之為物也，注焉而不滿，酌焉而不竭，吾將遊焉。」苑風曰：「夫子無意於橫目之民乎？願聞聖治。」諄芒曰：「聖治乎，官施而不失其宜，拔舉而不失其能，畢見其情事而行其所為，行言自為而天下化，手撓顧指，四方之民莫不俱至，此之謂聖治。」「願聞德人。」曰：「德人者，居無思，行無慮，不藏是非美惡。四海之內，共利之之謂悅，共給之之謂安；怊乎若嬰兒之失其母也，儻乎若行而失其道也。財用有餘而不知其所自來，飲食取足而不知其所從。此謂德人之容。」「願聞神人。」曰：「上神乘光，與形滅亡，此謂照曠。天地樂而萬事銷亡，萬物復情，此之謂混冥。」
**PY:** zhūn máng jiāng dōng zhī dà hè， shì yù yuàn fēng yú dōng hǎi zhī bīn。 yuàn fēng yuē：「 zi jiāng xī zhī？」 yuē：「 jiāng zhī dà hè。」 yuē：「 xī wèi yān？」 yuē：「 fū dà hè zhī wèi wù yě， zhù yān ér bù mǎn， zhuó yān ér bù jié， wú jiāng yóu yān。」 yuàn fēng yuē：「 fū zǐ wú yì yú héng mù zhī mín hū？ yuàn wén shèng zhì。」 zhūn máng yuē：「 shèng zhì hū， guān shī ér bù shī qí yí， bá jǔ ér bù shī qí néng， bì jiàn qí qíng shì ér xíng qí suǒ wèi， xíng yán zì wèi ér tiān xià huà， shǒu náo gù zhǐ， sì fāng zhī mín mò bù jù zhì， cǐ zhī wèi shèng zhì。」「 yuàn wén dé rén。」 yuē：「 dé rén zhě， jū wú sī， xíng wú lǜ， bù cáng shì fēi měi è。 sì hǎi zhī nèi， gòng lì zhī zhī wèi yuè， gòng gěi zhī zhī wèi ān； chāo hū ruò yīng ér zhī shī qí mǔ yě， tǎng hū ruò xíng ér shī qí dào yě。 cái yòng yǒu yú ér bù zhī qí suǒ zì lái， yǐn shí qǔ zú ér bù zhī qí suǒ cóng。 cǐ wèi dé rén zhī róng。」「 yuàn wén shén rén。」 yuē：「 shàng shén chéng guāng， yǔ xíng miè wáng， cǐ wèi zhào kuàng。 tiān dì lè ér wàn shì xiāo wáng， wàn wù fù qíng， cǐ zhī wèi hùn míng。」

**EN:** Zhunmang (諄芒), on his way east to the great gulf, happened to meet Yuanfeng (苑風) on the shore of the eastern sea. Yuanfeng said: "Where are you going?" He said: "To the great gulf." "What for?" He said: "The great gulf as a thing: pour into it and it does not fill; ladle from it and it does not drain. I am going to roam there." Yuanfeng said: "Have you no care for the level-eyed folk of men? I beg to hear of the sage's governing." Zhunmang said: "The sage's governing? He sets out offices and never misses the fitting; he selects and raises men and never misses the capable; he sees to the bottom of circumstances and affairs and does what is to be done; his deeds and words proceed of themselves and the world is transformed. He waves his hand, glances and points — and the people of the four quarters all come to him. This is called the sage's governing." "I beg to hear of the man of virtue." He said: "The man of virtue dwells without thought and moves without care; he harbors no right and wrong, fair and foul. Within the four seas, that all share in the profit is his joy; that all share in the provision is his peace. Sad — like an infant that has lost its mother; vague — like a traveler who has lost his road. Wealth and use are more than enough, and he knows not whence they come; food and drink are sufficient, and he knows not where they come from. This is the bearing of the man of virtue." "I beg to hear of the spirit-like man." He said: "He mounts the brightness on high, and his form is dissolved and gone. This is called the shining emptiness. Heaven and earth are his delight, and the ten thousand affairs melt away; the ten thousand things return to their true condition. This is called the merged obscurity."

## Section 15

**ZH:** 門無鬼與赤張滿稽，觀於武王之師。赤張滿稽曰：「不及有虞氏乎！故離此患也。」門無鬼曰：「天下均治而有虞氏治之邪，其亂而後治之與？」赤張滿稽曰：「天下均治之為願，而何計以有虞氏為？有虞氏之藥瘍也，禿而施髢，病而求醫。孝子操藥以修慈父，其色燋然，聖人羞之。至德之世，不尚賢，不使能；上如標枝，民如野鹿；端正而不知以為義，相愛而不知以為仁；實而不知以為忠，當而不知以為信；蠢動而相使，不以為賜。是故行而無迹，事而無傳。」
**PY:** mén wú guǐ yǔ chì zhāng mǎn jī， guān yú wǔ wáng zhī shī。 chì zhāng mǎn jī yuē：「 bù jí yǒu yú shì hū！ gù lí cǐ huàn yě。」 mén wú guǐ yuē：「 tiān xià jūn zhì ér yǒu yú shì zhì zhī xié， qí luàn ér hòu zhì zhī yǔ？」 chì zhāng mǎn jī yuē：「 tiān xià jūn zhì zhī wèi yuàn， ér hé jì yǐ yǒu yú shì wèi？ yǒu yú shì zhī yào yáng yě， tū ér shī dí， bìng ér qiú yī。 xiào zi cāo yào yǐ xiū cí fù， qí sè jiāo rán， shèng rén xiū zhī。 zhì dé zhī shì， bù shàng xián， bù shǐ néng； shàng rú biāo zhī， mín rú yě lù； duān zhèng ér bù zhī yǐ wèi yì， xiāng ài ér bù zhī yǐ wèi rén； shí ér bù zhī yǐ wèi zhōng， dāng ér bù zhī yǐ wèi xìn； chǔn dòng ér xiāng shǐ， bù yǐ wèi cì。 shì gù xíng ér wú jì， shì ér wú chuán。」

**EN:** Men Wugui (門無鬼) and Chizhang Manji (赤張滿稽) were watching the armies of King Wu. Chizhang Manji said: "He does not come up to the house of Youyu! That is why the world suffers this calamity." Men Wugui said: "Was the world in good order, and then the house of Youyu governed it? Or was it in disorder, and then he governed it?" Chizhang Manji said: "If the world were in good order as all wish, what use would there be for the house of Youyu? The house of Youyu was a medicine for a sore: the bald get hairpieces; the sick seek doctors. A filial son compounding drugs to cure a loving father, his face haggard — the sage would be ashamed of it. In the age of utmost virtue they did not exalt the worthy or employ the able. Those above were like the topmost twigs of a tree; the people were like wild deer. Upright, they did not know to call it rightness; loving one another, they did not know to call it humaneness; true, they did not know to call it loyalty; dependable, they did not know to call it good faith. Wriggling and stirring, they served one another without counting it a favor. Therefore their deeds left no tracks, and their affairs were handed down in no records."

## Section 16

**ZH:** 孝子不諛其親，忠臣不諂其君，臣子之盛也。親之所言而然，所行而善，則世俗謂之不肖子；君之所言而然，所行而善，則世俗謂之不肖臣。而未知此其必然邪！世俗之所謂然而然之，所謂善而善之，則不謂之道諛之人也。然則俗固嚴於親而尊於君邪！謂己道人，則勃然作色；謂己諛人，則怫然作色。而終身道人也，終身諛人也，合譬飾辭聚眾也，是始終本末不相坐。垂衣裳，設采色，動容貌，以媚一世，而不自謂道諛，與夫人之為徒，通是非，而不自謂眾人，愚之至也。知其愚者，非大愚也；知其惑者，非大惑也。大惑者，終身不解；大愚者，終身不靈。三人行而一人惑，所適者猶可致也，惑者少也；二人惑則勞而不至，惑者勝也。而今也以天下惑，予雖有祈嚮，不可得也。不亦悲乎！
**PY:** xiào zi bù yú qí qīn， zhōng chén bù chǎn qí jūn， chén zi zhī shèng yě。 qīn zhī suǒ yán ér rán， suǒ xíng ér shàn， zé shì sú wèi zhī bú xiào zǐ； jūn zhī suǒ yán ér rán， suǒ xíng ér shàn， zé shì sú wèi zhī bù xiào chén。 ér wèi zhī cǐ qí bì rán xié！ shì sú zhī suǒ wèi rán ér rán zhī， suǒ wèi shàn ér shàn zhī， zé bù wèi zhī dào yú zhī rén yě。 rán zé sú gù yán yú qīn ér zūn yú jūn xié！ wèi jǐ dào rén， zé bó rán zuò sè； wèi jǐ yú rén， zé fú rán zuò sè。 ér zhōng shēn dào rén yě， zhōng shēn yú rén yě， hé pì shì cí jù zhòng yě， shì shǐ zhōng běn mò bù xiāng zuò。 chuí yī shang， shè cǎi sè， dòng róng mào， yǐ mèi yī shì， ér bù zì wèi dào yú， yǔ fū rén zhī wèi tú， tōng shì fēi， ér bù zì wèi zhòng rén， yú zhī zhì yě。 zhī qí yú zhě， fēi dà yú yě； zhī qí huò zhě， fēi dà huò yě。 dà huò zhě， zhōng shēn bù jiě； dà yú zhě， zhōng shēn bù líng。 sān rén xíng ér yī rén huò， suǒ shì zhě yóu kě zhì yě， huò zhě shǎo yě； èr rén huò zé láo ér bù zhì， huò zhě shèng yě。 ér jīn yě yǐ tiān xià huò， yǔ suī yǒu qí xiàng， bù kě dé yě。 bù yì bēi hū！

**EN:** The filial son does not flatter his parents; the loyal minister does not toady to his lord — this is the height of minister and son. But one who assents to all his parents say and approves all they do, the world calls an unworthy son; one who assents to all his lord says and approves all he does, the world calls an unworthy minister. Yet who knows whether this rule is really necessary! One who assents to whatever the world calls right and approves whatever the world calls good is not called a flatterer and toady. Does the world then command more reverence than one's parents, more honor than one's lord! Call a man a flatterer and he bristles with anger; call him a toady and he darkens with rage. Yet all his life he flatters; all his life he toadies. He strings together metaphors and decks out phrases to gather the crowd — and so his beginning and end, root and branch, never have to answer to one another. [uncertain: 是始終本末不相坐 — rendered "beginning and end, root and branch are never brought to account against each other"; the force of 坐 ("be judged/penalized together") is disputed.] He drapes his robes, displays his colored patterns, composes his features to charm a whole generation — and does not call himself a flatterer; he is one of the herd, sharing their rights and wrongs — and does not call himself one of the crowd. This is the utmost folly. One who knows his folly is not a great fool; one who knows his delusion is not greatly deluded. The greatly deluded are never undeceived all their lives; the greatly foolish never understand all their lives. If three men walk together and one is deluded, they can still reach their destination, for the deluded are the fewer; but if two are deluded, they will labor and never arrive, for the deluded are the majority. And now the whole world is deluded: though I have my prayer and direction, I cannot attain it. Is it not sad!

## Section 17

**ZH:** 大聲不入於里耳，《折楊》、《皇荂》，則嗑然而笑。是故高言不止於眾人之心，至言不出，俗言勝也。以二缶鍾惑，而所適不得矣。而今也以天下惑，予雖有祈嚮，其庸可得邪？知其不可得也而強之，又一惑也，故莫若釋之而不推。不推，誰其比憂！厲之人夜半生其子，遽取火而視之，汲汲然惟恐其似己也。
**PY:** dà shēng bù rù yú lǐ ěr，《 zhé yáng》、《 huáng fū》， zé kē rán ér xiào。 shì gù gāo yán bù zhǐ yú zhòng rén zhī xīn， zhì yán bù chū， sú yán shèng yě。 yǐ èr fǒu zhōng huò， ér suǒ shì bù dé yǐ。 ér jīn yě yǐ tiān xià huò， yǔ suī yǒu qí xiàng， qí yōng kě dé xié？ zhī qí bù kě dé yě ér qiáng zhī， yòu yī huò yě， gù mò ruò shì zhī ér bù tuī。 bù tuī， shuí qí bǐ yōu！ lì zhī rén yè bàn shēng qí zi， jù qǔ huǒ ér shì zhī， jí jí rán wéi kǒng qí shì jǐ yě。

**EN:** The great music does not enter village ears; play *Breaking the Willow* (折楊) or *Bright Flowers* (皇荂) and they all guffaw with delight. Thus lofty words do not lodge in the hearts of the crowd. The utmost words do not come forth; vulgar words win the day. Two earthen pots drown out the bell — and the destination cannot be reached. And now the whole world is deluded: though I have my prayer and direction, how could I attain it! Knowing it cannot be attained, to force it is one more delusion. So nothing compares with letting go and not pushing on. If I do not push on, who can share my grief? An ugly woman bears a child in the middle of the night and snatches a torch to look at it — anxious, trembling, lest it should look like herself.

## Section 18

**ZH:** 百年之木，破為犧尊，青黃而文之，其斷在溝中。比犧尊於溝中之斷，則美惡有間矣，其於失性一也。跖與曾、史，行義有間矣，然其失性均也。且夫失性有五：一曰五色亂目，使目不明；二曰五聲亂耳，使耳不聰；三曰五臭薰鼻，困惾中顙；四曰五味濁口，使口厲爽；五曰趣舍滑心，使性飛揚。此五者，皆生之害也。而楊、墨乃始離跂自以為得，非吾所謂得也。夫得者困，可以為得乎？則鳩鴞之在於籠也，亦可以為得矣。且夫趣舍聲色以柴其內，皮弁、鷸冠、搢笏、紳修以約其外，內支盈於柴柵，外重纆繳，睆睆然在纆繳之中而自以為得，則是罪人交臂、歷指，而虎豹在於囊檻，亦可以為得矣。
**PY:** bǎi nián zhī mù， pò wèi xī zūn， qīng huáng ér wén zhī， qí duàn zài gōu zhōng。 bǐ xī zūn yú gōu zhōng zhī duàn， zé měi è yǒu jiān yǐ， qí yú shī xìng yī yě。 zhí yǔ céng、 shǐ， xíng yì yǒu jiān yǐ， rán qí shī xìng jūn yě。 qiě fū shī xìng yǒu wǔ： yī yuē wǔ sè luàn mù， shǐ mù bù míng； èr yuē wǔ shēng luàn ěr， shǐ ěr bù cōng； sān yuē wǔ chòu xūn bí， kùn zōng zhōng sǎng； sì yuē wǔ wèi zhuó kǒu， shǐ kǒu lì shuǎng； wǔ yuē qù shě huá xīn， shǐ xìng fēi yáng。 cǐ wǔ zhě， jiē shēng zhī hài yě。 ér yáng、 mò nǎi shǐ lí qí zì yǐ wèi dé， fēi wú suǒ wèi dé yě。 fū dé zhě kùn， kě yǐ wèi dé hū？ zé jiū xiāo zhī zài yú lóng yě， yì kě yǐ wèi dé yǐ。 qiě fū qù shě shēng sè yǐ chái qí nèi， pí biàn、 yù guān、 jìn hù、 shēn xiū yǐ yuē qí wài， nèi zhī yíng yú chái shān， wài zhòng mò jiǎo， huàn huàn rán zài mò jiǎo zhī zhōng ér zì yǐ wèi dé， zé shì zuì rén jiāo bì、 lì zhǐ， ér hǔ bào zài yú náng kǎn， yì kě yǐ wèi dé yǐ。

**EN:** A tree a hundred years old is cut up to make a sacrificial goblet, carved and patterned in blue and yellow — while the stump lies abandoned in the ditch. Compare the goblet with the stump in the ditch, and there is indeed a difference in beauty and ugliness — but in having lost their nature they are one. Robber Zhi on one side, Zeng and Shi on the other — there is indeed a difference in the rightness of their conduct — but in having lost their nature they are equal. Now there are five ways of losing one's nature. First: the five colors confuse the eye and make it unseeing. Second: the five notes confuse the ear and make it unhearing. Third: the five odors fumigate the nose and clog and trouble the brow. Fourth: the five flavors foul the mouth and make it harsh and ruined. Fifth: pursuits and aversions muddle the heart and make the nature flighty. These five are all injuries to life. Yet Yang and Mo stride on tiptoe, believing they have attained something — this is not what I call attainment. If the attained leaves a man hemmed in, can it be called attainment? Then the dove and owl in their cage could also be called attained. Moreover, pursuits and aversions, sounds and colors, pile up like firewood within; leather caps and snipe-feathered bonnets, tablets stuck in long sashes, bind and constrict without. Stuffed inside with fence-posts up to the brim, bound outside by layer upon layer of ropes, staring wide-eyed from within the ropes yet believing they have attained something — then the criminal with pinioned arms and squeezed fingers, and the tiger and leopard in sack and cage, could also be called attained.
