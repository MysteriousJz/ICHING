# The Floods of Autumn (秋水)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ The Floods of Autumn - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (18 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 秋水時至，百川灌河，涇流之大，兩涘渚崖之間，不辯牛馬。於是焉河伯欣然自喜，以天下之美為盡在己。順流而東行，至於北海，東面而視，不見水端，於是焉河伯始旋其面目，望洋向若而歎，曰：「野語有之曰『聞道百，以為莫己若』者，我之謂也。且夫我嘗聞少仲尼之聞而輕伯夷之義者，始吾弗信，今我睹子之難窮也，吾非至於子之門則殆矣，吾長見笑於大方之家。」
**PY:** qiū shuǐ shí zhì， bǎi chuān guàn hé， jīng liú zhī dà， liǎng sì zhǔ yá zhī jiān， bù biàn niú mǎ。 yú shì yān hé bó xīn rán zì xǐ， yǐ tiān xià zhī měi wèi jǐn zài jǐ。 shùn liú ér dōng xíng， zhì yú běi hǎi， dōng miàn ér shì， bù jiàn shuǐ duān， yú shì yān hé bó shǐ xuán qí miàn mù， wàng yáng xiàng ruò ér tàn， yuē：「 yě yǔ yǒu zhī yuē『 wén dào bǎi， yǐ wèi mò jǐ ruò』 zhě， wǒ zhī wèi yě。 qiě fū wǒ cháng wén shǎo zhòng ní zhī wén ér qīng bó yí zhī yì zhě， shǐ wú fú xìn， jīn wǒ dǔ zi zhī nán qióng yě， wú fēi zhì yú zi zhī mén zé dài yǐ， wú zhǎng jiàn xiào yú dà fāng zhī jiā。」

**EN:** The autumn floods came in their season, and a hundred streams poured into the Yellow River. The main current was so broad that between the two banks, across the islets and cliffs, one could not tell an ox from a horse. Thereupon the Earl of the River (河伯) was delighted with himself, believing that all the beauty under heaven was gathered in himself. Following the current east, he came to the Northern Sea. Looking eastward, he could not see the water's end. Thereupon the Earl of the River began to turn his face about; gazing at the boundless expanse, he sighed to Ruo (若) of the Northern Sea: "The rustic saying has it: 'Hearing the Way a hundred times over, one thinks none is his equal' — that describes me! And I had heard of those who slight Confucius's learning and think little of Boyi's rightness — at first I did not believe it. Now I behold your unfathomableness. Had I not come to your gate, I would have been in danger — I would long have been a laughingstock to the house of the great method!"

## Section 2

**ZH:** 北海若曰：「井蛙不可以語於海者，拘於虛也；夏蟲不可以語於冰者，篤於時也；曲士不可以語於道者，束於教也。今爾出於崖涘，觀於大海，乃知爾醜，爾將可與語大理矣。天下之水，莫大於海，萬川歸之，不知何時止而不盈；尾閭泄之，不知何時已而不虛；春秋不變，水旱不知。此其過江河之流，不可為量數。而吾未嘗以此自多者，自以比形於天地而受氣於陰陽，吾在天地之間，猶小石小木之在大山也，方存乎見少，又奚以自多！計四海之在天地之間也，不似礨空之在大澤乎？計中國之在海內，不似稊米之在大倉乎？號物之數謂之萬，人處一焉；人卒九州，穀食之所生，舟車之所通，人處一焉。此其比萬物也，不似豪末之在於馬體乎？五帝之所連，三王之所爭，仁人之所憂，任士之所勞，盡此矣。伯夷辭之以為名，仲尼語之以為博，此其自多也，不似爾向之自多於水乎？」
**PY:** běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 jǐng wā bù kě yǐ yǔ yú hǎi zhě， jū yú xū yě； xià chóng bù kě yǐ yǔ yú bīng zhě， dǔ yú shí yě； qū shì bù kě yǐ yǔ yú dào zhě， shù yú jiào yě。 jīn ěr chū yú yá sì， guān yú dà hǎi， nǎi zhī ěr chǒu， ěr jiāng kě yǔ yǔ dà lǐ yǐ。 tiān xià zhī shuǐ， mò dà yú hǎi， wàn chuān guī zhī， bù zhī hé shí zhǐ ér bù yíng； wěi lǘ xiè zhī， bù zhī hé shí yǐ ér bù xū； chūn qiū bù biàn， shuǐ hàn bù zhī。 cǐ qí guò jiāng hé zhī liú， bù kě wèi liàng shù。 ér wú wèi cháng yǐ cǐ zì duō zhě， zì yǐ bǐ xíng yú tiān dì ér shòu qì yú yīn yáng， wú zài tiān dì zhī jiān， yóu xiǎo shí xiǎo mù zhī zài dà shān yě， fāng cún hū jiàn shǎo， yòu xī yǐ zì duō！ jì sì hǎi zhī zài tiān dì zhī jiān yě， bù shì lěi kōng zhī zài dà zé hū？ jì zhōng guó zhī zài hǎi nèi， bù shì tí mǐ zhī zài dà cāng hū？ hào wù zhī shù wèi zhī wàn， rén chù yī yān； rén zú jiǔ zhōu， gǔ shí zhī suǒ shēng， zhōu chē zhī suǒ tōng， rén chù yī yān。 cǐ qí bǐ wàn wù yě， bù shì háo mò zhī zài yú mǎ tǐ hū？ wǔ dì zhī suǒ lián， sān wáng zhī suǒ zhēng， rén rén zhī suǒ yōu， rèn shì zhī suǒ láo， jǐn cǐ yǐ。 bó yí cí zhī yǐ wèi míng， zhòng ní yǔ zhī yǐ wèi bó， cǐ qí zì duō yě， bù shì ěr xiàng zhī zì duō yú shuǐ hū？」

**EN:** Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "You cannot speak of the sea to a frog in a well — it is bound by its dwelling. You cannot speak of ice to an insect of summer — it is fettered by its season. You cannot speak of the Way to a cramped scholar — he is bound by his teaching. Now that you have come out of your banks and gazed on the great sea and know your own shabbiness, you may begin to hear of the great pattern. Of the waters under heaven, none is greater than the sea: the ten thousand streams return to it, and none knows when they will stop, yet it is not filled; the Tailgate drains it, and none knows when that will cease, yet it is not emptied; spring and autumn it does not change; flood and drought it does not know. Its surpassing of the Yangzi and the Yellow River cannot be reckoned in numbers. Yet I have never on this account made much of myself. I take my form in comparison with heaven and earth; I receive my breath from the yin and yang. Between heaven and earth I am like a pebble, a sapling on a great mountain: I abide in being seen as small — on what account should I make much of myself? Reckon the four seas within heaven and earth — is it not like an ant-hole in a great marsh? Reckon the Middle Kingdom within the seas — is it not like a grain of wild rice in a great granary? We call the number of things 'the ten thousand'; man occupies one of them. Men crowd the nine provinces; where grain and food grow, where boats and carts pass — man occupies one place among them. Compare him with the ten thousand things — is he not like the tip of a hair on a horse's body? All that the five emperors handed down, that the three kings contended for, that humane men grieved over, that committed scholars toiled for — it is all within this. Boyi declined it for a name; Confucius discoursed on it to be deemed broad. In this making much of themselves — was it not like your just now making much of yourself over your waters?"

## Section 3

**ZH:** 河伯曰：「然則吾大天地而小毫末可乎？」北海若曰：「否。夫物，量無窮，時無止，分無常，終始無故。是故大知觀於遠近，故小而不寡，大而不多，知量無窮；證曏今故，故遙而不悶，掇而不跂，知時無止；察乎盈虛，故得而不喜，失而不憂，知分之無常也；明乎坦塗，故生而不說，死而不禍，知終始之不可故也。計人之所知，不若其所不知；其生之時，不若未生之時。以其至小，求窮其至大之域，是故迷亂而不能自得也。由此觀之，又何以知毫末之足以定至細之倪！又何以知天地之足以窮至大之域！」
**PY:** hé bó yuē：「 rán zé wú dà tiān dì ér xiǎo háo mò kě hū？」 běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 fǒu。 fū wù， liàng wú qióng， shí wú zhǐ， fēn wú cháng， zhōng shǐ wú gù。 shì gù dà zhī guān yú yuǎn jìn， gù xiǎo ér bù guǎ， dà ér bù duō， zhī liàng wú qióng； zhèng xiǎng jīn gù， gù yáo ér bù mèn， duō ér bù qí， zhī shí wú zhǐ； chá hū yíng xū， gù dé ér bù xǐ， shī ér bù yōu， zhī fēn zhī wú cháng yě； míng hū tǎn tú， gù shēng ér bù shuō， sǐ ér bù huò， zhī zhōng shǐ zhī bù kě gù yě。 jì rén zhī suǒ zhī， bù ruò qí suǒ bù zhī； qí shēng zhī shí， bù ruò wèi shēng zhī shí。 yǐ qí zhì xiǎo， qiú qióng qí zhì dà zhī yù， shì gù mí luàn ér bù néng zì dé yě。 yóu cǐ guān zhī， yòu hé yǐ zhī háo mò zhī zú yǐ dìng zhì xì zhī ní！ yòu hé yǐ zhī tiān dì zhī zú yǐ qióng zhì dà zhī yù！」

**EN:** The Earl of the River said: "Then may I count heaven and earth as great and the tip of a hair as small?" Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "No. Things: their measure is inexhaustible; their time is endless; their portion is inconstant; their end and beginning are never the same. Therefore great knowledge looks upon both near and far: the small is not found lacking, the great is not found much — knowing that measure is inexhaustible. It examines past and present: the remote does not oppress it, the close at hand does not tempt it to grasp — knowing that time is endless. It discerns fullness and emptiness: gaining, it does not rejoice; losing, it does not grieve — knowing that portion is inconstant. It is clear about the level road: living, it is not glad; dying, it does not count it calamity — knowing that end and beginning cannot be made constant. Reckon what man knows against what he does not know — the one does not compare with the other; the time he is alive does not compare with the time before he was born. With the utmost smallness to seek to sound the realm of the utmost greatness — therefore he is lost and confused and cannot keep hold of himself. Seen in this light, how do we know that the tip of a hair is fit to fix the boundary of the utmost fineness! And how do we know that heaven and earth are fit to exhaust the realm of the utmost greatness!"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 河伯曰：「世之議者皆曰：『至精無形，至大不可圍。』是信情乎？」北海若曰：「夫自細視大者不盡，自大視細者不明。夫精，小之微也，垺，大之殷也，故異便。此勢之有也。夫精粗者，期於有形者也；無形者，數之所不能分也；不可圍者，數之所不能窮也。可以言論者，物之粗也；可以意致者，物之精也；言之所不能論，意之所不能察致者，不期精粗焉。是故大人之行，不出乎害人，不多仁恩；動不為利，不賤門隸；貨財弗爭，不多辭讓；事焉不惜人，不多食乎力，不賤貪污；行殊乎俗，不多辟異；為在從眾，不賤佞諂；世之爵祿不足以為勸，戮恥不足以為辱；知是非之不可為分，細大之不可為倪。聞曰：『道人不聞，至德不得，大人無己，約分之至也。」
**PY:** hé bó yuē：「 shì zhī yì zhě jiē yuē：『 zhì jīng wú xíng， zhì dà bù kě wéi。』 shì xìn qíng hū？」 běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 fū zì xì shì dà zhě bù jǐn， zì dà shì xì zhě bù míng。 fū jīng， xiǎo zhī wēi yě， fū， dà zhī yīn yě， gù yì biàn。 cǐ shì zhī yǒu yě。 fū jīng cū zhě， qī yú yǒu xíng zhě yě； wú xíng zhě， shù zhī suǒ bù néng fēn yě； bù kě wéi zhě， shù zhī suǒ bù néng qióng yě。 kě yǐ yán lùn zhě， wù zhī cū yě； kě yǐ yì zhì zhě， wù zhī jīng yě； yán zhī suǒ bù néng lùn， yì zhī suǒ bù néng chá zhì zhě， bù qī jīng cū yān。 shì gù dà rén zhī xíng， bù chū hū hài rén， bù duō rén ēn； dòng bù wèi lì， bù jiàn mén lì； huò cái fú zhēng， bù duō cí ràng； shì yān bù xī rén， bù duō shí hū lì， bù jiàn tān wū； xíng shū hū sú， bù duō pì yì； wèi zài cóng zhòng， bù jiàn nìng chǎn； shì zhī jué lù bù zú yǐ wèi quàn， lù chǐ bù zú yǐ wèi rǔ； zhī shì fēi zhī bù kě wèi fēn， xì dà zhī bù kě wèi ní。 wén yuē：『 dào rén bù wén， zhì dé bù dé， dà rén wú jǐ， yuē fēn zhī zhì yě。」

**EN:** The Earl of the River said: "The disputers of the age all say: 'The utmost fine has no form; the utmost great cannot be encompassed.' Is that a true account?" Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "From the small, looking at the great, one cannot see to the end; from the great, looking at the small, one cannot see clearly. The fine is the minuteness of the small; the massive is the fullness of the great — therefore each has its convenience. This is inherent in their situation. Fineness and coarseness pertain to what has form. The formless — numbers cannot divide it. The unencompassable — numbers cannot exhaust it. What can be discussed in words is the coarseness of things; what can be reached by thought is the fineness of things. What words cannot discuss and thought cannot reach — that does not pertain to fineness or coarseness at all. Therefore the conduct of the great man never goes out to injure others, yet he does not make much of humaneness and favor; he does not move for profit, yet he does not despise the gatekeeper; he does not scramble for goods and wealth, yet he does not make much of declining and yielding; in affairs he does not rely on others, yet he does not make much of feeding on his own strength, nor despise the grasping and corrupt; his conduct stands apart from the vulgar, yet he does not make much of being singular; his action follows the crowd, yet he does not despise flatterers. The titles and stipends of the age are not enough to move him; punishment and disgrace are not enough to shame him. He knows that right and wrong cannot be divided, that fine and great admit no boundary. I have heard it said: 'The man of the Way is not heard of; utmost virtue attains nothing; the great man has no self' — the utmost in keeping to one's portion."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 河伯曰：「若物之外，若物之內，惡至而倪貴賤？惡至而倪小大？」北海若曰：「以道觀之，物無貴賤；以物觀之，自貴而相賤：以俗觀之，貴賤不在己。以差觀之，因其所大而大之，則萬物莫不大；因其所小而小之，則萬物莫不小。知天地之為稊米也，知豪末之為丘山也，則差數等矣。以功觀之，因其所有而有之，則萬物莫不有；因其所無而無之，則萬物莫不無。知東西之相反，而不可以相無，則功分定矣。以趣觀之，因其所然而然之，則萬物莫不然；因其所非而非之，則萬物莫不非。知堯、桀之自然而相非，則趣操睹矣。昔者堯、舜讓而帝，之、噲讓而絕；湯、武爭而王，白公爭而滅。由此觀之，爭讓之禮，堯、桀之行，貴賤有時，未可以為常也。梁麗可以衝城，而不可以窒穴，言殊器也；騏驥驊騮，一日而馳千里，捕鼠不如狸狌，言殊技也；鴟鵂夜撮蚤，察毫末，晝出瞋目而不見丘山，言殊性也。故曰：蓋師是而無非，師治而無亂乎？是未明天地之理，萬物之情者也。是猶師天而無地，師陰而無陽，其不可行明矣。然且語而不舍，非愚則誣也。帝王殊禪，三代殊繼。差其時，逆其俗者，謂之篡夫；當其時，順其俗者，謂之義徒。默默乎河伯！女惡知貴賤之門，大小之家！」
**PY:** hé bó yuē：「 ruò wù zhī wài， ruò wù zhī nèi， è zhì ér ní guì jiàn？ è zhì ér ní xiǎo dà？」 běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 yǐ dào guān zhī， wù wú guì jiàn； yǐ wù guān zhī， zì guì ér xiāng jiàn： yǐ sú guān zhī， guì jiàn bù zài jǐ。 yǐ chà guān zhī， yīn qí suǒ dà ér dà zhī， zé wàn wù mò bù dà； yīn qí suǒ xiǎo ér xiǎo zhī， zé wàn wù mò bù xiǎo。 zhī tiān dì zhī wèi tí mǐ yě， zhī háo mò zhī wèi qiū shān yě， zé chà shù děng yǐ。 yǐ gōng guān zhī， yīn qí suǒ yǒu ér yǒu zhī， zé wàn wù mò bù yǒu； yīn qí suǒ wú ér wú zhī， zé wàn wù mò bù wú。 zhī dōng xī zhī xiāng fǎn， ér bù kě yǐ xiāng wú， zé gōng fēn dìng yǐ。 yǐ qù guān zhī， yīn qí suǒ rán ér rán zhī， zé wàn wù mò bù rán； yīn qí suǒ fēi ér fēi zhī， zé wàn wù mò bù fēi。 zhī yáo、 jié zhī zì rán ér xiāng fēi， zé qù cāo dǔ yǐ。 xī zhě yáo、 shùn ràng ér dì， zhī、 kuài ràng ér jué； tāng、 wǔ zhēng ér wáng， bái gōng zhēng ér miè。 yóu cǐ guān zhī， zhēng ràng zhī lǐ， yáo、 jié zhī xíng， guì jiàn yǒu shí， wèi kě yǐ wèi cháng yě。 liáng lì kě yǐ chōng chéng， ér bù kě yǐ zhì xué， yán shū qì yě； qí jì huá liú， yī rì ér chí qiān lǐ， bǔ shǔ bù rú lí shēng， yán shū jì yě； chī xiū yè cuō zǎo， chá háo mò， zhòu chū chēn mù ér bù jiàn qiū shān， yán shū xìng yě。 gù yuē： gài shī shì ér wú fēi， shī zhì ér wú luàn hū？ shì wèi míng tiān dì zhī lǐ， wàn wù zhī qíng zhě yě。 shì yóu shī tiān ér wú dì， shī yīn ér wú yáng， qí bù kě xíng míng yǐ。 rán qiě yǔ ér bù shě， fēi yú zé wū yě。 dì wáng shū chán， sān dài shū jì。 chà qí shí， nì qí sú zhě， wèi zhī cuàn fū； dāng qí shí， shùn qí sú zhě， wèi zhī yì tú。 mò mò hū hé bó！ nǚ è zhī guì jiàn zhī mén， dà xiǎo zhī jiā！」

**EN:** The Earl of the River said: "Whether outside things or inside them, where does one arrive at a boundary of noble and base? Where does one arrive at a boundary of small and great?" Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "Looked at through the Way, things are neither noble nor base. Looked at through things, each counts itself noble and the other base. Looked at through custom, noble and base do not lie in oneself. Looked at through difference: by that whereby a thing is great, count it great — then among the ten thousand things none is not great; by that whereby a thing is small, count it small — then none is not small. Know that heaven and earth are a grain of wild rice; know that the tip of a hair is a hill or a mountain — then the scale of differences is settled. Looked at through function: by what a thing has, count it as having — then none is without; by what a thing lacks, count it as lacking — then none has. Know that east and west are opposites yet cannot be without each other — then the share of functions is fixed. Looked at through inclination: by what a thing approves, approve it — then none is not approved; by what it condemns, condemn it — then none is not condemned. Know that Yao and Jie each approved himself and condemned the other — then their inclinations and conduct are plain to see. Of old Yao and Shun yielded and became sovereigns; Zhi and Kuai (噲) yielded and were cut off. Tang and Wu contended and became kings; the Duke of Bai (白公) contended and was destroyed. Seen in this light, the rites of contending and yielding, the conduct of Yao and Jie — noble and base have their season; they cannot be held as constant. A ridge-beam can batter down a city wall but cannot plug a hole — this speaks of differing implements. Qiji and Hualiu (騏驥驊騮) can gallop a thousand li in a day, but for catching mice they are no match for a wildcat — this speaks of differing skills. The horned owl at night snaps up fleas and discerns the tip of a hair, but by day it opens its eyes wide and cannot see a hill or a mountain — this speaks of differing natures. Hence it is said: why not take right as teacher and be done with wrong, take order as teacher and be done with disorder? That is to fail to see the pattern of heaven and earth and the genuine condition of the ten thousand things. It is like taking Heaven as teacher and being done with earth, taking yin as teacher and being done with yang — the impossibility of it is plain. Yet men go on saying it without cease: they are either fools or deceivers. Emperors and kings abdicate differently; the three dynasties succeeded differently. One who misses the time and defies custom is called a usurper; one who meets the time and follows custom is called a man of right. Be silent, Earl of the River! How could you know the gate of noble and base, the house of small and great!"

## Section 6

**ZH:** 河伯曰：「然則我何為乎？何不為乎？吾辭受趣舍，吾終奈何？」北海若曰：「以道觀之，何貴何賤，是謂反衍，無拘而志，與道大蹇。何少何多，是謂謝施，無一而行，與道參差。嚴乎若國之有君，其無私德；繇繇乎若祭之有社，其無私福；泛泛乎其若四方之無窮，其無所畛域。兼懷萬物，其孰承翼？是謂無方。萬物一齊，孰短孰長？道無終始，物有死生，不恃其成；一虛一滿，不位乎其形。年不可舉，時不可止；消息盈虛，終則有始。是所以語大義之方，論萬物之理也。物之生也若驟若馳，無動而不變，無時而不移。何為乎？何不為乎？夫固將自化。」
**PY:** hé bó yuē：「 rán zé wǒ hé wèi hū？ hé bù wèi hū？ wú cí shòu qù shě， wú zhōng nài hé？」 běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 yǐ dào guān zhī， hé guì hé jiàn， shì wèi fǎn yǎn， wú jū ér zhì， yǔ dào dà jiǎn。 hé shǎo hé duō， shì wèi xiè shī， wú yī ér xíng， yǔ dào cān chà。 yán hū ruò guó zhī yǒu jūn， qí wú sī dé； yáo yáo hū ruò jì zhī yǒu shè， qí wú sī fú； fàn fàn hū qí ruò sì fāng zhī wú qióng， qí wú suǒ zhěn yù。 jiān huái wàn wù， qí shú chéng yì？ shì wèi wú fāng。 wàn wù yī qí， shú duǎn shú zhǎng？ dào wú zhōng shǐ， wù yǒu sǐ shēng， bù shì qí chéng； yī xū yī mǎn， bù wèi hū qí xíng。 nián bù kě jǔ， shí bù kě zhǐ； xiāo xī yíng xū， zhōng zé yǒu shǐ。 shì suǒ yǐ yǔ dà yì zhī fāng， lùn wàn wù zhī lǐ yě。 wù zhī shēng yě ruò zhòu ruò chí， wú dòng ér bù biàn， wú shí ér bù yí。 hé wèi hū？ hé bù wèi hū？ fū gù jiāng zì huà。」

**EN:** The Earl of the River said: "Then what am I to do? What am I not to do? In declining and accepting, pursuing and abandoning — how am I to finish?" Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "Looked at through the Way, what is noble, what is base? This is called the endless reversion. Do not constrain your will and hobble yourself against the Way. What is little, what is much? This is called give-and-take. Do not hold to one line and mismatch the Way. Be as solemn as a state's lord — no partial virtue; as free and easy as the altar at the sacrifice — no partial blessing; as boundless as the four quarters without end — no border or boundary. Embrace the ten thousand things together — which one receives your shelter? This is called having no direction. When the ten thousand things are one in measure, what is short, what is long? The Way is without end or beginning; things have death and life — do not lean on their completion. Now empty, now full, they do not stay fixed in their form. The years cannot be lifted and carried; time cannot be stopped. Waning, waxing, filling, emptying — at the end there is a beginning again. This is the way to speak of the method of the great meaning, to discourse on the pattern of the ten thousand things. The life of things is like a gallop, a race: with every motion they change; with every moment they shift. What to do? What not to do? They will surely transform of themselves."

## Section 7

**ZH:** 河伯曰：「然則何貴於道邪？」北海若曰：「知道者必達於理，達於理者必明於權，明於權者不以物害己。至德者，火弗能熱，水弗能溺，寒暑弗能害，禽獸弗能賊。非謂其薄之也，言察乎安危，寧於禍福，謹於去就，莫之能害也。故曰：天在內，人在外，德在乎天。知天人之行，本乎天，位乎得。蹢䠱而屈伸，反要而語極。」曰：「何謂天？何謂人？」北海若曰：「牛馬四足，是謂天；落馬首，穿牛鼻，是謂人。故曰：無以人滅天，無以故滅命，無以得殉名。謹守而勿失，是謂反其真。」
**PY:** hé bó yuē：「 rán zé hé guì yú dào xié？」 běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 zhī dào zhě bì dá yú lǐ， dá yú lǐ zhě bì míng yú quán， míng yú quán zhě bù yǐ wù hài jǐ。 zhì dé zhě， huǒ fú néng rè， shuǐ fú néng nì， hán shǔ fú néng hài， qín shòu fú néng zéi。 fēi wèi qí báo zhī yě， yán chá hū ān wēi， níng yú huò fú， jǐn yú qù jiù， mò zhī néng hài yě。 gù yuē： tiān zài nèi， rén zài wài， dé zài hū tiān。 zhī tiān rén zhī xíng， běn hū tiān， wèi hū dé。 dí zhú ér qū shēn， fǎn yào ér yǔ jí。」 yuē：「 hé wèi tiān？ hé wèi rén？」 běi hǎi ruò yuē：「 niú mǎ sì zú， shì wèi tiān； luò mǎ shǒu， chuān niú bí， shì wèi rén。 gù yuē： wú yǐ rén miè tiān， wú yǐ gù miè mìng， wú yǐ dé xùn míng。 jǐn shǒu ér wù shī， shì wèi fǎn qí zhēn。」

**EN:** The Earl of the River said: "Then what is there to prize in the Way?" Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "One who knows the Way is sure to penetrate the pattern; one who penetrates the pattern is sure to be clear about expediency; one who is clear about expediency does not let things injure himself. The man of utmost virtue — fire cannot burn him; water cannot drown him; cold and heat cannot harm him; birds and beasts cannot prey on him. This is not to say that he treats them lightly; it means that he examines safety and danger, rests content in misfortune and fortune, is careful in departing and approaching — so that nothing can injure him. Hence it is said: Heaven is within; the human is without; virtue lies in Heaven. Know the workings of Heaven and man: root yourself in Heaven and take your place in attainment. Hesitating or striding, bending or stretching — return to the gist and speak of the utmost." He said: "What do you mean by Heaven? What do you mean by the human?" Ruo of the Northern Sea said: "That oxen and horses have four feet — this is called Heaven. That a halter is put on the horse's head and the ox's nose is pierced — this is called the human. Hence it is said: do not let the human extinguish Heaven; do not let contrivance extinguish destiny; do not let attainment be martyred to fame. Keep careful watch and do not lose it — this is called returning to one's truth."

## Section 8

**ZH:** 夔憐蚿，蚿憐蛇，蛇憐風，風憐目，目憐心。
**PY:** kuí lián xián， xián lián shé， shé lián fēng， fēng lián mù， mù lián xīn。

**EN:** The Kui envies the millipede; the millipede envies the snake; the snake envies the wind; the wind envies the eye; the eye envies the mind.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 夔謂蚿曰：「吾以一足趻踔而行，予無如矣。今子之使萬足，獨奈何？」蚿曰：「不然。子不見夫唾者乎？噴則大者如珠，小者如霧，雜而下者不可勝數也。今予動吾天機，而不知其所以然。」
**PY:** kuí wèi xián yuē：「 wú yǐ yī zú chěn chuō ér xíng， yǔ wú rú yǐ。 jīn zi zhī shǐ wàn zú， dú nài hé？」 xián yuē：「 bù rán。 zi bù jiàn fū tuò zhě hū？ pēn zé dà zhě rú zhū， xiǎo zhě rú wù， zá ér xià zhě bù kě shèng shù yě。 jīn yǔ dòng wú tiān jī， ér bù zhī qí suǒ yǐ rán。」

**EN:** The Kui said to the millipede: "I hop and stumble along on one foot, and that is all I can manage. Now you drive ten thousand feet — how ever do you do it?" The millipede said: "It is not so. Have you never seen a man spit? When he spits, the big drops are like pearls, the small like mist, and the mingled spray falling is beyond counting. I simply move my heaven-given impulse, without knowing how it does it."

## Section 10

**ZH:** 蚿謂蛇曰：「吾以眾足行，而不及子之無足，何也？」蛇曰：「夫天機之所動，何可易邪？吾安用足哉！」
**PY:** xián wèi shé yuē：「 wú yǐ zhòng zú xíng， ér bù jí zi zhī wú zú， hé yě？」 shé yuē：「 fū tiān jī zhī suǒ dòng， hé kě yì xié？ wú ān yòng zú zāi！」

**EN:** The millipede said to the snake: "I walk on a multitude of feet, yet I cannot match you with no feet at all. Why is that?" The snake said: "What is moved by the heaven-given impulse — how could it be exchanged? What use have I for feet!"

## Section 11

**ZH:** 蛇謂風曰：「予動吾脊脅而行，則有似也。今子蓬蓬然起於北海，蓬蓬然入於南海，而似無有，何也？」風曰：「然。予蓬蓬然起於北海而入於南海也，然而指我則勝我，䠓我亦勝我。雖然，夫折大木，蜚大屋者，唯我能也，故以眾小不勝為大勝也。為大勝者，唯聖人能之。」
**PY:** shé wèi fēng yuē：「 yǔ dòng wú jí xié ér xíng， zé yǒu shì yě。 jīn zi péng péng rán qǐ yú běi hǎi， péng péng rán rù yú nán hǎi， ér shì wú yǒu， hé yě？」 fēng yuē：「 rán。 yǔ péng péng rán qǐ yú běi hǎi ér rù yú nán hǎi yě， rán ér zhǐ wǒ zé shèng wǒ， qiū wǒ yì shèng wǒ。 suī rán， fū zhé dà mù， fēi dà wū zhě， wéi wǒ néng yě， gù yǐ zhòng xiǎo bù shèng wèi dà shèng yě。 wèi dà shèng zhě， wéi shèng rén néng zhī。」

**EN:** The snake said to the wind: "I move my spine and ribs and walk — there is at least a semblance of something. But you — whirling, you rise from the Northern Sea; whirling, you arrive in the Southern Sea — with a semblance of nothing at all. How is that?" The wind said: "Just so. Whirling, I rise from the Northern Sea and arrive in the Southern Sea. Yet point a finger at me and it masters me; trample me and it masters me. Even so — to snap great trees and lift great roofs: that only I can do. Thus, out of many small defeats I make the great victory. To make the great victory — only the sage can do that."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 孔子遊於匡，宋人圍之數匝，而絃歌不惙。子路入見，曰：「何夫子之娛也？」孔子曰：「來！吾語女。我諱窮久矣，而不免，命也；求通久矣，而不得，時也。當堯、舜而天下無窮人，非知得也，當桀，紂而天下無通人，非知失也，時勢適然。夫水行不避蛟龍者，漁父之勇也；陸行不避兕虎者，獵夫之勇也；白刃交於前，視死若生者，烈士之勇也；知窮之有命，知通之有時，臨大難而不懼者，聖人之勇也。由處矣！吾命有所制矣。」無幾何，將甲者進，辭曰：「以為陽虎也，故圍之；今非也，請辭而退。」
**PY:** kǒng zi yóu yú kuāng， sòng rén wéi zhī shù zā， ér xián gē bù chuò。 zi lù rù jiàn， yuē：「 hé fū zǐ zhī yú yě？」 kǒng zi yuē：「 lái！ wú yǔ nǚ。 wǒ huì qióng jiǔ yǐ， ér bù miǎn， mìng yě； qiú tōng jiǔ yǐ， ér bù dé， shí yě。 dāng yáo、 shùn ér tiān xià wú qióng rén， fēi zhī dé yě， dāng jié， zhòu ér tiān xià wú tōng rén， fēi zhī shī yě， shí shì shì rán。 fū shuǐ xíng bù bì jiāo lóng zhě， yú fù zhī yǒng yě； lù xíng bù bì sì hǔ zhě， liè fū zhī yǒng yě； bái rèn jiāo yú qián， shì sǐ ruò shēng zhě， liè shì zhī yǒng yě； zhī qióng zhī yǒu mìng， zhī tōng zhī yǒu shí， lín dà nán ér bù jù zhě， shèng rén zhī yǒng yě。 yóu chù yǐ！ wú mìng yǒu suǒ zhì yǐ。」 wú jǐ hé， jiāng jiǎ zhě jìn， cí yuē：「 yǐ wèi yáng hǔ yě， gù wéi zhī； jīn fēi yě， qǐng cí ér tuì。」

**EN:** When Confucius was roaming at Kuang (匡), the men of Song surrounded him in several rings — yet he went on strumming and singing without pause. Zilu came in and said: "How can you be so merry, Master?" Confucius said: "Come, I will tell you. I have shunned failure for a long time, yet I have not escaped it — that is destiny. I have sought success for a long time, yet have not attained it — that is the times. In the time of Yao and Shun there were no failures in the world — not because their knowledge had gained something; in the time of Jie and Zhou there were no successes in the world — not because their knowledge had lost something. The times and their situation made it so. To travel the waters without avoiding flood-dragons — that is the fisherman's courage. To travel the land without avoiding rhino and tiger — that is the hunter's courage. To face crossed naked blades and look on death as life — that is the courage of the ardent man. To know that failure has its destiny and success its times, to meet great calamity without fear — that is the courage of the sage. You, sit still! My destiny has its governor." Not long after, the captain of the armored men came in and apologized: "We took you for Yang Hu (陽虎); that is why we surrounded you. Since you are not he, we beg leave to withdraw."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 公孫龍問於魏牟曰：「龍少學先生之道，長而明仁義之行，合同異，雜堅白，然不然，可不可，困百家之知，窮眾口之辯，吾自以為至達已。今吾聞莊子之言，汒焉異之，不知論之不及與，知之弗若與？今吾無所開吾喙，敢問其方。」
**PY:** gōng sūn lóng wèn yú wèi móu yuē：「 lóng shǎo xué xiān shēng zhī dào， zhǎng ér míng rén yì zhī xíng， hé tóng yì， zá jiān bái， rán bù rán， kě bù kě， kùn bǎi jiā zhī zhī， qióng zhòng kǒu zhī biàn， wú zì yǐ wèi zhì dá yǐ。 jīn wú wén zhuāng zi zhī yán， máng yān yì zhī， bù zhī lùn zhī bù jí yǔ， zhī zhī fú ruò yǔ？ jīn wú wú suǒ kāi wú huì， gǎn wèn qí fāng。」

**EN:** Gongsun Long (公孫龍) asked Wei Mou (魏牟): "In my youth I studied the way of the former kings; grown, I made clear the conduct of humaneness and rightness. I united same and different; I blended hard and white; I so-d the not-so and admissibled the inadmissible. I tied up the knowledge of the hundred schools; I exhausted the disputation of a multitude of mouths. I considered myself utterly penetrating. But now I have heard the words of Zhuangzi, and I am dazed by their strangeness. I do not know whether my argument falls short of his, or whether my knowledge is not his equal. Now I cannot open my beak at all. May I ask the method?"

## Section 14

**ZH:** 公子牟隱机太息，仰天而笑曰：「子獨不聞夫埳井之鼃乎？謂東海之鱉曰：『吾樂與！出跳梁乎井幹之上，入休乎缺甃之崖，赴水則接腋持頤，蹶泥則沒足滅跗，還虷蟹與科斗，莫吾能若也。且夫擅一壑之水，而跨跱埳井之樂，此亦至矣，夫子奚不時來入觀乎？』東海之鱉左足未入，而右膝已縶矣。於是逡巡而卻，告之海曰：『夫千里之遠，不足以舉其大；千仞之高，不足以極其深。禹之時，十年九潦，而水弗為加益；湯之時，八年七旱，而崖不為加損。夫不為頃久推移，不以多少進退者，此亦東海之大樂也。』於是埳井之鼃聞之，適適然驚，規規然自失也。且夫知不知是非之竟，而猶欲觀於莊子之言，是猶使蚊負山，商蚷馳河也，必不勝任矣。且夫知不知論極妙之言，而自適一時之利者，是非埳井之鼃與？且彼方跐黃泉而登大皇，無南無北，奭然四解，淪於不測；無東無西，始於玄冥，反於大通。子乃規規然而求之以察，索之以辯，是直用管窺天，用錐指地也，不亦小乎！子往矣！且子獨不聞壽陵餘子之學行於邯鄲與？未得國能，又失其故行矣，直匍匐而歸耳。今子不去，將忘子之故，失子之業。」
**PY:** gōng zi móu yǐn jī tài xī， yǎng tiān ér xiào yuē：「 zi dú bù wén fū kǎn jǐng zhī wā hū？ wèi dōng hǎi zhī biē yuē：『 wú lè yǔ！ chū tiào liáng hū jǐng gàn zhī shàng， rù xiū hū quē zhòu zhī yá， fù shuǐ zé jiē yè chí yí， jué ní zé méi zú miè fū， hái hán xiè yǔ kē dòu， mò wú néng ruò yě。 qiě fū shàn yī hè zhī shuǐ， ér kuà zhì kǎn jǐng zhī lè， cǐ yì zhì yǐ， fū zǐ xī bù shí lái rù guān hū？』 dōng hǎi zhī biē zuǒ zú wèi rù， ér yòu xī yǐ zhí yǐ。 yú shì qūn xún ér què， gào zhī hǎi yuē：『 fū qiān lǐ zhī yuǎn， bù zú yǐ jǔ qí dà； qiān rèn zhī gāo， bù zú yǐ jí qí shēn。 yǔ zhī shí， shí nián jiǔ lǎo， ér shuǐ fú wèi jiā yì； tāng zhī shí， bā nián qī hàn， ér yá bù wèi jiā sǔn。 fū bù wèi qǐng jiǔ tuī yí， bù yǐ duō shǎo jìn tuì zhě， cǐ yì dōng hǎi zhī dà lè yě。』 yú shì kǎn jǐng zhī wā wén zhī， shì shì rán jīng， guī guī rán zì shī yě。 qiě fū zhī bù zhī shì fēi zhī jìng， ér yóu yù guān yú zhuāng zi zhī yán， shì yóu shǐ wén fù shān， shāng jù chí hé yě， bì bù shèng rèn yǐ。 qiě fū zhī bù zhī lùn jí miào zhī yán， ér zì shì yī shí zhī lì zhě， shì fēi kǎn jǐng zhī wā yǔ？ qiě bǐ fāng cī huáng quán ér dēng dà huáng， wú nán wú běi， shì rán sì jiě， lún yú bù cè； wú dōng wú xī， shǐ yú xuán míng， fǎn yú dà tōng。 zi nǎi guī guī rán ér qiú zhī yǐ chá， suǒ zhī yǐ biàn， shì zhí yòng guǎn kuī tiān， yòng zhuī zhǐ dì yě， bù yì xiǎo hū！ zi wǎng yǐ！ qiě zi dú bù wén shòu líng yú zi zhī xué xíng yú hán dān yǔ？ wèi dé guó néng， yòu shī qí gù xíng yǐ， zhí pú fú ér guī ěr。 jīn zi bù qù， jiāng wàng zi zhī gù， shī zi zhī yè。」

**EN:** Prince Mou leaned on his armrest and sighed; then he looked up to the sky and laughed: "Have you alone never heard of the frog in the collapsed well? It said to the turtle of the Eastern Sea: 'How happy I am! I come out and hop about on the well's railing; I go in and rest on the edge where a brick is missing. Entering the water, it comes up under my arms and props up my chin; stamping in the mud, it covers my feet and buries my insteps. Looking round at the mosquito larvae, the crabs, the tadpoles — none can match me! Besides, to command the waters of a whole hollow and straddle the joy of a tumbledown well — this is really the utmost! Why do you not come in some time and see, sir?' Before the turtle of the Eastern Sea had brought his left foot in, his right knee was already stuck. Thereupon he backed out, hesitating, and told the frog of the sea: 'A thousand li would not be enough to measure its greatness; a thousand fathoms would not be enough to plumb its depth. In Yu's time there were floods nine years out of ten, yet its waters did not rise the least bit; in Tang's time there were droughts seven years out of eight, yet its shores did not recede the least bit. Not to shift with the duration of the moment, not to advance or retreat with muchness or littleness — this too is the great joy of the Eastern Sea.' At this the frog of the collapsed well was astonished and aghast, and shrank, lost in himself. Now you — whose knowledge cannot even tell the boundary of right and wrong — still wish to behold the words of Zhuangzi. That is like setting a mosquito to carry a mountain, or a water-bug to race the Yellow River — you will surely not be up to the task. And one whose knowledge cannot discourse on the uttermost of subtle words, yet contentedly chases the profit of a passing hour — is that not the frog in the collapsed well! Besides, Zhuangzi treads the Yellow Springs and ascends to the great empyrean; no south, no north — free and unbound on all four sides, sinking into the unfathomable; no east, no west — beginning in the dark depths, returning to the great pervasion. And you, little man, mincing and measuring, seek him out with scrutiny and track him down with disputation — this is simply peering at the sky through a tube, pointing at the earth with an awl. Is it not small! Be on your way! And have you alone never heard of the young men of Shouling (壽陵) who went to Handan to learn to walk? Before they had mastered the skill of that state, they had lost their old way of walking, and had to crawl home on all fours. Now if you do not go, you will forget what you had and lose your trade."

## Section 15

## Section 16

**ZH:** 莊子釣於濮水，楚王使大夫二人往先焉，曰：「願以境內累矣！」莊子持竿不顧，曰：「吾聞楚有神龜，死已三千歲矣，王巾笥而藏之廟堂之上。此龜者，寧其死為留骨而貴乎，寧其生而曳尾於塗中乎？」二大夫曰：「寧生而曳尾塗中。」莊子曰：「往矣！吾將曳尾於塗中。」
**PY:** zhuāng zi diào yú pú shuǐ， chǔ wáng shǐ dà fū èr rén wǎng xiān yān， yuē：「 yuàn yǐ jìng nèi lèi yǐ！」 zhuāng zi chí gān bù gù， yuē：「 wú wén chǔ yǒu shén guī， sǐ yǐ sān qiān suì yǐ， wáng jīn sì ér cáng zhī miào táng zhī shàng。 cǐ guī zhě， níng qí sǐ wèi liú gǔ ér guì hū， níng qí shēng ér yè wěi yú tú zhōng hū？」 èr dà fū yuē：「 níng shēng ér yè wěi tú zhōng。」 zhuāng zi yuē：「 wǎng yǐ！ wú jiāng yè wěi yú tú zhōng。」

**EN:** Zhuangzi was fishing in the Pu River (濮水). The king of Chu sent two great officers to go ahead of him, who said: "I wish to burden you with the affairs of the realm!" Zhuangzi held his rod and did not look round: "I have heard that in Chu there is a sacred tortoise, dead three thousand years now, which the king keeps wrapped in cloth in a bamboo case in his ancestral hall. This tortoise — would it rather be dead, leaving its bones to be honored? Or would it rather be alive, dragging its tail in the mud?" The two officers said: "It would rather be alive, dragging its tail in the mud." Zhuangzi said: "Be gone! I shall be dragging my tail in the mud."

## Section 17

**ZH:** 惠子相梁，莊子往見之。或謂惠子曰：「莊子來，欲代子相。」於是惠子恐，搜於國中三日三夜。莊子往見之，曰：「南方有鳥，其名為鵷鶵，子知之乎？夫鵷鶵發於南海而飛於北海，非梧桐不止，非練實不食，非醴泉不飲。於是鴟得腐鼠，鵷鶵過之，仰而視之曰：『嚇！』今子欲以子之梁國而嚇我邪？」
**PY:** huì zi xiāng liáng， zhuāng zi wǎng jiàn zhī。 huò wèi huì zi yuē：「 zhuāng zi lái， yù dài zi xiāng。」 yú shì huì zi kǒng， sōu yú guó zhōng sān rì sān yè。 zhuāng zi wǎng jiàn zhī， yuē：「 nán fāng yǒu niǎo， qí míng wèi yuān chú， zi zhī zhī hū？ fū yuān chú fā yú nán hǎi ér fēi yú běi hǎi， fēi wú tóng bù zhǐ， fēi liàn shí bù shí， fēi lǐ quán bù yǐn。 yú shì chī dé fǔ shǔ， yuān chú guò zhī， yǎng ér shì zhī yuē：『 xià！』 jīn zi yù yǐ zi zhī liáng guó ér xià wǒ xié？」

**EN:** When Huizi was minister in Liang (梁), Zhuangzi went to see him. Someone said to Huizi: "Zhuangzi has come — he wants to take your place as minister." Thereupon Huizi was afraid, and searched the state for three days and three nights. Zhuangzi went to see him and said: "In the south there is a bird; its name is the phoenix-chick (鵷鶵). Do you know it? The phoenix-chick sets out from the Southern Sea and flies to the Northern Sea. It will not stop except on the sterculia; it will not eat except the bamboo fruit; it will not drink except from a sweet spring. An owl had gotten a rotten rat, and as the phoenix-chick passed by, the owl looked up at it and said: 'Scat!' Now do you wish to 'scat!' me over your state of Liang?"

## Section 18

**ZH:** 莊子與惠子遊於濠梁之上。莊子曰：「儵魚出遊從容，是魚樂也。」惠子曰：「子非魚，安知魚之樂？」莊子曰：「子非我，安知我不知魚之樂？」惠子曰：「我非子，固不知子矣；子固非魚也，子之不知魚之樂全矣。」莊子曰：「請循其本。子曰『汝安知魚樂』云者，既已知吾知之而問我，我知之濠上也。」
**PY:** zhuāng zi yǔ huì zi yóu yú háo liáng zhī shàng。 zhuāng zi yuē：「 shū yú chū yóu cóng róng， shì yú lè yě。」 huì zi yuē：「 zi fēi yú， ān zhī yú zhī lè？」 zhuāng zi yuē：「 zi fēi wǒ， ān zhī wǒ bù zhī yú zhī lè？」 huì zi yuē：「 wǒ fēi zi， gù bù zhī zi yǐ； zi gù fēi yú yě， zi zhī bù zhī yú zhī lè quán yǐ。」 zhuāng zi yuē：「 qǐng xún qí běn。 zi yuē『 rǔ ān zhī yú lè』 yún zhě， jì yǐ zhī wú zhī zhī ér wèn wǒ， wǒ zhī zhī háo shàng yě。」

**EN:** Zhuangzi and Huizi were strolling on the bridge over the Hao (濠). Zhuangzi said: "The minnows dart out and roam so free and easy — that is the joy of fish." Huizi said: "You are not a fish — how do you know the joy of fish?" Zhuangzi said: "You are not me — how do you know that I do not know the joy of fish?" Huizi said: "I am not you — true, so I do not know you. But you are certainly not a fish — so that you do not know the joy of fish is complete and settled." Zhuangzi said: "Let us go back to the root of it. When you said, 'How do you know the joy of fish?' — you already knew that I knew it, and asked me. I know it here above the Hao."
