# Correcting the Nature (繕性)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ Correcting the Nature - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (6 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 繕性於俗，俗學以求復其初，滑欲於俗，思以求致其明，謂之蔽蒙之民。

**EN:** To patch up one's nature with vulgar learning, seeking through it to recover one's beginning; to let one's desires run slick with vulgar thoughts, seeking through them to attain clarity — such people are called the benighted and hoodwinked.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 古之治道者，以恬養知；知生而無以知為也，謂之以知養恬。知與恬交相養，而和理出其性。夫德，和也；道，理也。德無不容，仁也；道無不理，義也；義明而物親，忠也；中純實而反乎情，樂也；信行容體而順乎文，禮也。禮樂遍行，則天下亂矣。彼正而蒙己德，德則不冒，冒則物必失其性也。

**EN:** The ancients who worked at the Way used tranquility to nourish knowledge. When knowledge arose and they did not act on knowledge, this was called using knowledge to nourish tranquility. Knowledge and tranquility nourishing one another in turn — then harmony and pattern came forth from their nature. Now virtue is harmony; the Way is pattern. Virtue excluding nothing is humaneness; the Way patterning everything is rightness; rightness made clear, so that things draw near, is loyalty; the center pure and solid, returning to genuine feeling, is music; conduct true and bearing composed, following form, is ritual. But when ritual and music are practiced everywhere, the world falls into disorder. When the other is set right and veils one's own virtue — virtue will not overspread; and once it overspreads, things are sure to lose their nature. [uncertain: 彼正而蒙己德 — a difficult line; rendered "when the other is set right and veils one's own virtue," following the reading that 蒙 = to cover over; some take 蒙 as 'receive,' others rearrange the clause.]

## Section 3

**ZH:** 古之人在混芒之中，與一世而得澹漠焉。當是時也，陰陽和靜，鬼神不擾，四時得節，萬物不傷，群生不夭，人雖有知，無所用之，此之謂至一。當是時也，莫之為而常自然。

**EN:** The men of old dwelt in the midst of confusion and dimness, and with the whole age found calm indifference. In those times yin and yang were harmonious and still; ghosts and spirits caused no disturbance; the four seasons kept their measure; the ten thousand things were not injured; the teeming creatures did not die young. Though men had knowledge, they had no use for it. This is called the utmost oneness. In those times nothing was done, yet all was constantly so of itself.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 逮德下衰，及燧人、伏羲始為天下，是故順而不一。德又下衰，及神農、黃帝始為天下，是故安而不順。德又下衰，及唐、虞始為天下，興治化之流，澆淳散朴，離道以善，險德以行，然後去性而從於心。心與心識知而不足以定天下，然後附之以文，益之以博。文滅質，博溺心，然後民始惑亂，無以反其性情而復其初。

**EN:** When virtue declined a step, Suiren (燧人) and Fuxi (伏羲) began to govern the world — whereupon there was compliance, but no longer oneness. When virtue declined another step, Shennong (神農) and the Yellow Emperor (黃帝) began to govern the world — whereupon there was peace, but no longer compliance. When virtue declined another step, Tang Yao (唐) and Yu Shun (虞) began to govern the world: they set in motion the currents of government and transformation; they diluted purity and scattered the uncarved; they departed from the Way in their goodness and endangered virtue in their conduct. After that they abandoned nature and followed the heart. Heart with heart discerning and knowing — and it was not enough to settle the world; so they added ornament, and increased it with learning. Ornament extinguished substance; learning drowned the heart. After that the people first fell into delusion and disorder, with no way to return to their nature and genuine feeling and recover their beginning.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 由是觀之，世喪道矣，道喪世矣。世與道交相喪也。道之人何由興乎世，世亦何由興乎道哉！道無以興乎世，世無以興乎道，雖聖人不在山林之中，其德隱矣。隱，故不自隱。古之所謂隱士者，非伏其身而弗見也，非閉其言而不出也，非藏其知而不發也，時命大謬也。當時命而大行乎天下，則反一無跡；不當時命而大窮乎天下，則深根寧極而待。此存身之道也。古之行身者，不以辯飾知，不以知窮天下，不以知窮德，危然處其所而反其性，己又何為哉！道固不小行，德固不小識。小識傷德，小行傷道。故曰：正己而已矣。

**EN:** Seen in this light, the age has lost the Way, and the Way has lost the age; the age and the Way have lost one another. By what means could a man of the Way flourish in such an age? By what means could the age flourish in the Way? The Way having no means to flourish in the age, and the age no means to flourish in the Way — then, though the sage does not dwell in mountain forests, his virtue is hidden. Hidden — therefore he does not hide himself. The so-called recluse-scholars of old did not crouch their bodies out of sight; they did not seal their words and keep them from coming forth; they did not store their knowledge and leave it unissued — the times and their lot were greatly awry. When the times and their lot fit and one walks greatly through the world, one returns to the One and leaves no tracks. When the times and their lot do not fit and one is greatly pressed by the world, one deepens the roots, quiets the utmost, and waits. This is the way of preserving the person. The ancients who conducted their persons did not deck out knowledge with disputation; they did not use knowledge to exhaust the world; they did not use knowledge to exhaust virtue. Standing alone and perilous in their place, they returned to their nature — what more had they to do! The Way inherently does not walk in small deeds; virtue inherently does not recognize small recognitions. Small recognitions injure virtue; small deeds injure the Way. Hence it is said: rectify yourself — that is all.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 樂全之謂得志。古之所謂得志者，非軒冕之謂也，謂其無以益其樂而已矣。今之所謂得志者，軒冕之謂也。軒冕在身，非性命也，物之儻來，寄者也。寄之，其來不可圉，其去不可止。故不為軒冕肆志，不為窮約趨俗，其樂彼與此同，故無憂而已矣。今寄去則不樂，由是觀之，雖樂，未嘗不荒也。故曰：喪己於物，失性於俗者，謂之倒置之民。

**EN:** Joy kept whole is called attaining one's will. What the ancients called attaining one's will was not carriages and caps; it meant that nothing could be added to their joy. What is called attaining one's will today is carriages and caps. Carriages and caps on one's person are not one's inborn lot; they are things that come by chance — lodgers. As lodgers, their coming cannot be prevented; their going cannot be stopped. So do not let carriages and caps run riot with your will; do not let straits and constraint drive you toward the vulgar. The joy of this one and that one is the same — therefore be without care. But nowadays, when the lodger departs, there is no joy; seen in this light, even their joy has never been free of desolation. Hence it is said: to lose oneself in things, to forfeit one's nature to the vulgar — such people are called the upside-down.

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 性 → "nature"; 情 → "genuine feeling" (反其性情 "return to nature and genuine feeling"); 知 → "knowledge"; 恬 → "tranquility"; 道 → "the Way"; 德 → "virtue"; 樂全 → "joy kept whole"; 性命 → "inborn lot."
- The chapter's opening and closing form an inclusio: 蔽蒙之民 ("benighted and hoodwinked") … 倒置之民 ("the upside-down").
