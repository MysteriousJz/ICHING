# Ingrained Ideas (刻意)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ Ingrained Ideas - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (9 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 刻意尚行，離世異俗，高論怨誹，為亢而已矣，此山谷之士，非世之人，枯槁赴淵者之所好也。語仁義忠信，恭儉推讓，為修而已矣，此平世之士，教誨之人，遊居學者之所好也。語大功，立大名，禮君臣，正上下，為治而已矣，此朝廷之士，尊主強國之人，致功并兼者之所好也。就藪澤，處閒曠，釣魚閒處，無為而已矣，此江海之士，避世之人，閒暇者之所好也。吹呴呼吸，吐故納新，熊經鳥申，為壽而已矣，此道引之士，養形之人，彭祖壽考者之所好也。

**EN:** To sharpen the will and exalt one's conduct, to leave the world behind and stand apart from custom, to discourse loftily with resentment and censure, aiming only at being high — this is the taste of the scholars of mountain valleys, the fault-finders of the age, those who delight in withering away and casting themselves into the deep. To discourse on humaneness, rightness, loyalty, and good faith, on reverence, frugality, deference, and yielding, aiming only at cultivation — this is the taste of the scholars of a well-ordered age, the teachers and instructors, those who roam and those who stay to study. To discourse on great achievements, to establish a great name, to ritualize lord and minister and rectify above and below, aiming only at good order — this is the taste of the scholars of the court, the magnifiers of ruler and strengtheners of state, the bringers of achievement and annexation. To repair to marsh and lake, to dwell in quiet wilderness, to fish in idle peace, aiming only at non-action — this is the taste of the scholars of rivers and seas, the avoiders of the age, the lovers of leisure. To blow and breathe, exhale and inhale, to expel the old and take in the new, to hang like the bear and stretch like the bird, aiming only at long life — this is the taste of the gymnasts of guidance and stretching, the nourishers of the bodily frame, those who delight in the longevity of a Pengzu.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 若夫不刻意而高，無仁義而修，無功名而治，無江海而閒，不道引而壽，無不忘也，無不有也，澹然無極而眾美從之，此天地之道，聖人之德也。

**EN:** But to be high without sharpening the will, cultivated without humaneness and rightness, well-ordered without achievement and fame, at leisure without rivers and seas, long-lived without guidance and stretching — nothing forgotten, nothing possessed, calm and boundless while every beauty follows — this is the Way of heaven and earth, the virtue of the sage.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 故曰：夫恬惔寂寞，虛無無為，此天地之平而道德之質也。

**EN:** Hence it is said: calm placidity, silent indifference, empty nothingness, non-action — these are the level of heaven and earth and the substance of the Way and its virtue.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 故曰：聖人休，休焉則平易矣，平易則恬惔矣。平易恬惔，則憂患不能入，邪氣不能襲，故其德全而神不虧。

**EN:** Hence it is said: the sage finds rest. At rest, he is level and easy; level and easy, he is calm and placid. Level, easy, calm, and placid — worry and trouble cannot enter in, evil breaths cannot make their assault. Therefore his virtue is whole and his spirit is undiminished.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 故曰：聖人之生也天行，其死也物化；靜而與陰同德，動而與陽同波；不為福先，不為禍始；感而後應，迫而後動，不得已而後起。去知與故，循天之理，故無天災，無物累，無人非，無鬼責。其生若浮，其死若休；不思慮，不豫謀；光矣而不耀，信矣而不期；其寢不夢，其覺無憂；其神純粹，其魂不罷。虛無恬惔，乃合天德。

**EN:** Hence it is said: the sage's life is the course of Heaven; his death is a transformation with things. In stillness he shares the virtue of the yin; in motion he shares the wave of the yang. He is not the forerunner of fortune nor the beginner of calamity. Moved, he responds; pressed, he moves; unable to do otherwise, he rises. Putting away knowledge and contrivance, he follows Heaven's pattern. Therefore he has no calamity from Heaven, no entanglement with things, no censure from men, no reproach from ghosts. His life is like a drifting; his death like a rest. He does not ponder and scheme; he does not plan in advance. He is bright, yet does not dazzle; trustworthy, yet binds no one to expectations. He sleeps without dreaming; he wakes without care. His spirit is pure and simple; his soul never wearies. Empty nothingness, calm placidity — then he is joined with Heaven's virtue.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 故曰：悲樂者，德之邪；喜怒者，道之過；好惡者，德之失。故心不憂樂，德之至也；一而不變，靜之至也；無所於忤，虛之至也；不與物交，惔之至也；無所於逆，粹之至也。

**EN:** Hence it is said: sorrow and joy are the deforming of virtue; delight and anger are the straying of the Way; likes and dislikes are the failure of virtue. So a heart without worry or joy is the arrival of virtue; oneness without change is the arrival of stillness; meeting with no defiance is the arrival of emptiness; having no dealings with things is the arrival of placidity; running into no resistance is the arrival of purity.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 故曰：形勞而不休則弊，精用而不已則勞，勞則竭。水之性，不雜則清，莫動則平，鬱閉而不流，亦不能清，天德之象也。

**EN:** Hence it is said: if the body toils without rest, it wears out; if the essence is used without ceasing, it labors; and labor brings exhaustion. It is the nature of water: unmixed, it is clear; unmoved, it is level. But pent up and kept from flowing, it cannot stay clear either. This is the image of Heaven's virtue.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 故曰：純粹而不雜，靜一而不變，惔而無為，動而以天行，此養神之道也。

**EN:** Hence it is said: pure and simple, unmixed; still and one, unchanged; placid and without action; moving with the course of Heaven — this is the way to nourish the spirit.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 夫有干、越之劍者，柙而藏之，不敢用也，寶之至也。精神四達並流，無所不極，上際於天，下蟠於地，化育萬物，不可為象，其名為同帝。純素之道，惟神是守，守而勿失，與神為一，一之精通，合於天倫。野語有之曰：「眾人重利，廉士重名，賢人尚志，聖人貴精。」故素也者，謂其無所與雜也；純也者，謂其不虧其神也。能體純素，謂之真人。

**EN:** One who owns a sword from Gan or Yue keeps it in its case and does not dare use it — the utmost of treasuring. The spirit flows forth in all four directions, reaching everywhere without limit: above it borders the sky; below it coils upon the earth; it transforms and nurtures the ten thousand things and cannot be imaged. Its name is Sameness with the Lord. The way of the pure and simple is to keep watch over the spirit alone; keep watch and never lose it; become one with the spirit; and when the essence of that oneness penetrates through, it joins with Heaven's order. The rustic saying has it: "The multitude prizes profit; the incorrupt scholar prizes a name; the worthy man exalts his will; the sage treasures the essence." So the simple is that which has nothing mixed with it; the pure is that which does not diminish its spirit. One who can embody the pure and simple is called a true man.

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 聖人 → "the sage"; 真人 → "a true man"; 道 → "the Way"; 德 → "virtue"; 天德 → "Heaven's virtue"; 無為 → "non-action"; 精 → "the essence"; 神 → "the spirit."
- The five opening types (§1) are rendered as a parallel catalogue of scholarly temperaments — the chapter's critique is that all five fall short of §2's boundless calm.
