# The Revolution of Heaven (天運)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ The Revolution of Heaven - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (21 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 天其運乎？地其處乎？日月其爭於所乎？孰主張是？孰維綱是？孰居無事推而行是？意者其有機緘而不得已邪？意者其運轉而不能自止邪？雲者為雨乎？雨者為雲乎？孰隆施是？孰居無事淫樂而勸是？風起北方，一西一東，有上彷徨，孰噓吸是？孰居無事而披拂是？敢問何故？巫咸袑曰：「來！吾語女。天有六極五常，帝王順之則治，逆之則凶。九洛之事，治成德備，監照下土，天下戴之，此謂上皇。」

**EN:** Does heaven revolve? Does earth hold still? Do sun and moon contend for their places? Who stretches them out? Who holds the cord? Who sits at ease and pushes them on their way? Can it be there is a mechanism locking them so they cannot help it? Can it be they turn and cannot stop themselves? Do the clouds make the rain, or the rain the clouds? Who piles it up and showers it down? Who sits at ease, in idle pleasure urging it on? The wind rises in the north, now west, now east, wandering above — who breathes it out and in? Who sits at ease and fans it back and forth? May I ask the reason why? Shaman Xian Tiao (巫咸袑) said: "Come, I will tell you. Heaven has six extremities and five constants. When emperors and kings follow them, there is order; when they defy them, calamity. The works of the nine provinces — order achieved, virtue complete — look down and shine upon the earth below, and the world upholds them. This is called the Supreme Sovereign."

## Section 2

**ZH:** 商太宰蕩問仁於莊子。莊子曰：「虎狼，仁也。」曰：「何謂也？」莊子曰：「父子相親，何為不仁？」曰：「請問至仁。」莊子曰：「至仁無親。」太宰曰：「蕩聞之：無親則不愛，不愛則不孝。謂至仁不孝，可乎？」莊子曰：「不然。夫至仁尚矣，孝固不足以言之。此非過孝之言也，不及孝之言也。夫南行者至於郢，北面而不見冥山，是何也？則去之遠也。故曰：以敬孝易，以愛孝難；以愛孝易，以忘親難；忘親易，使親忘我難；使親忘我易，兼忘天下難；兼忘天下易，使天下兼忘我難。夫德遺堯、舜而不為也，利澤施於萬世，天下莫知也，豈直太息而言仁孝乎哉！夫孝悌仁義，忠信貞廉，此皆自勉以役其德者也，不足多也。故曰：至貴，國爵并焉；至富，國財并焉；至願，名譽并焉。是以道不渝。」

**EN:** Tang (蕩), Grand Steward of Shang (商), asked Zhuangzi about humaneness. Zhuangzi said: "Tigers and wolves are humane." He said: "What do you mean?" Zhuangzi said: "Father and son love one another — how is that not humane?" He said: "May I ask about utmost humaneness?" Zhuangzi said: "Utmost humaneness has no kin." The Grand Steward said: "I have heard that without kinship there is no love, and without love there is no filial piety. To say that utmost humaneness is not filial — can that be?" Zhuangzi said: "No. Utmost humaneness is loftier; filial piety is surely not enough to describe it. This is not a saying that surpasses filial piety; it is a saying that falls short of filial piety. A traveler going south reaches Ying (郢), and facing north can no longer see Mount Ming (冥山). Why? Because he has gone far from it. Hence it is said: to be filial through reverence is easy; to be filial through love is hard. To be filial through love is easy; to forget one's parents is hard. To forget one's parents is easy; to make one's parents forget me is hard. To make my parents forget me is easy; to forget the whole world as well is hard. To forget the whole world is easy; to make the whole world forget me is hard. Virtue that leaves Yao and Shun behind and does not act — its benefits shower down on ten thousand ages, and no one in the world knows of it. Would such virtue heave sighs and discourse on humaneness and filial piety! Filial piety, brotherly love, humaneness, rightness, loyalty, good faith, steadfastness, and integrity — these are all things by which one exerts oneself and toils in the service of virtue. They are not worth prizing. Hence it is said: utmost honor casts aside the state's titles; utmost wealth casts aside the state's riches; utmost desire casts aside name and fame. In this the Way does not alter."

## Section 3

**ZH:** 北門成問於黃帝曰：帝張咸池之樂於洞庭之野，吾始聞之懼，復聞之怠，卒聞之而惑，蕩蕩默默，乃不自得。」

**EN:** Beimen Cheng (北門成) asked the Yellow Emperor: "You set forth the music of Xianchi (咸池) in the wilds of Dongting (洞庭). When first I heard it, I was afraid; when I heard it again, I slackened; when I heard it to the end, I was bewildered — swept along, silenced, unable to keep hold of myself."

## Section 4

**ZH:** 帝曰：「女殆其然哉！吾奏之以人，徵之以天，行之以禮義，建之以太清。夫至樂者，先應之以人事，順之以天理，行之以五德，應之以自然，然後調理四時，太和萬物。四時迭起，萬物循生；一盛一衰，文武倫經；一清一濁，陰陽調和，流光其聲；蟄蟲始作，吾驚之以雷霆；其卒無尾，其始無首；一死一生，一僨一起；所常無窮，而一不可待。女故懼也。

**EN:** The Emperor said: "That is just how it should be with you! I played it through man, attested it through Heaven, conducted it through ritual and rightness, and founded it upon the great clarity. The utmost music first answers to human affairs, follows Heaven's pattern, proceeds through the five virtues, and responds to the natural — then it orders and arranges the four seasons and brings the ten thousand things into great harmony. The four seasons rise in turn; the ten thousand things are born in sequence; now flourishing, now declining, the martial and the civil in due order; now clear, now turbid, the yin and yang harmonized, their sounds flowing forth in light; the hibernating insects begin to stir — I startle them with thunder. Its ending has no tail; its beginning has no head; now a death, now a life; now a fall, now a rise. Its constancy is inexhaustible, and nothing can be awaited as one. That is why you were afraid."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 吾又奏之以陰陽之和，燭之以日月之明；其聲能短能長，能柔能剛；變化齊一，不主故常；在谷滿谷，在阬滿阬；塗郤守神，以物為量。其聲揮綽，其名高明。是故鬼神守其幽，日月星辰行其紀。吾止之於有窮，流之於無止。予欲慮之而不能知也，望之而不能見也，逐之而不能及也，儻然立於四虛之道，倚於槁梧而吟。目知窮乎所欲見，力屈乎所欲逐，吾既不及已夫！形充空虛，乃至委蛇。汝委蛇，故怠。

**EN:** "Next I played it through the harmony of yin and yang, lit it with the brightness of sun and moon. Its sounds could be short or long, soft or strong; changing and transforming yet one throughout, dwelling on no fixed constancy. In a valley they fill the valley; in a pit they fill the pit. They stop up the crevices and keep the spirit in its guard, taking each thing as its measure. The sounds are free and easy; the name is high and bright. Therefore ghosts and spirits keep to their darkness, and sun, moon, and stars run their courses. I stop it where there is an end; I flow it on where there is no end. You tried to think it out and could not know it; you gazed at it and could not see it; you chased it and could not catch it; you stood bewildered in the road of the fourfold emptiness, leaning on a withered sterculia, humming. Your eyes' knowledge was exhausted by what you wished to see; your strength gave out on what you wished to chase — and I am already beyond your reach! Your form filled with emptiness, and so you came to serpentine ease. You fell into serpentine ease — therefore you slackened."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 吾又奏之以無怠之聲，調之以自然之命，故若混逐叢生，林樂而無形；布揮而不曳，幽昏而無聲。動於無方，居於窈冥；或謂之死，或謂之生；或謂之實，或謂之榮；行流散徙，不主常聲。世疑之，稽於聖人。聖也者，達於情而遂於命也。天機不張而五官皆備，此之謂天樂，無言而心說。故有焱氏為之頌曰：『聽之不聞其聲，視之不見其形，充滿天地，苞裏六極。』汝欲聽之而無接焉，而故惑也。

**EN:** "Next I played it with the sound of the unslackening, tuned it with the destiny of the natural — so it was like all things in tumultuous pursuit, growing in thickets, a forest of music without form; spreading and waving without trailing; dark and dim without sound. It moves in no direction; it dwells in the deep and dark. Some call it death; some call it life; some call it fruit; some call it flower. It flows, scatters, and shifts, dwelling on no constant sound. The world, doubting it, refers it to the sage. The sage is one who penetrates the genuine condition and accords with destiny. The impulse of Heaven is not drawn taut, yet the five organs are all complete. This is called the music of Heaven — wordless, and the heart delights. Therefore the house of Youyan (有焱氏) made a hymn to it: 'Listen — you will not hear its sound; look — you will not see its form. It fills heaven and earth; it wraps and holds the six extremities.' You wished to hear it but had no way to make contact — therefore you were bewildered."

## Section 7

**ZH:** 樂也者，始於懼，懼故祟；吾又次之以怠，怠故遁；卒之於惑，惑故愚；愚故道，道可載而與之俱也。」

**EN:** "Music begins with fear; in fear there is haunting. Next I followed with slackening; in slackening there is flight. It ends in bewilderment; in bewilderment there is stupidity; in stupidity there is the Way — the Way that can carry you and keep you company."

## Section 8

**ZH:** 孔子西遊於衛。顏淵問師金，曰：「以夫子之行為奚如？」師金曰：「惜乎，而夫子其窮哉！」顏淵曰：「何也？」師金曰：「夫芻狗之未陳也，盛以篋衍，巾以文繡，尸祝齊戒以將之；及其已陳也，行者踐其首脊，蘇者取而爨之而已。將復取而盛以篋衍，巾以文繡，遊居寢臥其下，彼不得夢，必且數眯焉。今而夫子，亦取先王已陳芻狗，聚弟子游居寢臥其下。故伐樹於宋，削跡於衛，窮於商、周，是非其夢邪？圍於陳、蔡之間，七日不火食，死生相與鄰，是非其眯邪？

**EN:** Confucius was roaming west in Wei. Yan Yuan (顏淵) asked Music Master Jin (師金): "What do you make of my master's journey?" Master Jin said: "Alas! Your master will surely come to a dead end!" Yan Yuan said: "Why?" Master Jin said: "Before the straw dogs are set out at the sacrifice, they are kept in a bamboo case, covered with embroidered cloth; the priest fasts and keeps vigil to present them. But once they have been set out, passers-by trample their heads and backs, and grass-cutters gather them up and burn them for fuel. If someone were to take them again and keep them in a bamboo case, cover them with embroidered cloth, and roam, dwell, and sleep beneath them — even if he did not dream of it, he would surely be plagued again and again by nightmares. Now your master has likewise taken up the straw dogs long since set out by the former kings, and gathers his disciples to roam, dwell, and sleep beneath them. Hence the tree was cut down over him in Song; his tracks were wiped out in Wei; he came to a dead end in Shang and Zhou — are these not his dreams? He was besieged between Chen and Cai, seven days without a cooked meal, with death at his elbow — is that not his nightmare?"

## Section 9

**ZH:** 夫水行莫如用舟，而陸行莫如用車。以舟之可行於水也而求推之於陸，則沒世不行尋常。古今非水陸與？周、魯非舟車與？今蘄行周於魯，是猶推舟於陸也，勞而無功，身必有殃。彼未知夫無方之傳，應物而不窮者也。

**EN:** "For travel on water nothing compares with a boat; for travel on land nothing compares with a cart. But because a boat can travel on water, to try pushing it on land is to fail to go even a few fathoms in a lifetime. Are not ancient and modern like water and land? Are not Zhou and Lu like boat and cart? Now to long to practice Zhou's ways in Lu is like pushing a boat on land: toil without achievement, and certain misfortune for his own person. He has not learned of the directionless transmission that responds to things and is never exhausted."

## Section 10

**ZH:** 且子獨不見夫桔槔者乎？引之則俯，舍之則仰。彼，人之所引，非引人也，故俯仰而不得罪於人。故夫三皇、五帝之禮義法度，不矜於同而矜於治。故譬三皇、五帝之禮義法度，其猶柤梨橘柚邪！其味相反，而皆可於口。

**EN:** "Have you alone never seen a well-sweep? Pull it, and it bends down; let it go, and it swings up. It is pulled by men; it does not pull men — therefore, down or up, it gives no offense to men. So the rituals, rightness, laws, and measures of the three sovereigns and five emperors were prized not for being the same, but for ordering their times. Compare the rituals, rightness, laws, and measures of the three sovereigns and five emperors to the hawthorn, the pear, the orange, and the pomelo: their flavors are opposite, yet all are pleasing to the mouth."

## Section 11

**ZH:** 故禮義法度者，應時而變者也。今取猨狙而衣以周公之服，彼必齕齧挽裂，盡去而後慊。觀古今之異，猶猨狙之異乎周公也。故西施病心而矉其里，其里之醜人見而美之，歸亦捧心而矉其里。其里之富人見之，堅閉門而不出；貧人見之，挈妻子而去之走。彼知矉美而不知矉之所以美。惜乎！而夫子其窮哉！」

**EN:** "So rituals, rightness, laws, and measures change in answer to the times. Now take a gibbon and dress it in the robes of the Duke of Zhou — it will surely bite and tear and wrench them off, and only when every shred is gone will it be satisfied. Look at the difference between ancient and modern: it is like the difference between a gibbon and the Duke of Zhou. When Xishi's heart pained her, she frowned in her village; an ugly woman of the village saw it and thought it beautiful, and went home and went about the village clutching her heart and frowning too. The rich of the village saw her, bolted their doors, and would not come out; the poor saw her, took wife and children by the hand, and fled. She knew the frown was beautiful, but not why the frown was beautiful. Alas! Your master will surely come to a dead end!"

## Section 12

**ZH:** 孔子行年五十有一而不聞道，乃南之沛，見老聃。老聃曰：「子來乎？吾聞子北方之賢者也，子亦得道乎？」孔子曰：「未得也。」老子曰：「子惡乎求之哉？」曰：「吾求之於度數，五年而未得也。」老子曰：「子又惡乎求之哉？」曰：「吾求之於陰陽，十有二年而未得。」

**EN:** Confucius was fifty-one years old and had not yet heard the Way. So he went south to Pei (沛) to see Lao Dan. Lao Dan said: "You have come, have you? I hear you are a worthy of the north. Have you attained the Way?" Confucius said: "Not yet." Laozi said: "Where have you sought it?" He said: "I sought it in measures and numbers; five years, and I did not attain it." Laozi said: "And where else did you seek it?" He said: "I sought it in the yin and yang; twelve years, and I did not attain it."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 老子曰：「然。使道而可獻，則人莫不獻之於其君；使道而可進，則人莫不進之於其親；使道而可以告人，則人莫不告其兄弟；使道而可以與人，則人莫不與其子孫。然而不可者，無佗也，中無主而不止，外無正而不行。由中出者，不受於外，聖人不出；由外入者，無主於中，聖人不隱。名，公器也，不可多取。仁義，先王之蘧廬也，止可以一宿而不可以久處，覯而多責。古之至人，假道於仁，託宿於義，以遊逍遙之虛，食於苟簡之田，立於不貸之圃。逍遙，無為也；苟簡，易養也；不貸，無出也。古者謂是采真之遊。

**EN:** Laozi said: "Just so. If the Way could be presented as tribute, there is no one who would not present it to his lord; if the Way could be offered up, there is no one who would not offer it to his parents; if the Way could be told to others, there is no one who would not tell it to his brothers; if the Way could be given away, there is no one who would not give it to his children and grandchildren. That it cannot be so is for no other reason than this: without a lord within to keep it, it does not stay; without a confirmation without, it does not go forth. What comes out from within and is not received from without — the sage does not bring out. What enters from without and finds no lord within — the sage does not harbor. Fame is a public vessel; it must not be taken in excess. Humaneness and rightness are the lodging-hut of the former kings: one may pass a night in them, but not dwell in them long; to be seen lingering there is to invite much blame. The utmost men of old borrowed the road of humaneness, lodged for the night in rightness, and roamed in the empty land of free and easy wandering; they ate in the field of the rough and simple; they stood in the garden that lends nothing out. Free and easy wandering — that is non-action. Rough and simple — that is easy nourishment. Lending nothing out — that is never to give forth. The ancients called this the roaming that gathers the true."

## Section 14

**ZH:** 以富為是者，不能讓祿；以顯為是者，不能讓名；親權者，不能與人柄。操之則慄，舍之則悲，而一無所鑒，以闚其所不休者，是天之戮民也。怨、恩、取、與、諫、教、生、殺，八者，正之器也，唯循大變無所湮者，為能用之。故曰：正者，正也。其心以為不然者，天門弗開矣。」

**EN:** "One who counts wealth right cannot yield his stipend; one who counts eminence right cannot yield his fame; one who is fond of power cannot share the handle with others. Grasping them, they tremble; letting them go, they grieve — and they have not a single mirror in which to spy out what they cannot cease from. These are Heaven's condemned men. Resentment, kindness, taking, giving, remonstrance, instruction, life, death — these eight are the instruments of correction. Only one who follows the great transformation without being submerged can use them. Hence it is said: correction is being correct. He whose heart thinks otherwise — the gate of Heaven will not open for him."

## Section 15

**ZH:** 孔子見老聃而語仁義。老聃曰：「夫播穅眯目，則天地四方易位矣；蚊虻噆膚，則通昔不寐矣。夫仁義憯然，乃憤吾心，亂莫大焉。吾子使天下無失其朴，吾子亦放風而動，總德而立矣，又奚傑然若負建鼓而求亡子者邪？夫鵠不日浴而白，烏不日黔而黑。黑白之朴，不足以為辯；名譽之觀，不足以為廣。泉涸，魚相與處於陸，相呴以溼，相濡以沫，不若相忘於江湖。」

**EN:** Confucius saw Lao Dan and spoke of humaneness and rightness. Lao Dan said: "When winnowed chaff gets in the eyes, heaven, earth, and the four quarters change places; when mosquitoes and gadflies bite the skin, one does not sleep the whole night through. Your humaneness and rightness sting just as sorely — they throw my heart into turmoil, and no disorder is greater. You, sir — let the world not lose its uncarved simplicity! You too may move with the wind, stand holding fast to virtue — why stride about proudly as though shouldering a great drum to chase a runaway son? The swan is not white from bathing daily; the crow is not black from daily inking. The plainness of black and white is not worth disputing over; the spectacle of name and fame is not worth enlarging. When the springs dry up, the fish huddle together on the land, wetting one another with their breath and dampening one another with spit — better to forget one another in the rivers and lakes."

## Section 16

**ZH:** 孔子見老聃歸，三日不談。弟子問曰：「夫子見老聃，亦將何歸哉？」孔子曰：「吾乃今於是乎見龍。龍合而成體，散而成章，乘乎雲氣而養乎陰陽。予口張而不能嗋，予又何規老聃哉！」子貢曰：「然則人固有尸居而龍見，雷聲而淵默，發動如天地者乎？賜亦可得而觀乎？」遂以孔子聲見老聃。

**EN:** Confucius returned from seeing Lao Dan and did not speak for three days. His disciples asked: "Master, you saw Lao Dan — what did you make of him?" Confucius said: "Now at last I have seen the dragon. The dragon — gathered, it forms a body; dispersed, it forms a pattern; it rides the cloudy vapors and feeds on the yin and yang. My mouth fell open and could not close — how could I offer Lao Dan any instruction!" Zigong said: "Then is there really such a man — still as a corpse yet a dragon appearing, silent as the deep yet thunder sounding, stirring into motion like heaven and earth? Might I, Ci, also get to behold him?" So, on Confucius's introduction, he went to see Lao Dan.

## Section 17

**ZH:** 老聃方將倨堂而應微曰：「予年運而往矣，子將何以戒我乎？」子貢曰：「夫三王、五帝之治天下不同，其係聲名一也。而先生獨以為非聖人，如何哉？」老聃曰：「小子少進！子何以謂不同？」對曰：「堯授舜，舜授禹，禹用力而湯用兵，文王順紂而不敢逆，武王逆紂而不肯順，故曰不同。」

**EN:** Lao Dan was just then sitting sprawled in his hall. He answered faintly: "My years have run on and are gone. What have you to admonish me with?" Zigong said: "The three kings and five emperors governed the world differently, yet the fame that attached to them was one. Yet you alone, sir, hold that they were not sages. How is that?" Lao Dan said: "Come a little closer, young man! What do you mean by differently?" He answered: "Yao yielded to Shun; Shun yielded to Yu; Yu toiled with his strength while Tang employed arms; King Wen obeyed Zhou and dared not defy him; King Wu defied Zhou and would not obey. Therefore I say differently."

## Section 18

**ZH:** 老聃曰：「小子少進！余語汝三皇、五帝之治天下。黃帝之治天下，使民心一，民有其親死不哭而民不非也。堯之治天下，使民心親，民有為其親殺其殺而民不非也。舜之治天下，使民心競，民孕婦十月生子，子生五月而能言，不至乎孩而始誰，則人始有夭矣。禹之治天下，使民心變，人有心而兵有順，殺盜非殺，人自為種而天下耳，是以天下大駭，儒、墨皆起。其作始有倫，而今乎婦女，何言哉！余語汝：三皇、五帝之治天下，名曰治之，而亂莫甚焉。三皇之知，上悖日月之明，下睽山川之精，中墮四時之施。其知憯於蠣蠆之尾，鮮規之獸，莫得安其性命之情者，而猶自以為聖人，不可恥乎？其無恥也！」子貢蹴蹴然立不安。

**EN:** Lao Dan said: "Come a little closer, young man! Let me tell you of the three sovereigns' and five emperors' governing of the world. When the Yellow Emperor governed the world, he made the people's hearts one: among the people there were those who did not weep when their parents died, and the people did not condemn them. When Yao governed the world, he made the people's hearts partial: among the people there were those who graded their mourning down for their kin, and the people did not condemn them. When Shun governed the world, he made the people's hearts competitive: among the people pregnant women bore children in ten months [uncertain: 十月生子 — read with the main textual tradition as ten months; some texts read five months.], children born five months could already speak, and before they could smile they already knew who was who — and then men first began to die young. When Yu governed the world, he made the people's hearts changeable: men had their private designs, and arms were counted obedience; 'to kill a robber is not killing,' each man made his own kind the whole world — and so the world was thrown into terror, and the Confucians and Mohists all arose. Their rise at first had its order; but look at them now, down to the women — what more is there to say! I tell you: the three sovereigns and five emperors governed the world — 'governed' in name, but no disorder was ever greater. The knowledge of the three sovereigns dimmed above the brightness of sun and moon, violated below the essence of mountain and river, destroyed between them the working of the four seasons. Their knowledge is crueler than the tail of the scorpion; not even the least of shy, fleeing beasts can rest in the genuine condition of its inborn lot — and yet they consider themselves sages! Is it not shameful? They have no shame!" Zigong stood trembling, ill at ease.

## Section 19

**ZH:** 孔子謂老聃曰：「丘治《詩》、《書》、《禮》、《樂》、《易》、《春秋》六經，自以為久矣，孰知其故矣，以奸者七十二君，論先王之道而明周、召之跡，一君無所鉤用。甚矣夫！人之難說也，道之難明邪！」

**EN:** Confucius said to Lao Dan: "I have worked on the six classics — Odes, Documents, Ritual, Music, Changes, and Spring and Autumn — for a long time, I flatter myself; I know their old matters well. With them I have approached seventy-two lords, discoursing on the Way of the former kings and making clear the tracks of the Dukes of Zhou and Shao — and not one lord has found any use for me. How very hard men are to persuade! How hard the Way is to make clear!"

## Section 20

**ZH:** 老子曰：「幸矣，子之不遇治世之君也！夫六經，先王之陳跡也，豈其所以跡哉！今子之所言，猶迹也。夫迹，履之所出，而迹豈履哉！夫白鶂之相視，眸子不運而風化；蟲，雄鳴於上風，雌應於下風而風化。類自為雌雄，故風化。性不可易，命不可變，時不可止，道不可壅。苟得其道，無自而不可；失焉者，無自而可。」

**EN:** Laozi said: "How lucky for you that you met no lord fit to rule an age! The six classics are the stale footprints of the former kings — how could they be that which made the footprints! Now all that you speak of is footprints. Footprints come out of the shoe — but are footprints the shoe? The white fish-hawk stares at its mate; their pupils never move, and fertilization comes of it. The insect: the male cries on the windward, the female answers on the leeward, and fertilization comes of it. Each kind is male and female of itself; therefore fertilization comes of it. Nature cannot be exchanged; destiny cannot be altered; the times cannot be halted; the Way cannot be blocked. If you get the Way, nothing from which it is not admissible; if you lose it, nothing from which it is admissible."

## Section 21

**ZH:** 孔子不出三月，復見，曰：「丘得之矣。烏鵲孺，魚傅沫，細要者化，有弟而兄啼。久矣夫，丘不與化為人！不與化為人，安能化人！」老子曰：「可。丘得之矣。」

**EN:** Confucius did not go out for three months; then he saw Lao Dan again and said: "I have got it. Crows and magpies hatch their young; fish pass the foam mouth to mouth; slender-waisted wasps transform; when a younger brother is born, the elder weeps. For a long time now I have not made myself a companion of transformation! Not making myself a companion of transformation, how could I transform others!" Laozi said: "That will do. You have got it."
