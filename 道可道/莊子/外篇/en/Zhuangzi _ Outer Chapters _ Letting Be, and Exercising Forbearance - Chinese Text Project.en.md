# Letting Be, and Exercising Forbearance (在宥)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 聞在宥天下，不聞治天下也。在之也者，恐天下之淫其性也；宥之也者，恐天下之遷其德也。天下不淫其性，不遷其德，有治天下者哉！昔堯之治天下也，使天下欣欣焉人樂其性，是不恬也；桀之治天下也，使天下瘁瘁焉人苦其性，是不愉也。夫不恬不愉，非德也。非德也而可長久者，天下無之。人大喜邪，毗於陽。大怒邪，毗於陰。陰陽並毗，四時不至，寒暑之和不成，其反傷人之形乎！使人喜怒失位，居處無常，思慮不自得，中道不成章，於是乎天下始喬詰、卓鷙，而後有盜跖、曾、史之行。故舉天下以賞其善者不足，舉天下以罰其惡者不給，故天下之大不足以賞罰。自三代以下者，匈匈焉終以賞罰為事，彼何暇安其性命之情哉！而且說明邪，是淫於色也；說聰邪，是淫於聲也；說仁邪，是亂於德也；說義邪，是悖於理也；說禮邪，是相於技也；說樂邪，是相於淫也；說聖邪，是相於藝也；說知邪，是相於疵也。天下將安其性命之情，之八者，存可也；亡可也；天下將不安其性命之情，之八者，乃始臠卷、獊囊而亂天下也。而天下乃始尊之惜之，甚矣天下之惑也！豈直過也而去之邪！乃齊戒以言之，跪坐以進之，鼓歌以儛之，吾若是何哉！故君子不得已而臨邪天下，莫若無為。無為也，而後安其性命之情。故貴以身於為天下，則可以託天下；愛以身於為天下，則可以寄天下。故君子苟能無解其五藏，無擢其聰明，尸居而龍見，淵默而雷聲，神動而天隨，從容無為而萬物炊累焉。吾又何暇治天下哉！

**EN:** I have heard of letting the world be and forbearing with it; I have not heard of governing the world. Letting it be is for fear the world will strain its nature; forbearing with it is for fear the world will shift its virtue. If the world does not strain its nature or shift its virtue, what need is there of governing the world! Of old, when Yao governed the world, he made everyone gaily delighted in his nature — which is to be not at rest. When Jie governed the world, he made everyone miserably embittered in his nature — which is to be not content. To be neither at rest nor content is contrary to virtue; and that what is contrary to virtue should last long — nowhere under heaven has that ever happened. When men rejoice greatly, it injures the yang; when they rage greatly, it injures the yin. When yin and yang are both injured, the four seasons fail to arrive, the harmony of cold and heat is not achieved — will this not in turn injure the very bodies of men! It makes men's joy and anger miss their stations, their dwellings and resting-places lose constancy, their thoughts and cares find no satisfaction, and the middle way fail to take form. Thereupon the world first produced the haughty, the fault-finding, the aloof, and the fierce — and afterward the conduct of Robber Zhi on the one side and Zeng and Shi on the other. Offer the whole world as a reward for the good, and it is not enough; apply the whole world to punishing the bad, and it does not suffice. The world is not big enough for rewards and punishments. Since the three dynasties, in ceaseless hubbub, rewards and punishments have been the whole affair — what leisure have they had to rest in the genuine condition of their inborn lot! To delight in sharp sight is to strain after color; to delight in sharp hearing is to strain after sound; to delight in humaneness is to throw virtue into disorder; to delight in rightness is to turn one's back on principle; to delight in ritual is to encourage technique; to delight in music is to encourage excess; to delight in sagacity is to encourage artifice; to delight in knowledge is to encourage fault-finding. If the world is to rest in the genuine condition of its inborn lot, these eight may be present or may be absent — it makes no difference. But if the world is not to rest in it, these eight begin to coil and clutch and throw the world into disorder. And then the world honors them and treasures them — how deep is the world's delusion! Not content to let them pass and be gone, they fast and keep vigil to discourse on them, kneel in formal posture to present them, drum and sing and dance to celebrate them. What am I to do with such a world! Therefore, if the gentleman cannot avoid presiding over the world, nothing compares with non-action. In non-action, and only then, he may rest in the genuine condition of his inborn lot. So: to prize one's person more than ruling the world — such a one may be entrusted with the world; to love one's person more than ruling the world — such a one may be charged with the world. So if the gentleman can but keep from bursting his five organs, keep from straining his sight and hearing; still as a corpse yet a dragon appearing, silent as the deep yet thunder sounding; spirit moving and Heaven following, calm and unhurried in non-action while the ten thousand things rise and settle like steam — what leisure have I to govern the world!

## Section 2

**ZH:** 崔瞿問於老聃曰：「不治天下，安藏人心？」老聃曰：「汝慎無攖人心。人心排下而進上，上下囚殺，淖約柔乎剛強。廉劌彫琢，其熱焦火，其寒凝冰。其疾俛仰之間，而再撫四海之外，其居也淵而靜，其動也縣而天。僨驕而不可係者，其唯人心乎！昔者黃帝始以仁義攖人之心，堯、舜於是乎股無胈，脛無毛，以養天下之形，愁其五藏以為仁義，矜其血氣以規法度。然猶有不勝也。堯於是放讙兜於崇山，投三苗於三峗，流共工於幽都，此不勝天下也夫！施及三王而天下大駭矣。下有桀、跖，上有曾、史，而儒、墨畢起。於是乎喜怒相疑，愚知相欺，善否相非，誕信相譏，而天下衰矣；大德不同，而性命爛漫矣；天下好知，而百姓求竭矣。於是乎釿鋸制焉，繩墨殺焉，椎鑿決焉。天下脊脊大亂，罪在攖人心。故賢者伏處大山嵁巖之下，而萬乘之君憂慄乎廟堂之上。今世殊死者相枕也，桁楊者相推也，刑戮者相望也，而儒、墨乃始離跂攘臂乎桎梏之間。意！甚矣哉！其無愧而不知恥也甚矣！吾未知聖知之不為桁楊椄槢也，仁義之不為桎梏、鑿枘也，焉知曾、史之不為桀、跖嚆矢也！故曰：『絕聖棄知而天下大治。』」

**EN:** Cui Qu (崔瞿) asked Lao Dan: "If one does not govern the world, how are men's hearts to be held in?" Lao Dan said: "Take care that you do not disturb the hearts of men. The heart of man — push it down and it springs up; up and down, captive and slayer, yet soft and yielding it can bend the hard and strong. Its edge can cut and carve; its heat is scorching fire, its cold is freezing ice. Swift as a nod, it ranges twice beyond the four seas. At rest, it is still as the deep; in motion, it hangs suspended from the sky. Rearing, proud, and impossible to tether — is it not the heart of man! Of old the Yellow Emperor first disturbed men's hearts with humaneness and rightness. Then Yao and Shun wore their thighs hairless and their shanks bare to nourish the bodies of the world; they grieved their five organs in the practice of humaneness and rightness, strained their blood and breath to establish rules and measures. And still they could not prevail. So Yao banished Huandou (讙兜) to Mount Chong (崇山), cast the Three Miao tribes into Sanwei (三峗), and exiled Gonggong (共工) to the Dark City — such was their not-prevailing over the world! By the time it came down to the three kings, the world was in terror and confusion. Below there were Jie and Robber Zhi; above there were Zeng and Shi; and the Confucians and Mohists rose up on every side. Thereupon joy and anger eyed one another with suspicion, fool and sage cheated one another, good and bad condemned one another, boastful and true reviled one another — and the world declined. Great virtue fell out of unity, and the inborn lot rotted and scattered; the world loved knowledge, and the hundred clans were wrung dry. Thereupon the saw and hatchet governed them, the inked line slaughtered them, the mallet and chisel cut them through. The world, back torn with wounds, fell into great disorder — the crime lay in disturbing the hearts of men. So worthy men crouch in hiding beneath the cliffs of great mountains, while the lord of ten thousand chariots trembles with anxiety in his hall. Today the executed lie in heaps, the pilloried jostle one another, the punished stand within sight of one another — and the Confucians and Mohists stride on tiptoe, sleeves rolled, right in among the fetters and manacles! Ah — it is too much! How shameless they are, beyond all measure! I have yet to learn that the knowledge of sages is not the rivet of the pillory, that humaneness and rightness are not the tenon of the manacles — how do I know that Zeng and Shi are not the whistling arrow announcing Jie and Robber Zhi! Hence it is said: 'Cut off sagacity, cast away knowledge, and the world will be in great order.'"

## Section 3

**ZH:** 黃帝立為天子十九年，令行天下，聞廣成子在於空同之上，故往見之，曰：「我聞吾子達於至道，敢問至道之精。吾欲取天地之精，以佐五穀，以養民人；吾又欲官陰陽，以遂群生。為之奈何？」廣成子曰：「而所欲問者，物之質也；而所欲官者，物之殘也。自而治天下，雲氣不待族而雨，草木不待黃而落，日月之光益以荒矣。而佞人之心翦翦者，又奚足以語至道！」黃帝退，捐天下，築特室，席白茅，閒居三月，復往邀之。廣成子南首而臥，黃帝順下風膝行而進，再拜稽首而問曰：「聞吾子達於至道，敢問治身奈何而可以長久？」廣成子蹶然而起，曰：「善哉問乎！來！吾語女至道。至道之精，窈窈冥冥；至道之極，昏昏默默。無視無聽，抱神以靜，形將自正。必靜必清，無勞女形，無搖女精，乃可以長生。目無所見，耳無所聞，心無所知，女神將守形，形乃長生。慎女內，閉女外，多知為敗。我為女遂於大明之上矣，至彼至陽之原也；為女入於窈冥之門矣，至彼至陰之原也。天地有官，陰陽有藏，慎守女身，物將自壯。我守其一，以處其和，故我修身千二百歲矣，吾形未嘗衰。」黃帝再拜稽首曰：「廣成子之謂天矣！」廣成子曰：「來！吾語女。彼其物無窮，而人皆以為有終；彼其物無測，而人皆以為有極。得吾道者，上為皇而下為王；失吾道者，上見光而下為土。今夫百昌，皆生於土而反於土，故余將去女，入無窮之門，以遊無極之野。吾與日月參光，吾與天地為常。當我，緡乎！遠我，昏乎！人其盡死，而我獨存乎！」

**EN:** The Yellow Emperor had stood as Son of Heaven for nineteen years, and his commands were carried out through the world. Hearing that Guangchengzi (廣成子) was living atop Mount Kongtong (空同), he went to see him, saying: "I have heard that you, sir, have attained the utmost Way. May I ask about the essence of the utmost Way? I wish to take the essence of heaven and earth to aid the five grains and nourish the people; and I wish to govern the yin and yang to bring all living things to fulfillment. How is it to be done?" Guangchengzi said: "What you wish to ask about is the substance of things; what you wish to govern is but the dregs of things. Since you began governing the world, the vapors of the clouds rain before they have gathered, the leaves of the grass and trees fall before they have yellowed, and the light of sun and moon grows ever dimmer. And the heart of a glib, shallow man like you — what is it fit to hear of the utmost Way!" The Yellow Emperor withdrew and renounced the world. He built a solitary hut, spread a mat of white rushes, and dwelt in retirement for three months; then he went again to seek him out. Guangchengzi was lying with his head to the south. The Yellow Emperor advanced on his knees, downwind, bowed twice with forehead to the ground, and asked: "I have heard that you, sir, have attained the utmost Way. May I ask how to order the body so that it may long endure?" Guangchengzi leapt up: "A fine question! Come, I will tell you the utmost Way. The essence of the utmost Way is deep and dark; the utmost reach of the utmost Way is dim and silent. Without seeing, without hearing, embrace the spirit in stillness, and the form will right itself. Be still, be pure; do not labor your form; do not stir your essence — then you may live long. Let the eye see nothing, the ear hear nothing, the mind know nothing: your spirit will guard the form, and the form will live long. Keep careful watch within, shut fast the gates without — much knowledge is ruin. I have led you up above the great brightness, to the source of the utmost yang; I have led you in through the gate of the dark depths, to the source of the utmost yin. Heaven and earth have their offices; yin and yang have their storehouses. Carefully guard your body, and things will grow strong of themselves. I keep watch over the One and dwell in its harmony; therefore I have cultivated this body for twelve hundred years, and my form has never decayed." The Yellow Emperor bowed twice with forehead to the ground: "Guangchengzi may be called Heaven itself!" Guangchengzi said: "Come, I will tell you. That thing is inexhaustible, yet all men think it has an end; that thing is unfathomable, yet all men think it has a limit. Those who attain my Way are august sovereigns above and kings below; those who lose my Way see the light above and become dust below. Now all that flourishes is born of the earth and returns to the earth. So I shall take my leave of you, enter the gate of the inexhaustible, and roam the fields of the limitless. I share in the triple light of sun and moon; I am a constant with heaven and earth. What comes to me — dim! What departs from me — dark! All men may die, and I alone endure!"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 雲將東遊，過扶搖之枝，而適遭鴻蒙。鴻蒙方將拊髀雀躍而遊。雲將見之，倘然止，贄然立，曰：「叟何人邪？叟何為此？」鴻蒙拊髀雀躍不輟，對雲將曰：「遊。」雲將曰：「朕願有問也。」鴻蒙仰而視雲將曰：「吁！」雲將曰：「天氣不合，地氣鬱結，六氣不調，四時不節。今我願合六氣之精，以育群生，為之奈何？」鴻蒙拊髀雀躍掉頭曰：「吾弗知，吾弗知。」雲將不得問。又三年，東遊，過有宋之野，而適遭鴻蒙。雲將大喜，行趨而進曰：「天忘朕邪？天忘朕邪？」再拜稽首，願聞於鴻蒙。鴻蒙曰：「浮游不知所求，猖狂不知所往，遊者鞅掌，以觀無妄，朕又何知！」雲將曰：「朕也自以為猖狂，而百姓隨予所往；朕也不得已於民，今則民之放也。願聞一言。」鴻蒙曰：「亂天之經，逆物之情，玄天弗成；解獸之群，而鳥皆夜鳴；災及草木，禍及止蟲。意！治人之過也！」雲將曰：「然則吾奈何？」鴻蒙曰：「意！毒哉！僊僊乎歸矣！」雲將曰：「吾遇天難，願聞一言。」鴻蒙曰：「意！心養。汝徒處無為，而物自化。墮爾形體，吐爾聰明；倫與物忘，大同乎涬溟；解心釋神，莫然無魂。萬物云云，各復其根，各復其根而不知。渾渾沌沌，終身不離；若彼知之，乃是離之。無問其名，無闚其情，物故自生。」雲將曰：「天降朕以德，示朕以默，躬身求之，乃今也得。」再拜稽首，起辭而行。
**PY:** yún jiāng dōng yóu， guò fú yáo zhī zhī， ér shì zāo hóng méng。 hóng méng fāng jiāng fǔ bì què yuè ér yóu。 yún jiāng jiàn zhī， tǎng rán zhǐ， zhì rán lì， yuē：「 sǒu hé rén xié？ sǒu hé wèi cǐ？」 hóng méng fǔ bì què yuè bù chuò， duì yún jiāng yuē：「 yóu。」 yún jiāng yuē：「 zhèn yuàn yǒu wèn yě。」 hóng méng yǎng ér shì yún jiāng yuē：「 xū！」 yún jiāng yuē：「 tiān qì bù hé， dì qì yù jié， liù qì bù diào， sì shí bù jié。 jīn wǒ yuàn hé liù qì zhī jīng， yǐ yù qún shēng， wèi zhī nài hé？」 hóng méng fǔ bì què yuè diào tóu yuē：「 wú fú zhī， wú fú zhī。」 yún jiāng bù dé wèn。 yòu sān nián， dōng yóu， guò yǒu sòng zhī yě， ér shì zāo hóng méng。 yún jiāng dà xǐ， xíng qū ér jìn yuē：「 tiān wàng zhèn xié？ tiān wàng zhèn xié？」 zài bài qǐ shǒu， yuàn wén yú hóng méng。 hóng méng yuē：「 fú yóu bù zhī suǒ qiú， chāng kuáng bù zhī suǒ wǎng， yóu zhě yāng zhǎng， yǐ guān wú wàng， zhèn yòu hé zhī！」 yún jiāng yuē：「 zhèn yě zì yǐ wèi chāng kuáng， ér bǎi xìng suí yǔ suǒ wǎng； zhèn yě bù dé yǐ yú mín， jīn zé mín zhī fàng yě。 yuàn wén yī yán。」 hóng méng yuē：「 luàn tiān zhī jīng， nì wù zhī qíng， xuán tiān fú chéng； jiě shòu zhī qún， ér niǎo jiē yè míng； zāi jí cǎo mù， huò jí zhǐ chóng。 yì！ zhì rén zhī guò yě！」 yún jiāng yuē：「 rán zé wú nài hé？」 hóng méng yuē：「 yì！ dú zāi！ xiān xiān hū guī yǐ！」 yún jiāng yuē：「 wú yù tiān nán， yuàn wén yī yán。」 hóng méng yuē：「 yì！ xīn yǎng。 rǔ tú chù wú wèi， ér wù zì huà。 duò ěr xíng tǐ， tǔ ěr cōng míng； lún yǔ wù wàng， dà tóng hū xìng míng； jiě xīn shì shén， mò rán wú hún。 wàn wù yún yún， gè fù qí gēn， gè fù qí gēn ér bù zhī。 hún hún dùn dùn， zhōng shēn bù lí； ruò bǐ zhī zhī， nǎi shì lí zhī。 wú wèn qí míng， wú kuī qí qíng， wù gù zì shēng。」 yún jiāng yuē：「 tiān jiàng zhèn yǐ dé， shì zhèn yǐ mò， gōng shēn qiú zhī， nǎi jīn yě dé。」 zài bài qǐ shǒu， qǐ cí ér xíng。

**EN:** Yunjiang (雲將, Master Cloud), roaming in the east, passed by the branches of the Whirlwind, where he happened upon Hongmeng (鴻蒙, Vast Obscurity). Hongmeng was just then slapping his thighs and hopping about like a sparrow. Yunjiang saw him, stopped in bewilderment, and stood respectfully, saying: "Who are you, old sir? What are you doing here?" Hongmeng, slapping his thighs and hopping like a sparrow without pause, answered Yunjiang: "Roaming!" Yunjiang said: "I have something I would ask." Hongmeng looked up at him and said: "Hm!" Yunjiang said: "The breath of heaven does not harmonize; the breath of earth is knotted and blocked; the six breaths are out of tune; the four seasons are disordered. Now I wish to blend the essences of the six breaths to nourish all living things. How is it to be done?" Hongmeng, slapping his thighs and hopping like a sparrow, turned his head away: "I don't know! I don't know!" Yunjiang got no answer. Three years later, again roaming in the east, he passed through the wilds of Song and again happened upon Hongmeng. Yunjiang was overjoyed; he hurried forward, saying: "Has Heaven forgotten me? Has Heaven forgotten me?" He bowed twice with forehead to the ground, begging to hear from Hongmeng. Hongmeng said: "Drifting at random, knowing nothing sought; wild and free, knowing nothing pursued; a roamer in idle ease, watching what is without contrivance. What should I know!" Yunjiang said: "I too consider myself wild and free, yet the hundred clans follow wherever I go. I cannot help it with the people, and now they take their pattern from me. I beg to hear one word." Hongmeng said: "Disordering Heaven's warp, defying the genuine condition of things — dark Heaven will not perfect it; scattering the herds of beasts, setting all the birds crying in the night; disaster reaching to grass and tree, calamity reaching to the very insects. Ah — these are the faults of governing men!" Yunjiang said: "Then what am I to do?" Hongmeng said: "Ah — how pestilent! Away with you — off you go!" Yunjiang said: "I have met with heavenly difficulty; I beg to hear one word." Hongmeng said: "Ah — nourish the heart. Merely dwell in non-action, and things will transform of themselves. Cast off your limbs and body, spit out your sight and hearing; sink your kind into oblivion with things, become greatly one with the dark and fathomless; undo the mind, release the spirit, and be blankly soulless. The ten thousand things, teeming and teeming, each returns to its root — each returns to its root and knows it not. Chaotic, unformed, all life long never parted from it; if they knew it, that would be to part from it. Do not ask its name, do not spy out its true condition — things of themselves come to birth." Yunjiang said: "Heaven has sent down its virtue upon me and shown me silence. I sought it in my own person, and now I have attained it." He bowed twice with forehead to the ground, rose, took his leave, and went.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 世俗之人，皆喜人之同乎己，而惡人之異於己也。同於己而欲之、異於己而不欲者，以出乎眾為心也。夫以出於眾為心者，曷嘗出乎眾哉！因眾以寧所聞，不如眾技眾矣。而欲為人之國者，此攬乎三王之利，而不見其患者也。此以人之國僥倖也，幾何僥倖而不喪人之國乎！其存人之國也，無萬分之一；而喪人之國也，一不成而萬有餘喪矣。悲夫！有土者之不知也！
**PY:** shì sú zhī rén， jiē xǐ rén zhī tóng hū jǐ， ér è rén zhī yì yú jǐ yě。 tóng yú jǐ ér yù zhī、 yì yú jǐ ér bù yù zhě， yǐ chū hū zhòng wèi xīn yě。 fū yǐ chū yú zhòng wèi xīn zhě， hé cháng chū hū zhòng zāi！ yīn zhòng yǐ níng suǒ wén， bù rú zhòng jì zhòng yǐ。 ér yù wèi rén zhī guó zhě， cǐ lǎn hū sān wáng zhī lì， ér bù jiàn qí huàn zhě yě。 cǐ yǐ rén zhī guó jiǎo xìng yě， jǐ hé jiǎo xìng ér bù sàng rén zhī guó hū！ qí cún rén zhī guó yě， wú wàn fēn zhī yī； ér sàng rén zhī guó yě， yī bù chéng ér wàn yǒu yú sàng yǐ。 bēi fū！ yǒu tǔ zhě zhī bù zhī yě！

**EN:** The men of the vulgar world all delight in others' agreeing with themselves, and hate others' differing from themselves. To desire agreement with oneself and to dislike difference — this comes of setting one's heart on standing out from the crowd. But one whose heart is set on standing out from the crowd — has he ever really stood out from the crowd? To rest one's convictions on the crowd is to fall short of the crowd's many skills combined. And one who then wishes to take charge of another's state is grasping at the profits of the three kings without seeing their perils. This is to gamble another's state on a lucky chance — and how many lucky chances has a man before he loses the state? The odds of preserving it are not one in ten thousand; of losing it — fail even once and the losses mount beyond ten thousand. Alas, that those who possess the land do not know this!

## Section 6

**ZH:** 夫有土者，有大物也。有大物者，不可以物物；而不物，故能物物。明乎物物者之非物也，豈獨治天下百姓而已哉！出入六合，遊乎九州，獨往獨來，是謂獨有。獨有之人，是謂至貴。
**PY:** fū yǒu tǔ zhě， yǒu dà wù yě。 yǒu dà wù zhě， bù kě yǐ wù wù； ér bù wù， gù néng wù wù。 míng hū wù wù zhě zhī fēi wù yě， qǐ dú zhì tiān xià bǎi xìng ér yǐ zāi！ chū rù liù hé， yóu hū jiǔ zhōu， dú wǎng dú lái， shì wèi dú yǒu。 dú yǒu zhī rén， shì wèi zhì guì。

**EN:** To possess the land is to possess a great thing. One who possesses a great thing cannot treat it as a mere thing; and precisely by not treating it as a thing, he can treat things as things. To be clear that what treats things as things is itself no thing — how could such a one be fit only for governing the world's hundred clans! He goes out and in through the six coordinates, roams the nine provinces, going alone, coming alone — this is called sole possession. The man of sole possession may be called the most exalted of all.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 大人之教，若形之於影，聲之於響。有問而應之，盡其所懷，為天下配。處乎無響，行乎無方。挈汝適復之撓撓，以遊無端，出入無旁，與日無始，頌論形軀，合乎大同，大同而無己。無己，惡乎得有有！睹有者，昔之君子；睹無者，天地之友。
**PY:** dà rén zhī jiào， ruò xíng zhī yú yǐng， shēng zhī yú xiǎng。 yǒu wèn ér yīng zhī， jǐn qí suǒ huái， wèi tiān xià pèi。 chù hū wú xiǎng， xíng hū wú fāng。 qiè rǔ shì fù zhī náo náo， yǐ yóu wú duān， chū rù wú páng， yǔ rì wú shǐ， sòng lùn xíng qū， hé hū dà tóng， dà tóng ér wú jǐ。 wú jǐ， è hū dé yǒu yǒu！ dǔ yǒu zhě， xī zhī jūn zǐ； dǔ wú zhě， tiān dì zhī yǒu。

**EN:** The teaching of the great man is like the shadow to the form, the echo to the sound. Questioned, he responds, giving out all that is in his heart, and thus he is the world's counterpart. He dwells in the echoless; he moves in the directionless. He takes you by the hand back to the place of tumult and teeming, to roam in the beginningless; he goes out and comes in without sideways limit, sharing with the sun a beginninglessness; his discourse takes the form of the body, joins with the great sameness — and in the great sameness there is no self. With no self, how could he possess even a something! Those who see a something were the gentlemen of old; those who see a nothing are the friends of heaven and earth.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 賤而不可不任者，物也；卑而不可不因者，民也；匿而不可不為者，事也；麤而不可不陳者，法也；遠而不可不居者，義也；親而不可不廣者，仁也；節而不可不積者，禮也；中而不可不高者，德也；一而不可不易者，道也；神而不可不為者，天也。故聖人觀於天而不助，成於德而不累，出於道而不謀，會於仁而不恃，薄於義而不積，應於禮而不諱，接於事而不辭，齊於法而不亂，恃於民而不輕，因於物而不去。物者莫足為也，而不可不為。不明於天者，不純於德；不通於道者，無自而可。不明於道者，悲夫！
**PY:** jiàn ér bù kě bù rèn zhě， wù yě； bēi ér bù kě bù yīn zhě， mín yě； nì ér bù kě bù wèi zhě， shì yě； cū ér bù kě bù chén zhě， fǎ yě； yuǎn ér bù kě bù jū zhě， yì yě； qīn ér bù kě bù guǎng zhě， rén yě； jié ér bù kě bù jī zhě， lǐ yě； zhōng ér bù kě bù gāo zhě， dé yě； yī ér bù kě bù yì zhě， dào yě； shén ér bù kě bù wèi zhě， tiān yě。 gù shèng rén guān yú tiān ér bù zhù， chéng yú dé ér bù lèi， chū yú dào ér bù móu， huì yú rén ér bù shì， báo yú yì ér bù jī， yīng yú lǐ ér bù huì， jiē yú shì ér bù cí， qí yú fǎ ér bù luàn， shì yú mín ér bù qīng， yīn yú wù ér bù qù。 wù zhě mò zú wèi yě， ér bù kě bù wèi。 bù míng yú tiān zhě， bù chún yú dé； bù tōng yú dào zhě， wú zì ér kě。 bù míng yú dào zhě， bēi fū！

**EN:** Things are lowly, yet one cannot but employ them; the people are base, yet one cannot but rely on them; affairs are trifling, yet one cannot but do them; laws are crude, yet one cannot but set them forth; rightness is remote, yet one cannot but dwell in it; humaneness is partial, yet one cannot but extend it; ritual is a restraint, yet one cannot but accumulate it; virtue is inward, yet one cannot but raise it high; the Way is one, yet one cannot but change with it; Heaven is spirit-like, yet one cannot but act on it. Therefore the sage contemplates Heaven but does not assist it; he is completed in virtue but not entangled; he goes out on the Way but does not scheme; he accords with humaneness but does not lean on it; he presses close to rightness but does not amass it; he responds with ritual but does not shun it; he meets with affairs but does not refuse them; he is ordered by law but does not fall into disorder; he relies on the people but does not slight them; he follows things but does not discard them. As for things — they are not worth doing, yet they cannot but be done. One who is not clear about Heaven will not be pure in virtue; one who does not penetrate the Way has no way forward at all. One who is not clear about the Way — alas for him!

## Section 9

**ZH:** 何謂道？有天道，有人道。無為而尊者，天道也；有為而累者，人道也。主者，天道也；臣者，人道也。天道之與人道也，相去遠矣，不可不察也。
**PY:** hé wèi dào？ yǒu tiān dào， yǒu rén dào。 wú wèi ér zūn zhě， tiān dào yě； yǒu wèi ér lèi zhě， rén dào yě。 zhǔ zhě， tiān dào yě； chén zhě， rén dào yě。 tiān dào zhī yǔ rén dào yě， xiāng qù yuǎn yǐ， bù kě bù chá yě。

**EN:** What is meant by the Way? There is the Way of Heaven, and there is the way of man. To be without action and be exalted — that is the Way of Heaven; to be full of action and entangled — that is the way of man. The lord is the Way of Heaven; the minister is the way of man. The Way of Heaven and the way of man are far apart — this cannot but be discerned.
