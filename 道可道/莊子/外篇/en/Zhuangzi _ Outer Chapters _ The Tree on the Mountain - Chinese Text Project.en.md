# The Tree on the Mountain (山木)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ The Tree on the Mountain - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (24 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 莊子行於山中，見大木，枝葉盛茂，伐木者止其旁而不取也。問其故。曰：「無所可用。」莊子曰：「此木以不材得終其天年。」夫子出於山，舍於故人之家。故人喜，命豎子殺鴈而烹之。豎子請曰：「其一能鳴，其一不能鳴，請奚殺？」主人曰：「殺不能鳴者。」

**EN:** Zhuangzi was walking in the mountains when he saw a great tree, thick with branches and leaves. Woodcutters stood beside it but did not take it. He asked the reason. They said: "It is of no use whatever." Zhuangzi said: "This tree, by being timberless, gets to live out its heaven-allotted years." The master came down out of the mountains and lodged at an old friend's house. The friend was delighted and ordered a servant boy to kill a goose and cook it. The boy asked: "One can cackle, one cannot. Which should I kill?" The host said: "Kill the one that cannot cackle."

## Section 2

**ZH:** 明日，弟子問於莊子曰：「昨日山中之木，以不材得終其天年；今主人之鴈，以不材死。先生將何處？」莊子笑曰：「周將處夫材與不材之間。材與不材之間，似之而非也，故未免乎累。若夫乘道德而浮游則不然。無譽無訾，一龍一蛇，與時俱化，而無肯專為；一上一下，以和為量，浮游乎萬物之祖；物物而不物於物，則胡可得而累邪！此黃帝、神農之法則也。若夫萬物之情，人倫之傳，則不然。合則離，成則毀，廉則挫，尊則議，有為則虧，賢則謀，不肖則欺，胡可得而必乎哉？悲夫！弟子志之，其唯道德之鄉乎！」

**EN:** The next day his disciples asked Zhuangzi: "Yesterday the tree on the mountain, by being timberless, got to live out its heaven-allotted years; today the host's goose, by being timberless, died. Where will you take your stand, Master?" Zhuangzi laughed: "Zhou will take his stand between timber and timberless. Between timber and timberless — it seems the place, yet it is not; therefore one still cannot escape entanglement. But to ride the Way and its virtue and drift and roam — that is not so. Neither praised nor blamed; now a dragon, now a snake; transforming along with the times, never willing to be one thing alone; now up, now down, taking harmony as the measure; drifting and roaming at the ancestor of the ten thousand things; treating things as things, but never being treated as a thing by things — how then could one be entangled! This was the rule of the Yellow Emperor and Shennong. But the genuine condition of the ten thousand things, the transmissions of human relations — these are not so. What joins is torn apart; what completes is destroyed; what is sharp is blunted; what is exalted is pulled down; what acts is undermined; what is worthy is schemed against; what is unworthy is cheated — how could anything be made sure! Alas! Remember this, my disciples: only in the land of the Way and its virtue!"

## Section 3

**ZH:** 市南宜僚見魯侯，魯侯有憂色。市南子曰：「君有憂色，何也？」魯侯曰：「吾學先王之道，修先君之業，吾敬鬼尊賢，親而行之，無須臾離居，然不免於患，吾是以憂。」

**EN:** Yiliao (宜僚) of Shinan (市南) was received by the marquis of Lu. The marquis wore a worried look. The master of Shinan said: "You wear a worried look, my lord. Why?" The marquis said: "I study the Way of the former kings; I cultivate the works of the former lords; I revere the ghosts and honor the worthy; I practice these in my own person without a moment's neglect or rest — and still I cannot escape calamity. That is why I am worried."

## Section 4

**ZH:** 市南子曰：「君之除患之術淺矣。夫豐狐文豹，棲於山林，伏於巖穴，靜也；夜行晝居，戒也；雖飢渴隱約，猶旦胥疏於江湖之上而求食焉，定也。然且不免於罔羅機辟之患，是何罪之有哉？其皮為之災也。今魯國獨非君之皮邪？吾願君刳形去皮，洒心去欲，而遊於無人之野。南越有邑焉，名為建德之國。其民愚而朴，少私而寡欲；知作而不知藏，與而不求其報；不知義之所適，不知禮之所將；猖狂妄行，乃蹈乎大方；其生可樂，其死可葬。吾願君去國捐俗，與道相輔而行。」

**EN:** The master of Shinan said: "Your art for removing calamity is shallow, my lord. The sleek fox and the patterned leopard lodge in the mountain forests and crouch in rocky caves — that is their stillness. They walk by night and keep by day — that is their caution. Even pressed by hunger and thirst, they will still range a whole day over rivers and lakes seeking food — that is their steadiness. And still they cannot escape the calamity of net and snare. What crime is theirs? Their hides are their disaster. Now is the state of Lu not your hide, my lord? I would have you hollow out your form and cast off your hide, wash your heart and put away desire, and roam in the land where no man is. In southern Yue there is a settlement called the State of Established Virtue. Its people are simple and plain, with little selfishness and few desires; they know how to work but not how to hoard; they give but do not seek repayment; they do not know where rightness should go; they do not know what ritual should carry; wild and free, they walk at random — yet they tread the great method. In life they can be joyful; in death they can be buried. I would have you leave your state, cast off its customs, and journey with the Way as your companion."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 君曰：「彼其道遠而險，又有江山，我無舟車，奈何？」市南子曰：「君無形倨，無留居，以為舟車。」

**EN:** The marquis said: "The road there is far and perilous, with rivers and mountains between. I have no boat, no carriage. What is to be done?" The master of Shinan said: "Be not haughty in form, nor settled in dwelling — take these as your boat and carriage."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 君曰：「彼其道幽遠而無人，吾誰與為鄰？吾無糧，我無食，安得而至焉？」市南子曰：「少君之費，寡君之欲，雖無糧而乃足。君其涉於江而浮於海，望之而不見其崖，愈往而不知其所窮。送君者皆自崖而反，君自此遠矣。故有人者累，見有於人者憂。故堯非有人，非見有於人也。吾願去君之累，除君之憂，而獨與道遊於大莫之國。方舟而濟於河，有虛船來觸舟，雖有惼心之人不怒；有一人在其上，則呼張歙之；一呼而不聞，再呼而不聞，於是三呼邪，則必以惡聲隨之。向也不怒而今也怒，向也虛而今也實。人能虛己以遊世，其孰能害之！」

**EN:** The marquis said: "The road there is dark and far and without men — whom shall I have for neighbor? I have no grain, I have no food — how could I ever arrive?" The master of Shinan said: "Lessen your expenses, thin your desires, and though you have no grain it will be enough. You will ford the river and float upon the sea, gazing and never seeing its shore; the farther you go, the more you will not know where it ends. Those who see you off all turn back at the shore — from there on, you are far away. So one who possesses others is burdened; one who is possessed by others is worried. Therefore Yao neither possessed others nor was possessed by others. I would take away your burden, relieve your worry, and roam alone with the Way in the land of Great Nothing. Two boats lashed together were crossing the river, and an empty boat came drifting and struck them. Though there was a narrow-hearted man aboard, he was not angry. But had there been a man in it, he would have shouted to sheer off — one shout unheard, a second unheard, and by the third shout curses would surely have followed. A moment ago he was not angry, and now he is: a moment ago it was empty, and now it is occupied. If a man can empty himself and roam the world — who can harm him!"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 北宮奢為衛靈公賦斂以為鐘，為壇乎國門之外，三月而成上下之縣。王子慶忌見而問焉，曰：「子何術之設？」奢曰：「一之間，無敢設也。奢聞之：『既彫既琢，復歸於朴。』侗乎其無識，儻乎其怠疑；萃乎芒乎，其送往而迎來；來者勿禁，往者勿止；從其彊梁，隨其曲傅，因其自窮。故朝夕賦斂而毫毛不挫，而況有大塗者乎！」

**EN:** Beigong She (北宮奢), levying for Duke Ling of Wei to make a set of bells, built a platform outside the state gates, and in three months the two tiers, upper and lower, were hung. Prince Qingji (王子慶忌) saw it and asked: "What art did you set to this?" She said: "Within the One, I dared not set any art. I have heard it said: 'Carved and chiseled through, return to the uncarved block.' Blank as though unknowing; artless as though dull and doubting; gathered, vague — seeing off what goes, welcoming what comes; what comes I do not forbid; what goes I do not stop; I follow the forceful, I accommodate the bending, and let each run its own course. So levies day and night, and not a hair is harmed — how much more for one who has the great road!"

## Section 8

**ZH:** 孔子圍於陳、蔡之間，七日不火食。大公任往弔之，曰：「子幾死乎？」曰：「然。」「子惡死乎？」曰：「然。」

**EN:** When Confucius was besieged between Chen and Cai, seven days without a cooked meal, Grand Duke Ren (大公任) went to condole with him: "Are you near death?" He said: "Yes." "Do you hate death?" He said: "Yes."

## Section 9

**ZH:** 任曰：「予嘗言不死之道。東海有鳥焉，其名曰意怠。其為鳥也，翂翂翐翐，而似無能；引援而飛，迫脅而棲；進不敢為前，退不敢為後；食不敢先嘗，必取其緒。是故其行列不斥，而外人卒不得害，是以免於患。直木先伐，甘井先竭。子其意者飾知以驚愚，修身以明汙，昭昭乎若揭日月而行，故不免也。昔吾聞之大成之人曰：『自伐者無功，功成者墮，名成者虧。』孰能去功與名而還與眾人！道流而不明居，得行而不名處；純純常常，乃比於狂；削跡捐勢，不為功名。是故無責於人，人亦無責焉。至人不聞，子何喜哉？」

**EN:** Ren said: "Let me try to speak of the way of not dying. In the Eastern Sea there is a bird called the Idlehood. As a bird it flaps and flutters as though it could not fly; it takes wing only when drawn along; it roosts only when pressed in among others. Advancing, it dares not go first; retreating, it dares not bring up the rear; eating, it dares not taste first — it takes only what is left over. Therefore it is not cast out of the flock, and outsiders in the end can do it no harm; thus it escapes calamity. The straight tree is first to be felled; the sweet well is first drained dry. You, I suppose, deck out your knowledge to startle the ignorant; you cultivate your person to make filth shine; you go bright and blazing as though carrying sun and moon on your shoulders — therefore you have not escaped. Of old I heard a man of great completion say: 'The self-praising have no merit; the completed in merit are pulled down; the completed in name are diminished.' Who can put away merit and name and return to be one with the crowd! The Way flows and does not dwell in brightness; attainment moves and takes no lodging in a name. Plain and constant, he is compared with the mad; he erases his tracks, casts off power, and does not work for merit and name. Therefore he has no demands on men, and men have no demands on him. The utmost man is not heard of — why do you so delight in being heard of?"

## Section 10

**ZH:** 孔子曰：「善哉！」辭其交遊，去其弟子，逃於大澤；衣裘褐，食杼栗；入獸不亂群，入鳥不亂行。鳥獸不惡，而況人乎！

**EN:** Confucius said: "Splendid!" He took leave of his acquaintances, sent away his disciples, and fled into a great marsh. He wore skins and coarse cloth, ate acorns and chestnuts; he went among the beasts and did not disturb their herds; he went among the birds and did not disturb their ranks. If even birds and beasts did not hate him — how much less men!

## Section 11

**ZH:** 孔子問子桑雽曰：「吾再逐於魯，伐樹於宋，削跡於衛，窮於商、周，圍於陳、蔡之間。吾犯此數患，親交益疏，徒友益散，何與？」

**EN:** Confucius asked Master Sang Hu (子桑雽): "I was twice driven out of Lu; a tree was cut down over me in Song; my tracks were wiped out in Wei; I came to a dead end in Shang and Zhou; I was besieged between Chen and Cai. I have met these many calamities; my kin and acquaintances grow ever more distant; my disciples and friends scatter ever more. Why is this?"

## Section 12

**ZH:** 子桑雽曰：「子獨不聞假人之亡與？林回棄千金之璧，負赤子而趨。或曰：『為其布與？赤子之布寡矣。為其累與？赤子之累多矣。棄千金之璧，負赤子而趨，何也？』林回曰：『彼以利合，此以天屬也。』夫以利合者，迫窮禍患害相棄也；以天屬者，迫窮禍患害相收也。夫相收之與相棄亦遠矣。且君子之交淡若水，小人之交甘若醴；君子淡以親，小人甘以絕。彼無故以合者，則無故以離。」

**EN:** Master Sang Hu said: "Have you alone not heard of the flight of the man of Jia (假)? Lin Hui (林回) abandoned a jade disk worth a thousand pieces of gold and fled with his infant son on his back. Someone said: 'Was it for the worth? An infant's worth is little. Was it for the burden? An infant's burden is great. To abandon a jade worth a thousand and flee with an infant on his back — why?' Lin Hui said: 'That joined me by profit; this is bound to me by Heaven.' Those joined by profit abandon one another when pressed by straits, calamity, trouble, and harm; those bound by Heaven gather one another in when pressed by straits, calamity, trouble, and harm. Gathering in and abandoning — how far apart they are! The gentleman's friendship is bland as water; the small man's friendship is sweet as wine. The gentleman is bland and so close; the small man is sweet and so cut off. What joins without cause parts without cause."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 孔子曰：「敬聞命矣。」徐行翔佯而歸，絕學捐書，弟子無挹於前，其愛益加進。

**EN:** Confucius said: "I have heard your instruction with reverence." He walked slowly, wandered at ease on his way home; he ended his studies and cast away his books. His disciples no longer bowed before him — and his love from them grew ever deeper.

## Section 14

**ZH:** 異日，桑雽又曰：「舜之將死，真泠禹曰：『汝戒之哉！形莫若緣，情莫若率。緣則不離，率則不勞；不離不勞，則不求文以待形；不求文以待形，固不待物。』」

**EN:** Another day Sang Hu said further: "When Shun was about to die, he gave this true charge to Yu: 'Be warned! For the form, nothing compares with following along; for the feelings, nothing compares with frankness. Following along, one is not parted; being frank, one does not toil. Not parted, not toiling, one does not seek ornament to wait upon the form; not seeking ornament to wait upon the form — one surely does not wait upon things at all.'"

## Section 15

**ZH:** 莊子衣大布而補之，正緳係履而過魏王。魏王曰：「何先生之憊邪？」莊子曰：「貧也，非憊也。士有道德不能行，憊也。衣弊履穿，貧也，非憊也，此所謂非遭時也。王獨不見夫騰猿乎？其得柟、梓、豫、章也，攬蔓其枝，而王長其間，雖羿、蓬蒙不能眄睨也。及其得柘、棘、枳、枸之閒也，危行側視，振動悼慄，此筋骨非有加急而不柔也，處勢不便，未足以逞其能也。今處昏上亂相之間，而欲無憊，奚可得邪？此比干之見剖心，徵也夫！」

**EN:** Zhuangzi, wearing a patched robe of coarse cloth, with his shoes tied on by strings, went past the king of Wei. The king said: "How worn-out you look, sir!" Zhuangzi said: "It is poverty, not weariness. When a scholar has the Way and its virtue and cannot practice them — that is weariness. Torn clothes and broken shoes — that is poverty, not weariness. It is what is called not meeting one's time. Has my king never seen the leaping gibbon? Give it the nanmu, the catalpa, the camphor, and the cedar, and it clutches their twining branches and kings it over their midst; even Yi and Pengmeng could not get a sidelong aim at it. But put it among the zhe-thorn, the bramble, the trifoliate, and the briar, and it walks warily, glancing sideways, trembling and shivering — not because its sinews and bones have suddenly stiffened and lost their suppleness, but because it stands in a situation not to its advantage, and cannot show what it can do. Now I stand between a benighted ruler and his deluded ministers — how could I not look worn out! That Bigan had his heart cut out — there is your proof!"

## Section 16

**ZH:** 孔子窮於陳、蔡之間，七日不火食，左據槁木，右擊槁枝，而歌猋氏之風，有其具而無其數，有其聲而無宮角，木聲與人聲，犁然有當於人心。

**EN:** When Confucius was in straits between Chen and Cai, seven days without a cooked meal, he leaned against a withered tree on his left and beat time on a withered branch with his right, singing the airs of Biaoshi (猋氏). He had the instrument but not the measure; the sound, but not the modes of gong and jue. Yet the wood's sound and the man's sound, crisp and clear, struck home in men's hearts.

## Section 17

**ZH:** 顏回端拱還目而窺之。仲尼恐其廣己而造大也，愛己而造哀也，曰：「回！無受天損易，無受人益難。無始而非卒也，人與天一也。夫今之歌者其誰乎？」

**EN:** Yan Hui stood upright with folded arms, turning his eyes to watch him. Confucius feared that he would make much of him and arrive at grandiosity, or love him and arrive at sorrow, so he said: "Hui! Not to accept Heaven's diminishments is easy; not to accept men's enrichments is hard. There is no beginning that is not also an ending; man and Heaven are one. Who is it that sings now?"

## Section 18

**ZH:** 回曰：「敢問無受天損易。」仲尼曰：「飢溺寒暑，窮桎不行，天地之行也，運物之泄也，言與之偕逝之謂也。為人臣者，不敢去之。執臣之道猶若是，而況乎所以待天乎！」

**EN:** Hui said: "May I ask about 'not to accept Heaven's diminishments is easy'?" Confucius said: "Hunger and flood, cold and heat, straits and shackles that will not move — these are the course of heaven and earth, the outflow of things in motion. The saying means to pass away together with them. A subject dares not abandon his lord. If one who holds the way of a subject is still thus, how much more one who waits upon Heaven!"

## Section 19

**ZH:** 「何謂無受人益難？」仲尼曰：「始用四達，爵祿並至而不窮，物之所利，乃非己也，吾命有在外者也。君子不為盜，賢人不為竊。吾若取之，何哉？故曰：鳥莫知於鷾鴯，目之所不宜處，不給視，雖落其實，棄之而走。其畏人也，而襲諸人間，社稷存焉爾。」

**EN:** "What of 'not to accept men's enrichments is hard'?" Confucius said: "First employed, one prospers in all four directions; titles and stipends arrive together without end. But what profits things is not one's own; my destiny has something outside me. The gentleman does not rob; the worthy man does not steal. If I were to take it, what would I be! Hence it is said: no bird knows better than the swallow — if a place does not suit its eyes, it gives it no second look; though it drops its morsel, it abandons it and flies away. It fears men, yet nests among them — its altars of soil and grain are there."

## Section 20

**ZH:** 「何謂無始而非卒？」仲尼曰：「化其萬物而不知其禪之者，焉知其所終？焉知其所始？正而待之而已耳。」

**EN:** "What of 'there is no beginning that is not also an ending'?" Confucius said: "It transforms the ten thousand things, and none knows who hands them on — how could one know its end? How could one know its beginning? Be upright and wait for it — that is all."

## Section 21

**ZH:** 「何謂天與人一邪？」仲尼曰：「有人，天也；有天，亦天也。人之不能有天，性也，聖人晏然體逝而終矣。」

**EN:** "What of 'man and Heaven are one'?" Confucius said: "That there is man — that is Heaven. That there is Heaven — that too is Heaven. That man cannot have Heaven — that is nature. The sage, calm, embodies the passing-away, and so ends."

## Section 22

**ZH:** 莊周遊乎雕陵之樊，睹一異鵲自南方來者，翼廣七尺，目大運寸，感周之顙而集於栗林。莊周曰：「此何鳥哉？翼殷不逝，目大不覩。」蹇裳躩步，執彈而留之。睹一蟬方得美蔭而忘其身；螳蜋執翳而搏之，見得而忘其形；異鵲從而利之，見利而忘其真。莊周怵然曰：「噫！物固相累，二類相召也。」捐彈而反走，虞人逐而誶之。

**EN:** Zhuang Zhou was roaming in the preserve of Diaoling (雕陵) when he saw a strange magpie coming from the south: wings seven feet across, eyes an inch around. It brushed Zhou's forehead and settled in a chestnut grove. Zhuang Zhou said: "What bird is this? Such abundant wings, yet it does not fly far; such great eyes, yet it does not see." Hitching up his robe, he tiptoed forward, pellet-bow in hand, and waited there. He saw a cicada that had just found a fine shade and forgotten its body; a mantis, taking cover behind a leaf, seized it — seeing its catch, it forgot its own form; the strange magpie profited from behind — seeing its profit, it forgot its truth. Zhuang Zhou shuddered: "Ah! Things indeed entangle one another; two kinds summon one another!" He threw down the bow and ran back — and the gamekeeper chased after him, cursing.

## Section 23

**ZH:** 莊周反入，三月不庭。藺且從而問之：「夫子何為頃間甚不庭乎？」莊周曰：「吾守形而忘身，觀於濁水而迷於清淵。且吾聞諸夫子曰：『入其俗，從其俗。』今吾遊於雕陵而忘吾身，異鵲感吾顙，遊於栗林而忘真，栗林虞人以吾為戮，吾所以不庭也。」

**EN:** Zhuang Zhou went back in, and for three months did not come out into the courtyard. Lin Ju (藺且) followed and asked him: "Why have you lately kept so from the courtyard, Master?" Zhuang Zhou said: "I was guarding my outer form and forgot my own person; I gazed into the muddy water and lost myself from the clear deep. Moreover, I heard from the master: 'Entering a custom, follow the custom.' Now I roamed in Diaoling and forgot my own person; a strange magpie brushed my forehead; roaming in the chestnut grove I forgot my truth; and the keeper of the chestnut grove took me for a poacher. That is why I do not come out into the courtyard."

## Section 24

**ZH:** 陽子之宋，宿於逆旅。逆旅有妾二人，其一人美，其一人惡，惡者貴而美者賤。陽子問其故，逆旅小子對曰：「其美者自美，吾不知其美也；其惡者自惡，吾不知其惡也。」陽子曰：「弟子記之！行賢而去自賢之行，安往而不愛哉？」

**EN:** Yangzi, going to Song, lodged at an inn. The innkeeper had two concubines: one beautiful, one ugly. The ugly was honored; the beautiful was slighted. Yangzi asked the reason. The innkeeper's boy answered: "The beautiful one thinks herself beautiful — I no longer notice her beauty. The ugly one thinks herself ugly — I no longer notice her ugliness." Yangzi said: "Remember this, my disciples! Practice worthiness and put away the conduct of thinking oneself worthy — then wherever you go, how could you not be loved!"
