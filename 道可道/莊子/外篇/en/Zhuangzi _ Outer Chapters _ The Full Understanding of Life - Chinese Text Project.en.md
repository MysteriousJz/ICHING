# The Full Understanding of Life (達生)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ The Full Understanding of Life - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (17 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 達生之情者，不務生之所無以為；達命之情者，不務知之所無奈何。養形必先之以物，物有餘而形不養者有之矣；有生必先無離形，形不離而生亡者有之矣。生之來不能卻，其去不能止。悲夫！世之人以為養形足以存生，而養形果不足以存生，則世奚足為哉！雖不足為而不可不為者，其為不免矣。

**EN:** One who penetrates the genuine condition of life does not strive after what life can do nothing with; one who penetrates the genuine condition of destiny does not strive after what knowledge can do nothing about. To nourish the bodily frame one must first have things — yet there are those with things in plenty whose frame goes unnourished. To keep life one must first not part from the frame — yet there are those whose frame is whole whose life is lost. Life's coming cannot be refused; its going cannot be stopped. Alas! The men of the world think that nourishing the frame is enough to preserve life — yet nourishing the frame is really not enough to preserve life. Then what in the world is worth doing? Yet though not worth doing, it cannot but be done — and to do it is unavoidable.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 夫欲免為形者，莫如棄世。棄世則無累，無累則正平，正平則與彼更生，更生則幾矣。事奚足棄而生奚足遺？棄事則形不勞，遺生則精不虧。夫形全精復，與天為一。天地者，萬物之父母也，合則成體，散則成始。形精不虧，是謂能移；精而又精，反以相天。

**EN:** For one who wishes to escape toiling for the frame, nothing compares with abandoning the world. Abandoning the world, he is unburdened; unburdened, he is upright and level; upright and level, he is born afresh along with things; born afresh, he is nearly there. Why should affairs be worth abandoning, or life worth letting go? Abandon affairs and the frame is unwearied; let life go and the essence is undiminished. The frame made whole, the essence restored — he is one with Heaven. Heaven and earth are the father and mother of the ten thousand things: joined, they form the body; parted, they form a new beginning. Frame and essence undiminished — this is called being able to shift. Essence and then more essence — he turns and helps Heaven along.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 子列子問關尹曰：「至人潛行不窒，蹈火不熱，行乎萬物之上而不慄。請問何以至於此？」關尹曰：「是純氣之守也，非知巧果敢之列。居！吾語女。凡有貌象聲色者，皆物也，物與物何以相遠？夫奚足以至乎先？是色而已。則物之造乎不形，而止乎無所化，夫得是而窮之者，物焉得而止焉！彼將處乎不淫之度，而藏乎無端之紀，遊乎萬物之所終始，壹其性，養其氣，合其德，以通乎物之所造。夫若是者，其天守全，其神無郤，物奚自入焉！夫醉者之墜車，雖疾不死。骨節與人同，而犯害與人異，其神全也，乘亦不知也，墜亦不知也，死生驚懼不入乎其胷中，是故遻物而不慴。彼得全於酒而猶若是，而況得全於天乎！聖人藏於天，故莫之能傷也。」復讎者不折鏌、干，雖有忮心者不怨飄瓦，是以天下平均。故無攻戰之亂，無殺戮之刑者，由此道也。不開人之天，而開天之天，開天者德生，開人者賊生。不厭其天，不忽於人，民幾乎以其真。

**EN:** Master Liezi asked Guanyin (關尹): "The utmost man walks beneath the water and is not stopped; treads fire and is not burned; walks above the ten thousand things and does not tremble. May I ask how one arrives at this?" Guanyin said: "This is the guarding of the pure breath; it is not of the class of knowledge, skill, resolution, or daring. Sit down! I will tell you. Whatever has face, form, sound, and color is a thing; and how could one thing stand far from another? And what could suffice to arrive before them? It is all a matter of color and form. But when things are fashioned in the formless and come to rest where there is no transformation — one who gets this and goes to the bottom of it: how could things stop him! He will dwell in the measure that is never exceeded; hide in the warp that has no beginning; roam where the ten thousand things end and begin; make one his nature, nourish his breath, join his virtue — and so commune with that from which things are fashioned. Being such, his heavenly guard is whole, his spirit has no seam — through what could things enter in! A drunken man who falls from a cart, however fast it goes, does not die. His bones and joints are the same as other men's, yet the harm he meets differs — because his spirit is whole. He does not know he is riding; he does not know he has fallen. Death and life, alarm and fear, do not enter his breast; therefore he meets things without terror. If one who finds his wholeness in wine can still be like this, how much more one who finds his wholeness in Heaven! The sage hides in Heaven; therefore nothing can injure him." An avenger does not break the Moye sword that slew his kin; even a spiteful man does not resent the windblown tile that hits him — and by this the world is evened and leveled. Thus that there are no disorders of assault and war, no punishments of slaughter, comes of this way. Do not open the heaven in man; open the Heaven in heaven. Opening Heaven gives birth to virtue; opening the human gives birth to thieves. Do not weary of Heaven; do not be heedless of men — and the people will nearly attain their truth.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 仲尼適楚，出於林中，見痀僂者承蜩，猶掇之也。仲尼曰：「子巧乎？有道邪？」曰：「我有道也。五六月累丸，二而不墜，則失者錙銖；累三而不墜，則失者十一；累五而不墜，猶掇之也。吾處身也若厥株拘，吾執臂也若槁木之枝，雖天地之大，萬物之多，而唯蜩翼之知。吾不反不側，不以萬物易蜩之翼，何為而不得！」孔子顧謂弟子曰：「用志不分，乃凝於神，其痀僂丈人之謂乎！」

**EN:** Confucius was on his way to Chu. Coming out of a wood, he saw a hunchback catching cicadas with a sticky rod — as easily as picking them up with his fingers. Confucius said: "Are you skillful, or do you have a Way?" He said: "I have a Way. In the fifth and sixth months I balance balls: with two balls that do not fall, I miss only a fraction; with three that do not fall, I miss one in ten; with five that do not fall — it is like picking them up with my fingers. I plant my body like a rooted stump; I hold out my arm like the branch of a withered tree. Great as heaven and earth are, many as the ten thousand things are — I know only cicada wings. I do not turn, do not lean; I would not exchange the cicada's wings for all the ten thousand things. How could I fail to get them!" Confucius turned to his disciples: "'Use the will undivided, and it congeals into spirit' — does that not describe this hunchbacked old man!"

## Section 5

**ZH:** 顏淵問仲尼曰：「吾嘗濟乎觴深之淵，津人操舟若神。吾問焉，曰：『操舟可學邪？』曰：『可。善游者數能。若乃夫沒人，則未嘗見舟而便操之也。』吾問焉而不吾告，敢問何謂也？」仲尼曰：「善游者數能，忘水也。若乃夫沒人之未嘗見舟而便操之也，彼視淵若陵，視舟之覆猶其車卻也。覆卻萬方陳乎前而不得入其舍，惡往而不暇！以瓦注者巧，以鉤注者憚，以黃金注者殙。其巧一也，而有所矜，則重外也。凡外重者內拙。」

**EN:** Yan Yuan asked Confucius: "I once crossed the deep at Shangshen (觴深). The ferryman handled the boat like a spirit. I asked him: 'Can boat-handling be learned?' He said: 'It can. A good swimmer picks it up in a few tries. But a diver — though he has never seen a boat, he handles it at once.' I asked him more but he would not tell me. May I ask what he meant?" Confucius said: "A good swimmer picks it up quickly because he has forgotten the water. As for the diver handling the boat at once though he has never seen one — he looks on the deep as a hillside; he looks on the boat's capsizing as a cart rolling backward. Capsizings and backward rolls in ten thousand shapes can be laid out before him and never enter his lodging — wherever he goes, what could fluster him! Bet with a tile and you are clever; bet with a belt-buckle and you grow wary; bet with gold and your wits swim. The cleverness is the same, but where there is something at stake, the outer weighs heavy. Whoever is weighted by the outer is clumsy within."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 田開之見周威公。威公曰：「吾聞祝腎學生。吾子與祝腎游，亦何聞焉？」田開之曰：「開之操拔篲以倚門庭，亦何聞於夫子！」威公曰：「田子無讓！寡人願聞之。」開之曰：「聞之夫子曰：『善養生者，若牧羊然，視其後者而鞭之。』」威公曰：「何謂也？」田開之曰：「魯有單豹者，巖居而水飲，不與民共利，行年七十而猶有嬰兒之色，不幸遇餓虎，餓虎殺而食之。有張毅者，高門、懸薄，無不走也，行年四十而有內熱之病以死。豹養其內而虎食其外，毅養其外而病攻其內，此二子者，皆不鞭其後者也。」

**EN:** Tian Kaizhi (田開之) was received by Duke Wei of Zhou (周威公). The duke said: "I hear that Zhu Shen (祝腎) studies life. You, sir, have kept company with him — what have you heard?" Tian Kaizhi said: "Kaizhi wields a broom at your gates and courtyards — what could I have heard from the master!" The duke said: "Do not be so modest, Tian. I wish to hear it." Kaizhi said: "I heard the master say: 'One who is good at nourishing life is like a shepherd of sheep: he watches for the stragglers and whips them on.'" The duke said: "What does that mean?" Tian Kaizhi said: "In Lu there was a Shan Bao (單豹), who dwelt among the cliffs and drank from the streams, sharing no profit with the people. At seventy his face still had the color of an infant — but unluckily he met a hungry tiger, and the hungry tiger killed and ate him. There was also a Zhang Yi (張毅), who scurried to every high gate and curtained carriage. At forty he died of an inward-fevering sickness. Bao nourished what was within, and the tiger ate what was without; Yi nourished what was without, and sickness attacked what was within. These two men were both ones who did not whip on their stragglers."

## Section 7

**ZH:** 仲尼曰：「無入而藏，無出而陽，柴立其中央。三者若得，其名必極。夫畏塗者，十殺一人，則父子兄弟相戒也，必盛卒徒而後敢出焉，不亦知乎！人之所取畏者，衽席之上，飲食之間，而不知為之戒者，過也。」

**EN:** Confucius said: "Do not go in and hide; do not come out and shine; stand like a wooden post at the center. If these three are gotten, the name is sure to reach the utmost. Men fear a road where one traveler in ten is killed: father and son, elder and younger brother warn one another, and dare not go out without a strong band of followers — is that not wise! But what men should take as fearsome is upon the mat of the bed and amid food and drink — and not to know to be warned of these is error."

## Section 8

**ZH:** 祝宗人玄端以臨牢筴，說彘曰：「汝奚惡死？吾將三月豢汝，十日戒，三日齊，藉白茅，加汝肩尻乎彫俎之上，則汝為之乎？」為彘謀曰：「不如食以糠糟，而錯之牢筴之中。」自為謀，則苟生有軒冕之尊，死得於腞、楯之上，聚僂之中，則為之。為彘謀則去之，自為謀則取之，所異彘者何也？

**EN:** The invoker in his dark robes and square cap came to the pen and persuaded the pigs: "Why do you hate death? I will fatten you for three months; I will fast ten days and keep vigil three; I will spread white rushes and lay your shoulder and rump upon the carved sacrificial stand. Would you not agree to that?" Planning for the pigs, one would say: "Better to be fed on chaff and dregs and left in the pen." Planning for himself: if in life he could have the honor of carriage and cap, and in death be laid upon the bier among the carved and painted — he would agree to it. Planning for the pigs, he rejects it; planning for himself, he accepts it. Wherein, pray, does he differ from the pigs?

## Section 9

**ZH:** 桓公田於澤，管仲御，見鬼焉。公撫管仲之手曰：「仲父何見？」對曰：「臣無所見。」公反，誒詒為病，數日不出。齊士有皇子告敖者曰：「公則自傷，鬼惡能傷公！夫忿滀之氣，散而不反，則為不足；上而不下，則使人善怒；下而不上，則使人善忘；不上不下，中身當心，則為病。」桓公曰：「然則有鬼乎？」曰：「有。沈有履，灶有髻。戶內之煩壤，雷霆處之；東北方之下者，倍阿、鮭蠪躍之；西北方之下者，則泆陽處之。水有罔象，丘有峷，山有夔，野有彷徨，澤有委蛇。」公曰：「請問委蛇之狀何如？」皇子曰：「委蛇，其大如轂，其長如轅，紫衣而朱冠。其為物也惡，聞雷車之聲，則捧其首而立。見之者殆乎霸。」桓公囅然而笑曰：「此寡人之所見者也。」於是正衣冠與之坐，不終日而不知病之去也。

**EN:** Duke Huan was hunting in the marshes, with Guan Zhong (管仲) as his charioteer, when he saw a ghost. The duke took Guan Zhong's hand: "What do you see, father Zhong?" He answered: "Your servant sees nothing." The duke returned home, fell mumbling into illness, and for days did not come out. A scholar of Qi, Huangzi Gao'ao (皇子告敖), said: "The duke injures himself — how could a ghost injure the duke! When the breath of anger and fullness scatters and does not return, there is insufficiency; when it mounts without descending, it makes a man quick to anger; when it descends without mounting, it makes a man forgetful; when it neither mounts nor descends, but gathers in the body's center at the heart, it makes illness." Duke Huan said: "Then are there ghosts?" He said: "There are. In the mire dwells Lu; at the stove, Ji. In the clutter within the door dwells Thunder; in the northeast's nether parts, Beia and Salong leap about; in the northwest's nether parts dwells Yiyang. In the waters dwells Wangxiang; on the hills, Shen; in the mountains, Kui; in the wilds, Panghuang; in the marshes, Weiyi." The duke said: "May I ask what a Weiyi looks like?" Huangzi said: "The Weiyi is big as a wheel-hub, long as a carriage-pole; it wears a purple robe and a scarlet cap. As a creature it hates the sound of thundering chariots: hearing them, it clutches its head and stands. One who sees it is nearly sure to become a hegemon." Duke Huan burst into laughter: "That is the very thing I saw!" Thereupon he straightened his robe and cap and sat with Huangzi, and before the day was out his illness had gone without his knowing it.

## Section 10

**ZH:** 紀渻子為王養鬥雞。十日而問：「雞已乎？」曰：「未也。方虛憍而恃氣。」十日又問。曰：「未也。猶應嚮景。」十日又問。曰：「未也。猶疾視而盛氣。」十日又問。曰：「幾矣。雞雖有鳴者，已無變矣，望之似木雞矣，其德全矣，異雞無敢應者，反走矣。」

**EN:** Ji Shengzi (紀渻子) was rearing a fighting cock for the king. After ten days the king asked: "Is the cock ready?" He said: "Not yet. He is still puffed with vanity and leaning on his spirit." After ten days the king asked again. He said: "Not yet. He still answers to sounds and shadows." After ten days the king asked again. He said: "Not yet. He still glares fiercely and brims with fury." After ten days the king asked again. He said: "Nearly there. Another cock may crow — he does not so much as change. Look at him: he is like a wooden cock. His virtue is complete. The other cocks dare not face him; they turn and run."

## Section 11

**ZH:** 孔子觀於呂梁，縣水三十仞，流沫四十里，黿鼉魚龞之所不能游也。見一丈夫游之，以為有苦而欲死也，使弟子並流而拯之。數百步而出，被髮行歌而游於塘下。孔子從而問焉，曰：「吾以子為鬼，察子則人也。請問蹈水有道乎？」曰：「亡，吾無道。吾始乎故，長乎性，成乎命。與齊俱入，與汩偕出，從水之道而不為私焉。此吾所以蹈之也。」孔子曰：「何謂始乎故，長乎性，成乎命？」曰：「吾生於陵而安於陵，故也；長於水而安於水，性也；不知吾所以然而然，命也。」

**EN:** Confucius was viewing the falls at Lüliang (呂梁). The water hung down thirty fathoms; the racing foam ran forty li; not even tortoises, crocodiles, fish, or turtles could swim there. He saw a man swimming in it and took him for one in despair who wished to die; he sent his disciples along the bank to rescue him. Several hundred paces downstream the man emerged, hair loose, walking and singing as he roamed beneath the embankment. Confucius went after him and asked: "I took you for a ghost; on inspection you are a man. May I ask whether you have a Way for treading water?" He said: "No — I have no Way. I began in what was given; I grew in my nature; I was completed in destiny. I go in with the whirlpool; I come out with the torrent; I follow the way of the water and make nothing private of it. This is how I tread it." Confucius said: "What do you mean by beginning in what was given, growing in your nature, being completed in destiny?" He said: "I was born on the dry hills and am at ease on the hills — that is what was given. I grew up in the water and am at ease in the water — that is my nature. I do not know why it is so, yet it is so — that is destiny."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 梓慶削木為鐻，鐻成，見者驚猶鬼神。魯侯見而問焉，曰：「子何術以為焉？」對曰：「臣工人，何術之有！雖然，有一焉。臣將為鐻，未嘗敢以耗氣也，必齊以靜心。齊三日，而不敢懷慶賞爵祿；齊五日，不敢懷非譽巧拙；齊七日，輒然忘吾有四枝形體也。當是時也，無公朝，其巧專而外骨消；然後入山林，觀天性；形軀至矣，然後成見鐻，然後加手焉；不然則已。則以天合天，器之所以疑神者，其是與？」

**EN:** Woodworker Qing (梓慶) carved wood into a bell-stand; when the bell-stand was finished, all who saw it marveled as at the work of ghosts and spirits. The marquis of Lu saw it and asked: "What art do you make it with?" He answered: "Your servant is a workman — what art could he have! Still, there is one thing. When your servant is about to make a bell-stand, he never dares to let his breath be wasted; he is sure to fast and quiet the heart. Fasting three days, he no longer dares to harbor thoughts of reward, celebration, title, or stipend. Fasting five days, he no longer dares to harbor thoughts of blame or praise, skill or clumsiness. Fasting seven days, he is suddenly still, forgetting that he has four limbs and a body. At that time there is no duke, no court; his skill is concentrated and all outer bones melt away. Only then does he enter the mountain forest and behold the heavenly nature; when the form of a tree arrives complete, only then does he see the bell-stand finished in it; only then does he lay hand to it. Otherwise he lets it be. Thus he joins heaven to heaven — is it not for this that his work is suspected of being spirit-like?"

## Section 13

**ZH:** 東野稷以御見莊公，進退中繩，左右旋中規。莊公以為文弗過也，使之鉤百而反。顏闔遇之，入見曰：「稷之馬將敗。」公密而不應。少焉，果敗而反。公曰：「子何以知之？」曰：「其馬力竭矣，而猶求焉，故曰敗。」

**EN:** Dongye Ji (東野稷) displayed his driving before Duke Zhuang. Forward and back he hit the inked line; wheeling left and right he hit the compass. Duke Zhuang thought no pattern could surpass it, and bade him drive a hundred turns and return. Yan He met him on the road, came in, and said: "Ji's horses are going to break down." The duke kept silent and did not answer. Presently the horses did break down, and Ji returned. The duke said: "How did you know?" He said: "The strength of his horses was spent, and still he demanded more — therefore I said they would break down."

## Section 14

**ZH:** 工倕旋而蓋規矩，指與物化，而不以心稽，故其靈臺一而不桎。忘足，履之適也；忘要，帶之適也；知忘是非，心之適也；不內變，不外從，事會之適也。始乎適而未嘗不適者，忘適之適也。

**EN:** Craftsman Chui could draw circles freehand that covered compass and square; his fingers transformed along with the thing, and he did not check them with his mind — therefore his spirit-tower was one and unfettered. Forgetting the feet is the shoe's fitting; forgetting the waist is the belt's fitting; knowing to forget right and wrong is the heart's fitting; no change within, no following without is the fitting of circumstance. Beginning in fitting and never once unfitting — that is the fitting that forgets even fitting.

## Section 15

**ZH:** 有孫休者，踵門而詫子扁慶子曰：「休居鄉不見謂不修，臨難不見謂不勇，然而田原不遇歲，事君不遇世，賓於鄉里，逐於州部，則胡罪乎天哉？休惡遇此命也？」扁子曰：「子獨不聞夫至人之自行邪？忘其肝膽，遺其耳目，芒然彷徨乎塵垢之外，逍遙乎無事之業，是謂『為而不恃，長而不宰』。今汝飾知以驚愚，修身以明汙，昭昭乎若揭日月而行也。汝得全而形軀，具而九竅，無中道夭於聾盲跛蹇而比於人數，亦幸矣，又何暇乎天之怨哉！子往矣！」

**EN:** There was a Sun Xiu (孫休) who came hobbling to the gate, astonished, and said to Master Bian Qingzi (扁慶子): "In my village, none ever said Xiu lacked cultivation; facing difficulty, none ever said I lacked courage. Yet when I farm the fields, the years do not meet me; when I serve a lord, the age does not meet me. I am pushed out of village and ward, driven from district and province. What crime have I committed against Heaven? What did I do to meet this lot?" Bianzi said: "Have you alone never heard of the utmost man's conduct of himself? He forgets his liver and gall; he lets go of his ears and eyes; dim and vague, he wanders beyond the dust and grime, roams free and easy in the work of having no affairs. This is called 'acting without depending, rearing without ruling.' Now you deck out your knowledge to startle the ignorant; you cultivate your person to make filth shine; you go bright and blazing as though carrying sun and moon on your shoulders. That you have gotten to keep your bodily frame whole, that your nine openings are furnished complete, that you have not been cut off in mid-course by deafness, blindness, lameness, or crippling, and still count among the number of men — that is already luck! What leisure have you to complain against Heaven! Be on your way!"

## Section 16

**ZH:** 孫子出。扁子入坐，有間，仰天而歎。弟子問曰：「先生何為歎乎？」扁子曰：「向者休來，吾告之以至人之德，吾恐其驚而遂至於惑也。」弟子曰：「不然。孫子之所言是邪，先生之所言非邪，非固不能惑是。孫子所言非邪，先生所言是邪，彼固惑而來矣，又奚罪焉？」

**EN:** Sunzi went out. Bianzi went in and sat down; after a while he looked up to heaven and sighed. His disciples asked: "Why do you sigh, sir?" Bianzi said: "Just now Xiu came, and I told him of the utmost man's virtue. I fear he will be startled by it and so arrive at bewilderment." His disciples said: "Not so. If what Sunzi said is right and what you said is wrong — the wrong certainly cannot bewilder the right. If what Sunzi said is wrong and what you said is right — he came bewildered already. What blame is yours?"

## Section 17

**ZH:** 扁子曰：「不然。昔者有鳥止於魯郊，魯君說之，為具太牢以饗之，奏九韶以樂之，鳥乃始憂悲眩視，不敢飲食。此之謂以己養養鳥也。若夫以鳥養養鳥者，宜棲之深林，浮之江湖，食之以委蛇，則平陸而已矣。今休，款啟寡聞之民也，吾告以至人之德，譬之若載鼷以車馬，樂鴳以鐘鼓也。彼又奚能無驚乎哉？」

**EN:** Bianzi said: "Not so. Of old a bird alighted in the outskirts of Lu. The lord of Lu was delighted with it, had the grand sacrifice set out to feast it, and had the Nine Shao played for its delight. The bird began to grieve, eyes swimming, daring neither to drink nor eat. This is called nourishing a bird as one would nourish oneself. To nourish a bird as a bird should be nourished, one should roost it in the deep forest, float it on rivers and lakes, feed it at its ease — then it would be content on the level land. Now this Xiu is a man of narrow apertures and little learning. I told him of the utmost man's virtue — it is as though I had carried a mouse home in a carriage and pair, or delighted a quail with bells and drums. How could he fail to be startled!"
