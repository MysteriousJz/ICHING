# Perfect Enjoyment (至樂)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ Perfect Enjoyment - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (11 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 天下有至樂無有哉？有可以活身者無有哉？今奚為奚據？奚避奚處？奚就奚去？奚樂奚惡？

**EN:** Is there perfect enjoyment under heaven, or is there not? Is there something that can keep the body alive, or is there not? Now what is to be done, what relied upon? What avoided, what dwelt in? What approached, what departed from? What enjoyed, what hated?

## Section 2

**ZH:** 夫天下之所尊者，富貴壽善也；所樂者，身安、厚味、美服、好色、音聲也；所下者，貧賤夭惡也；所苦者，身不得安逸，口不得厚味，形不得美服，目不得好色，耳不得音聲；若不得者，則大憂以懼。其為形也亦愚哉！

**EN:** What the world honors is riches, rank, long life, and a good name; what it enjoys is ease of body, rich flavors, fine clothes, beautiful sights, music of sound; what it counts low is poverty, meanness, early death, and ill repute; what it bitterly suffers is the body denied ease, the mouth denied rich flavors, the person denied fine clothes, the eye denied beautiful sights, the ear denied music. Denied these, men fall into great worry and fear. Such care for the bodily frame — is it not foolish!

## Section 3

**ZH:** 夫富者，苦身疾作，多積財而不得盡用，其為形也亦外矣。夫貴者，夜以繼日，思慮善否，其為形也亦疏矣。人之生也，與憂俱生，壽者惛惛，久憂不死，何苦也！其為形也亦遠矣。烈士為天下見善矣，未足以活身。吾未知善之誠善邪，誠不善邪？若以為善矣，不足活身；以為不善矣，足以活人。故曰：「忠諫不聽，蹲循勿爭。」故夫子胥爭之以殘其形，不爭，名亦不成。誠有善無有哉？今俗之所為與其所樂，吾又未知樂之果樂邪，果不樂邪？吾觀夫俗之所樂，舉群趣者，誙誙然如將不得已，而皆曰樂者，吾未之樂也，亦未之不樂也。果有樂無有哉？吾以無為誠樂矣，又俗之所大苦也。故曰：「至樂無樂，至譽無譽。」

**EN:** The rich man wearies his body with toiling labor; he heaps up wealth in piles and can never use it all — such care for the bodily frame is quite beside the point. The man of rank labors night and day, brooding over good and bad outcomes — such care for the bodily frame is wide of the mark. Man is born, and care is born with him. The long-lived grow dull and doting, dragging out their cares, refusing to die — what bitterness! Such care for the bodily frame is far astray. The ardent man is found good by all the world, yet it is not enough to keep his body alive. I do not know whether this goodness is really good or really not good. Count it good — it is not enough to keep his body alive; count it not good — it is enough to keep others alive. Hence it is said: "If loyal remonstrance is not heard, retire and do not contend." Yet Zixu contended and mangled his body; had he not contended, he would not have made his name. Is there really goodness, or is there not? Now as for what the vulgar do and what they delight in — I do not know whether their delight is really delight or really not delight. I watch what the vulgar delight in: the whole herd races after it, heads down, as though they could not help it — and all call it delight. I find no delight in it; yet I find no not-delight either. Is there really delight, or is there not? I count non-action the true delight — and the vulgar count it the great bitterness. Hence it is said: "Perfect enjoyment is without enjoyment; perfect praise is without praise."

## Section 4

**ZH:** 天下是非果未可定也。雖然，無為可以定是非。至樂活身，唯無為幾存。請嘗試言之。天無為以之清，地無為以之寧，故兩無為相合，萬物皆化。芒乎芴乎，而無從出乎！芴乎芒乎，而無有象乎！萬物職職，皆從無為殖。故曰：「天地無為也，而無不為也。」人也，孰能得無為哉！

**EN:** Whether right and wrong under heaven can really be fixed has never been settled. Even so, non-action can settle right and wrong. Perfect enjoyment, keeping the body alive — only in non-action is it nearly preserved. Let me try to say it. Heaven, without action, is thereby clear; earth, without action, is thereby still. So the two non-actions joined together, and the ten thousand things were all transformed. Vague and dim! — with nothing it comes forth from. Dim and vague! — with no image to it. The ten thousand things, teeming and teeming, all are bred from non-action. Hence it is said: "Heaven and earth do nothing, and there is nothing they do not do." As for man — who can attain non-action!

## Section 5

**ZH:** 莊子妻死，惠子弔之，莊子則方箕踞鼓盆而歌。惠子曰：「與人居長子，老身死，不哭亦足矣，又鼓盆而歌，不亦甚乎！」莊子曰：「不然。是其始死也，我獨何能無概然！察其始而本無生，非徒無生也，而本無形，非徒無形也，而本無氣。雜乎芒芴之間，變而有氣，氣變而有形，形變而有生，今又變而之死，是相與為春秋冬夏四時行也。人且偃然寢於巨室，而我噭噭然隨而哭之，自以為不通乎命，故止也。」

**EN:** When Zhuangzi's wife died, Huizi came to mourn her. Zhuangzi was sitting with his legs sprawled out, drumming on a tub and singing. Huizi said: "She lived with you, raised your children, grew old, and died. Not to weep would be enough — but to drum on a tub and sing! Is that not too much!" Zhuangzi said: "Not so. When she first died, how could I alone not feel the pang! But I looked back to her beginning: before she was born, there was no life; and not only no life, but no form; and not only no form, but no breath. Mingled in the vague and dim, a change came and there was breath; the breath changed and there was form; the form changed and there was life; now another change has come and she is dead. This is to join in the procession of the four seasons — spring and autumn, winter and summer. She lies at rest, sleeping in the great house; and if I had gone on wailing and howling after her, I judged it would show I had not penetrated destiny. So I stopped."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 支離叔與滑介叔觀於冥伯之丘，崑崙之虛，黃帝之所休。俄而柳生其左肘，其意蹶蹶然惡之。支離叔曰：「子惡之乎？」滑介叔曰：「亡。予何惡？生者，假借也；假之而生生者，塵垢也。死生為晝夜。且吾與子觀化而化及我，我又何惡焉？」

**EN:** Uncle Splay-limb (支離叔) and Uncle Slip-joint (滑介叔) were contemplating on the hill of Dark Earl, in the waste of Kunlun, where the Yellow Emperor once rested. All at once a willow sprouted from Uncle Slip-joint's left elbow. [uncertain: 柳 — most commentators read it as a loan for 癭 ('tumor, goiter'); the parable works either way, and the received character is translated here.] He looked startled, as though he hated it. Uncle Splay-limb said: "Do you hate it?" Uncle Slip-joint said: "No — why should I hate it? Life is a borrowing; and what is borrowed to bring forth life is dust and grime. Death and life are day and night. You and I came out to contemplate the transformation — and now the transformation has reached me. Why should I hate it!"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 莊子之楚，見空髑髏，髐然有形，撽以馬捶，因而問之曰：「夫子貪生失理，而為此乎？將子有亡國之事，斧鉞之誅，而為此乎？將子有不善之行，愧遺父母妻子之醜，而為此乎？將子有凍餒之患，而為此乎？將子之春秋故及此乎？」於是語卒，援髑髏枕而臥。

**EN:** On his way to Chu, Zhuangzi saw an empty skull, its shape bleached and bare. He poked it with his riding-whip and then asked it: "Did you, sir, covet life and lose all reason, that you came to this? Did you meet with a ruined state and the executioner's axe, that you came to this? Was it some shameful conduct, disgrace you were ashamed to leave to father and mother, wife and children, that you came to this? Was it the misery of cold and hunger, that you came to this? Or was it simply that your allotted springs and autumns brought you to this?" Having said his piece, he pulled the skull over, pillowed his head on it, and slept.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 夜半，髑髏見夢曰：「子之談者似辯士。視子所言，皆生人之累也，死則無此矣。子欲聞死之說乎？」莊子曰：「然。」髑髏曰：「死，無君於上，無臣於下，亦無四時之事，從然以天地為春秋，雖南面王樂，不能過也。」莊子不信，曰：「吾使司命復生子形，為子骨肉肌膚，反子父母妻子、閭里、知識，子欲之乎？」髑髏深矉蹙頞曰：「吾安能棄南面王樂而復為人間之勞乎？」

**EN:** At midnight the skull appeared to him in a dream: "Your talk sounds like a disputing scholar's. All that you spoke of is the burden of living men; in death there is none of it. Would you like to hear the account of death?" Zhuangzi said: "Yes." The skull said: "In death there is no lord above, no subject below; there are not even the tasks of the four seasons. Untroubled, we count heaven and earth as our springs and autumns. The joy of a king facing south could not surpass it." Zhuangzi did not believe him: "Suppose I had the Master of Fate give you back your form, make you bones and flesh and skin, return you to your father and mother, wife and children, village and acquaintances — would you want that?" The skull knit its brows deep and wrinkled the bridge of its nose: "How could I cast aside the joy of a king facing south to take up the toil of the human world again!"

## Section 9

**ZH:** 顏淵東之齊，孔子有憂色。子貢下席而問曰：「小子敢問：回東之齊，夫子有憂色，何邪？」孔子曰：「善哉汝問！昔者管子有言，丘甚善之，曰：『褚小者不可以懷大，綆短者不可以汲深。』夫若是者，以為命有所成而形有所適也，夫不可損益。吾恐回與齊侯言堯、舜、黃帝之道，而重以燧人、神農之言。彼將內求於己而不得，不得則惑，人惑則死。且女獨不聞邪？昔者海鳥止於魯郊，魯侯御而觴之於廟，奏九韶以為樂，具太牢以為善。鳥乃眩視憂悲，不敢食一臠，不敢飲一杯，三日而死。此以己養養鳥也，非以鳥養養鳥也。夫以鳥養養鳥者，宜栖之深林，遊之壇陸，浮之江湖，食之鰍鰷，隨行列而止，委蛇而處。彼唯人言之惡聞，奚以夫譊譊為乎！咸池、九韶之樂，張之洞庭之野，鳥聞之而飛，獸聞之而走，魚聞之而下入，人卒聞之，相與還而觀之。魚處水而生，人處水而死，故必相與異，其好惡故異也。故先聖不一其能，不同其事。名止於實，義設於適，是之謂條達而福持。」

**EN:** Yan Yuan was going east to Qi. Confucius wore a worried look. Zigong stepped down from his mat and asked: "Your pupil ventures to ask: Hui is going east to Qi, and you, Master, wear a worried look. Why?" Confucius said: "A good question! Of old Guanzi had a saying that I much approve: 'A small bag cannot hold what is large; a short well-rope cannot draw from the deep.' It is so because destiny has its completion and the form has its fitting, which can be neither lessened nor increased. I fear that Hui will discourse to the marquis of Qi on the ways of Yao, Shun, and the Yellow Emperor, and pile on top of them the words of Suiren and Shennong. The marquis will seek within himself and not find them; not finding them, he will be bewildered; and when a man is bewildered by such words, the speaker dies. Have you alone not heard? Of old a seabird alighted in the outskirts of Lu. The marquis of Lu received it and feasted it in the temple; the Nine Shao was played for its delight; the grand sacrifice of ox, sheep, and pig was set out as its fare. The bird's eyes swam with sorrow and fear; it dared not eat one slice, dared not drink one cup, and in three days it died. This is nourishing a bird as one would nourish oneself — not nourishing a bird as a bird should be nourished. To nourish a bird as a bird should be nourished, one should roost it in the deep forest, let it roam the banks and islets, float it on rivers and lakes, feed it loaches and minnows, let it settle in the ranks of its kind, and dwell at its ease. That bird hated nothing so much as the sounds of men — what was the use of all that hubbub! Play the music of Xianchi and Nine Shao in the wilds of Dongting: birds hearing it fly away; beasts hearing it run off; fish hearing it dive below; but men hearing it gather round to gaze. Fish dwell in water and live; men dwell in water and die. They are bound to differ — therefore their likes and dislikes differ too. So the former sages did not make abilities one, did not make undertakings the same. The name stopped at the substance; rightness was set up for the fitting. This is called being smoothly through, and keeping hold of fortune."

## Section 10

**ZH:** 列子行食於道，從見百歲髑髏，攓蓬而指之曰：「唯予與汝知而未嘗死，未嘗生也。若果養乎？予果歡乎？」

**EN:** Liezi was traveling and stopped to eat by the road, where he saw a hundred-year-old skull. Parting the weeds, he pointed at it and said: "Only you and I know that you have never died and never lived. Are you really at peace? Am I really happy?"

## Section 11

**ZH:** 種有幾，得水則為㡭，得水土之際則為蛙蠙之衣，生於陵屯則為陵舄，陵舄得鬱棲則為烏足，烏足之根為蠐螬，其葉為蝴蝶。胡蝶，胥也化而為蟲，生於灶下，其狀若脫，其名為鴝掇。鴝掇千日為鳥，其名曰乾餘骨。乾餘骨之沬為斯彌，斯彌為食醯。頤輅生乎食醯，黃軦生乎九猷，瞀芮生乎腐蠸。羊奚比乎不筍，久竹生青寧，青寧生程，程生馬，馬生人，人又反入於機。萬物皆出於機，皆入於機。

**EN:** The seeds have their subtle springs. Meeting water, they become the thread-weed; meeting the margin of water and land, they become the frog-and-oyster's robe; growing on the banks and hills, they become the plantain; the plantain, getting a heap of rot, becomes the crow's-foot; the crow's-foot's root becomes the grub; its leaves become butterflies. The butterfly — quick! — transforms into an insect, born beneath the stove, shaped as though freshly shed, its name the magpie-plucker. After a thousand days the magpie-plucker becomes a bird; its name is Dried Spare Bones. The spittle of Dried Spare Bones becomes the misty Simi; the Simi becomes the vinegar-midge. The Yilu is born of the vinegar-midge; the Yellow Kuang is born of the Nine You; the Dizzy Gnat is born of the rotten Firefly. The Sheep's-Delight couples with the bamboo that never shoots; the old bamboo gives birth to the Green Repose; the Green Repose gives birth to the leopard; the leopard gives birth to the horse; the horse gives birth to man; and man returns again into the subtle springs. The ten thousand things all come forth from the subtle springs; all go back into the subtle springs. [uncertain: the transformation chain in this section uses many names attested nowhere else; I have rendered them descriptively or by sound-gloss where the tradition offers one (程 as 'leopard' is the common reading). The passage functions as a litany of unbroken transformation rather than as natural history, and no rendering can recover its lost particulars.]
