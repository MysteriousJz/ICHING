# The Way of Heaven (天道)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ The Way of Heaven - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (13 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 天道運而無所積，故萬物成；帝道運而無所積，故天下歸；聖道運而無所積，故海內服。明於天，通於聖，六通四辟於帝王之德者，其自為也，昧然無不靜者矣。聖人之靜也，非曰靜也善，故靜也，萬物無足以鐃心者，故靜也。水靜則明燭鬚眉，平中準，大匠取法焉。水靜猶明，而況精神！聖人之心靜乎，天地之鑑也，萬物之鏡也。夫虛靜恬淡，寂漠無為者，天地之平而道德之至，故帝王聖人休焉。休則虛，虛則實，實者倫矣。虛則靜，靜則動，動則得矣。靜則無為，無為也，則任事者責矣。無為則俞俞，俞俞者憂患不能處，年壽長矣。夫虛靜恬淡，寂寞無為者，萬物之本也。明此以南鄉，堯之為君也；明此以北面，舜之為臣也。以此處上，帝王天子之德也；以此處下，玄聖素王之道也。以此退居而閒游，江海山林之士服；以此進為而撫世，則功大名顯而天下一也。靜而聖，動而王，無為也而尊，樸素而天下莫能與之爭美。夫明白於天地之德者，此之謂大本大宗，與天和者也；所以均調天下，與人和者也。與人和者，謂之人樂；與天和者，謂之天樂。
**PY:** tiān dào yùn ér wú suǒ jī， gù wàn wù chéng； dì dào yùn ér wú suǒ jī， gù tiān xià guī； shèng dào yùn ér wú suǒ jī， gù hǎi nèi fú。 míng yú tiān， tōng yú shèng， liù tōng sì pì yú dì wáng zhī dé zhě， qí zì wèi yě， mèi rán wú bù jìng zhě yǐ。 shèng rén zhī jìng yě， fēi yuē jìng yě shàn， gù jìng yě， wàn wù wú zú yǐ náo xīn zhě， gù jìng yě。 shuǐ jìng zé míng zhú xū méi， píng zhōng zhǔn， dà jiàng qǔ fǎ yān。 shuǐ jìng yóu míng， ér kuàng jīng shén！ shèng rén zhī xīn jìng hū， tiān dì zhī jiàn yě， wàn wù zhī jìng yě。 fū xū jìng tián dàn， jì mò wú wèi zhě， tiān dì zhī píng ér dào dé zhī zhì， gù dì wáng shèng rén xiū yān。 xiū zé xū， xū zé shí， shí zhě lún yǐ。 xū zé jìng， jìng zé dòng， dòng zé dé yǐ。 jìng zé wú wèi， wú wèi yě， zé rèn shì zhě zé yǐ。 wú wèi zé yú yú， yú yú zhě yōu huàn bù néng chù， nián shòu zhǎng yǐ。 fū xū jìng tián dàn， jì mò wú wèi zhě， wàn wù zhī běn yě。 míng cǐ yǐ nán xiāng， yáo zhī wèi jūn yě； míng cǐ yǐ běi miàn， shùn zhī wèi chén yě。 yǐ cǐ chù shàng， dì wáng tiān zi zhī dé yě； yǐ cǐ chù xià， xuán shèng sù wáng zhī dào yě。 yǐ cǐ tuì jū ér xián yóu， jiāng hǎi shān lín zhī shì fú； yǐ cǐ jìn wèi ér fǔ shì， zé gōng dà míng xiǎn ér tiān xià yī yě。 jìng ér shèng， dòng ér wáng， wú wèi yě ér zūn， pǔ sù ér tiān xià mò néng yǔ zhī zhēng měi。 fū míng bái yú tiān dì zhī dé zhě， cǐ zhī wèi dà běn dà zōng， yǔ tiān hé zhě yě； suǒ yǐ jūn diào tiān xià， yǔ rén hé zhě yě。 yǔ rén hé zhě， wèi zhī rén lè； yǔ tiān hé zhě， wèi zhī tiān lè。

**EN:** The Way of Heaven moves without accumulating anywhere — therefore the ten thousand things are completed. The Way of emperors moves without accumulating anywhere — therefore the world comes home to them. The Way of sages moves without accumulating anywhere — therefore all within the seas submit. To be clear about Heaven, to penetrate the sage, to have the virtue of emperors and kings open six ways and open four ways — such a one, left to himself, is dim and dark, and nothing in him is not still. The sage is still — not because he says stillness is good and therefore is still, but because none of the ten thousand things can disturb his heart — therefore he is still. Water at rest is clear enough to light up beard and brows; its level answers the water-level, and great craftsmen take their rule from it. If resting water is so bright, how much more the spirit! The heart of the sage at rest is the mirror of heaven and earth, the glass of the ten thousand things. Emptiness, stillness, calm placidity, silent indifference, non-action — these are the level of heaven and earth and the very arrival of the Way and its virtue; therefore emperors, kings, and sages find their rest in them. At rest, one is empty; empty, one is full; and fullness is the ordering. Empty, one is still; still, one moves; and in moving, one attains. Still, one is without action; without action, those who bear affairs fulfill their charge. Without action, one is at ease; and at ease, trouble and anxiety find no place to dwell, and the years of life grow long. Emptiness, stillness, calm placidity, silent indifference, non-action — these are the root of the ten thousand things. Clear about this and facing south — such was Yao as lord. Clear about this and facing north — such was Shun as minister. With this, to stand above is the virtue of emperors, kings, and Sons of Heaven; with this, to stand below is the way of the dark sage and the uncrowned king. With this, to retire and roam at ease — the scholars of river, sea, mountain, and forest submit; with this, to come forward and soothe the age — one's work is great, one's name illustrious, and the world is one. In stillness a sage; in motion a king; without action and so exalted; plain and simple, so that none in the world can vie with him in beauty. To be clear about the virtue of heaven and earth — this is called the great root and great source, and one harmonizes with Heaven; by it the world is evened and tuned, and one harmonizes with men. Harmonizing with men is called the joy of man; harmonizing with Heaven is called the joy of Heaven.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 莊子曰：「吾師乎！吾師乎！虀萬物而不為戾，澤及萬世而不為仁，長於上古而不為壽，覆載天地、刻雕眾形而不為朽，此之謂天樂。故曰：知天樂者，其生也天行，其死也物化；靜而與陰同德，動而與陽同波。故知天樂者，無天怨，無人非，無物累，無鬼責。故曰：其動也天，其靜也地，一心定而王天下；其鬼不祟，其魂不疲，一心定而萬物服。言以虛靜推於天地，通於萬物，此之謂天樂。天樂者，聖人之心，以蓄天下也。」
**PY:** zhuāng zi yuē：「 wú shī hū！ wú shī hū！ jī wàn wù ér bù wèi lì， zé jí wàn shì ér bù wèi rén， zhǎng yú shàng gǔ ér bù wèi shòu， fù zài tiān dì、 kè diāo zhòng xíng ér bù wèi xiǔ， cǐ zhī wèi tiān lè。 gù yuē： zhī tiān lè zhě， qí shēng yě tiān xíng， qí sǐ yě wù huà； jìng ér yǔ yīn tóng dé， dòng ér yǔ yáng tóng bō。 gù zhī tiān lè zhě， wú tiān yuàn， wú rén fēi， wú wù lèi， wú guǐ zé。 gù yuē： qí dòng yě tiān， qí jìng yě dì， yī xīn dìng ér wàng tiān xià； qí guǐ bù suì， qí hún bù pí， yī xīn dìng ér wàn wù fú。 yán yǐ xū jìng tuī yú tiān dì， tōng yú wàn wù， cǐ zhī wèi tiān lè。 tiān lè zhě， shèng rén zhī xīn， yǐ xù tiān xià yě。」

**EN:** Zhuangzi said: "My master! My master! He grinds the ten thousand things to dust, yet is not cruel; his blessing reaches down ten thousand ages, yet he is not humane; he is older than high antiquity, yet not long-lived; he covers and bears up heaven and earth, carves and fashions all the forms, yet he is not worn away. This is called the joy of Heaven. Hence it is said: one who knows the joy of Heaven lives the course of Heaven, and dying, is transformed with things. In stillness he shares the virtue of the yin; in motion he shares the wave of the yang. So one who knows the joy of Heaven has no complaint against Heaven, no censure from men, no entanglement with things, no reproach from ghosts. Hence it is said: his motion is Heaven, his stillness is earth; the one mind composed, he kings it over the world. His ghosts bring no hauntings; his soul knows no weariness; the one mind composed, the ten thousand things submit. This is to say: with emptiness and stillness he moves through heaven and earth and courses through the ten thousand things — this is called the joy of Heaven. The joy of Heaven is the sage's heart, by which he shepherds the world."

## Section 3

**ZH:** 夫帝王之德，以天地為宗，以道德為主，以無為為常。無為也，則用天下而有餘；有為也，則為天下用而不足。故古之人貴夫無為也。上無為也，下亦無為也，是下與上同德，下與上同德則不臣；下有為也，上亦有為也，是上與下同道，上與下同道則不主。上必無為而用天下，下必有為為天下用，此不易之道也。故古之王天下者，知雖落天地，不自慮也；辯雖彫萬物，不自說也；能雖窮海內，不自為也。天不產而萬物化，地不長而萬物育，帝王無為而天下功。故曰：莫神於天，莫富於地，莫大於帝王。故曰：帝王之德配天地。此乘天地，馳萬物，而用人群之道也。
**PY:** fū dì wáng zhī dé， yǐ tiān dì wèi zōng， yǐ dào dé wèi zhǔ， yǐ wú wèi wèi cháng。 wú wèi yě， zé yòng tiān xià ér yǒu yú； yǒu wèi yě， zé wèi tiān xià yòng ér bù zú。 gù gǔ zhī rén guì fū wú wèi yě。 shàng wú wèi yě， xià yì wú wèi yě， shì xià yǔ shàng tóng dé， xià yǔ shàng tóng dé zé bù chén； xià yǒu wèi yě， shàng yì yǒu wèi yě， shì shàng yǔ xià tóng dào， shàng yǔ xià tóng dào zé bù zhǔ。 shàng bì wú wèi ér yòng tiān xià， xià bì yǒu wèi wèi tiān xià yòng， cǐ bù yì zhī dào yě。 gù gǔ zhī wàng tiān xià zhě， zhī suī luò tiān dì， bù zì lǜ yě； biàn suī diāo wàn wù， bù zì shuō yě； néng suī qióng hǎi nèi， bù zì wèi yě。 tiān bù chǎn ér wàn wù huà， dì bù zhǎng ér wàn wù yù， dì wáng wú wèi ér tiān xià gōng。 gù yuē： mò shén yú tiān， mò fù yú dì， mò dà yú dì wáng。 gù yuē： dì wáng zhī dé pèi tiān dì。 cǐ chéng tiān dì， chí wàn wù， ér yòng rén qún zhī dào yě。

**EN:** The virtue of emperors and kings takes heaven and earth as its source, the Way and its virtue as its lord, non-action as its constant. Without action, one employs the world and has some to spare; full of action, one is employed by the world and never has enough. Therefore the ancients prized non-action. If those above are without action and those below are also without action, below and above share the same virtue — and when below and above share the same virtue, there is no ministry. If those below are full of action and those above are also full of action, above and below share the same way — and when above and below share the same way, there is no lordship. Those above must be without action and employ the world; those below must be full of action and be employed by the world. This is the unvarying way. Therefore the kings of the world in ancient times, though their knowledge netted heaven and earth, did not plan for themselves; though their disputation carved the ten thousand things, did not speak for themselves; though their abilities exhausted all within the seas, did not act for themselves. Heaven does not produce, yet the ten thousand things are transformed; earth does not rear, yet the ten thousand things are nurtured; emperors and kings do nothing, yet the world achieves its work. Hence it is said: nothing more spirit-like than Heaven, nothing richer than earth, nothing greater than emperors and kings. Hence it is said: the virtue of emperors and kings is a match for heaven and earth. This is the way to ride heaven and earth, gallop the ten thousand things, and employ the human herd.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 本在於上，末在於下；要在於主，詳在於臣。三軍、五兵之運，德之末也；賞罰利害，五刑之辟，教之末也；禮法度數，形名比詳，治之末也；鐘鼓之音，羽毛之容，樂之末也；哭泣衰絰，隆殺之服，哀之末也。此五末者，須精神之運，心術之動，然後從之者也。末學者，古人有之，而非所以先也。
**PY:** běn zài yú shàng， mò zài yú xià； yào zài yú zhǔ， xiáng zài yú chén。 sān jūn、 wǔ bīng zhī yùn， dé zhī mò yě； shǎng fá lì hài， wǔ xíng zhī pì， jiào zhī mò yě； lǐ fǎ dù shù， xíng míng bǐ xiáng， zhì zhī mò yě； zhōng gǔ zhī yīn， yǔ máo zhī róng， lè zhī mò yě； kū qì shuāi dié， lóng shā zhī fú， āi zhī mò yě。 cǐ wǔ mò zhě， xū jīng shén zhī yùn， xīn shù zhī dòng， rán hòu cóng zhī zhě yě。 mò xué zhě， gǔ rén yǒu zhī， ér fēi suǒ yǐ xiān yě。

**EN:** The root is above; the branches are below. The gist belongs to the lord; the details belong to the minister. The use of the three armies and the five weapons is the branch-end of virtue. Rewards and punishments, benefit and harm, the penalties of the five punishments are the branch-end of instruction. Rituals, laws, measures, and numbers; forms and names compared in detail — these are the branch-end of government. The sounds of bells and drums, the bearings of feathered dancers — these are the branch-end of music. Weeping and wailing, mourning garments of graded heaviness — these are the branch-end of grief. These five branch-ends must wait upon the motion of the spirit, the stirring of the heart's art — only then can they follow. Branch-end learning existed among the ancients, but it is not what they put first.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 君先而臣從，父先而子從，兄先而弟從，長先而少從，男先而女從，夫先而婦從。夫尊卑先後，天地之行也，故聖人取象焉。天尊地卑，神明之位也；春夏先，秋冬後，四時之序也。萬物化作，萌區有狀，盛衰之殺，變化之流也。夫天地至神，而有尊卑先後之序，而況人道乎！宗廟尚親，朝廷尚尊，鄉黨尚齒，行事尚賢，大道之序也。語道而非其序者，非其道也；語道而非其道者，安取道！
**PY:** jūn xiān ér chén cóng， fù xiān ér zi cóng， xiōng xiān ér dì cóng， zhǎng xiān ér shǎo cóng， nán xiān ér nǚ cóng， fū xiān ér fù cóng。 fū zūn bēi xiān hòu， tiān dì zhī xíng yě， gù shèng rén qǔ xiàng yān。 tiān zūn dì bēi， shén míng zhī wèi yě； chūn xià xiān， qiū dōng hòu， sì shí zhī xù yě。 wàn wù huà zuò， méng qū yǒu zhuàng， shèng shuāi zhī shā， biàn huà zhī liú yě。 fū tiān dì zhì shén， ér yǒu zūn bēi xiān hòu zhī xù， ér kuàng rén dào hū！ zōng miào shàng qīn， cháo tíng shàng zūn， xiāng dǎng shàng chǐ， xíng shì shàng xián， dà dào zhī xù yě。 yǔ dào ér fēi qí xù zhě， fēi qí dào yě； yǔ dào ér fēi qí dào zhě， ān qǔ dào！

**EN:** The lord goes first and the minister follows; the father first and the son follows; the elder brother first and the younger follows; the old first and the young follows; man first and woman follows; husband first and wife follows. This precedence of exalted and humble, earlier and later, is the course of heaven and earth — therefore the sage takes it as his model. Heaven exalted, earth humble — this is the position of the spirit-bright. Spring and summer first, autumn and winter after — this is the sequence of the four seasons. The ten thousand things transform and arise, sprouting and budding each in its own shape; the gradation of flourishing and decline — this is the current of change and transformation. Heaven and earth are the utmost in spirit-likeness, and yet there is among them this sequence of exalted and humble, earlier and later — how much more in the way of man! In the ancestral temple, kinship is honored; at court, rank; in the village, age; in undertakings, worth. This is the sequence of the great Way. To speak of the Way and deny its sequence is to deny its Way; to speak of the Way and deny its Way — where can such a one get the Way!

## Section 6

**ZH:** 是故古之明大道者，先明天而道德次之，道德已明而仁義次之，仁義已明而分守次之，分守已明而形名次之，形名已明而因任次之，因任已明而原省次之，原省已明而是非次之，是非已明而賞罰次之。賞罰已明而愚知處宜，貴賤履位，仁賢不肖襲情，必分其能，必由其名。以此事上，以此畜下，以此治物，以此修身，知謀不用，必歸其天，此之謂太平，治之至也。
**PY:** shì gù gǔ zhī míng dà dào zhě， xiān míng tiān ér dào dé cì zhī， dào dé yǐ míng ér rén yì cì zhī， rén yì yǐ míng ér fēn shǒu cì zhī， fēn shǒu yǐ míng ér xíng míng cì zhī， xíng míng yǐ míng ér yīn rèn cì zhī， yīn rèn yǐ míng ér yuán shěng cì zhī， yuán shěng yǐ míng ér shì fēi cì zhī， shì fēi yǐ míng ér shǎng fá cì zhī。 shǎng fá yǐ míng ér yú zhī chù yí， guì jiàn lǚ wèi， rén xián bù xiào xí qíng， bì fēn qí néng， bì yóu qí míng。 yǐ cǐ shì shàng， yǐ cǐ chù xià， yǐ cǐ zhì wù， yǐ cǐ xiū shēn， zhī móu bù yòng， bì guī qí tiān， cǐ zhī wèi tài píng， zhì zhī zhì yě。

**EN:** Therefore the ancients who made the great Way clear first made Heaven clear, and the Way and its virtue came next; having made the Way and its virtue clear, humaneness and rightness came next; having made these clear, stations and duties came next; then forms and names; then fitting men to their offices; then examining them against their origins; then right and wrong; and last, rewards and punishments. With rewards and punishments made clear, fool and sage each occupied what was fitting; noble and lowly each walked in his place; the humane, the worthy, and the unworthy each wore his true condition; abilities were distinguished, names were followed. With this they served those above, with this they shepherded those below, with this they ordered things, with this they cultivated themselves. Knowledge and schemes were not used; all was returned to Heaven. This is called Great Peace, the height of order.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 故《書》曰：「有形有名。」形名者，古人有之，而非所以先也。古之語大道者，五變而形名可舉，九變而賞罰可言也。驟而語形名，不知其本也；驟而語賞罰，不知其始也。倒道而言，迕道而說者，人之所治也，安能治人！驟而語形名賞罰，此有知治之具，非知治之道；可用於天下，不足以用天下。此之謂辯士，一曲之人也。禮法度數，形名比詳，古人有之，此下之所以事上，非上之所以畜下也。
**PY:** gù《 shū》 yuē：「 yǒu xíng yǒu míng。」 xíng míng zhě， gǔ rén yǒu zhī， ér fēi suǒ yǐ xiān yě。 gǔ zhī yǔ dà dào zhě， wǔ biàn ér xíng míng kě jǔ， jiǔ biàn ér shǎng fá kě yán yě。 zhòu ér yǔ xíng míng， bù zhī qí běn yě； zhòu ér yǔ shǎng fá， bù zhī qí shǐ yě。 dào dào ér yán， wù dào ér shuō zhě， rén zhī suǒ zhì yě， ān néng zhì rén！ zhòu ér yǔ xíng míng shǎng fá， cǐ yǒu zhī zhì zhī jù， fēi zhī zhì zhī dào； kě yòng yú tiān xià， bù zú yǐ yòng tiān xià。 cǐ zhī wèi biàn shì， yī qū zhī rén yě。 lǐ fǎ dù shù， xíng míng bǐ xiáng， gǔ rén yǒu zhī， cǐ xià zhī suǒ yǐ shì shàng， fēi shàng zhī suǒ yǐ chù xià yě。

**EN:** Therefore the Book says: "There are forms and there are names." Forms and names existed among the ancients, but they are not what they put first. The ancients, in speaking of the great Way, went through five shifts before forms and names could be raised, and nine shifts before rewards and punishments could be spoken of. To leap straight to forms and names is not to know their root; to leap straight to rewards and punishments is not to know their beginning. One who speaks against the Way's order, discoursing athwart the Way, is fit to be governed by others — how could he govern others! To leap straight to forms and names and rewards and punishments is to know the tools of government but not the Way of government; one may be employed in the world, but is not fit to employ the world. Such a one is called a disputing scholar, a man of one cramped corner. Rituals, laws, measures, numbers; forms and names compared in detail — these existed among the ancients as the means by which inferiors served superiors, not the means by which superiors shepherded inferiors.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 昔者舜問於堯曰：「天王之用心何如？」堯曰：「吾不敖無告，不廢窮民，苦死者，嘉孺子而哀婦人。此吾所以用心也。」舜曰：「美則美矣，而未大也。」堯曰：「然則何如？」舜曰：「天德而出寧，日月照而四時行，若晝夜之有經，雲行而雨施矣。」堯曰：「膠膠擾擾乎！子，天之合也；我，人之合也。」夫天地者，古之所大也，而黃帝、堯、舜之所共美也。故古之王天下者，奚為哉？天地而已矣。
**PY:** xī zhě shùn wèn yú yáo yuē：「 tiān wáng zhī yòng xīn hé rú？」 yáo yuē：「 wú bù áo wú gào， bù fèi qióng mín， kǔ sǐ zhě， jiā rú zi ér āi fù rén。 cǐ wú suǒ yǐ yòng xīn yě。」 shùn yuē：「 měi zé měi yǐ， ér wèi dà yě。」 yáo yuē：「 rán zé hé rú？」 shùn yuē：「 tiān dé ér chū níng， rì yuè zhào ér sì shí xíng， ruò zhòu yè zhī yǒu jīng， yún xíng ér yǔ shī yǐ。」 yáo yuē：「 jiāo jiāo rǎo rǎo hū！ zi， tiān zhī hé yě； wǒ， rén zhī hé yě。」 fū tiān dì zhě， gǔ zhī suǒ dà yě， ér huáng dì、 yáo、 shùn zhī suǒ gòng měi yě。 gù gǔ zhī wàng tiān xià zhě， xī wèi zāi？ tiān dì ér yǐ yǐ。

**EN:** Of old Shun asked Yao: "What is the use of the heart for a heaven-ordained king?" Yao said: "I do not turn proud against those with nowhere to appeal; I do not cast off the people in straits; I grieve for the dead, cherish the children, and pity the widows. This is the use of my heart." Shun said: "Fine — fine, but not yet great." Yao said: "Then what?" Shun said: "With Heaven's virtue one comes forth in tranquility, as sun and moon shine and the four seasons proceed, as constant as day and night, as the clouds move and the rain falls." Yao said: "How I have been fussing and fretting! You are the join of Heaven; I am the join of man." Heaven and earth are what the ancients held great, and what the Yellow Emperor, Yao, and Shun together found beautiful. Therefore, what did the ancient kings of the world do? Heaven and earth — that was all.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 孔子西藏書於周室，子路謀曰：「由聞周之徵藏史有老聃者，免而歸居。夫子欲藏書，則試往因焉。」孔子曰：「善。」往見老聃，而老聃不許，於是繙十二經以說。老聃中其說，曰：「大謾，願聞其要。」孔子曰：「要在仁義。」老聃曰：「請問：仁義，人之性邪？」孔子曰：「然。君子不仁則不成，不義則不生。仁義，真人之性也，又將奚為矣？」老聃曰：「請問何謂仁義？」孔子曰：「中心物愷，兼愛無私，此仁義之情也。」老聃曰：「意！幾乎後言！夫兼愛，不亦迂乎！無私焉，乃私也。夫子若欲使天下無失其牧乎？則天地固有常矣，日月固有明矣，星辰固有列矣，禽獸固有群矣，樹木固有立矣。夫子亦放德而行，循道而趨，已至矣，又何偈偈乎揭仁義，若擊鼓而求亡子焉？意！夫子亂人之性也！」
**PY:** kǒng zi xī zàng shū yú zhōu shì， zi lù móu yuē：「 yóu wén zhōu zhī zhēng cáng shǐ yǒu lǎo dān zhě， miǎn ér guī jū。 fū zǐ yù cáng shū， zé shì wǎng yīn yān。」 kǒng zi yuē：「 shàn。」 wǎng jiàn lǎo dān， ér lǎo dān bù xǔ， yú shì fān shí èr jīng yǐ shuō。 lǎo dān zhōng qí shuō， yuē：「 dà mán， yuàn wén qí yào。」 kǒng zi yuē：「 yào zài rén yì。」 lǎo dān yuē：「 qǐng wèn： rén yì， rén zhī xìng xié？」 kǒng zi yuē：「 rán。 jūn zǐ bù rén zé bù chéng， bù yì zé bù shēng。 rén yì， zhēn rén zhī xìng yě， yòu jiāng xī wèi yǐ？」 lǎo dān yuē：「 qǐng wèn hé wèi rén yì？」 kǒng zi yuē：「 zhōng xīn wù kǎi， jiān ài wú sī， cǐ rén yì zhī qíng yě。」 lǎo dān yuē：「 yì！ jǐ hū hòu yán！ fū jiān ài， bù yì yū hū！ wú sī yān， nǎi sī yě。 fū zǐ ruò yù shǐ tiān xià wú shī qí mù hū？ zé tiān dì gù yǒu cháng yǐ， rì yuè gù yǒu míng yǐ， xīng chén gù yǒu liè yǐ， qín shòu gù yǒu qún yǐ， shù mù gù yǒu lì yǐ。 fū zǐ yì fàng dé ér xíng， xún dào ér qū， yǐ zhì yǐ， yòu hé jì jì hū jiē rén yì， ruò jī gǔ ér qiú wáng zi yān？ yì！ fū zǐ luàn rén zhī xìng yě！」

**EN:** Confucius wished to store his books in the west, in the house of Zhou. Zilu (子路) counseled him: "I have heard that the keeper of archives of Zhou, one Lao Dan, has retired and gone home. If you, Master, wish to store your books, you might try going to enlist his help." Confucius said: "Good." He went to see Lao Dan, but Lao Dan would not agree; so Confucius began to explain by way of the Twelve Classics. Lao Dan cut him short: "Too verbose. I would hear the gist." Confucius said: "The gist lies in humaneness and rightness." Lao Dan said: "May I ask: are humaneness and rightness the nature of man?" Confucius said: "They are. The gentleman cannot be complete without humaneness, cannot live without rightness. Humaneness and rightness are truly the nature of man — what more could there be?" Lao Dan said: "May I ask what is meant by humaneness and rightness?" Confucius said: "At the center of the heart to delight in things, to love all without partiality — this is the genuine condition of humaneness and rightness." Lao Dan said: "Ah — how nearly you have spoken the sequel! Loving all — is that not far-fetched! And being without partiality is itself a partiality. If you, sir, wish to keep the world from losing its shepherd — then heaven and earth have their constancy already; sun and moon have their brightness already; the stars have their ranks already; birds and beasts have their flocks already; trees have their standing already. You too, sir — walk in the wake of virtue, hasten along the Way, and you will have arrived. Why strain and struggle hoisting humaneness and rightness like a man beating a drum to search for a runaway son? Ah — you, sir, are disordering the nature of man!"

## Section 10

**ZH:** 士成綺見老子而問曰：「吾聞夫子聖人也，吾固不辭遠道而來，願見，百舍重趼而不敢息。今吾觀子，非聖人也。鼠壤有餘蔬，而棄妹之者，不仁也；生熟不盡於前，而積歛無崖。」老子漠然不應。士成綺明日復見，曰：「昔者吾有刺於子，今吾心正卻矣，何故也？」老子曰：「夫巧知神聖之人，吾自以為脫焉。昔者子呼我牛也而謂之牛，呼我馬也而謂之馬。苟有其實，人與之名而弗受，再受其殃。吾服也恒服，吾非以服有服。」士成綺雁行避影，履行，遂進而問：「修身若何？」老子曰：「而容崖然，而目衝然，而顙頯然，而口闞然，而狀義然，似繫馬而止也。動而持，發也機，察而審，知巧而睹於泰，凡以為不信。邊竟有人焉，其名為竊。」
**PY:** shì chéng qǐ jiàn lǎo zi ér wèn yuē：「 wú wén fū zǐ shèng rén yě， wú gù bù cí yuǎn dào ér lái， yuàn jiàn， bǎi shè chóng jiǎn ér bù gǎn xī。 jīn wú guān zi， fēi shèng rén yě。 shǔ rǎng yǒu yú shū， ér qì mèi zhī zhě， bù rén yě； shēng shú bù jǐn yú qián， ér jī hān wú yá。」 lǎo zi mò rán bù yīng。 shì chéng qǐ míng rì fù jiàn， yuē：「 xī zhě wú yǒu cì yú zi， jīn wú xīn zhèng què yǐ， hé gù yě？」 lǎo zi yuē：「 fū qiǎo zhī shén shèng zhī rén， wú zì yǐ wèi tuō yān。 xī zhě zi hū wǒ niú yě ér wèi zhī niú， hū wǒ mǎ yě ér wèi zhī mǎ。 gǒu yǒu qí shí， rén yǔ zhī míng ér fú shòu， zài shòu qí yāng。 wú fú yě héng fú， wú fēi yǐ fú yǒu fú。」 shì chéng qǐ yàn háng bì yǐng， lǚ xíng， suì jìn ér wèn：「 xiū shēn ruò hé？」 lǎo zi yuē：「 ér róng yá rán， ér mù chōng rán， ér sǎng kuí rán， ér kǒu kàn rán， ér zhuàng yì rán， shì xì mǎ ér zhǐ yě。 dòng ér chí， fā yě jī， chá ér shěn， zhī qiǎo ér dǔ yú tài， fán yǐ wèi bù xìn。 biān jìng yǒu rén yān， qí míng wèi qiè。」

**EN:** Shi Chengqi (士成綺) saw Laozi and said to him: "I heard that you, Master, were a sage. I did not shrink from the long road to come — a hundred stages, with layer upon layer of callus, not daring to rest — hoping to see you. Now that I look at you, you are no sage. There is leftover grain in the rat-holes, and to cast it aside like a younger sister cast off [uncertain: 棄妹之者 — the phrase is obscure; one line reads "casting aside food like abandoning one's kin," another emends 妹 to 昧, 'to hide away grain ignorantly.'] is not humane. You do not finish the raw and cooked food before you, yet hoard and gather without limit." Laozi, indifferent, did not answer. The next day Shi Chengqi came again and said: "Yesterday I spoke sharply to you. Today my heart is set right and has fallen back. Why is that?" Laozi said: "The man of clever knowledge and spirit-like sagacity — I consider myself to have sloughed him off. Yesterday you called me an ox and I answered to ox; you called me a horse and I answered to horse. If I had the substance, and men gave me the name and I refused it, I would only invite the misfortune twice. My compliance is constant compliance; I do not comply in order to be seen complying." Shi Chengqi sidled like a goose avoiding its shadow, came forward step by step, and asked: "How is one to cultivate the person?" Laozi said: "Your bearing is aloof; your eyes stare; your brow juts; your mouth gapes; your whole look is overbearing — like a horse held on its tether. You move yet hold back; you release like a trigger; you scrutinize and judge; with clever knowledge you gaze at grandeur — all of it untrustworthy. On the border there are men of this kind. Their name is: thief."

## Section 11

**ZH:** 夫子曰：「夫道，於大不終，於小不遺，故萬物備。廣廣乎其無不容也，淵乎其不可測也。形德仁義，神之末也，非至人孰能定之！夫至人有世，不亦大乎！而不足以為之累。天下奮柄而不與之偕，審乎無假而不與利遷，極物之真，能守其本，故外天地，遺萬物，而神未嘗有所困也。通乎道，合乎德，退仁義，賓禮樂，至人之心有所定矣。」
**PY:** fū zǐ yuē：「 fū dào， yú dà bù zhōng， yú xiǎo bù yí， gù wàn wù bèi。 guǎng guǎng hū qí wú bù róng yě， yuān hū qí bù kě cè yě。 xíng dé rén yì， shén zhī mò yě， fēi zhì rén shú néng dìng zhī！ fū zhì rén yǒu shì， bù yì dà hū！ ér bù zú yǐ wèi zhī lèi。 tiān xià fèn bǐng ér bù yǔ zhī xié， shěn hū wú jiǎ ér bù yǔ lì qiān， jí wù zhī zhēn， néng shǒu qí běn， gù wài tiān dì， yí wàn wù， ér shén wèi cháng yǒu suǒ kùn yě。 tōng hū dào， hé hū dé， tuì rén yì， bīn lǐ lè， zhì rén zhī xīn yǒu suǒ dìng yǐ。」

**EN:** The Master said: "The Way — toward the great it does not terminate; toward the small it leaves nothing out; therefore the ten thousand things are furnished in it. How broad, that there is nothing it does not hold! How deep, that it cannot be fathomed! Forms, virtue, humaneness, rightness — these are the branch-ends of the spirit; who but the utmost man could settle them! The utmost man possesses the world — is that not great! — yet it does not suffice to burden him. The world seizes the handles of power and he does not go along. Discerning the flawless, he does not shift with profit. He reaches to the truth of things and can hold fast to the root. Therefore he sets heaven and earth outside, lets the ten thousand things go, and his spirit has never known distress. Penetrating the Way, joining with virtue, putting humaneness and rightness in retreat, sending ritual and music packing — the heart of the utmost man has something fixed."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 世之所貴道者，書也，書不過語，語有貴也。語之所貴者，意也，意有所隨。意之所隨者，不可以言傳也，而世因貴言傳書。世雖貴之，我猶不足貴也，為其貴非其貴也。故視而可見者，形與色也；聽而可聞者，名與聲也。悲夫！世人以形色名聲為足以得彼之情！夫形色名聲果不足以得彼之情，則知者不言，言者不知，而世豈識之哉！
**PY:** shì zhī suǒ guì dào zhě， shū yě， shū bù guò yǔ， yǔ yǒu guì yě。 yǔ zhī suǒ guì zhě， yì yě， yì yǒu suǒ suí。 yì zhī suǒ suí zhě， bù kě yǐ yán chuán yě， ér shì yīn guì yán chuán shū。 shì suī guì zhī， wǒ yóu bù zú guì yě， wèi qí guì fēi qí guì yě。 gù shì ér kě jiàn zhě， xíng yǔ sè yě； tīng ér kě wén zhě， míng yǔ shēng yě。 bēi fū！ shì rén yǐ xíng sè míng shēng wèi zú yǐ dé bǐ zhī qíng！ fū xíng sè míng shēng guǒ bù zú yǐ dé bǐ zhī qíng， zé zhī zhě bù yán， yán zhě bù zhī， ér shì qǐ shí zhī zāi！

**EN:** What the world prizes as the Way is books; but books are nothing beyond words, and words have something they prize. What words prize is meaning; and meaning has something it follows. What meaning follows cannot be handed down in words — yet the world, prizing words, hands down books. The world prizes them; I still do not count them worthy of prizing, for what it prizes is not what is to be prized. What can be seen by looking is form and color; what can be heard by listening is names and sounds. Alas — the men of the world take form, color, name, and sound as sufficient to attain its true condition! If form, color, name, and sound really are not sufficient to attain its true condition, then those who know do not speak, and those who speak do not know — and how would the world ever discern this!

## Section 13

**ZH:** 桓公讀書於堂上，輪扁斲輪於堂下，釋椎鑿而上，問桓公曰：「敢問公之所讀者何言邪？」公曰：「聖人之言也。」曰：「聖人在乎？」公曰：「已死矣。」曰：「然則君之所讀者，古人之糟魄已夫！」桓公曰：「寡人讀書，輪人安得議乎！有說則可，無說則死。」輪扁曰：「臣也，以臣之事觀之。斲輪，徐則甘而不固，疾則苦而不入。不徐不疾，得之於手而應於心，口不能言，有數存焉於其間。臣不能以喻臣之子，臣之子亦不能受之於臣，是以行年七十而老斲輪。古之人與其不可傳也死矣，然則君之所讀者，古人之糟魄已夫。」
**PY:** huán gōng dú shū yú táng shàng， lún biǎn zhuó lún yú táng xià， shì chuí záo ér shàng， wèn huán gōng yuē：「 gǎn wèn gōng zhī suǒ dú zhě hé yán xié？」 gōng yuē：「 shèng rén zhī yán yě。」 yuē：「 shèng rén zài hū？」 gōng yuē：「 yǐ sǐ yǐ。」 yuē：「 rán zé jūn zhī suǒ dú zhě， gǔ rén zhī zāo pò yǐ fū！」 huán gōng yuē：「 guǎ rén dú shū， lún rén ān dé yì hū！ yǒu shuō zé kě， wú shuō zé sǐ。」 lún biǎn yuē：「 chén yě， yǐ chén zhī shì guān zhī。 zhuó lún， xú zé gān ér bù gù， jí zé kǔ ér bù rù。 bù xú bù jí， dé zhī yú shǒu ér yīng yú xīn， kǒu bù néng yán， yǒu shù cún yān yú qí jiān。 chén bù néng yǐ yù chén zhī zi， chén zhī zi yì bù néng shòu zhī yú chén， shì yǐ xíng nián qī shí ér lǎo zhuó lún。 gǔ zhī rén yǔ qí bù kě chuán yě sǐ yǐ， rán zé jūn zhī suǒ dú zhě， gǔ rén zhī zāo pò yǐ fū。」

**EN:** Duke Huan (桓公) was reading a book in the upper hall. Wheelwright Bian (輪扁) was cutting a wheel in the lower hall. Setting down his mallet and chisel, he came up and asked Duke Huan: "May I ask what words my lord is reading?" The duke said: "The words of the sages." "Are the sages still alive?" The duke said: "They are long dead." "Then what my lord is reading is nothing but the dregs and lees of the ancients!" Duke Huan said: "When I read a book, how dare a wheel-maker pass judgment! If you have an explanation, well; if not — death." Wheelwright Bian said: "Your servant looks at it through his own trade. In cutting a wheel: too slack and it slides sweetly but will not hold; too tight and it jams and will not enter. Neither slack nor tight — it is gotten in the hand and answered in the heart; the mouth cannot speak it, yet there is a knack in it there. I cannot convey it to my son, and my son cannot receive it from me; therefore, at seventy years, I am old and still cutting wheels. The men of old, together with what they could not hand down, are dead. So what my lord is reading is nothing but the dregs and lees of the ancients!"

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 天道 → "the Way of Heaven"; 天樂 → "the joy of Heaven"; 無為 → "non-action"; 至人 → "the utmost man"; 聖人 → "the sage"; 道德 → "the Way and its virtue"; 性 → "the nature"; 情 → "genuine condition"; 玄聖素王 → "the dark sage and the uncrowned king."
- §§5–7 argue for a hierarchy of precedence that many readers find discordant with the rest of the book (they are often classed as later insertions); they are translated as transmitted, without harmonizing.
- `[uncertain]` flags: 1 (§10 棄妹之者).
- §13 (Wheelwright Bian) is the classic statement of the book's teaching on words and transmission; "dregs and lees" (糟魄, with 魄 read as 粕) follows the received text.
