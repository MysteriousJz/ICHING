# Tian Zi-fang (田子方)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Outer Chapters _ Tian Zi-fang - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (22 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 田子方侍坐於魏文侯，數稱谿工。文侯曰：「谿工，子之師邪？」子方曰：「非也。無擇之里人也，稱道數當，故無擇稱之。」文侯曰：「然則子無師邪？」子方曰：「有。」曰：「子之師誰邪？」子方曰：「東郭順子。」文侯曰：「然則夫子何故未嘗稱之？」子方曰：「其為人也真，人貌而天虛，緣而葆真，清而容物。物無道，正容以悟之，使人之意也消。無擇何足以稱之！」

**EN:** Tian Zifang, in attendance seated beside Marquis Wen of Wei, repeatedly praised Xigong (谿工). Marquis Wen said: "Is Xigong your teacher?" Zifang said: "No. He is a neighbor of my ward; his discourse on the Way often hits the mark, so I praise him." Marquis Wen said: "Then have you no teacher?" Zifang said: "I have." "Who is your teacher?" Zifang said: "Shunzi (順子) of Dongguo (東郭)." Marquis Wen said: "Then why have you never praised him?" Zifang said: "As a man he is true: a human face and Heaven's emptiness; following along, he keeps his truth; clear, he makes room for things. If a thing is without the Way, he sets his countenance right and awakens it, so that the other's conceit melts away. How could the likes of me be fit to praise him!"

## Section 2

**ZH:** 子方出，文侯儻然終日不言，召前立臣，而語之曰：「遠矣全德之君子！始吾以聖知之言、仁義之行為至矣，吾聞子方之師，吾形解而不欲動，口鉗而不欲言。吾所學者直土梗耳，夫魏真為我累耳！」

**EN:** When Zifang had gone out, Marquis Wen sat dazed the whole day without speaking. He called the attending ministers before him and said to them: "How far away is the gentleman of whole virtue! Until now I took the words of sages and wise men, the conduct of humaneness and rightness, for the utmost. Having heard of Zifang's teacher, my frame is loosened and I do not wish to move; my mouth is clamped and I do not wish to speak. What I have learned is nothing but clay dolls. And Wei — truly, Wei is my burden!"

## Section 3

**ZH:** 溫伯雪子適齊，舍於魯。魯人有請見之者，溫伯雪子曰：「不可。吾聞中國之君子，明乎禮義而陋於知人心，吾不欲見也。」至於齊，反舍於魯，是人也又請見。溫伯雪子曰：「往也蘄見我，今也又蘄見我，是必有以振我也。」出而見客，入而歎。明日見客，又入而歎。其僕曰：「每見之客也，必入而歎，何邪？」曰：「吾固告子矣：『中國之民，明乎禮義而陋乎知人心。』昔之見我者，進退一成規，一成矩；從容一若龍，一若虎；其諫我也似子，其道我也似父。是以歎也。」

**EN:** Wen Boxue (溫伯雪子), on his way to Qi, lodged in Lu. A man of Lu begged to see him. Wen Boxue said: "No. I have heard that the gentlemen of the Middle Kingdom are clear about ritual and rightness but crude in knowing the hearts of men. I do not wish to see him." Having been to Qi, he lodged again in Lu on his return, and the same man begged to see him. Wen Boxue said: "Before, he longed to see me; now again he longs to see me. He must have something with which to rouse me." He went out and saw the guest; coming back in, he sighed. The next day he saw him again, and again came back in and sighed. His servant said: "Each time you see this guest, you come back in and sigh. Why?" He said: "I told you before: 'The people of the Middle Kingdom are clear about ritual and rightness but crude in knowing the hearts of men.' The one who came to see me — advancing and withdrawing he was a perfect compass, a perfect square; at ease he was now a dragon, now a tiger; he remonstrated with me like a son; he guided me like a father. That is why I sigh."

## Section 4

**ZH:** 仲尼見之而不言。子路曰：「吾子欲見溫伯雪子久矣，見之而不言，何邪？」仲尼曰：「若夫人者，目擊而道存矣，亦不可以容聲矣。」

**EN:** Confucius saw him and said nothing. Zilu said: "You have long wished to see Wen Boxue, Master; having seen him, you say nothing. Why?" Confucius said: "With such a man as this, the eyes strike and the Way is present — there is no room even for a voice."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 顏淵問於仲尼曰：「夫子步亦步，夫子趨亦趨，夫子馳亦馳，夫子奔逸絕塵，而回瞠若乎後矣。」夫子曰：「回，何謂邪？」曰：「夫子步亦步也，夫子言亦言也，夫子趨亦趨也，夫子辯亦辯也，夫子馳亦馳也，夫子言道，回亦言道也。及奔逸絕塵，而回瞠若乎後者，夫子不言而信，不比而周，無器而民滔乎前，而不知所以然而已矣。」

**EN:** Yan Yuan asked Confucius: "When you, Master, walk, I walk too; when you hasten, I hasten too; when you gallop, I gallop too; but when you race beyond the dust and leave it behind, I can only stare after you from behind." The Master said: "Hui, what do you mean?" He said: "When you walk, I walk too — that is, when you speak, I speak too. When you hasten, I hasten too — that is, when you dispute, I dispute too. When you gallop, I gallop too — that is, when you discourse on the Way, I discourse on the Way too. But when you race beyond the dust and leave it behind, and I can only stare after you from behind — that is: you are trusted without speaking; you are all-embracing without taking sides; you have no office, yet the people surge forward before you — and I do not know how it is so."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 仲尼曰：「惡！可不察與！夫哀莫大於心死，而人死亦次之。日出東方而入於西極，萬物莫不比方。有目有趾者，待是而後成功，待晝而作。是出則存，是入則亡。萬物亦然，有待也而死，有待也而生。吾一受其成形，而不化以待盡，效物而動，日夜無隙，而不知其所終，薰然其成形，知命不能規乎其前，丘以是日徂。吾終身與汝交一臂而失之，可不哀與！女殆著乎吾所以著也。彼已盡矣，而女求之以為有，是求馬於唐肆也。吾服女也甚忘，女服吾也亦甚忘。雖然，女奚患焉！雖忘乎故吾，吾有不忘者存。」

**EN:** Confucius said: "Ah — can you fail to examine it! No grief is greater than the death of the heart; the death of the person comes only second. The sun comes out of the east and sinks at the western extreme; none of the ten thousand things fails to take its direction from it. Those that have eyes and feet wait upon it and then accomplish their work; they wait upon daylight to act. When it comes out, they live; when it goes in, they perish. The ten thousand things are also so: there is that on which they wait to die; there is that on which they wait to be born. Once I received this completed form, I do not transform but wait out my end. I move in imitation of things, day and night without a seam, not knowing where it will end — gently and steadily this form came to be, and knowledge of destiny cannot measure out what lies before it. In this I go onward day by day. All my life I have crossed arms with you and lost you in the crossing — can I not grieve! What you see of me is surely only what can be seen of me. That has already perished utterly, and you go seeking it as though it were still there — it is like seeking a horse in an empty market. What I have given you service in, you forget deeply; what you have given me service in, I also forget deeply. Still, why should you be distressed! Though you forget the me that was, there is something of me that cannot be forgotten."

## Section 7

**ZH:** 孔子見老聃，老聃新沐，方將被髮而乾，慹然似非人。孔子便而待之，少焉見曰：「丘也眩與？其信然與？向者先生形體掘若槁木，似遺物離人而立於獨也。」老聃曰：「吾遊心於物之初。」

**EN:** Confucius went to see Lao Dan. Lao Dan had just washed his head and was letting his hair hang loose to dry. He sat rapt and still, as though not a man. Confucius stood aside and waited. After a little he presented himself and said: "Am I dazzled, or was it really so? Just now your bodily frame, sir, stood stark as a withered tree, as though you had left things behind and parted from men, standing in the Solitary." Lao Dan said: "My mind was roaming in the beginning of things."

## Section 8

**ZH:** 孔子曰：「何謂邪？」曰：「心困焉而不能知，口辟焉而不能言，嘗為汝議乎其將。至陰肅肅，至陽赫赫；肅肅出乎天，赫赫發乎地；兩者交通成和而物生焉，或為之紀而莫見其形。消息滿虛，一晦一明，日改月化，日有所為，而莫見其功。生有所乎萌，死有所乎歸，始終相反乎無端，而莫知其所窮。非是也，且孰為之宗！」

**EN:** Confucius said: "What does that mean?" He said: "The heart is blocked there and cannot know it; the mouth gapes there and cannot speak it. Let me try to tell you the gist of it. The utmost yin, cold and severe; the utmost yang, blazing and bright. Cold and severe, it comes forth from Heaven; blazing and bright, it issues from earth. The two commune and pass into harmony, and things are born from it. Something keeps the measure of it, yet none sees its form. Waning and waxing, filling and emptying; now dark, now bright; daily changing, monthly transforming; daily it does what it does, and none sees its work. Life has that from which it sprouts; death has that to which it returns. Beginning and ending follow one another without seam, and none knows its exhaustion. If it were not for this — who could be their source!"

## Section 9

**ZH:** 孔子曰：「請問遊是。」老聃曰：「夫得是，至美至樂也。得至美而遊乎至樂，謂之至人。」孔子曰：「願聞其方。」曰：「草食之獸不疾易藪，水生之蟲不疾易水，行小變而不失其大常也，喜怒哀樂不入於胸次。夫天下也者，萬物之所一也。得其所一而同焉，則四支百體將為塵垢，而死生終始將為晝夜而莫之能滑，而況得喪禍福之所介乎！棄隸者若棄泥塗，知身貴於隸也，貴在於我而不失於變。且萬化而未始有極也，夫孰足以患心！已為道者解乎此。」

**EN:** Confucius said: "May I ask about roaming in this?" Lao Dan said: "To get this is the utmost beauty, the utmost joy. To get the utmost beauty and roam in the utmost joy — such a one is called the utmost man." Confucius said: "I beg to hear the method." He said: "Grass-eating beasts are not troubled at changing their marsh; water-born insects are not troubled at changing their water. One undergoes small changes without losing the great constancy: joy and anger, sorrow and delight, do not enter the breast. This world is that in which the ten thousand things are one. Getting that in which they are one and being one with it — then the four limbs and hundred parts of the body become dust and grime, and death and life, ending and beginning, become day and night, and nothing can disturb one — how much less the tip-ends of gain and loss, misfortune and fortune! One casts off a slave's station as one casts off a clod of mud, knowing that the person is more precious than the station; the preciousness lies in oneself and is not lost in change. Besides, the ten thousand transformations have never begun to have a limit — what in them is enough to trouble the heart! One who has already made the Way understands this."

## Section 10

**ZH:** 孔子曰：「夫子德配天地，而猶假至言以修心，古之君子，孰能脫焉？」老聃曰：「不然。夫水之於汋也，無為而才自然矣。至人之於德也，不修而物不能離焉，若天之自高，地之自厚，日月之自明，夫何修焉！」

**EN:** Confucius said: "Your virtue, sir, is a match for heaven and earth, and still you borrow utmost words to cultivate the heart. Of the gentlemen of old, who could escape this?" Lao Dan said: "Not so. Water's welling forth: without doing anything, its capacity is so of itself. The utmost man's virtue: without cultivating it, things cannot part from him — like heaven being high of itself, earth being thick of itself, sun and moon being bright of themselves. What is there to cultivate!"

## Section 11

**ZH:** 孔子出，以告顏回曰：「丘之於道也，其猶醯雞與！微夫子之發吾覆也，吾不知天地之大全也。」

**EN:** Confucius came out and told Yan Hui: "Qiu, next to the Way, is like a midge in the vinegar jar! Had the master not lifted my lid, I would not have known the great wholeness of heaven and earth."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 莊子見魯哀公。哀公曰：「魯多儒士，少為先生方者。」莊子曰：「魯少儒。」哀公曰：「舉魯國而儒服，何謂少乎？」莊子曰：「周聞之：儒者冠圜冠者，知天時；履句屨者，知地形；緩佩玦者，事至而斷。君子有其道者，未必為其服也；為其服者，未必知其道也。公固以為不然，何不號於國中曰『無此道而為此服者，其罪死』？」於是哀公號之五日，而魯國無敢儒服者。獨有一丈夫儒服而立乎公門，公即召而問以國事，千轉萬變而不窮。莊子曰：「以魯國而儒者一人耳，可謂多乎？」

**EN:** Zhuangzi saw Duke Ai of Lu. Duke Ai said: "Lu is full of Ru scholars; few practice your method, sir." Zhuangzi said: "Lu has few Ru." Duke Ai said: "The whole state of Lu wears the Ru robe — how can you say few!" Zhuangzi said: "Zhou has heard that Ru who wear the round cap know the seasons of heaven; who tread the square-toed shoes know the shape of earth; who hang the ring-pendants loose at the belt decide affairs as they come. A gentleman who has the Way does not necessarily wear its robe; one who wears the robe does not necessarily know its Way. If you firmly hold otherwise, why not proclaim throughout the state: 'Whoever wears this robe without having this Way — his crime is death'?" Thereupon Duke Ai proclaimed it, and after five days no one in the state of Lu dared wear the Ru robe. One single man, in the Ru robe, stood at the duke's gate. The duke summoned him at once and questioned him on the affairs of state: through a thousand turns and ten thousand shifts, he was never exhausted. Zhuangzi said: "In all the state of Lu, one Ru. Can that be called many?"

## Section 13

**ZH:** 百里奚爵祿不入於心，故飯牛而牛肥，使秦穆公忘其賤，與之政也。有虞氏死生不入於心，故足以動人。

**EN:** Baili Xi (百里奚) never let titles and stipends enter his heart; therefore he fed cattle and the cattle grew fat, and Duke Mu of Qin forgot his lowliness and gave him the government. The house of Youyu never let death and life enter the heart; therefore it sufficed to move men.

## Section 14

**ZH:** 宋元君將畫圖。眾史皆至，受揖而立；舐筆和墨，在外者半。有一史後至者，儃儃然不趨，受揖不立，因之舍。公使人視之，則解衣般礡，臝。君曰：「可矣，是真畫者也。」

**EN:** Lord Yuan of Song (宋元君) wished to have a picture drawn. The crowd of court painters all arrived, received their bows, and stood; licking their brushes, mixing their ink — half of them still outside. One painter arrived late: unhurried, he did not hasten; he received his bow and did not stand, but went straight to his lodging. The lord sent someone to look at him — he had taken off his clothes, was sitting sprawled, naked. The lord said: "That will do. He is the true painter."

## Section 15

**ZH:** 文王觀於臧，見一丈夫釣，而其釣莫釣，非持其釣，有釣者也，常釣也。

**EN:** King Wen, observing at Zang (臧), saw an old man fishing. But his fishing was no fishing; he did not hold his fishing for the catch — there was fishing in him, a constant fishing.

## Section 16

**ZH:** 文王欲舉而授之政，而恐大臣父兄之弗安也；欲終而釋之，而不忍百姓之無天也。於是旦而屬之夫夫曰：「昔者寡人夢，見良人黑色而髯，乘駁馬而偏朱蹄，號曰：『寓而政於臧丈人，庶幾乎民有瘳乎！』」諸大夫蹴然曰：「先君王也。」文王曰：「然則卜之。」諸大夫曰：「先君之命王，其無它，又何卜焉！」

**EN:** King Wen wished to raise him and hand him the government, but feared the great ministers, his uncles and brothers, would be uneasy; he wished to let the matter drop and release him, but could not bear that the hundred clans should be without their Heaven. So at dawn he addressed his great officers: "Last night I dreamt I saw a fine man, dark of face and bearded, riding a piebald horse with one red hoof, who commanded me: 'Entrust your government to the old man of Zang — perhaps the people will be healed!'" The great officers started: "It was the former king, your father!" King Wen said: "Then let us divine it." The great officers said: "The former king commands you, without any doubt — why divine it further!"

## Section 17

**ZH:** 遂迎臧丈人而授之政。典洗無更，偏令無出。三年，文王觀於國，則列士壞植散群，長官者不成德，斔斛不敢入於四竟。列士壞植散群，則尚同也；長官者不成德，則同務也；斔斛不敢入於四竟，則諸侯無二心也。文王於是焉以為大師，北面而問曰：「政可以及天下乎？」臧丈人昧然而不應，泛然而辭，朝令而夜遁，終身無聞。

**EN:** So he welcomed the old man of Zang and handed him the government. No statute was altered; no partial decree was issued. After three years, King Wen observed in the state: the ranked scholars had broken their cliques and scattered their parties; the office-holders did not complete any virtue of their own; and bushel-measures from elsewhere dared not enter the four borders. That the ranked scholars had broken their cliques and scattered their parties meant they honored sameness; that office-holders completed no virtue of their own meant they shared one task; that outside bushels dared not enter the four borders meant the lords had no divided heart. Thereupon King Wen made him Grand Master and, facing north, asked: "Could this government reach to all under heaven?" The old man of Zang, dim and blank, did not answer; vaguely, he declined it; that morning he was bidden, that night he fled — and was never heard of again.

## Section 18

**ZH:** 顏淵問於仲尼曰：「文王其猶未邪？又何以夢為乎？」仲尼曰：「默！汝無言！夫文王盡之也，而又何論刺焉！彼直以循斯須也。」

**EN:** Yan Yuan asked Confucius: "Was King Wen then still wanting? Why did he need the dream?" Confucius said: "Silence! Not a word from you! King Wen went to the uttermost — what is there for you to carp at! He merely followed the need of the moment."

## Section 19

**ZH:** 列御寇為伯昏無人射，引之盈貫，措杯水其肘上，發之，適矢復沓，方矢復寓。當是時，猶象人也。伯昏無人曰：「是射之射，非不射之射也。嘗與汝登高山，履危石，臨百仞之淵，若能射乎？」於是無人遂登高山，履危石，臨百仞之淵，背逡巡，足二分垂在外，揖御寇而進之。御寇伏地，汗流至踵。伯昏無人曰：「夫至人者，上闚青天，下潛黃泉，揮斥八極，神氣不變。今汝怵然有恂目之志，爾於中也殆矣夫！」

**EN:** Lie Yikou (列御寇) was showing his archery to Bahun Wuren. He drew the bow to the full, set a cup of water on his elbow, and let fly: as one arrow flew, a second was already nocked; as that one flew, a third was already in place. All the while he was like a statue. Bahun Wuren said: "This is the archery of shooting; it is not the archery of not-shooting. Try climbing a high mountain with me, treading the perilous rocks, looking over a hundred-fathom deep — could you shoot there?" So Wuren climbed a high mountain, trod the perilous rocks, looked over a hundred-fathom deep, backed slowly until two-fifths of his feet hung over the edge, and with a bow invited Yikou to come forward. Yikou prostrated himself on the ground, sweat streaming to his heels. Bahun Wuren said: "The utmost man gazes up into the blue sky, dives down into the Yellow Springs, hurls himself to the eight extremes — and his spirit and breath do not change. Now you, trembling, have a dizzy look in your eyes — your inner center is in peril indeed!"

## Section 20

**ZH:** 肩吾問於孫叔敖曰：「子三為令尹而不榮華，三去之而無憂色。吾始也疑子，今視子之鼻間栩栩然，子之用心獨奈何？」孫叔敖曰：「吾何以過人哉！吾以其來不可卻也，其去不可止也，吾以為得失之非我也，而無憂色而已矣。我何以過人哉！且不知其在彼乎，其在我乎？其在彼邪，亡乎我；在我邪，亡乎彼。方將躊躇，方將四顧，何暇至乎人貴人賤哉！」

**EN:** Jianwu asked Sunshu Ao (孫叔敖): "Three times you became chief minister and took no glory in it; three times you were dismissed and wore no worried look. At first I doubted you; now I watch the easy stirring about your nostrils. How do you use your heart?" Sunshu Ao said: "How could I surpass other men! I hold that what comes cannot be refused and what goes cannot be stopped; I hold that gain and loss are not mine — and I wear no worried look. That is all. How could I surpass other men! Besides, I do not know whether it lies in the office or in me. If it lies in the office, it is gone from me; if it lies in me, it is gone from the office. Just now I am poised and lingering; just now I am gazing in all four directions — what leisure have I to come to men's honor and men's baseness!"

## Section 21

**ZH:** 仲尼聞之曰：「古之真人，知者不得說，美人不得濫，盜人不得劫，伏戲、黃帝不得友。死生亦大矣，而無變乎己，況爵祿乎！若然者，其神經乎大山而無介，入乎淵泉而不濡，處卑細而不憊，充滿天地，既以與人，己愈有。」

**EN:** Confucius, hearing of this, said: "The true men of old — the knowing could not persuade them; beauties could not debauch them; robbers could not rob them; Fuxi and the Yellow Emperor could not befriend them. Death and life are great indeed, yet they made no change in them — how much less titles and stipends! Men like that: their spirit passes through great mountains without a seam; enters the deep springs without being wet; dwells in mean and narrow straits without being worn. Filling heaven and earth, the more they give to others, the more they have."

## Section 22

**ZH:** 楚王與凡君坐，少焉，楚王左右曰「凡亡」者三。凡君曰：「凡之亡也，不足以喪吾存。夫『凡之亡也，不足以喪吾存』，則楚之存不足以存存。由是觀之，則凡未始亡而楚未始存也。」

**EN:** The king of Chu was seated with the lord of Fan (凡). Presently, the king of Chu's attendants said three times over: "Fan is lost." The lord of Fan said: "Fan's being lost is not enough to destroy what I preserve. And if Fan's being lost is not enough to destroy what I preserve, then Chu's being preserved is not enough to preserve what it preserves. Seen in this light — Fan has never yet been lost, and Chu has never yet been preserved."
