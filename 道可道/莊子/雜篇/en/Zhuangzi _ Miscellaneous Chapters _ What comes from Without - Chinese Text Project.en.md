# What comes from Without (外物)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Miscellaneous Chapters _ What comes from Without - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (16 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 外物不可必，故龍逢誅，比干戮，箕子狂，惡來死，桀、紂亡。人主莫不欲其臣之忠，而忠未必信，故伍員流於江，萇弘死於蜀，藏其血三年，化而為碧。人親莫不欲其子之孝，而孝未必愛，故孝己憂而曾參悲。

**EN:** What comes from without cannot be made certain. Therefore Longfeng was executed; Bigan was slaughtered; Jizi feigned madness; E'lai died; Jie and Zhou were destroyed. Every lord wishes his ministers loyal — yet loyalty is not necessarily believed. Thus Wu Yuan was floated down the river; Chang Hong died in Shu — his blood, stored away, turned green after three years. Every parent wishes his child filial — yet filial piety is not necessarily loved. Thus Xiaoji grieved and Zeng Shen sorrowed.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 木與木相摩則然，金與火相守則流。陰陽錯行，則天地大絯，於是乎有雷有霆，水中有火，乃焚大槐。有甚憂兩陷而無所逃，螴蜳不得成，心若縣於天地之間，慰睯沈屯，利害相摩，生火甚多，眾人焚和。月固不勝火，於是乎有僓然而道盡。

**EN:** Wood rubbed against wood kindles; metal kept with fire melts. When yin and yang run awry, heaven and earth are greatly startled: then there is thunder and lightning, fire in the midst of the waters, burning the great locust tree. There are those sunk in double worry, with nowhere to escape: fluttering, they cannot complete anything; the heart hangs suspended between heaven and earth; fretful and dull, sunk and stifled; benefit and harm grind one another; much fire is born within, and the crowd burns up their harmony. The moon's water cannot overcome such fire — and so some fall utterly, and the Way is at an end in them. [uncertain: this compressed psychological passage (兩陷, 螴蜳, 慰睯沈屯, 月固不勝火) has many divergent readings; rendered following the dominant commentarial line that it describes inward turmoil consuming the heart's harmony.]

## Section 3

**ZH:** 莊周家貧，故往貸粟於監河侯。監河侯曰：「諾。我將得邑金，將貸子三百金，可乎？」莊周忿然作色曰：「周昨來，有中道而呼者。周顧視車轍中，有鮒魚焉。周問之曰：『鮒魚來！子何為者邪？』對曰：『我，東海之波臣也。君豈有斗升之水而活我哉？』周曰：『諾。我且南遊吳、越之王，激西江之水而迎子，可乎？』鮒魚忿然作色曰：『吾失我常與，我無所處。吾得斗升之水然活耳，君乃言此，曾不如早索我於枯魚之肆！』」

**EN:** Zhuang Zhou's family was poor, so he went to borrow grain from the Marquis who Oversees the River. The marquis said: "Certainly. I am about to receive the revenue of my fief; I will lend you three hundred pieces of gold. Will that do?" Zhuang Zhou flushed with anger: "Yesterday, as I came, there was a cry from mid-road. I looked into the cart-rut — there lay a gudgeon. I asked it: 'Gudgeon! What are you doing there?' It answered: 'I am a wave-servant of the Eastern Sea. Could you spare a peck or a pint of water to keep me alive?' I said: 'Certainly. I am about to roam south to the kings of Wu and Yue; I will stir up the waters of the Western River and bring them to meet you. Will that do?' The gudgeon flushed with anger: 'I have lost what I constantly lived in; I have nowhere to stay. I could live on a peck or a pint of water — and you speak to me of this! You might as well look for me early in the dried-fish shop!'"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 任公子為大鉤巨緇，五十犗以為餌，蹲乎會稽，投竿東海，旦旦而釣，期年不得魚。已而大魚食之，牽巨鉤錎沒而下，騖揚而奮鬐，白波若山，海水震蕩，聲侔鬼神，憚赫千里。任公子得若魚，離而腊之，自制河以東，蒼梧以北，莫不厭若魚者。

**EN:** The prince of Ren made a great hook on a giant black line, baited it with fifty oxen, crouched at Kuaiji, cast his rod into the Eastern Sea, and fished day after day. A full year, and he caught no fish. Then a great fish took the bait: it dragged the great hook, plunged diving downward, then shot up flailing its fins — the white waves were like mountains, the sea-waters shook, the sound matched ghosts and gods, terror for a thousand li. The prince of Ren got this fish; he cut it up and dried it — and from east of the Zhi River to north of Cangwu, there was no one who did not feast on that fish.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 已而後世輇才諷說之徒，皆驚而相告也。夫揭竿累，趣灌瀆，守鯢鮒，其於得大魚難矣；飾小說以干縣令，其於大達亦遠矣。是以未嘗聞任氏之風俗，其不可與經於世亦遠矣。

**EN:** Afterward, the small-talented tale-tellers of later ages all told it to one another in amazement. But to hoist a rod and line, hurry to a watering ditch, and watch for minnows and gudgeons — getting a great fish that way is hard. To dress up petty sayings to petition the district magistrate — that is far from great attainment. So those who have never heard the ways of the house of Ren are far indeed from being fit to order the world.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 儒以《詩》、《禮》發冢。大儒臚傳曰：「東方作矣，事之何若？」小儒曰：「未解裙襦，口中有珠。《詩》固有之曰：『青青之麥，生於陵陂。生不布施，死何含珠為？』接其鬢，壓其顪，儒以金椎控其頤，徐別其頰，無傷口中珠！」

**EN:** The Ru rob graves by the Odes and Ritual. The senior Ru passes down the word: "The east is brightening — how goes the work?" The junior Ru says: "Not yet undressed the skirt and robe; there is a pearl in the mouth. The Odes do indeed have it: 'Green, green the wheat, growing on the slope of the mound. In life you never gave alms — in death, why a pearl in your mouth?' Take hold of his sideburns, press down his chin, with the metal awl prize the jaws apart, gently separate the cheeks — do not harm the pearl in his mouth!"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 老萊子之弟子出薪，遇仲尼，反以告曰：「有人於彼，修上而趨下，末僂而後耳，視若營四海，不知其誰氏之子。」老萊子曰：「是丘也，召而來！」仲尼至。曰：「丘！去汝躬矜與汝容知，斯為君子矣。」仲尼揖而退，蹙然改容而問曰：「業可得進乎？」老萊子曰：「夫不忍一世之傷，而驁萬世之患，抑固窶邪？亡其略弗及邪？惠以歡為驁，終身之醜，中民之行進焉耳，相引以名，相結以隱。與其譽堯而非桀，不如兩忘而閉其所譽。反無非傷也，動無非邪也。聖人躊躇以興事，以每成功。奈何哉其載焉終矜爾！」

**EN:** Laolaizi's disciple, out gathering firewood, met Confucius. Returning, he reported: "There is a man there — long above, short below, bent-backed, ears set behind, gazing as though he held the four seas in his care. I do not know whose son he is." Laolaizi said: "That is Qiu. Call him here!" Confucius came. Laolaizi said: "Qiu! Put away your personal pride and your knowing looks — then you will be a gentleman." Confucius bowed and stepped back; then, his countenance changed with concern, he asked: "May my work make progress?" Laolaizi said: "Unable to bear one generation's wound, you would run reckless into ten thousand generations' trouble — is that fixed poverty, or is it that your devices do not reach? Favors given to please are recklessness; a whole life's shame. The conduct of middling men advances a little, drawn on by names, bound up in secrecy. Rather than praising Yao and condemning Jie, better to forget them both and shut up what they praise. Turning back, nothing is not wounded; moving, nothing is not astray. The sage, poised and lingering, raises affairs, and so each succeeds. What are you to do, carrying your pride to the very end!"

## Section 8

**ZH:** 宋元君夜半而夢人被髮闚阿門，曰：「予自宰路之淵，予為清江使河伯之所，漁者余且得予。」元君覺，使人占之，曰：「此神龜也。」君曰：「漁者有余且乎？」左右曰：「有。」君曰：「令余且會朝。」明日，余且朝。君曰：「漁何得？」對曰：「且之網，得白龜焉，其圓五尺。」君曰：「獻若之龜。」龜至，君再欲殺之，再欲活之，心疑，卜之，曰：「殺龜以卜，吉。」乃刳龜，七十二鑽而無遺筴。

**EN:** Lord Yuan of Song dreamt at midnight of a man with loose hair peering through the side gate, who said: "I come from the deep of Zailu. As envoy of the Clear River on my way to the Earl of the River, the fisherman Yu Ju caught me." Waking, the lord had it divined: "It is the sacred tortoise." The lord said: "Is there a fisherman named Yu Ju?" His attendants said: "There is." The lord said: "Let Yu Ju come to court." The next day Yu Ju appeared. The lord said: "What have you caught?" He answered: "In Ju's net, a white tortoise, five spans round." The lord said: "Present your tortoise." The tortoise arrived. The lord twice wished to kill it, twice wished to spare it; his heart in doubt, he divined: "To kill the tortoise for divination — auspicious." So he gutted the tortoise, and with it made seventy-two borings without a single failed answer.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 仲尼曰：「神龜能見夢於元君而不能避余且之網；知能七十二鑽而無遺筴，不能避刳腸之患。如是，則知有所困，神有所不及也。雖有至知，萬人謀之。魚不畏網而畏鵜鶘。去小知而大知明，去善而自善矣。」嬰兒生無石師而能言，與能言者處也。

**EN:** Confucius said: "The sacred tortoise could appear in Lord Yuan's dream, yet could not avoid Yu Ju's net; its knowledge could answer seventy-two borings without a miss, yet could not avoid the calamity of having its belly gutted. Thus knowledge has its limits; the spirit has what it cannot reach. However utmost the knowledge, ten thousand men will scheme against it. Fish do not fear the net but fear the pelican. Put aside small knowledge and great knowledge is bright; put aside goodness and one is good of oneself." An infant is born without a master teacher and yet can speak — because it dwells among those who can speak.

## Section 10

**ZH:** 惠子謂莊子曰：「子言無用。」莊子曰：「知無用而始可與言用矣。夫地非不廣且大也，人之所用容足耳。然則廁足而墊之，致黃泉，人尚有用乎？」惠子曰：「無用。」莊子曰：「然則無用之為用也亦明矣。」

**EN:** Huizi said to Zhuangzi: "Your words are useless." Zhuangzi said: "Only one who knows the useless can be talked with about the useful. The earth is not lacking in breadth and greatness, yet what man uses is room for his feet. But set the feet aside and dig down to the Yellow Springs — is the man still of any use?" Huizi said: "No use." Zhuangzi said: "Then the use of the useless is clear indeed."

## Section 11

**ZH:** 莊子曰：「人有能遊，且得不遊乎？人而不能遊，且得遊乎？夫流遁之志，決絕之行，噫！其非至知厚德之任與！覆墜而不反，火馳而不顧，雖相與為君臣，時也，易世而無以相賤。故曰：至人不留行焉。夫尊古而卑今，學者之流也。且以豨韋氏之流觀今之世，夫孰能不波？唯至人乃能遊於世而不僻，順人而不失己，彼教不學，承意不彼。

**EN:** Zhuangzi said: "If a man can roam, can he then not roam? If a man cannot roam, can he then roam? The will that drifts and flees, the conduct that cuts and breaks off — ah! Are these not burdens for utmost knowledge and thick virtue! Falling and toppling and never returning; galloping like fire and never looking back — though men are bound as lord and minister, that is the times; change the age and neither can slight the other. Hence it is said: the utmost man leaves no fixed course. To honor the ancient and slight the present is the current of scholars. And if one views the present age with the current of the house of Xiwei — who could fail to be swept along? Only the utmost man can roam in the world without going astray, follow men without losing himself. Their teaching he does not study; their meanings he receives without becoming other."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 目徹為明，耳徹為聰，鼻徹為顫，口徹為甘，心徹為知，知徹為德。凡道不欲壅，壅則哽，哽而不止則跈，跈則眾害生。物之有知者恃息，其不殷，非天之罪。天之穿之，日夜無降，人則顧塞其竇。胞有重閬，心有天遊。室無空虛，則婦姑勃谿；心無天遊，則六鑿相攘。大林丘山之善於人也，亦神者不勝。

**EN:** The eye penetrating is sight; the ear penetrating is hearing; the nose penetrating is smell; the mouth penetrating is taste; the heart penetrating is knowledge; knowledge penetrating is virtue. In the Way, generally, one does not want blockage: blocked, it chokes; choked and unchecked, it trips; tripping, every harm is born. Creatures that have knowledge rely on their breath; when it does not thicken, it is not Heaven's fault: Heaven bores it through, day and night without ceasing, but men stop up its passage. The womb has its double hollow; the heart has its heavenly roaming. If a room has no empty space, wife and mother-in-law clash in it; if the heart has no heavenly roaming, the six apertures plunder one another. That great forests, hills, and mountains are good for men is because the spirit cannot bear the crowd. [uncertain: the doublet 胞有重閬，心有天遊 and the physiological imagery are variously glossed; rendered "the womb has its double hollow; the heart has its heavenly roaming" following the anatomical reading of 胞.]

## Section 13

**ZH:** 德溢乎名，名溢乎暴，謀稽乎誸，知出乎爭，柴生乎守，官事果乎眾宜。春雨日時，草木怒生，銚鎒於是乎始修，草木之到植者過半，而不知其然。

**EN:** Virtue overflows from fame; fame overflows from show; schemes are examined through craft; knowledge comes out of contention; the stump is born of clinging; official affairs bear fruit in the multitude's convenience. When spring rains come in season, grass and trees burst into growth; then hoe and spade are first set in order; and more than half of the grass and trees that had been uprooted by planting [uncertain: 到植 — read variously as "planted upside down" and as "still living"; rendered "uprooted by planting"] flourish again — and none knows how it is so.

## Section 14

**ZH:** 靜然可以補病，眥搣可以休老，寧可以止遽。雖然，若是，勞者之務也，非佚者之所未嘗過而問焉。聖人之所以駴天下，神人未嘗過而問焉；賢人所以駴世，聖人未嘗過而問焉；君子所以駴國，賢人未嘗過而問焉；小人所以合時，君子未嘗過而問焉。

**EN:** Stillness can mend illness; kneading the corners of the eyes can ease old age; calm can stop haste. Even so, these are the tasks of the laboring; the idle have never bothered to ask. What the sage uses to startle the world — the spirit-like man has never bothered to ask; what the worthy uses to startle the age — the sage has never bothered to ask; what the gentleman uses to startle the state — the worthy has never bothered to ask; what the small man uses to suit the times — the gentleman has never bothered to ask.

## Section 15

**ZH:** 演門有親死者，以善毀，爵為官師，其黨人毀而死者半。堯與許由天下，許由逃之；湯與務光天下，務光怒之。紀他聞之，帥弟子而踆於窾水，諸侯弔之三年，申徒狄因以踣河。

**EN:** At Yan Gate there was one whose parent died: he excelled at wasting away in mourning, and was ennobled as Master of Officers — and half the men of his neighborhood wasted away and died. Yao offered the world to Xu You — Xu You fled from it. Tang offered the world to Wuguang — Wuguang raged at it. Ji Ta heard of it, led his disciples, and crouched by the Kuan River; the lords condoled with him for three years — and Shentu Di thereupon threw himself into the Yellow River.

## Section 16

**ZH:** 荃者所以在魚，得魚而忘荃；蹄者所以在兔，得兔而忘蹄；言者所以在意，得意而忘言。吾安得忘言之人而與之言哉？」

**EN:** The fish-trap exists for the fish; having got the fish, one forgets the trap. The snare exists for the rabbit; having got the rabbit, one forgets the snare. Words exist for the meaning; having got the meaning, one forgets the words. Where can I find a man who has forgotten words, and talk with him!

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 聖人 → "the sage"; 神人 → "the spirit-like man"; 至人 → "the utmost man"; 知 → "knowledge"; 德 → "virtue"; 道 → "the Way"; 無為 not prominent here; 忘言 → "forgetting words."
- §16's trap-and-snare close is the book's most famous statement on language and meaning; rendered to keep the fishing imagery concrete.
