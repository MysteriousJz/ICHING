# Xu Wu-gui (徐无鬼)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Miscellaneous Chapters _ Xu Wu-gui - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (31 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 徐無鬼因女商見魏武侯，武侯勞之曰：「先生病矣！苦於山林之勞，故乃肯見於寡人。」徐無鬼曰：「我則勞於君，君有何勞於我？君將盈耆欲，長好惡，則性命之情病矣；君將黜耆欲，掔好惡，則耳目病矣。我將勞君，君有何勞於我？」武侯超然不對。

**EN:** Xu Wugui, through the introduction of Nüshang (女商), had an audience with Marquis Wu of Wei. Marquis Wu comforted him: "You look worn, sir! Suffering from the toils of mountain forests — that is why you have consented to see me." Xu Wugui said: "It is I who should comfort you, my lord — what have you to comfort me about? If you are going to glut your cravings and desires, and fatten your likes and dislikes, then the genuine condition of your inborn lot falls ill; if you are going to cut off your cravings and desires, and wrench out your likes and dislikes, then your ears and eyes fall ill. It is I who shall comfort you, my lord — what have you to comfort me about?" Marquis Wu, greatly taken aback, made no answer.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 少焉，徐無鬼曰：「嘗語君，吾相狗也。下之質，執飽而止，是狸德也；中之質，若視日；上之質，若亡其一。吾相狗，又不若吾相馬也。吾相馬，直者中繩，曲者中鉤，方者中矩，圓者中規，是國馬也，而未若天下馬也。天下馬有成材，若卹若失，若喪其一，若是者，超軼絕塵，不知其所。」武侯大悅而笑。

**EN:** After a little, Xu Wugui said: "Let me tell you, my lord, how I judge dogs. The lowest quality: catching its fill and stopping — that is the wildcat's kind. The middling quality: as though gazing at the sun. The highest quality: as though it had lost its oneness. But my judging of dogs does not compare with my judging of horses. In judging horses: the straight hits the line, the curved hits the hook, the square hits the square, the round hits the compass — that is a horse of the realm, but still not a match for the horse of the world. The horse of the world has its material ready-made: as though anxious, as though forlorn, as though it had lost its oneness. Being such, it overtakes, outruns, and leaves the dust behind — none knows where it ends." Marquis Wu was greatly delighted and laughed.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 徐無鬼出，女商曰：「先生獨何以說吾君乎？吾所以說吾君者，橫說之則以《詩》、《書》、《禮》、《樂》，從說之則以金板、六弢，奉事而大有功者不可為數，而吾君未嘗啟齒。今先生何以說吾君，使吾君說若此乎？」徐無鬼曰：「吾直告之吾相狗馬耳。」女商曰：「若是乎」？曰：「子不聞夫越之流人乎？去國數日，見其所知而喜；去國旬月，見其所嘗見於國中者喜；及期年也，見似人者而喜矣。不亦去人滋久，思人滋深乎！夫逃虛空者，藜、藋柱乎鼪、鼬之逕，踉位其空，聞人足音跫然而喜矣，而況乎兄弟親戚之謦欬其側者乎！久矣夫！莫以真人之言謦欬吾君之側乎！」

**EN:** When Xu Wugui had gone out, Nüshang said: "With what, sir, did you just now please my lord? What I use to please my lord: across, I persuade him with the Odes, Documents, Ritual, and Music; along, with the Metal Tablets and Six Bow-cases. Those who have served him with great merit are past counting — yet my lord has never bared his teeth. With what did you please my lord, to make him so pleased?" Xu Wugui said: "I simply told him how I judge dogs and horses." Nüshang said: "Is that all?" He said: "Have you not heard of the exiles of Yue? A few days from home, they are glad to see an acquaintance. A month from home, they are glad to see anyone they ever saw at home. At a full year, they are glad to see anything that looks like a man. Is it not so that the longer one is parted from men, the deeper one's longing for men grows! A man who has fled into the empty waste, where bindweed and goosefoot choke the paths of weasels and ferrets, standing long in the emptiness — at the sound of a human footfall, how he rejoices! How much more at the cough and chatter of brothers and kin beside him! Long, long has it been since anyone coughed the words of a true man beside my lord!"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 徐無鬼見武侯，武侯曰：「先生居山林，食芧栗，厭蔥韭，以賓寡人，久矣夫！今老邪？其欲干酒肉之味邪？其寡人亦有社稷之福邪？」徐無鬼曰：「無鬼生於貧賤，未嘗敢飲食君之酒肉，將來勞君也。」君曰：「何哉？奚勞寡人？」曰：「勞君之神與形。」武侯曰：「何謂邪？」徐無鬼曰：「天地之養也一，登高不可以為長，居下不可以為短。君獨為萬乘之主，以苦一國之民，以養耳目鼻口，夫神者不自許也。夫神者，好和而惡姦。夫姦，病也，故勞之。唯君所病之，何也？」

**EN:** Xu Wugui had an audience with Marquis Wu. Marquis Wu said: "You dwell in mountain forests, sir, eating acorns and chestnuts, sating yourself on scallions and chives — you have kept me at a distance for a long time now! Have you grown old? Do you long for a taste of wine and meat? Or is there some blessing for my altars of soil and grain?" Xu Wugui said: "Wugui was born to poverty and meanness; I have never dared to eat or drink your wine and meat, my lord. I have come to comfort you." The lord said: "What? Comfort me for what?" He said: "To comfort your spirit and your form." Marquis Wu said: "What does that mean?" Xu Wugui said: "The nourishment of heaven and earth is one: to climb high does not make one long; to dwell low does not make one short. You alone, as lord of ten thousand chariots, embitter the people of one state to nourish your ears, eyes, nose, and mouth — and the spirit does not consent to that. The spirit loves harmony and hates intrusion. Intrusion is a sickness — therefore I comfort you. Only you, my lord, suffer from this sickness. Why?"

## Section 5

**ZH:** 武侯曰：「欲見先生久矣。吾欲愛民而為義偃兵，可乎？」徐無鬼曰：「不可。愛民，害民之始也；為義偃兵，造兵之本也。君自此為之，則殆不成。凡成美，惡器也。君雖為仁義，幾且偽哉！形固造形，成固有伐，變固外戰。君亦必無盛鶴列於麗譙之間，無徒驥於錙壇之宮，無藏逆於得，無以巧勝人，無以謀勝人，無以戰勝人。夫殺人之士民，兼人之土地，以養吾私與吾神者，其戰不知孰善？勝之惡乎在？君若勿已矣，修胸中之誠，以應天地之情而勿攖。夫民死已脫矣，君將惡乎用夫偃兵哉！」

**EN:** Marquis Wu said: "I have long wished to see you, sir. I wish to love the people and, for rightness' sake, lay down arms. May that do?" Xu Wugui said: "It may not. Loving the people is the beginning of harming the people; laying down arms for rightness' sake is the root of raising arms. If you proceed from this, my lord, you will surely not succeed. Whatever completes a beauty is an ill instrument. Though you practice humaneness and rightness, you will be nearly a fraud! Form inherently begets form; completion inherently brings boasting; change inherently brings outward war. You must neither parade your columns in stately ranks between the gate-towers, nor drill your infantry and horse in the palace of the Zi altar; do not store up contrariness in what you get; do not overcome men by skill; do not overcome men by schemes; do not overcome men by war. To slay another's soldiers and people, to annex another's land, in order to nourish my private person and my spirit — of that war, who can say which side was good? Wherein does the victory lie? If you cannot help it, my lord — cultivate the sincerity in your breast, answer the genuine condition of heaven and earth, and do not intrude. Then the people are already delivered from death — what use would you have for laying down arms!"

## Section 6

**ZH:** 黃帝將見大隗乎具茨之山，方明為御，昌宇驂乘，張若、謵朋前馬，昆閽、滑稽後車。至於襄城之野，七聖皆迷，無所問塗。適遇牧馬童子，問塗焉，曰：「若知具茨之山乎？」曰：「然。」「若知大隗之所存乎？」曰：「然。」黃帝曰：「異哉小童！非徒知具茨之山，又知大隗之所存。請問為天下。」小童曰：「夫為天下者，亦若此而已矣，又奚事焉？予少而自遊於六合之內，予適有瞀病，有長者教予曰：『若乘日之車，而遊於襄城之野。』今予病少痊，予又且復遊於六合之外。夫為天下，亦若此而已。予又奚事焉？」黃帝曰：「夫為天下者，則誠非吾子之事。雖然，請問為天下。」小童辭。黃帝又問。小童曰：「夫為天下者，亦奚以異乎牧馬者哉？亦去其害馬者而已矣。」黃帝再拜稽首，稱天師而退。

**EN:** The Yellow Emperor was going to see Great Obscurity (大隗) on Mount Juci (具茨). Fangming drove; Changyu rode at his side; Zhangruo and Xipeng ran before the horses; Kunhun and Huaji brought up the rear. Coming to the wilds of Xiangcheng, all seven sages lost their way, with no one to ask the road. They happened upon a boy herding horses, and asked him the way: "Do you know Mount Juci?" He said: "Yes." "Do you know where Great Obscurity dwells?" He said: "Yes." The Yellow Emperor said: "What a strange boy! Not only does he know Mount Juci — he even knows where Great Obscurity dwells. May I ask about the governing of the world?" The boy said: "The governing of the world — it is just like this, that is all; what more is there to do? When I was small I roamed by myself within the six coordinates; then I fell ill with a dizziness, and an elder taught me: 'Ride the chariot of the sun and roam in the wilds of Xiangcheng.' Now my illness has somewhat mended, and I am about to roam again beyond the six coordinates. The governing of the world — it is just like this. What more is there for me to do?" The Yellow Emperor said: "The governing of the world is truly no business of yours, young sir. Still, I beg to ask about the governing of the world." The boy declined. The Yellow Emperor asked again. The boy said: "How is governing the world any different from herding horses? It is nothing but removing what harms the horses." The Yellow Emperor bowed twice with forehead to the ground, addressed him as Heavenly Master, and withdrew.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 知士無思慮之變則不樂，辯士無談說之序則不樂，察士無淩誶之事則不樂，皆囿於物者也。招世之士興朝，中民之士榮官，筋力之士矜難，勇敢之士奮患，兵革之士樂戰，枯槁之士宿名，法律之士廣治，禮教之士敬容，仁義之士貴際。農夫無草萊之事則不比，商賈無市井之事則不比。庶人有旦暮之業則勸，百工有器械之巧則壯。錢財不積則貪者憂，權勢不尤則夸者悲。勢物之徒樂變，遭時有所用，不能無為也。此皆順比於歲，不物於易者也，馳其形性，潛之萬物，終身不反，悲夫！

**EN:** The scholar of knowledge is not happy without changes for his thoughts; the scholar of disputation is not happy without sequences for his talk; the scholar of inspection is not happy without affairs to pry into and scold. These are all bound fast by things. The scholar who courts the age flourishes at court; the scholar who hits the crowd glories in office; the scholar of sinew and strength prides himself on difficulty; the scholar of bold daring rouses himself for peril; the scholar of arms and armor delights in war; the withered scholar lodges in a name; the scholar of law enlarges government; the scholar of ritual teaching reveres bearing; the scholar of humaneness and rightness prizes communion. The farmer without grassland work is ill at ease; the merchant without market affairs is ill at ease. The common man with daily labor is encouraged; the artisan with mastery of his tools grows strong. When wealth does not pile up, the greedy worry; when power is not supreme, the boaster grieves. The followers of circumstance delight in change: meeting a time when they can be used, they cannot be without action. All these follow the year's measure, bound to what does not change [uncertain: 不物於易者也 — rendered "bound to what does not change"; the line is compressed and variously read], galloping body and nature, sunk in the ten thousand things, never to return all life long — alas!

## Section 8

**ZH:** 莊子曰：「射者非前期而中，謂之善射，天下皆羿也，可乎？」惠子曰：「可。」莊子曰：「天下非有公是也，而各是其所是，天下皆堯也，可乎？」惠子曰：「可。」

**EN:** Zhuangzi said: "If an archer hits without aiming beforehand and we call him a good shot, then all under heaven are Yi — is that admissible?" Huizi said: "Admissible." Zhuangzi said: "If there is no publicly right right under heaven, and each counts his own right as right, then all under heaven are Yao — is that admissible?" Huizi said: "Admissible."

## Section 9

**ZH:** 莊子曰：「然則，儒、墨、楊、秉四，與夫子為五，果孰是邪？或者若魯遽者邪？其弟子曰：『我得夫子之道矣，吾能冬爨鼎而夏造冰矣。』魯遽曰：『是直以陽召陽，以陰召陰，非吾所謂道也。吾示子乎吾道。』於是為之調瑟，廢一於堂，廢一於室，鼓宮宮動，鼓角角動，音律同矣。夫或改調一弦，於五音無當也，鼓之二十五弦皆動，未始異於聲，而音之君已。且若是者邪？」

**EN:** Zhuangzi said: "Then — Confucians, Mohists, Yang, and Bing make four, and with you, sir, five. Which of you is really right? Is it perhaps like Lu Ju (魯遽)? His disciple said: 'I have gotten my master's Way; I can fire a cauldron in winter and make ice in summer.' Lu Ju said: 'That is merely calling yang to yang, calling yin to yin — not what I call the Way. Let me show you my Way.' Thereupon he tuned two zithers, set one in the hall and one in the chamber: strike the gong note and the gong answers; strike the jue note and the jue answers — for their pitch-pipes were the same. Now if one retunes a single string so that it fits none of the five notes, and strikes it, all twenty-five strings vibrate: it has not begun to differ in sound, yet it is the lord of the notes. Is your case like that?"

## Section 10

**ZH:** 惠子曰：「今夫儒、墨、楊、秉，且方與我以辯，相拂以辭，相鎮以聲，而未始吾非也，則奚若矣？」莊子曰：「齊人蹢子於宋者，其命閽也不以完，其求鈃鍾也以束縛，其求唐子也而未始出域，有遺類矣夫！楚人寄而蹢閽者，夜半於無人之時而與舟人鬥，未始離於岑，而足以造於怨也。」

**EN:** Huizi said: "Now the Confucians, Mohists, Yang, and Bing are disputing with me: we refute one another with words, we bear one another down with voices — and none has yet proved me wrong. So what of it?" Zhuangzi said: "A man of Qi hobbled his son off to Song; he assigned him gatekeeper's work without regard for his wholeness; when he sought a bell or a pot, he kept it bound up tight; when he sought his missing son, he never went beyond his own borders — there are such families left! A man of Chu, lodging as a guest, hobbled a gatekeeper; at midnight, when no one was about, he brawled with the boatman — before ever leaving the shore, he had done enough to breed a feud. [uncertain: both miniatures are fragmentary in transmission; the point — guarding small things while missing the large — is clear, but the particulars cannot be fully reconstructed.]"

## Section 11

**ZH:** 莊子送葬，過惠子之墓，顧謂從者曰：「郢人堊慢其鼻端若蠅翼，使匠石斲之。匠石運斤成風，聽而斲之，盡堊而鼻不傷，郢人立不失容。宋元君聞之，召匠石曰：『嘗試為寡人為之。』匠石曰：『臣則嘗能斲之。雖然，臣之質死久矣。』自夫子之死也，吾無以為質矣，吾無與言之矣。」

**EN:** Zhuangzi, attending a funeral, passed by Huizi's grave. He turned to his followers and said: "A man of Ying had a smear of plaster on the tip of his nose, thin as a fly's wing. He had Carpenter Shi cut it off. Carpenter Shi swung his axe into a wind, heard and cut — the plaster was all gone and the nose unharmed, while the man of Ying stood without change of countenance. Lord Yuan of Song heard of it and summoned Carpenter Shi: 'Do it for me, I pray.' Carpenter Shi said: 'Your servant once could cut it. Even so — the substance I worked upon died long ago.' Since your death, Master, I have had no substance to work upon — I have no one to speak with."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 管仲有病，桓公問之曰：「仲父之病病矣，可不謂云，至於大病，則寡人惡乎屬國而可？」管仲曰：「公誰欲與？」公曰：「鮑叔牙。」曰：「不可。其為人，絜廉善士也，其於不己若者不比之；又一聞人之過，終身不忘。使之治國，上且鉤乎君，下且逆乎民。其得罪於君也，將弗久矣。」公曰：「然則孰可？」對曰：「勿已，則隰朋可。其為人也，上忘而下畔，愧不若黃帝而哀不己若者。以德分人謂之聖，以財分人謂之賢。以賢臨人，未有得人者也；以賢下人，未有不得人者也。其於國有不聞也，其於家有不見也。勿已，則隰朋可。」

**EN:** Guan Zhong fell ill. Duke Huan asked him: "Your illness is serious, father Zhong. If it should come to the great illness — to whom may I entrust the state?" Guan Zhong said: "Whom does the duke wish to give it to?" The duke said: "Bao Shuya." He said: "He will not do. As a man he is pure, incorrupt, and good, but those who are not his equals he will not suffer; and once he has heard of a man's fault, he never forgets it to the end of his life. Set him to govern the state: above, he will catch at the lord; below, he will go against the people. It will not be long before he gives offense to you, my lord." The duke said: "Then who will do?" He answered: "If it cannot be helped — Xipeng (隰朋) will do. As a man he forgets those above and sides with those below; he is ashamed he is no match for the Yellow Emperor, and he pities those who are no match for himself. To share virtue with others is called sagacity; to share wealth with others is called worthiness. To overlook men with one's worthiness — no one has ever won men that way; to humble oneself before men with one's worthiness — no one has ever failed to win them. In the state there are things he does not hear; in the household there are things he does not see. If it cannot be helped — Xipeng will do."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 吳王浮於江，登乎狙之山。眾狙見之，恂然棄而走，逃於深蓁。有一狙焉，委蛇攫搔，見巧乎王王射之，敏給搏捷矢。王命相者趨射，狙執死。王顧謂其友顏不疑曰：「之狙也，伐其巧、恃其便，以敖予，以至此殛也。戒之哉！嗟乎，無以汝色驕人哉！」顏不疑歸而師董梧，以助其色，去樂辭顯，三年而國人稱之。

**EN:** The king of Wu, sailing on the river, climbed Mount Monkey. The monkeys, seeing him, fled in terror, abandoning everything, and escaped into the deep thicket. One monkey, winding and twisting, snatching and scratching, displayed its cleverness to the king. The king shot at it; quick and nimble, it seized the arrow in flight. The king ordered his attendants to shoot together — the monkey was taken and died. The king turned to his friend Yan Buyi (顏不疑) and said: "That monkey boasted of its cleverness and trusted its nimbleness to flaunt itself before me — and so it came to this death. Be warned! Alas — do not show men a proud face!" Yan Buyi went home and took Dong Wu (董梧) as his teacher, to help him wear away his expression; he put aside music and declined eminence — and after three years the people of the state praised him.

## Section 14

**ZH:** 南伯子綦隱几而坐，仰天而噓。顏成子入見曰：「夫子，物之尤也。形固可使若槁骸，心固可使若死灰乎？」曰：「吾嘗居山穴之中矣。當是時也，田禾一覩我，而齊國之眾三賀之。我必先之，彼故知之；我必賣之，彼故鬻之。若我而不有之，彼惡得而知之？若我而不賣之，彼惡得而鬻之？嗟乎！我悲人之自喪者，吾又悲夫悲人者，吾又悲夫悲人之悲者，其後而日遠矣。」

**EN:** Nanbo Ziqi, leaning on his armrest, looked up to Heaven and sighed. Yanchengzi came in and said: "Master, you are the best of things! Can the form really be made like a withered skeleton, and the heart like dead ashes?" He said: "I once dwelt in a mountain cave. At that time, Tian He saw me once — and the multitudes of Qi congratulated him three times over. I must have shown myself first — that is how he knew of me; I must have sold myself — that is how he traded on me. If I had not possessed it, how could he have known it? If I had not sold it, how could he have traded on it? Alas! I grieve for men who lose themselves; I grieve also for the griever of men; and I grieve also for the grief of the griever of men — and thereafter, day by day, it goes farther away."

## Section 15

**ZH:** 仲尼之楚，楚王觴之，孫叔敖執爵而立，市南宜僚受酒而祭曰：「古之人乎！於此言已。」曰：「丘也聞不言之言矣，未之嘗言，於此乎言之。市南宜僚弄丸而兩家之難解，孫叔敖甘寢秉羽而郢人投兵。丘願有喙三尺。」

**EN:** When Confucius went to Chu, the king of Chu feasted him. Sunshu Ao stood holding the goblet; Yiliao of Shinan received the wine and made the libation, saying: "Men of old! Let the words be spoken here." Confucius said: "Qiu has heard of speech without words. I have never yet spoken it — so here I speak it. Yiliao of Shinan juggled his balls, and the trouble between the two houses was resolved; Sunshu Ao slept sweetly, fan in hand, and the men of Ying cast down their arms. Qiu wishes he had a beak three feet long." [uncertain: the libation framing and Confucius's closing wish are elliptical; the sense — he envies speechless efficacy — is kept.]

## Section 16

**ZH:** 彼之謂不道之道，此之謂不言之辯。故德總乎道之所一，而言休乎知之所不知，至矣。道之所一者，德不能同也；知之所不能知者，辯不能舉也。名若儒、墨而凶矣。故海不辭東流，大之至也。聖人并包天地，澤及天下，而不知其誰氏。是故生無爵，死無諡，實不聚，名不立，此之謂大人。

**EN:** That is called the way that is not a way; this is called the disputation that is not spoken. Therefore: virtue gathered in the oneness of the Way, and words resting where knowledge cannot know — that is the utmost. What the Way makes one, virtue cannot share; what knowledge cannot know, disputation cannot raise. To make names as the Confucians and Mohists do is to court calamity. So the sea does not refuse the eastward streams — the utmost of greatness. The sage embraces heaven and earth; his blessing reaches the whole world — and none knows his clan or name. Therefore in life he has no title; in death no posthumous name; substance does not accumulate; the name is not established. This is called the great man.

## Section 17

**ZH:** 狗不以善吠為良，人不以善言為賢，而況為大乎！夫為大不足以為大，而況為德乎！夫大備矣，莫若天地；然奚求焉，而大備矣。知大備者，無求、無失、無棄，不以物易己也。反己而不窮，循古而不摩，大人之誠。

**EN:** A dog is not good for barking well; a man is not worthy for speaking well — how much less for doing the great! Doing the great is not enough to be the great — how much less doing virtue! Of the greatly furnished, none compares with heaven and earth — yet what do they seek, and are greatly furnished? To know the great furnishing is to have no seeking, no losing, no abandoning — not to exchange oneself for things. To return to oneself without exhaustion, to follow the ancient without polishing it away — this is the sincerity of the great man.

## Section 18

**ZH:** 子綦有八子，陳諸前，召九方歅曰：「為我相吾子，孰為祥？」九方歅曰：「梱也為祥。」子綦瞿然喜曰：「奚若？」曰：「梱也將與國君同食以終其身。」子綦索然出涕曰：「吾子何為以至於是極也！」九方歅曰：「夫與國君同食，澤及三族，而況父母乎？今夫子聞之而泣，是禦福也。子則祥矣，父則不祥。」

**EN:** Ziqi had eight sons. Lining them up before him, he summoned Jiufang Yin (九方歅) and said: "Read my sons for me — which is the lucky one?" Jiufang Yin said: "Kun (梱) is the lucky one." Ziqi, startled with joy, said: "How so?" He said: "Kun will eat at the same table with the lord of a state to the end of his days." Ziqi, desolate, let fall tears: "What has my son done to come to this extremity!" Jiufang Yin said: "To eat at the same table with the lord of a state — the blessing reaches to the three kindreds, how much more to father and mother! Now you, Master, hear it and weep — that is to ward off fortune. The son is lucky; the father is unlucky."

## Section 19

**ZH:** 子綦曰：「歅！汝何足以識之？而梱祥邪，盡於酒肉，入於鼻口矣。而何足以知其所自來？吾未嘗為牧而牂生於奧，未嘗好田而鶉生於宎，若勿怪，何邪？吾所與吾子遊者，遊於天地。吾與之邀樂於天，吾與之邀食於地；吾不與之為事，不與之為謀，不與之為怪；吾與之乘天地之誠而不以物與之相攖，吾與之一委蛇而不與之為事所宜。今也然有世俗之償焉！凡有怪徵者，必有怪行。殆乎！非我與吾子之罪，幾天與之也！吾是以泣也。」

**EN:** Ziqi said: "Yin! How could you know this! That Kun is lucky — a fortune of wine and meat entering through nose and mouth. How could you know whence it comes! I have never kept flocks, yet lambs are born in the southwest corner; I have never loved hunting, yet quail are born in the southeast corner. If that is not strange, what is? What I roam in with my son is heaven and earth. I invite him to joy from Heaven; I invite him to food from earth; I do not join him in undertakings; I do not join him in schemes; I do not join him in oddities. I ride with him the sincerity of heaven and earth and let nothing come between us; I am one with him in winding ease and do not join him in what affairs call fitting. And now there comes to him the recompense of the vulgar world! Whatever has a strange omen is sure to bring a strange lot. Perilous! It is no fault of mine or my son's — Heaven must be sending it! That is why I weep."

## Section 20

**ZH:** 無幾何而使梱之於燕，盜得之於道，全而鬻之則難，不若刖之則易，於是乎刖而鬻之於齊，適當渠公之街，然身食肉而終。

**EN:** Not long after, Kun was sent on a mission to Yan. Robbers seized him on the road: to sell him whole was hard; to sell him footless was easy — so they cut off his feet and sold him in Qi, where he happened to serve as keeper of Duke Qu's street — and there his body ate meat to the end of its days. [uncertain: 適當渠公之街 — rendered "keeper of Duke Qu's street/gate"; the name and office are variously glossed, and the dark irony of the fulfillment is the point.]

## Section 21

**ZH:** 齧缺遇許由，曰：「子將奚之？」曰：「將逃堯。」曰：「奚謂邪？」曰：「夫堯，畜畜然仁，吾恐其為天下笑。後世其人與人相食與！夫民不難聚也，愛之則親，利之則至，譽之則勸，致其所惡則散。愛利出乎仁義，捐仁義者寡，利仁義者眾。夫仁義之行，唯且無誠，且假乎禽貪者器。是以一人之斷制利天下，譬之猶一覕也。夫堯知賢人之利天下也，而不知其賊天下也，夫唯外乎賢者知之矣。」

**EN:** Nie Que met Xu You and said: "Where are you going?" He said: "To flee Yao." He said: "What do you mean?" He said: "Yao, so diligently humane — I fear he will be a laughingstock to the world. In later ages men will eat one another! The people are not hard to gather: love them and they draw near; profit them and they come; praise them and they are encouraged; bring them what they hate and they scatter. Love and profit come out of humaneness and rightness. Those who would cast aside humaneness and rightness are few; those who profit by humaneness and rightness are many. The practice of humaneness and rightness has not only no sincerity — it lends its instruments to the ravenous and greedy. So for one man's judgment to profit the world is like a single glance. Yao knows that the worthy profit the world; he does not know that they prey on the world. Only one outside the worthy knows this."

## Section 22

**ZH:** 有暖姝者，有濡需者，有卷婁者。

**EN:** There are the self-satisfied, the snug-and-soggy, and the curl-bound.

## Section 23

**ZH:** 所謂暖姝者，學一先生之言，則暖暖姝姝而私自說也，自以為足矣，而未知未始有物也，是以謂暖姝者也。

**EN:** What is called self-satisfied: to learn the words of one master, and then preen and glow in private delight, considering oneself sufficient — never knowing that nothing has yet begun to be. This is called the self-satisfied.

## Section 24

**ZH:** 濡需者，豕蝨是也。擇疏鬣，自以為廣宮大囿，奎蹄曲隈，乳閒股腳，自以為安室利處，不知屠者之一旦鼓臂、布草、操煙火，而己與豕俱焦也。此以域進，此以域退，此其所謂濡需者也。

**EN:** The snug-and-soggy — that is the louse on a pig. Choosing the sparse bristles, it counts itself in a broad palace, a great park; in the crevices of rump and hoof, between teats and thighs, it counts itself housed secure and well placed — not knowing that one morning the butcher will swing his arms, spread the straw, take up the torch, and it will be scorched along with the pig. Advancing with its region, retreating with its region — this is called the snug-and-soggy.

## Section 25

**ZH:** 卷婁者，舜也。羊肉不慕蟻，蟻慕羊肉，羊肉羶也。舜有羶行，百姓悅之，故三徙成都，至鄧之虛而十有萬家。堯聞舜之賢，舉之童土之地，曰冀得其來之澤。舜舉乎童土之地，年齒長矣，聰明衰矣，而不得休歸，所謂卷婁者也。

**EN:** The curl-bound — that is Shun. The mutton does not long for the ants; the ants long for the mutton — mutton is rank. Shun's conduct had the rankness; the hundred clans delighted in it. Therefore though he moved thrice and formed a capital each time, by the waste of Deng there were over a hundred thousand households. Yao, hearing of Shun's worthiness, raised him to a childless land, saying he hoped to get his blessing from afar. Raised to a childless land, with his years grown long and his sight and hearing decayed, he could not rest and come home. This is called the curl-bound.

## Section 26

**ZH:** 是以神人惡眾至，眾至則不比，不比則不利也。故無所甚親，無所甚疏，抱德煬和，以順天下，此謂真人。於蟻棄知，於魚得計，於羊棄意。以目視目，以耳聽耳，以心復心，若然者，其平也繩，其變也循。古之真人，以天待之，不以人入天。

**EN:** Therefore the spirit-like man hates the crowd's arrival: where the crowd arrives, there is no peace; where there is no peace, nothing profits. So nothing too close, nothing too distant; embracing virtue, warming into harmony, to follow the world — this is called the true man. To the ant, abandon knowledge; with the fish, attain the design; to the mutton, abandon intention. With the eye to look at eyes; with the ear to listen to ears; with the heart to recover the heart — being such, his level is the inked line; his changes follow the course. The true men of old answered things with Heaven; they did not bring the human into Heaven.

## Section 27

**ZH:** 古之真人，得之也生，失之也死；得之也死，失之也生。藥也，其實堇也。桔梗也，雞壅也，豕零也，是時為帝者也，何可勝言！

**EN:** The true men of old: getting it, they lived; losing it, they died; getting it, they died; losing it, they lived. As with medicines: it may be aconite; it may be bellflower, chicken-heap, or pig-drip — whichever the season makes sovereign. How could words suffice!

## Section 28

**ZH:** 句踐也以甲楯三千，棲於會稽。唯種也能知亡之所以存，唯種也不知身之所以愁。故曰：鴟目有所適，鶴脛有所節，解之也悲。故曰：風之過河也有損焉，日之過河也有損焉。請只風與日相與守河，而河以為未始其攖也，恃源而往者也。故水之守土也審，影之守人也審，物之守物也審。

**EN:** When Goujian (句踐) perched on Kuaiji with three thousand shields, only Zhong (種) could know how the lost might yet survive; yet Zhong did not know the trouble awaiting his own person. Hence it is said: the owl's eye has its fitting place; the crane's leg has its proper measure — to undo them is sorrow. Hence it is said: the wind passing over the river diminishes it; the sun passing over the river diminishes it. But let wind and sun together keep watch on the river, and the river counts itself never intruded upon — for it trusts its source and flows on. So water keeps to earth with care; the shadow keeps to the man with care; a thing keeps to things with care.

## Section 29

**ZH:** 故目之於明也殆，耳之於聰也殆，心之於殉也殆。凡能其於府也殆，殆之成也不給改。禍之長也茲萃，其反也緣功，其果也待久。而人以為己寶，不亦悲乎！故有亡國戮民無已，不知問是也。

**EN:** So the eye, in its seeing, is in peril; the ear, in its hearing, is in peril; the heart, in its martyrdom, is in peril. Whatever ability lies in the storehouse is in peril — and once the peril is formed, there is no time to change it. Calamity grows and thickens; returning from it takes work; coming to fruit takes long. Yet men count these their own treasures — is it not sad! Therefore there are ruined states and slaughtered people without end, and none knows to ask after this.

## Section 30

**ZH:** 故足之於地也踐，雖踐，恃其所不蹍而後善博也；人之於知也少，雖少，恃其所不知而後知天之所謂也。知大一，知大陰，知大目，知大均，知大方，知大信，知大定，至矣。大一通之，大陰解之，大目視之，大均緣之，大方體之，大信稽之，大定持之。

**EN:** The foot treads but a little of the earth; yet treading on that little, it is by relying on what it does not tread that it walks abroad well. Man's knowledge is little; yet little as it is, it is by relying on what he does not know that he knows what Heaven means. To know the Great One, the Great Darkness, the Great Eye, the Great Level, the Great Method, the Great Trust, the Great Fixity — that is the utmost. The Great One passes it through; the Great Darkness unlooses it; the Great Eye sees it; the Great Level follows it; the Great Method embodies it; the Great Trust examines it; the Great Fixity holds it.

## Section 31

**ZH:** 盡有天，循有照，冥有樞，始有彼。則其解之也似不解之者，其知之也似不知之也，不知而後知之。其問之也，不可以有崖，而不可以無崖。頡滑有實，古今不代，而不可以虧，則可不謂有大揚搉乎！闔不亦問是已，奚惑然為！以不惑解惑，復於不惑，是尚大不惑。

**EN:** At the end there is Heaven; in following there is light; in darkness there is a hinge; in the beginning there is that other. So his understanding of it is like one who does not understand; his knowing of it is like one who does not know — not knowing, and afterward he knows. In asking about it, one may neither set a bound nor do without a bound. The slippery and sliding has its substance; past and present do not replace it — and it cannot be diminished. May this not be called a great disclosure! Why not then ask after this — what is there to be bewildered about! To undo bewilderment with the unbewildered, to return to the unbewildered — this is to honor the great unbewilderment.
