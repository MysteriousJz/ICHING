# Metaphorical Language (寓言)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Miscellaneous Chapters _ Metaphorical Language - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (8 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 寓言十九，重言十七，卮言日出，和以天倪。寓言十九，藉外論之。親父不為其子媒。親父譽之，不若非其父者也；非吾罪也，人之罪也。與己同則應，不與己同則反，同於己為是之，異於己為非之。重言十七，所以已言也，是為耆艾。年先矣，而無經緯本末以期年耆者，是非先也。人而無以先人，無人道也；人而無人道，是之謂陳人。
**PY:** yù yán shí jiǔ， zhòng yán shí qī， zhī yán rì chū， hé yǐ tiān ní。 yù yán shí jiǔ， jí wài lùn zhī。 qīn fù bù wèi qí zi méi。 qīn fù yù zhī， bù ruò fēi qí fù zhě yě； fēi wú zuì yě， rén zhī zuì yě。 yǔ jǐ tóng zé yīng， bù yǔ jǐ tóng zé fǎn， tóng yú jǐ wèi shì zhī， yì yú jǐ wèi fēi zhī。 zhòng yán shí qī， suǒ yǐ yǐ yán yě， shì wèi qí ài。 nián xiān yǐ， ér wú jīng wěi běn mò yǐ qī nián qí zhě， shì fēi xiān yě。 rén ér wú yǐ xiān rén， wú rén dào yě； rén ér wú rén dào， shì zhī wèi chén rén。

**EN:** Lodging-words are nine in ten; weighty words are seven in ten; goblet-words come forth daily, harmonized by the natural boundaries. Lodging-words are nine in ten: they borrow what is outside to discuss it. A father does not act as go-between for his own son. A father's praising his son does not compare with another's praising him. It is not my fault — it is the fault of men: they answer what agrees with themselves and oppose what does not; they count agreement with themselves right and difference from themselves wrong. Weighty words are seven in ten: they are the words by which speech is ended — the words of elders. But where the years are first, yet there is neither warp nor woof, root nor branch, to fulfill the elder's years — that is not truly first. A man with nothing to set himself ahead of others has no humanity's way; a man without humanity's way is called a stale man.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 卮言日出，和以天倪，因以曼衍，所以窮年。不言則齊，齊與言不齊，言與齊不齊也，故曰無言。言無言，終身言，未嘗言；終身不言，未嘗不言。有自也而可，有自也而不可；有自也而然，有自也而不然。惡乎然？然於然。惡乎不然？不然於不然。惡乎可？可於可。惡乎不可？不可於不可。物固有所然，物固有所可，無物不然，無物不可。非卮言日出，和以天倪，孰得其久！萬物皆種也，以不同形相禪，始卒若環，莫得其倫，是謂天均。天均者，天倪也。
**PY:** zhī yán rì chū， hé yǐ tiān ní， yīn yǐ màn yǎn， suǒ yǐ qióng nián。 bù yán zé qí， qí yǔ yán bù qí， yán yǔ qí bù qí yě， gù yuē wú yán。 yán wú yán， zhōng shēn yán， wèi cháng yán； zhōng shēn bù yán， wèi cháng bù yán。 yǒu zì yě ér kě， yǒu zì yě ér bù kě； yǒu zì yě ér rán， yǒu zì yě ér bù rán。 è hū rán？ rán yú rán。 è hū bù rán？ bù rán yú bù rán。 è hū kě？ kě yú kě。 è hū bù kě？ bù kě yú bù kě。 wù gù yǒu suǒ rán， wù gù yǒu suǒ kě， wú wù bù rán， wú wù bù kě。 fēi zhī yán rì chū， hé yǐ tiān ní， shú dé qí jiǔ！ wàn wù jiē zhǒng yě， yǐ bù tóng xíng xiāng chán， shǐ zú ruò huán， mò dé qí lún， shì wèi tiān jūn。 tiān jūn zhě， tiān ní yě。

**EN:** Goblet-words come forth daily, harmonized by the natural boundaries, followed as they flow and spread — this is the way to live out one's years. Without speech there is evenness; evenness with speech is unevenness; speech with evenness is unevenness. Hence it is said: speechlessness. Speak speechlessness: a whole life of speaking, and nothing has been spoken; a whole life of not speaking, and nothing has been unspoken. There is that from which it is admissible; there is that from which it is inadmissible; there is that from which it is so; there is that from which it is not so. How so? It is so in being so. How not so? It is not so in being not so. How admissible? It is admissible in being admissible. How inadmissible? It is inadmissible in being inadmissible. Things inherently have that by which they are so; things inherently have that by which they are admissible. There is nothing that is not so; nothing that is not admissible. But for goblet-words coming forth daily, harmonized by the natural boundaries — who could last long! The ten thousand things are all seeds; with different forms they hand on to one another; beginning and end are like a ring; none can find its order. This is called the potter's wheel of Heaven. The potter's wheel of Heaven is the natural boundaries.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 莊子謂惠子曰：「孔子行年六十而六十化，始時所是，卒而非之，未知今之所謂是之非五十九年非也。」惠子曰：「孔子勤志服知也。」莊子曰：「孔子謝之矣，而其未之嘗言。孔子云：『夫受才乎大本，復靈以生。』鳴而當律，言而當法，利義陳乎前，而好惡是非直服人之口而已矣。使人乃以心服而不敢蘁立，定天下之定。已乎已乎！吾且不得及彼乎！」
**PY:** zhuāng zi wèi huì zi yuē：「 kǒng zi xíng nián liù shí ér liù shí huà， shǐ shí suǒ shì， zú ér fēi zhī， wèi zhī jīn zhī suǒ wèi shì zhī fēi wǔ shí jiǔ nián fēi yě。」 huì zi yuē：「 kǒng zi qín zhì fú zhī yě。」 zhuāng zi yuē：「 kǒng zi xiè zhī yǐ， ér qí wèi zhī cháng yán。 kǒng zi yún：『 fū shòu cái hū dà běn， fù líng yǐ shēng。』 míng ér dāng lǜ， yán ér dāng fǎ， lì yì chén hū qián， ér hǎo è shì fēi zhí fú rén zhī kǒu ér yǐ yǐ。 shǐ rén nǎi yǐ xīn fú ér bù gǎn wù lì， dìng tiān xià zhī dìng。 yǐ hū yǐ hū！ wú qiě bù dé jí bǐ hū！」

**EN:** Zhuangzi said to Huizi: "Confucius at sixty had transformed sixty times; what he at first approved he afterward rejected. He could not know whether what he now calls right is not the wrong of fifty-nine years." Huizi said: "Confucius labored his will and served his knowledge." Zhuangzi said: "Confucius has renounced all that — yet he never said so. Confucius said: 'Receive the capacity from the great root; return to spirit to live.' His cry fits the pitch-pipes; his speech fits the law; benefit and rightness lie before him, but likes and dislikes, right and wrong, are merely what subdues men's mouths. To make men submit in heart and not dare to stand defiant — that settles the settlement of the world. Enough, enough! I shall not come up to him!"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 曾子再仕而心再化，曰：「吾及親仕，三釜而心樂；後仕，三千鍾而不洎，吾心悲。」弟子問於仲尼曰：「若參者，可謂無所縣其罪乎？」曰：「既已縣矣。夫無所縣者，可以有哀乎？彼視三釜、三千鍾，如觀雀蚊虻相過乎前也。」
**PY:** céng zi zài shì ér xīn zài huà， yuē：「 wú jí qīn shì， sān fǔ ér xīn lè； hòu shì， sān qiān zhōng ér bù jì， wú xīn bēi。」 dì zǐ wèn yú zhòng ní yuē：「 ruò cān zhě， kě wèi wú suǒ xiàn qí zuì hū？」 yuē：「 jì yǐ xiàn yǐ。 fū wú suǒ xiàn zhě， kě yǐ yǒu āi hū？ bǐ shì sān fǔ、 sān qiān zhōng， rú guān què wén méng xiāng guò hū qián yě。」

**EN:** Zengzi, twice in office, had his heart twice transformed. He said: "While my parents lived and I served, three cauldrons of pay, and my heart was glad. Serving afterward, three thousand measures, never reaching them — my heart was grieved." A disciple asked Confucius: "Can Shen be said to be free of the hanging fault?" He said: "He is already hung. Were he free of the hanging, could he feel sorrow? He would look on three cauldrons and three thousand measures as sparrows, mosquitoes, and flies passing before him."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 顏成子游謂東郭子綦曰：自吾聞子之言，一年而野，二年而從，三年而通，四年而物，五年而來，六年而鬼入，七年而天成，八年而不知死、不知生，九年而大妙。
**PY:** yán chéng zi yóu wèi dōng guō zi qí yuē： zì wú wén zi zhī yán， yī nián ér yě， èr nián ér cóng， sān nián ér tōng， sì nián ér wù， wǔ nián ér lái， liù nián ér guǐ rù， qī nián ér tiān chéng， bā nián ér bù zhī sǐ、 bù zhī shēng， jiǔ nián ér dà miào。

**EN:** Yancheng Ziyou said to Dongguo Ziqi: "Since I heard your words: in one year I grew wild; in two years I followed; in three years I was through; in four years I was one with things; in five years it came; in six years ghosts entered; in seven years Heaven completed; in eight years I knew not death, knew not life; in nine years — the great mystery."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 生有為，死也。勸公：以其死也，有自也；而生陽也，無自也。而果然乎？惡乎其所適？惡乎其所不適？天有曆數，地有人據，吾惡乎求之？莫知其所終，若之何其無命也？莫知其所始，若之何其有命也？有以相應也，若之何其無鬼邪？無以相應也，若之何其有鬼邪？」
**PY:** shēng yǒu wèi， sǐ yě。 quàn gōng： yǐ qí sǐ yě， yǒu zì yě； ér shēng yáng yě， wú zì yě。 ér guǒ rán hū？ è hū qí suǒ shì？ è hū qí suǒ bù shì？ tiān yǒu lì shù， dì yǒu rén jù， wú è hū qiú zhī？ mò zhī qí suǒ zhōng， ruò zhī hé qí wú mìng yě？ mò zhī qí suǒ shǐ， ruò zhī hé qí yǒu mìng yě？ yǒu yǐ xiāng yīng yě， ruò zhī hé qí wú guǐ xié？ wú yǐ xiāng yīng yě， ruò zhī hé qí yǒu guǐ xié？」

**EN:** "Life, in having action, is death. I counsel the common: as for its dying, there is that from which it comes; as for life's light, there is nothing from which it comes. [uncertain: §6's opening argument on 有為/無自 is among the chapter's densest cruxes; rendered literally with the ambiguity kept.] Are you really so? What is fitting for you? What is unfitting for you? Heaven has its calendar and numbers; earth has what men rely upon — where shall I seek it? None knows its end — how can there be no destiny? None knows its beginning — how can there be destiny? Where there is something answering — how can there be no ghosts? Where there is nothing answering — how can there be ghosts?"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 眾罔兩問於景曰：「若向也俯而今也仰，向也括而今被髮，向也坐而今也起，向也行而今也止，何也？」景曰：「搜搜也，奚稍問也？予有而不知其所以。予，蜩甲也，蛇蛻也，似之而非也。火與日，吾屯也；陰與夜，吾代也。彼，吾所以有待邪？而況乎以有待者乎！彼來則我與之來，彼往則我與之往，彼強陽則我與之強陽。強陽者，又何以有問乎！」
**PY:** zhòng wǎng liǎng wèn yú jǐng yuē：「 ruò xiàng yě fǔ ér jīn yě yǎng， xiàng yě kuò ér jīn bèi fà， xiàng yě zuò ér jīn yě qǐ， xiàng yě xíng ér jīn yě zhǐ， hé yě？」 jǐng yuē：「 sōu sōu yě， xī shāo wèn yě？ yǔ yǒu ér bù zhī qí suǒ yǐ。 yǔ， tiáo jiǎ yě， shé tuì yě， shì zhī ér fēi yě。 huǒ yǔ rì， wú tún yě； yīn yǔ yè， wú dài yě。 bǐ， wú suǒ yǐ yǒu dài xié？ ér kuàng hū yǐ yǒu dài zhě hū！ bǐ lái zé wǒ yǔ zhī lái， bǐ wǎng zé wǒ yǔ zhī wǎng， bǐ qiáng yáng zé wǒ yǔ zhī qiáng yáng。 qiáng yáng zhě， yòu hé yǐ yǒu wèn hū！」

**EN:** The crowd of penumbras asked Shadow: "A moment ago you were bowed, now you are upright; a moment ago bound-haired, now loose-haired; a moment ago sitting, now standing; a moment ago walking, now stopping. Why?" Shadow said: "Such trifles — why bother asking! I have it, without knowing how. I am the cicada's husk, the snake's slough: like it, yet not it. Fire and sun — I gather; shade and night — I vanish. Is that other what I depend upon? How much more when that other itself depends! It comes, and I come with it; it goes, and I go with it; it roams strong, and I roam strong with it. One that roams strong — what is there to ask about!"

## Section 8

**ZH:** 陽子居南之沛，老聃西遊於秦，邀於郊，至於梁而遇老子。老子中道仰天而歎曰：「始以汝為可教，今不可也。」陽子居不答。至舍，進盥漱巾櫛，脫屨戶外，膝行而前曰：「向者弟子欲請夫子，夫子行不閒，是以不敢。今閒矣，請問其過。」老子曰：「而睢睢盱盱，而誰與居？大白若辱，盛德若不足。」陽子居蹴然變容曰：「敬聞命矣。」其往也，舍者迎將其家，公執席，妻執巾櫛，舍者避席，煬者避灶。其反也，舍者與之爭席矣。
**PY:** yáng zi jū nán zhī pèi， lǎo dān xī yóu yú qín， yāo yú jiāo， zhì yú liáng ér yù lǎo zi。 lǎo zi zhōng dào yǎng tiān ér tàn yuē：「 shǐ yǐ rǔ wèi kě jiào， jīn bù kě yě。」 yáng zi jū bù dá。 zhì shě， jìn guàn shù jīn zhì， tuō jù hù wài， xī xíng ér qián yuē：「 xiàng zhě dì zǐ yù qǐng fū zǐ， fū zǐ xíng bù xián， shì yǐ bù gǎn。 jīn xián yǐ， qǐng wèn qí guò。」 lǎo zi yuē：「 ér suī suī xū xū， ér shuí yǔ jū？ dà bái ruò rǔ， shèng dé ruò bù zú。」 yáng zi jū cù rán biàn róng yuē：「 jìng wén mìng yǐ。」 qí wǎng yě， shě zhě yíng jiāng qí jiā， gōng zhí xí， qī zhí jīn zhì， shě zhě bì xí， yáng zhě bì zào。 qí fǎn yě， shě zhě yǔ zhī zhēng xí yǐ。

**EN:** Yang Ziju was going south to Pei; Lao Dan was roaming west in Qin. Yang Ziju went out to the outskirts to meet him, and at Liang he encountered Laozi. Laozi, mid-road, looked up to heaven and sighed: "At first I thought you could be taught; now you cannot." Yang Ziju made no answer. Coming to the lodging, he presented the washing-water, towel, and comb, left his shoes outside the door, and came forward on his knees: "Just now, your disciple wished to ask you, Master; you were traveling without leisure, so I dared not. Now there is leisure — I beg to ask about my fault." Laozi said: "You — so puffed and staring — who could dwell with you! Greatest whiteness looks soiled; abundant virtue looks insufficient." Yang Ziju, startled, changed countenance: "I have heard your instruction with reverence." On his going, the inn's master had come out to welcome his party, the lord himself held his mat, the wife held his towel and comb; the other guests left their mats; the fire-warmers left the stove. On his return, the guests contended with him for mats.

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 天倪 → "the natural boundaries"; 天均 → "the potter's wheel of Heaven"; 寓言/重言/卮言 → "lodging-words / weighty words / goblet-words"; 道 → "the Way"; 德 → "virtue"; 情 not prominent here.
- §§1–2 are the book's own poetics — the three modes of speech it uses — and §5's nine-year course and §7's penumbra dialogue are set pieces; all rendered with their formal structure intact.
- `[uncertain]` flags: 1 (§6 opening argument).
