# The Robber Zhi (盜跖)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Miscellaneous Chapters _ The Robber Zhi - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (19 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 孔子與柳下季為友。柳下季之弟名曰盜跖。盜跖從卒九千人，橫行天下，侵暴諸侯，穴室樞戶，驅人牛馬，取人婦女，貪得忘親，不顧父母兄弟，不祭先祖。所過之邑，大國守城，小國入保，萬民苦之。

**EN:** Confucius was friends with Liuxia Ji (柳下季). Liuxia Ji's younger brother was named Robber Zhi (盜跖). Robber Zhi, with nine thousand followers, rampaged across the world, raiding and ravaging the lords, boring through chambers and wrenching doors, driving off men's cattle and horses, carrying off men's wives and daughters, greedy for gain and forgetful of kin, with no thought for father, mother, or brothers, and no sacrifice to the ancestors. Wherever his band passed, great states manned their walls and small states fled into forts; the ten thousand people suffered.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 孔子謂柳下季曰：「夫為人父者，必能詔其子；為人兄者，必能教其弟。若父不能詔其子，兄不能教其弟，則無貴父子兄弟之親矣。今先生，世之才士也，弟為盜跖，為天下害，而弗能教也，丘竊為先生羞之。丘請為先生往說之。」柳下季曰：「先生言『為人父者必能詔其子，為人兄者必能教其弟』，若子不聽父之詔，弟不受兄之教，雖今先生之辯，將奈之何哉？且跖之為人也，心如涌泉，意如飄風，強足以距敵，辯足以飾非，順其心則喜，逆其心則怒，易辱人以言。先生必無往。」

**EN:** Confucius said to Liuxia Ji: "One who is a father must be able to instruct his son; one who is an elder brother must be able to teach his younger brother. If the father cannot instruct his son and the brother cannot teach his brother, then the kinship of father, son, and brothers is worth nothing. Now you, sir, are the talented scholar of the age — and your brother is Robber Zhi, a plague on the world, and you cannot teach him. Qiu, in private, is ashamed for you. I beg leave to go and persuade him for you." Liuxia Ji said: "You say, sir, 'a father must instruct his son; an elder brother must teach his younger brother.' But if the son will not heed the father's instruction and the brother will not take the brother's teaching, what can even your persuasion, sir, do about it? Besides, Zhi as a man: his heart is a gushing spring; his will a whirlwind; his strength suffices to repel foes; his eloquence suffices to adorn wrong; please his heart and he is glad; cross his heart and he rages; and he is quick to insult men with words. You must certainly not go, sir."

## Section 3

**ZH:** 孔子不聽，顏回為御，子貢為右，往見盜跖。盜跖乃方休卒徒太山之陽，膾人肝而餔之。孔子下車而前，見謁者曰：「魯人孔丘，聞將軍高義，敬再拜謁者。」謁者入通，盜跖聞之大怒，目如明星，髮上指冠，曰：「此夫魯國之巧偽人孔丘非邪？為我告之：『爾作言造語，妄稱文、武，冠枝木之冠，帶死牛之脅，多辭繆說，不耕而食，不織而衣，搖脣鼓舌，擅生是非，以迷天下之主，使天下學士不反其本，妄作孝弟而儌倖於封侯富貴者也。子之罪大極重，疾走歸！不然，我將以子肝益晝餔之膳。』」

**EN:** Confucius did not heed him. With Yan Hui driving and Zigong at his right, he went to see Robber Zhi. Robber Zhi was just then resting his troops on the south side of Mount Tai, mincing men's livers for his meal. Confucius dismounted and came forward; addressing the usher, he said: "Kong Qiu of Lu, having heard of the general's lofty rightness, respectfully bows twice to the usher." The usher went in and announced him. Robber Zhi, hearing it, raged greatly: his eyes like bright stars, his hair bristling up under his cap. "Is this not that crafty fraud Kong Qiu of Lu? Tell him for me: 'You fashion words and fabricate speech; you recklessly invoke Kings Wen and Wu; you wear a branching-tree cap and a dead-ox belt; you prattle and babble; you eat without ploughing and dress without weaving; you wag your lips and drum your tongue, breeding right and wrong at will, to delude the lords of the world, so that the scholars of the world never return to their root, and vainly ape filial piety and brotherhood to fish for fiefs and riches. Your crime is most grave and heavy. Run home at once! Otherwise I will add your liver to my midday meal.'"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 孔子復通曰：「丘得幸於季，願望履幕下。」謁者復通，盜跖曰：「使來前！」孔子趨而進，避席反走，再拜盜跖。盜跖大怒，兩展其足，案劍瞋目，聲如乳虎，曰：「丘來前！若所言，順吾意則生，逆吾心則死。」

**EN:** Confucius again requested admittance: "Qiu is favored with Ji; I beg leave to tread beneath his tent." The usher again announced him. Robber Zhi said: "Let him come forward!" Confucius hurried in, drew back from the mat, and bowed twice to Robber Zhi. Robber Zhi, raging greatly, stretched out both legs, hand on his sword, glaring, his voice like a nursing tigress: "Qiu, come forward! What you are about to say — if it pleases my mind you live; if it crosses my heart you die."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 孔子曰：「丘聞之，凡天下有三德：生而長大，美好無雙，少長貴賤見而皆說之，此上德也；知維天地，能辯諸物，此中德也；勇悍果敢，聚眾率兵，此下德也。凡人有此一德者，足以南面稱孤矣。今將軍兼此三者，身長八尺二寸，面目有光，脣如激丹，齒如齊貝，音中黃鐘，而名曰盜跖，丘竊為將軍恥不取焉。將軍有意聽臣，臣請南使吳、越，北使齊、魯，東使宋、衛，西使晉、楚，使為將軍造大城數百里，立數十萬戶之邑，尊將軍為諸侯，與天下更始，罷兵休卒，收養昆弟，共祭先祖。此聖人才士之行，而天下之願也。」

**EN:** Confucius said: "I have heard that under heaven there are three virtues. To be born tall and great, fair beyond compare, so that young and old, high and low, all delight to look upon him — that is the highest virtue. Knowledge that takes heaven and earth in its compass, ability to distinguish all things — that is the middle virtue. Courage, boldness, daring resolve, gathering the multitude and leading troops — that is the lowest virtue. Any man having one of these virtues suffices to face south and call himself lord. Now you, General, possess all three: eight feet two inches in stature, your face radiant, your lips like red lacquer, your teeth like even shells, your voice attuned to Yellow Bell — and you are named 'Robber Zhi.' In private I am ashamed for you, General, that you will not take better. If you, General, are willing to hear me, I beg to go south as envoy to Wu and Yue, north to Qi and Lu, east to Song and Wei, west to Jin and Chu, to have them build you a great walled city of several hundred li, establish a town of several hundred thousand households, honor you as a lord, and begin anew with the world: laying down arms and resting the troops, gathering and nourishing your brothers, and joining in sacrifice to the ancestors. This is the conduct of sages and talented scholars, and the wish of all the world."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 盜跖大怒曰：「丘來前！夫可規以利而可諫以言者，皆愚陋恆民之謂耳。今長大美好，人見而悅之者，此吾父母之遺德也。丘雖不吾譽，吾獨不自知邪？且吾聞之：『好面譽人者，亦好背而毀之。』今丘告我以大城眾民，是欲規我以利而恆民畜我也，安可久長也？城之大者，莫大乎天下矣。堯、舜有天下，子孫無置錐之地，湯、武立為天子而後世絕滅，非以其利大故邪？

**EN:** Robber Zhi raged greatly: "Qiu, come forward! Men who can be counseled with profit and admonished with words — those are called foolish, vulgar, ordinary folk. Now as to my being tall and fair, so that men delight to look on me — that is the virtue left me by my father and mother. Even if you, Qiu, had not praised me, would I not know it myself? Besides, I have heard it said: 'One who loves to praise you to your face loves to slander you behind your back.' Now you, Qiu, tell me of a great city and many people — you wish to counsel me with profit and keep me like ordinary cattle. How could that last long! The greatest of cities is none greater than the world. Yao and Shun had the world — their descendants had not ground to stand an awl on. Tang and Wu stood as Sons of Heaven — their posterity is extinct and cut off. Is that not because their profit was great?"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 且吾聞之：古者禽獸多而人少，於是民皆巢居以避之，晝拾橡栗，暮栖木上，故命之曰有巢氏之民。古者民不知衣服，夏多積薪，冬則煬之，故命之曰知生之民。神農之世，臥則居居，起則于于，民知其母，不知其父，與麋鹿共處，耕而食，織而衣，無有相害之心，此至德之隆也。然而黃帝不能致德，與蚩尤戰於涿鹿之野，流血百里。堯、舜作，立群臣，湯放其主，武王殺紂。自是之後，以強陵弱，以眾暴寡。湯、武以來，皆亂人之徒也。

**EN:** "Moreover, I have heard: in ancient times beasts were many and men were few; so the people all dwelt in nests to avoid them, gathering acorns and chestnuts by day and roosting in the trees by night. Therefore they are called the people of the Nest-Having house. In ancient times the people knew no clothing; in summer they heaped much firewood; in winter they warmed themselves by it. Therefore they are called the Life-Knowing people. In the age of Shennong, lying down they were at peace; rising they were at ease; the people knew their mothers, not their fathers; they dwelt together with elk and deer; they ploughed and ate, wove and were clothed, with no thought of injuring one another. This was the height of utmost virtue. Yet the Yellow Emperor could not attain such virtue: he fought Chiyou on the field of Zhuolu, and the blood flowed a hundred li. Yao and Shun arose and set up the host of ministers; Tang exiled his sovereign; King Wu killed Zhou. From then on, the strong have oppressed the weak, the many have ravaged the few. From Tang and Wu down, all have been men of disorder."

## Section 8

**ZH:** 今子修文、武之道，掌天下之辯，以教後世，縫衣淺帶，矯言偽行，以迷惑天下之主，而欲求富貴焉，盜莫大於子。天下何故不謂子為盜丘而乃謂我為盜跖？子以甘辭說子路而使從之，使子路去其危冠，解其長劍，而受教於子，天下皆曰『孔丘能止暴禁非』。其卒之也，子路欲殺衛君而事不成，身菹於衛東門之上，是子教之不至也。子自謂才士聖人邪！則再逐於魯，削跡於衛，窮於齊，圍於陳、蔡，不容身於天下。子教子路菹此患，上無以為身，下無以為人，子之道豈足貴邪？

**EN:** "Now you cultivate the ways of Wen and Wu, wield the world's disputations to teach later ages; in your sewn robes and shallow sash, with feigned words and false conduct, you delude the lords of the world while seeking riches and rank. No thief is greater than you. Why does the world not call you 'Robber Qiu' instead of calling me 'Robber Zhi'? With sweet words you persuaded Zilu to follow you; you made Zilu put off his tall cap, ungird his long sword, and receive your teaching — and all the world said, 'Kong Qiu can stop violence and forbid wrong.' Yet in the end, Zilu tried to kill the lord of Wei and failed, and his body was pickled above Wei's eastern gate — such is the insufficiency of your teaching. You call yourself a talented scholar, a sage! Yet twice driven out of Lu, your tracks wiped out in Wei, dead-ended in Qi, besieged between Chen and Cai, with no room for your person under heaven. You taught Zilu to be pickled for that calamity: above, no way to keep yourself; below, no way to help others. How could your way be worth prizing!"

## Section 9

**ZH:** 世之所高，莫若黃帝，黃帝尚不能全德，而戰涿鹿之野，流血百里。堯不慈，舜不孝，禹偏枯，湯放其主，武王伐紂，文王拘羑里。此六子者，世之所高也，孰論之，皆以利惑其真而強反其情性，其行乃甚可羞也！

**EN:** "Whom the world exalts — none more than the Yellow Emperor. Yet even the Yellow Emperor could not keep virtue whole: he fought on the field of Zhuolu, and the blood flowed a hundred li. Yao was not kind; Shun was not filial; Yu was half-withered; Tang exiled his sovereign; King Wu attacked Zhou; King Wen was jailed at Youli. These six are the men the world exalts. Reckon them up: every one, confused by profit, lost his truth and forcibly turned his genuine nature backward. Their conduct is most shameful!"

## Section 10

**ZH:** 世之所謂賢士，伯夷、叔齊，伯夷、叔齊辭孤竹之君，而餓死於首陽之山，骨肉不葬。鮑焦飾行非世，抱木而死。申徒狄諫而不聽，負石自投於河，為魚鱉所食。介子推至忠也，自割其股以食文公，文公後背之，子推怒而去，抱木而燔死。尾生與女子期於梁下，女子不來，水至不去，抱梁柱而死。此六子者，無異於磔犬、流豕、操瓢而乞者，皆離名輕死，不念本養壽命者也。

**EN:** "Whom the world calls worthy scholars — Boyi and Shuqi. Boyi and Shuqi declined the lordship of Guzhu and starved to death on Mount Shouyang, their bones unburied. Bao Jiao adorned his conduct to condemn the age and died hugging a tree. Shentu Di remonstrated and was not heeded; bearing a stone, he threw himself in the river and was eaten by fish and turtles. Jie Zhitui, loyal to the utmost, cut flesh from his own thigh to feed Duke Wen; when Duke Wen afterward turned on him, Zhitui, enraged, withdrew, hugged a tree, and was burned to death. Weisheng made tryst with a girl beneath a bridge; the girl did not come; the waters came, and he would not leave; hugging the bridge-pillar, he died. These six men — no different from a butchered dog, a drowned pig, a beggar with his gourd: all clung to a name, made light of death, and gave no thought to the root or to nourishing their allotted span."

## Section 11

**ZH:** 世之所謂忠臣者，莫若王子比干、伍子胥，子胥沈江，比干剖心。此二子者，世謂忠臣也，然卒為天下笑。自上觀之，至於子胥、比干，皆不足貴也。

**EN:** "Whom the world calls loyal ministers — none more than Prince Bigan and Wu Zixu. Zixu was sunk in the river; Bigan had his heart cut open. These two the world calls loyal ministers — yet they ended as the world's laughingstock. Seen from above, down to Zixu and Bigan, none is worth prizing."

## Section 12

**ZH:** 丘之所以說我者，若告我以鬼事，則我不能知也；若告我以人事者，不過此矣，皆吾所聞知也。今吾告子以人之情：目欲視色，耳欲聽聲，口欲察味，志氣欲盈。人上壽百歲，中壽八十，下壽六十，除病瘦、死喪、憂患，其中開口而笑者，一月之中不過四五日而已矣。天與地無窮，人死者有時，操有時之具而託於無窮之間，忽然無異騏驥之馳過隙也。不能說其志意，養其壽命者，皆非通道者也。丘之所言，皆吾之所棄也，亟去走歸，無復言之！子之道，狂狂汲汲，詐巧虛偽事也，非可以全真也，奚足論哉？」

**EN:** "What you use to persuade me — if you tell me of ghostly matters, I cannot judge them; if you tell me of human matters, it is no more than this — all of it I have heard and know. Now let me tell you of the genuine feelings of men. The eye wishes to see colors; the ear wishes to hear sounds; the mouth wishes to discern flavors; the will and breath wish to be fulfilled. Long life is a hundred years; middling, eighty; short, sixty. Take away illness, mourning, worry, and trouble — the days in a month when a man opens his mouth and laughs are no more than four or five. Heaven and earth are endless; man's death has its time. To take a tool of a fixed time and lodge it in the endless — sudden as a thoroughbred racing past a crack. One who cannot delight his will and nourish his allotted span is no one who has penetrated the Way. All that you, Qiu, say, I cast away. Begone at once, run home, and speak no more! Your way is mad and bustling — crafty, false, deceitful work. It cannot keep the truth whole. Why is it worth discussing!"

## Section 13

**ZH:** 孔子再拜趨走，出門上車，執轡三失，目芒然無見，色若死灰，據軾低頭，不能出氣。歸到魯東門外，適遇柳下季。柳下季曰：「今者闕然數日不見，車馬有行色，得微往見跖邪？」孔子仰天而歎曰：「然。」柳下季曰：「跖得無逆汝意若前乎？」孔子曰：「然。丘所謂無病而自灸也，疾走料虎頭，編虎須，幾不免虎口哉！」

**EN:** Confucius bowed twice and hurried out; mounting the carriage, he fumbled the reins three times; his eyes were blank and sightless, his face like dead ashes; leaning on the rail with bowed head, he could not breathe. Returning to outside Lu's eastern gate, he happened to meet Liuxia Ji. Liuxia Ji said: "These several days you have been missing; your carriage has the look of travel. Did you perhaps go to see Zhi?" Confucius looked up to heaven and sighed: "Yes." Liuxia Ji said: "Did Zhi not cross your mind, as I warned?" Confucius said: "Yes. What they call moxa-burning oneself without illness — I ran to poke a tiger's head and plait its whiskers; I nearly did not escape the tiger's mouth!"

## Section 14

**ZH:** 子張問於滿苟得曰：「盍不為行？無行則不信，不信則不任，不任則不利。故觀之名，計之利，而義真是也。若棄名利，反之於心，則夫士之為行，不可一日不為乎？」滿苟得曰：「無恥者富，多信者顯。夫名利之大者，幾在無恥而信。故觀之名，計之利，而信真是也。若棄名利，反之於心，則夫士之為行，抱其天乎！」

**EN:** Zizhang (子張) asked Man Goude (滿苟得): "Why do you not practice conduct? Without conduct one is not trusted; untrusted, one is not employed; unemployed, one does not profit. So looking to name and reckoning profit — rightness is true indeed. But if one casts aside name and profit and returns it to the heart — then a scholar's practice of conduct must not be neglected a single day, must it?" Man Goude said: "The shameless grow rich; the much-trusted win fame. The greatest of name and profit nearly lies in being shameless and trusted. So looking to name and reckoning profit — trust is true indeed. But if one casts aside name and profit and returns it to the heart — then a scholar's practice of conduct is to embrace his Heaven!"

## Section 15

**ZH:** 子張曰：「昔者桀、紂貴為天子，富有天下，今謂臧聚曰『汝行如桀、紂』，則有怍色，有不服之心者，小人所賤也。仲尼、墨翟，窮為匹夫，今謂宰相曰『子行如仲尼、墨翟』，則變容易色稱不足者，士誠貴也。故勢為天子，未必貴也；窮為匹夫，未必賤也。貴賤之分，在行之美惡。」滿苟得曰：「小盜者拘，大盜者為諸侯，諸侯之門，義士存焉。昔者桓公小白殺兄入嫂而管仲為臣，田成子常殺君竊國而孔子受幣。論則賤之，行則下之，則是言行之情悖戰於胸中也，不亦拂乎！故《書》曰：『孰惡孰美？成者為首，不成者為尾。』」

**EN:** Zizhang said: "Of old Jie and Zhou were exalted as Sons of Heaven, rich with all the world; yet tell a common servant 'your conduct is like Jie and Zhou' and he blushes — the defiant heart of small men counts them base. Confucius and Mo Di were poor commoners; yet tell a chief minister 'your conduct is like Confucius and Mo Di' and he changes countenance and calls himself unworthy — that scholars are truly honored. So to stand as Son of Heaven is not necessarily honor; to be a poor commoner is not necessarily baseness. The division of high and low lies in the beauty and ugliness of conduct." Man Goude said: "The petty thief is jailed; the great thief becomes a lord; at the lords' gates the righteous scholars are found. Of old Duke Huan Xiaobai killed his brother and took his sister-in-law, and Guan Zhong served as his minister; Tian Chengzi Chang killed his lord and stole the state, and Confucius took his silks. To condemn them in discourse and bow to them in conduct — then words and deeds war in the breast. Is that not a perversion! Therefore the Book says: 'Who is ugly, who is fair? He who succeeds is the head; he who fails is the tail.'"

## Section 16

**ZH:** 子張曰：「子不為行，即將疏戚無倫，貴賤無義，長幼無序，五紀六位將何以為別乎？」滿苟得曰：「堯殺長子，舜流母弟，疏戚有倫乎？湯放桀，武王伐紂，貴賤有義乎？王季為適，周公殺兄，長幼有序乎？儒者偽辭，墨者兼愛，五紀六位將有別乎？且子正為名，我正為利。名利之實，不順於理，不監於道。吾日與子訟於無約，曰：『小人殉財，君子殉名。其所以變其情，易其性，則異矣；乃至於棄其所為而殉其所不為，則一也。』故曰：無為小人，反殉而天；無為君子，從天之理。若枉若直，相而天極，面觀四方，與時消息。若是若非，執而圓機，獨成而意，與道徘徊。無轉而行，無成而義，將失而所為。無赴而富，無殉而成，將棄而天。比干剖心，子胥抉眼，忠之禍也；直躬證父，尾生溺死，信之患也；鮑子立乾，申子不自理，廉之害也；孔子不見母，匡子不見父，義之失也。此上世之所傳，下世之所語，以為士者正其言，必其行，故服其殃，離其患也。」

**EN:** Zizhang said: "If you do not practice conduct, then distant and close will have no order, high and low no rightness, old and young no sequence — how are the five rules and six stations to be distinguished?" Man Goude said: "Yao killed his eldest son; Shun banished his own brother — was there order in distant and close? Tang exiled Jie; King Wu attacked Zhou — was there rightness in high and low? King Ji took the succession; the Duke of Zhou killed his elder brother — was there sequence in old and young? The Confucians' feigned words, the Mohists' universal love — are the five rules and six stations thereby distinguished? Besides, you are rectified for name; I am rectified for profit. The substance of name and profit is neither in accord with pattern nor mirrored in the Way. I debated with you once at Wuyue (無約), saying: 'The small man is martyred to wealth; the gentleman is martyred to a name. Wherein they change their feelings and exchange their nature differs; but that they abandon what they do and martyr themselves to what they do not do — that is one.' Hence it is said: Do not be a small man — turn back and be martyred to your Heaven; do not be a gentleman — follow Heaven's pattern. Whether crooked or straight, look to Heaven's utmost; face the four quarters; wax and wane with the times. Whether so or not so, hold the round hinge; perfect your meaning alone; wander with the Way. Do not turn your conduct; do not complete your rightness — you will lose what you do. Do not chase riches; do not martyr yourself to success — you will cast away your Heaven. Bigan had his heart cut open; Zixu had his eyes gouged out — the calamity of loyalty. Zhi Gong testified against his father; Weisheng drowned — the calamity of trust. Master Bao stood and dried out; Master Shen would not justify himself — the calamity of integrity. Confucius did not see his mother; Kuangzi did not see his father — the failing of rightness. These are what the former ages handed down and the later ages speak of, counting the scholar rectified in word and committed in deed — therefore they suffered their calamities and met their troubles."

## Section 17

**ZH:** 無足問於知和曰：「人卒未有不興名就利者。彼富則人歸之，歸則下之，下則貴之。夫見下貴者，所以長生、安體、樂意之道也。今子獨無意焉，知不足邪？意知而力不能行邪？故推正不忘邪？」知和曰：「今夫此人以為與己同時而生、同鄉而處者，以為夫絕俗過世之士焉，是專無主正，所以覽古今之時，是非之分也，與俗化世。去至重，棄至尊，以為其所為也，此其所以論長生、安體、樂意之道，不亦遠乎！慘怛之疾，恬愉之安，不監於體；怵惕之恐，欣懽之喜，不監於心。知為為而不知所以為，是以貴為天子，富有天下，而不免於患也。」

**EN:** Wuzu (無足) asked Zhihe (知和): "Every man, without exception, delights in name and approaches profit. When a man is rich, others flock to him; flocking to him, they bow; bowing, they honor him. To be bowed-to and honored is the way to long life, ease of body, and delight of will. Now you alone have no mind for it — is your knowledge insufficient? Or do you know, yet your strength cannot practice it? Or do you hold to rectitude and cannot forget?" Zhihe said: "Now, such a man — that men born in his own time, dwelling in his own village, should count him a scholar beyond the vulgar and surpassing the age — that is surely to have no lord and rectitude. So I look on the times of past and present and the division of right and wrong as changing with the vulgar. To cast away what is heaviest, abandon what is most honored, and make that one's doing — this way of discussing long life, ease of body, delight of will, is far off indeed! Pained illness or placid ease — not mirrored in the body; fearful dread or happy delight — not mirrored in the heart. To know the doing of things and not know their wherefore — therefore, exalted as Son of Heaven, rich with all the world, he cannot escape trouble."

## Section 18

**ZH:** 無足曰：「夫富之於人，無所不利，窮美究埶，至人之所不得逮，賢人之所不能及，俠人之勇力而不為威強，秉人之知謀以為明察，因人之德以為賢良，非享國而嚴若君父。且夫聲色、滋味、權勢之於人，心不待學而樂之，體不待象而安之。夫欲惡避就，固不待師，此人之性也。天下雖非我，孰能辭之！」知和曰：「知者之為，故動以百姓，不違其度，是以足而不爭，無以為故不求。不足故求之，爭四處而不自以為貪；有餘故辭之，棄天下而不自以為廉。廉貪之實，非以迫外也，反監之度。勢為天子而不以貴驕人，富有天下而不以財戲人。計其患，慮其反，以為害於性，故辭而不受也，非以要名譽也。堯、舜為帝而雍，非仁天下也，不以美害生也；善卷、許由得帝而不受，非虛辭讓也，不以事害己。此皆就其利，辭其害，而天下稱賢焉，則可以有之，彼非以興名譽也。」

**EN:** Wuzu said: "Riches, for a man, profit in everything: the utmost of beauty and the peak of power, which the utmost man could not attain nor the worthy reach. To take another's courage and strength and make it one's might; to wield another's knowledge and schemes and make it one's clarity; to lean on another's virtue and make it one's goodness — without enjoying a state, as august as lord or father. Moreover, music and beauty, flavors and tastes, power and standing — the heart delights in them without learning; the body rests in them without example. That desire and aversion, avoidance and approach, inherently need no teacher — this is the nature of man. Though the world may condemn me — who can decline it!" Zhihe said: "What the knowing do: they move for the hundred clans' sake and do not defy the measure; therefore sufficed, they do not contend; having no cause to act, they do not seek. Unsufficed, they seek; contending on all four sides, they do not count themselves greedy. Having more than enough, they decline; casting away the world, they do not count themselves incorrupt. The substance of incorruption and greed is not forced from without: turn back and mirror the measure. Standing as Son of Heaven without arrogating honor over men; rich with all the world without playing with wealth over men. Reckoning the calamity, pondering the reversal, counting it harmful to nature — therefore he declines and does not accept, not to fish for fame. Yao and Shun as emperors were concordant — not from humaneness to the world, but because they would not let beauty harm their lives. Shan Juan and Xu You were offered the empire and did not accept — not in empty deference, but because they would not let affairs harm themselves. All these approached their profit and declined their harm — and the world calls them worthy. That may be had; but they did not do it to raise a name."

## Section 19

**ZH:** 無足曰：「必持其名，苦體絕甘，約養以持生，則亦久病長阨而不死者也。」知和曰：「平為福，有餘為害者，物莫不然，而財其甚者也。今富人耳營鐘鼓筦籥之聲，口嗛於芻豢醪醴之味，以感其意，遺忘其業，可謂亂矣；侅溺於馮氣，若負重行而上也，可謂苦矣；貪財而取慰，貪權而取竭，靜居則溺，體澤則馮，可謂疾矣；為欲富就利，故滿若堵耳而不知避，且馮而不舍，可謂辱矣；財積而無用，服膺而不舍，滿心戚醮，求益而不止，可謂憂矣；內則疑劫請之賊，外則畏寇盜之害，內周樓疏，外不敢獨行，可謂畏矣。此六者，天下之至害也，皆遺忘而不知察，及其患至，求盡性竭財，單以反一日之無故而不可得也。故觀之名則不見，求之利則不得，繚意體而爭此，不亦惑乎！」

**EN:** Wuzu said: "But to hold fast to one's name — to embitter the body, abstain from sweetness, keep to scant nourishment to prolong life — that is to be long ill and long straitened without dying." Zhihe said: "Levelness is fortune; excess is harm — nothing is not so, and wealth most of all. Now the rich man: his ears ring with the sound of bells, drums, pipes, and flutes; his mouth gluts on the taste of fat beasts, ale, and sweet wine, delighting his fancy while his work is forgotten — call that disorder. Bloated, drowned in swollen breath, like carrying a heavy load up a hill — call that suffering. Greedy for wealth he takes his ease; greedy for power he takes exhaustion; in quiet he is sunk; in bodily glow he is swollen — call that sickness. Desiring riches, approaching profit, filled to the ears like a wall and not knowing to avoid it, swollen and unable to let go — call that disgrace. Wealth heaped and unused, worn on the breast and not let go, the heart full of worry and scorched, seeking ever more without ceasing — call that care. Within, he suspects burglars and beggars; without, he fears robbers and thieves; within, he rings his house with watch-towers; without, he dares not walk alone — call that fear. These six are the greatest harms under heaven, and all forget and fail to examine them. When the calamity arrives, they beg to use up their nature and exhaust their wealth, if only to get back a single day without trouble — and cannot get it. So looking to the name, it is not seen; seeking the profit, it is not gotten; to wind up the will and body in contending for this — is that not delusion!"
