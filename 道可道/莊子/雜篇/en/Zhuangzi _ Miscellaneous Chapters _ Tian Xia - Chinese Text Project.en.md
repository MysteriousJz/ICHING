# Tian Xia — Under Heaven (天下)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Miscellaneous Chapters _ Tian Xia - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (18 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 天下之治方術者多矣，皆以其有為不可加矣。古之所謂道術者，果惡乎在？曰：「無乎不在。」曰：「神何由降？明何由出？」「聖有所生，王有所成，皆原於一。」
**PY:** tiān xià zhī zhì fāng shù zhě duō yǐ， jiē yǐ qí yǒu wèi bù kě jiā yǐ。 gǔ zhī suǒ wèi dào shù zhě， guǒ è hū zài？ yuē：「 wú hū bù zài。」 yuē：「 shén hé yóu jiàng？ míng hé yóu chū？」「 shèng yǒu suǒ shēng， wáng yǒu suǒ chéng， jiē yuán yú yī。」

**EN:** Those under heaven who work at the methods of the schools are many, and each counts what he has as unsurpassable. The methods of the Way spoken of by the ancients — where indeed are they? It is said: "There is nowhere they are not." It is said: "Whence does the spirit descend? Whence does the clarity come forth?" — "The sage has that from which he is born; the king has that from which he is completed; both take their source from the One."

## Section 2

**ZH:** 不離於宗，謂之天人。不離於精，謂之神人。不離於真，謂之至人。以天為宗，以德為本，以道為門，兆於變化，謂之聖人。以仁為恩，以義為理，以禮為行，以樂為和，薰然慈仁，謂之君子。以法為分，以名為表，以參為驗，以稽為決，其數一二三四是也。百官以此相齒，以事為常，以衣食為主，蕃息畜藏，老弱孤寡為意，皆有以養，民之理也。
**PY:** bù lí yú zōng， wèi zhī tiān rén。 bù lí yú jīng， wèi zhī shén rén。 bù lí yú zhēn， wèi zhī zhì rén。 yǐ tiān wèi zōng， yǐ dé wèi běn， yǐ dào wèi mén， zhào yú biàn huà， wèi zhī shèng rén。 yǐ rén wèi ēn， yǐ yì wèi lǐ， yǐ lǐ wèi xíng， yǐ lè wèi hé， xūn rán cí rén， wèi zhī jūn zǐ。 yǐ fǎ wèi fēn， yǐ míng wèi biǎo， yǐ cān wèi yàn， yǐ jī wèi jué， qí shù yī èr sān sì shì yě。 bǎi guān yǐ cǐ xiāng chǐ， yǐ shì wèi cháng， yǐ yī shí wèi zhǔ， fān xī chù cáng， lǎo ruò gū guǎ wèi yì， jiē yǒu yǐ yǎng， mín zhī lǐ yě。

**EN:** One who does not part from the source is called a man of Heaven. One who does not part from the essence is called a spirit-like man. One who does not part from the true is called an utmost man. To take Heaven as source, virtue as root, the Way as gate, and to mark change and transformation — such a one is called a sage. To take humaneness as favor, rightness as pattern, ritual as conduct, music as harmony, fragrant in kindness and humaneness — such a one is called a gentleman. To take law for distinctions, names for markings, comparison for proof, examination for decision — such are their numbers, one, two, three, four. The hundred offices are ordered thereby as teeth on teeth; affairs are their constant; clothing and food are their chief; multiplying, growing, storing, and keeping; caring for the old, the weak, the orphan, and the widow; each having means to be nourished — that is the pattern of the people.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 古之人其備乎！配神明，醇天地，育萬物，和天下，澤及百姓，明於本數，係於末度，六通四辟，小大精粗，其運無乎不在。其明而在數度者，舊法世傳之史尚多有之。其在於《詩》、《書》、《禮》、《樂》者，鄒、魯之士、搢紳先生多能明之。《詩》以道志，《書》以道事，《禮》以道行，《樂》以道和，《易》以道陰陽，《春秋》以道名分。其數散於天下而設於中國者，百家之學時或稱而道之。
**PY:** gǔ zhī rén qí bèi hū！ pèi shén míng， chún tiān dì， yù wàn wù， hé tiān xià， zé jí bǎi xìng， míng yú běn shù， xì yú mò dù， liù tōng sì pì， xiǎo dà jīng cū， qí yùn wú hū bù zài。 qí míng ér zài shù dù zhě， jiù fǎ shì chuán zhī shǐ shàng duō yǒu zhī。 qí zài yú《 shī》、《 shū》、《 lǐ》、《 lè》 zhě， zōu、 lǔ zhī shì、 jìn shēn xiān shēng duō néng míng zhī。《 shī》 yǐ dào zhì，《 shū》 yǐ dào shì，《 lǐ》 yǐ dào héng，《 lè》 yǐ dào hé，《 yì》 yǐ dào yīn yáng，《 chūn qiū》 yǐ dào míng fèn。 qí shù sàn yú tiān xià ér shè yú zhōng guó zhě， bǎi jiā zhī xué shí huò chēng ér dào zhī。

**EN:** The men of old were complete! They matched the spirit-bright, were pure with heaven and earth, nurtured the ten thousand things, harmonized the world, and their blessing reached the hundred clans. Clear on the root-numbers, bound to the branch-measures, open six ways and four ways — small and great, fine and coarse, their working was present everywhere. What of it is bright in numbers and measures: the old laws and the histories handed down still have much of it. What of it lies in the Odes, Documents, Ritual, and Music: the scholars of Zou and Lu, the sash-girt masters, can mostly make it clear. The Odes discourse the will; the Documents discourse affairs; the Ritual discourses conduct; the Music discourses harmony; the Changes discourses yin and yang; the Spring and Autumn discourses names and stations. Of its numbers scattered through the world and established in the Middle Kingdom, the hundred schools from time to time praise and discourse them.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 天下大亂，賢聖不明，道德不一，天下多得一察焉以自好。譬如耳目鼻口，皆有所明，不能相通。猶百家眾技也，皆有所長，時有所用。雖然，不該不遍，一曲之士也。判天地之美，析萬物之理，察古人之全，寡能備於天地之美，稱神明之容。是故內聖外王之道，闇而不明，鬱而不發，天下之人各為其所欲焉以自為方。悲夫！百家往而不反，必不合矣。後世之學者，不幸不見天地之純，古人之大體，道術將為天下裂。
**PY:** tiān xià dà luàn， xián shèng bù míng， dào dé bù yī， tiān xià duō dé yī chá yān yǐ zì hǎo。 pì rú ěr mù bí kǒu， jiē yǒu suǒ míng， bù néng xiāng tōng。 yóu bǎi jiā zhòng jì yě， jiē yǒu suǒ zhǎng， shí yǒu suǒ yòng。 suī rán， bù gāi bù biàn， yī qū zhī shì yě。 pàn tiān dì zhī měi， xī wàn wù zhī lǐ， chá gǔ rén zhī quán， guǎ néng bèi yú tiān dì zhī měi， chēng shén míng zhī róng。 shì gù nèi shèng wài wáng zhī dào， àn ér bù míng， yù ér bù fā， tiān xià zhī rén gè wèi qí suǒ yù yān yǐ zì wèi fāng。 bēi fū！ bǎi jiā wǎng ér bù fǎn， bì bù hé yǐ。 hòu shì zhī xué zhě， bù xìng bù jiàn tiān dì zhī chún， gǔ rén zhī dà tǐ， dào shù jiāng wèi tiān xià liè。

**EN:** The world fell into great disorder; the worthy and sage grew dim; the Way and its virtue were not one; and each man of the world got one corner and favored it. It is like ear, eye, nose, and mouth: each has its brightness, and they cannot commune. So it is with the hundred schools' many arts: each has its strength; each has its season of use. Even so, none encompasses, none is whole — each is a scholar of one cramped corner. To cut up the beauty of heaven and earth, analyze the pattern of the ten thousand things, examine the wholeness of the men of old — few can be complete in the beauty of heaven and earth, and answer the bearing of the spirit-bright. Therefore the Way of the inward sage and outward king is darkened and unbright, pent up and unissued, and the men of the world each make a method of what they themselves desire. Alas! The hundred schools go forth and do not return; they are sure never to join again. The scholars of later ages, unblessed, never see the purity of heaven and earth, the great body of the men of old — and the methods of the Way will be split apart by the world.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 不侈於後世，不靡於萬物，不暉於數度，以繩墨自矯，而備世之急，古之道術有在於是者。墨翟、禽滑釐聞其風而說之。為之大過，己之大循。作為《非樂》，命之曰《節用》，生不歌，死無服。墨子汎愛兼利而非鬥，其道不怒；又好學而博，不異，不與先王同，毀古之禮樂。
**PY:** bù chǐ yú hòu shì， bù mí yú wàn wù， bù huī yú shù dù， yǐ shéng mò zì jiǎo， ér bèi shì zhī jí， gǔ zhī dào shù yǒu zài yú shì zhě。 mò dí、 qín huá lí wén qí fēng ér shuō zhī。 wèi zhī dà guò， jǐ zhī dà xún。 zuò wèi《 fēi lè》， mìng zhī yuē《 jié yòng》， shēng bù gē， sǐ wú fú。 mò zi fàn ài jiān lì ér fēi dòu， qí dào bù nù； yòu hǎo xué ér bó， bù yì， bù yǔ xiān wáng tóng， huǐ gǔ zhī lǐ lè。

**EN:** Not to indulge later ages; not to be lavished on the ten thousand things; not to be dazzled by numbers and measures; to correct oneself by the inked line and provide for the world's pressing need — of the methods of the Way among the ancients there was some in this. Mo Di (墨翟) and Qin Guli (禽滑釐), hearing of its way, delighted in it. But they practiced it in excess; they restrained themselves overmuch. They composed "Against Music," and named it "Moderation in Use": in life no singing; in death no mourning garb. Mozi loved universally and benefited jointly, and condemned strife; his way was without anger. He was also fond of learning and broad; he did not differentiate; he did not accord with the former kings; he destroyed the rituals and music of the ancients.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 黃帝有《咸池》，堯有《大章》，舜有《大韶》，禹有《大夏》，湯有《大濩》，文王有辟雍之樂，武王、周公作《武》。古之喪禮，貴賤有儀，上下有等，天子棺槨七重，諸侯五重，大夫三重，士再重。今墨子獨生不歌，死不服，桐棺三寸而無槨，以為法式。以此教人，恐不愛人；以此自行，固不愛己。未敗墨子道，雖然，歌而非歌，哭而非哭，樂而非樂，是果類乎？其生也勤，其死也薄，其道大觳，使人憂，使人悲，其行難為也，恐其不可以為聖人之道，反天下之心，天下不堪。墨子雖能獨任，奈天下何！離於天下，其去王也遠矣。
**PY:** huáng dì yǒu《 xián chí》， yáo yǒu《 dà zhāng》， shùn yǒu《 dà sháo》， yǔ yǒu《 dà xià》， tāng yǒu《 dà huò》， wén wáng yǒu pì yōng zhī lè， wǔ wáng、 zhōu gōng zuò《 wǔ》。 gǔ zhī sàng lǐ， guì jiàn yǒu yí， shàng xià yǒu děng， tiān zi guān guǒ qī zhòng， zhū hóu wǔ zhòng， dà fū sān chóng， shì zài zhòng。 jīn mò zi dú shēng bù gē， sǐ bù fú， tóng guān sān cùn ér wú guǒ， yǐ wèi fǎ shì。 yǐ cǐ jiào rén， kǒng bù ài rén； yǐ cǐ zì xíng， gù bù ài jǐ。 wèi bài mò zi dào， suī rán， gē ér fēi gē， kū ér fēi kū， lè ér fēi lè， shì guǒ lèi hū？ qí shēng yě qín， qí sǐ yě báo， qí dào dà hú， shǐ rén yōu， shǐ rén bēi， qí xíng nán wèi yě， kǒng qí bù kě yǐ wèi shèng rén zhī dào， fǎn tiān xià zhī xīn， tiān xià bù kān。 mò zi suī néng dú rèn， nài tiān xià hé！ lí yú tiān xià， qí qù wáng yě yuǎn yǐ。

**EN:** The Yellow Emperor had the Xianchi; Yao had the Dazhang; Shun had the Dashao; Yu had the Daxia; Tang had the Dahu; King Wen had the music of the Circular Moat; King Wu and the Duke of Zhou composed the Wu. In the ancient mourning rites, high and low had their forms, above and below their grades: the Son of Heaven's coffin and vault were seven layers; the lords', five; the great officers', three; the scholars', two. Now Mozi alone practices: in life no singing; in death no mourning garb; a plain coffin three inches thick and no vault — as model and measure. Teaching men thus, I fear he does not love men; practicing it himself, surely he does not love himself. This does not defeat Mozi's way; still, to sing yet not sing, to weep yet not weep, to be glad yet not be glad — is that really of a kind? In life toil; in death meagerness: his way is a great austerity. It makes men worry; it makes men grieve; its practice is hard to perform; I fear it cannot serve as the Way of the sage. It runs counter to the hearts of the world; the world cannot bear it. Though Mozi can bear it alone — what can the world do! Parted from the world, he is far from kingship.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 墨子稱道曰：「昔者禹之湮洪水，決江河而通四夷九州也，名山三百，支川三千，小者無數。禹親自操稿耜而九雜天下之川，腓無胈，脛無毛，沐甚雨，櫛疾風，置萬國。禹，大聖也，而形勞天下也如此。」使後世之墨者多以裘褐為衣，以跂蹻為服，日夜不休，以自苦為極，曰：「不能如此，非禹之道也，不足謂墨。」相里勤之弟子五侯之徒，南方之墨者苦獲、已齒、鄧陵子之屬，俱誦《墨經》，而倍譎不同，相謂別墨，以堅白、同異之辯相訾，以觭偶不仵之辭相應，以巨子為聖人，皆願為之尸，冀得為其後世，至今不決。
**PY:** mò zi chēng dào yuē：「 xī zhě yǔ zhī yān hóng shuǐ， jué jiāng hé ér tōng sì yí jiǔ zhōu yě， míng shān sān bǎi， zhī chuān sān qiān， xiǎo zhě wú shù。 yǔ qīn zì cāo gǎo sì ér jiǔ zá tiān xià zhī chuān， féi wú bá， jìng wú máo， mù shèn yǔ， zhì jí fēng， zhì wàn guó。 yǔ， dà shèng yě， ér xíng láo tiān xià yě rú cǐ。」 shǐ hòu shì zhī mò zhě duō yǐ qiú hè wèi yī， yǐ qí juē wèi fú， rì yè bù xiū， yǐ zì kǔ wèi jí， yuē：「 bù néng rú cǐ， fēi yǔ zhī dào yě， bù zú wèi mò。」 xiāng lǐ qín zhī dì zǐ wǔ hóu zhī tú， nán fāng zhī mò zhě kǔ huò、 yǐ chǐ、 dèng líng zi zhī shǔ， jù sòng《 mò jīng》， ér bèi jué bù tóng， xiāng wèi bié mò， yǐ jiān bái、 tóng yì zhī biàn xiāng zī， yǐ jī ǒu bù wǔ zhī cí xiāng yīng， yǐ jù zi wèi shèng rén， jiē yuàn wèi zhī shī， jì dé wèi qí hòu shì， zhì jīn bù jué。

**EN:** Mozi praised the Way, saying: "Of old, when Yu dammed the floodwaters, cut open the rivers, and made passage to the four wilds and nine provinces — three hundred great streams, three thousand branches, and smaller ones past counting — Yu himself took up spade and basket and united the streams of the world. His calves had no hair; his shins were bare; drenched by the driving rain, combed by the raging wind, he set up the ten thousand states. Yu was a great sage — and he wore his body out for the world like this." So the Mohists of later ages mostly wear skins and coarse cloth, shod in sandals, never resting day or night, taking self-burden for the utmost, saying: "Who cannot do this does not have the Way of Yu and is not fit to be called a Mohist." The disciples of Xiangli Qin and the followers of Wuhou; the Mohists of the south, Kuhuo, Yichi, Denglingzi, and their kind — all recite the Mohist Canon, yet differ and contradict one another, calling one another schism-Mohists. They revile one another with disputations of hard-and-white and same-and-different; they answer one another with phrases of odd and even mismatched. They take the Master as a sage, and all wish to be his corpse-lord, hoping to become his successors — undecided to this day.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 墨翟、禽滑釐之意則是，其行則非也。將使後世之墨者必自苦以腓無胈、脛無毛，相進而已矣。亂之上也，治之下也。雖然，墨子真天下之好也，將求之不得也，雖枯槁不舍也，才士也！
**PY:** mò dí、 qín huá lí zhī yì zé shì， qí xíng zé fēi yě。 jiāng shǐ hòu shì zhī mò zhě bì zì kǔ yǐ féi wú bá、 jìng wú máo， xiāng jìn ér yǐ yǐ。 luàn zhī shàng yě， zhì zhī xià yě。 suī rán， mò zi zhēn tiān xià zhī hǎo yě， jiāng qiú zhī bù dé yě， suī kū gǎo bù shě yě， cái shì yě！

**EN:** The intention of Mo Di and Qin Guli was right; their practice was wrong. They would have the Mohists of later ages burden themselves to hairless calves and bare shins — driving one another on, that is all. For disorder, the best; for order, the worst. Even so — Mozi truly loved the world. One may seek his like and not find it: withered and gaunt, he would not let go. A talented scholar!

## Section 9

**ZH:** 夫不累於俗，不飾於物，不苟於人，不忮於眾，願天下之安寧以活民命，人我之養畢足而止，以此白心，古之道術有在於是者。宋鈃、尹文聞其風而悅之。作為華山之冠以自表，接萬物以別宥為始。語心之容，命之曰心之行，以聏合驩，以調海內，請欲置之以為主。見侮不辱，救民之鬥；禁攻寢兵，救世之戰。以此周行天下，上說下教，雖天下不取，強聒而不舍者也。故曰：「上下見厭而強見也。」雖然，其為人太多，其自為太少，曰：「請欲固置五升之飯足矣，先生恐不得飽，弟子雖飢，不忘天下。」日夜不休，曰：「我必得活哉！」圖傲乎救世之士哉！曰：「君子不為苛察，不以身假物。」以為無益於天下者，明之不如已也。以禁攻寢兵為外，以情欲寡淺為內，其小大精粗，其行適至是而止。
**PY:** fū bù lèi yú sú， bù shì yú wù， bù gǒu yú rén， bù zhì yú zhòng， yuàn tiān xià zhī ān níng yǐ huó mín mìng， rén wǒ zhī yǎng bì zú ér zhǐ， yǐ cǐ bái xīn， gǔ zhī dào shù yǒu zài yú shì zhě。 sòng xíng、 yǐn wén wén qí fēng ér yuè zhī。 zuò wèi huá shān zhī guān yǐ zì biǎo， jiē wàn wù yǐ bié yòu wèi shǐ。 yǔ xīn zhī róng， mìng zhī yuē xīn zhī xíng， yǐ ér hé huān， yǐ diào hǎi nèi， qǐng yù zhì zhī yǐ wèi zhǔ。 jiàn wǔ bù rǔ， jiù mín zhī dòu； jìn gōng qǐn bīng， jiù shì zhī zhàn。 yǐ cǐ zhōu xíng tiān xià， shàng shuō xià jiào， suī tiān xià bù qǔ， qiáng guā ér bù shě zhě yě。 gù yuē：「 shàng xià jiàn yàn ér qiáng jiàn yě。」 suī rán， qí wèi rén tài duō， qí zì wèi tài shǎo， yuē：「 qǐng yù gù zhì wǔ shēng zhī fàn zú yǐ， xiān shēng kǒng bù dé bǎo， dì zǐ suī jī， bù wàng tiān xià。」 rì yè bù xiū， yuē：「 wǒ bì děi huó zāi！」 tú ào hū jiù shì zhī shì zāi！ yuē：「 jūn zǐ bù wèi kē chá， bù yǐ shēn jiǎ wù。」 yǐ wèi wú yì yú tiān xià zhě， míng zhī bù rú yǐ yě。 yǐ jìn gōng qǐn bīng wèi wài， yǐ qíng yù guǎ qiǎn wèi nèi， qí xiǎo dà jīng cū， qí xíng shì zhì shì ér zhǐ。

**EN:** Not to be bound by custom; not to be adorned by things; not to be exacting of men; not to be envious of the crowd; to wish the world's peace for the living of the people's lives; to count the nourishment of others and self both sufficient and stop — making their hearts clear in this: of the methods of the Way among the ancients there was some in this. Song Xing (宋鈃) and Yin Wen (尹文), hearing of its way, delighted in it. They made the Hua Mountain cap to mark themselves; in receiving the ten thousand things they began with distinguishing the bounded. They spoke of the heart's receptivity, and named it the heart's conduct: to blend and gather joy, to harmonize all within the seas, they begged to establish it as the chief thing. To meet insult and not be shamed — to save the people from fighting; to forbid assault and lay down arms — to save the age from war. With this they walked all round the world, persuading above and teaching below; though the world would not take it, they pressed their clamor and would not let go. Hence it is said: "Above and below loathed the sight of them, and still they forced themselves to be seen." Even so, they did too much for others and too little for themselves, saying: "We beg to settle on five measures of rice as enough. The masters fear they cannot eat their fill; though the disciples go hungry, they do not forget the world." Day and night without rest, saying: "I must surely live!" — scheming proudly as the age-saving scholars. They said: "The gentleman does not pick things to pieces; he does not lend his body to things." Whatever they held to be of no profit to the world, they taught it is better to let be. With forbidding assault and laying down arms as their outside, and desiring few and shallow wants as their inside — small and great, fine and coarse — their practice went just this far and stopped.

## Section 10

**ZH:** 公而不當，易而無私，決然無主，趣物而不兩，不顧於慮，不謀於知，於物無擇，與之俱往，古之道術有在於是者。彭蒙、田駢、慎到聞其風而說之。齊萬物以為首，曰：「天能覆之而不能載之，地能載之而不能覆之，大道能包之而不能辯之。」知萬物皆有所可，有所不可，故曰：「選則不遍，教則不至，道則無遺者矣。」是故慎到，棄知去己，而緣不得已，泠汰於物以為道理，曰：「知不知，將薄知而後鄰傷之者也。」謑髁無任而笑天下之尚賢也，縱脫無行而非天下之大聖，椎拍輐斷，與物宛轉，舍是與非，苟可以免，不師知慮，不知前後，魏然而已矣。推而後行，曳而後往，若飄風之還，若羽之旋，若磨石之隧，全而無非，動靜無過，未嘗有罪。是何故？夫無知之物，無建己之患，無用知之累，動靜不離於理，是以終身無譽。故曰：「至於若無知之物而已，無用賢聖，夫塊不失道。」豪桀相與笑之曰：「慎到之道，非生人之行而至死人之理，適得怪焉。」田駢亦然，學於彭蒙，得不教焉。彭蒙之師曰：「古之道人，至於莫之是、莫之非而已矣。其風窢然，惡可而言？」常反人，不見觀，而不免於鯇斷。其所謂道非道，而所言之韙不免於非。彭蒙、田駢、慎到不知道。雖然，概乎皆嘗有聞者也。
**PY:** gōng ér bù dāng， yì ér wú sī， jué rán wú zhǔ， qù wù ér bù liǎng， bù gù yú lǜ， bù móu yú zhī， yú wù wú zé， yǔ zhī jù wǎng， gǔ zhī dào shù yǒu zài yú shì zhě。 péng méng、 tián pián、 shèn dào wén qí fēng ér shuō zhī。 qí wàn wù yǐ wèi shǒu， yuē：「 tiān néng fù zhī ér bù néng zài zhī， dì néng zài zhī ér bù néng fù zhī， dà dào néng bāo zhī ér bù néng biàn zhī。」 zhī wàn wù jiē yǒu suǒ kě， yǒu suǒ bù kě， gù yuē：「 xuǎn zé bù biàn， jiào zé bù zhì， dào zé wú yí zhě yǐ。」 shì gù shèn dào， qì zhī qù jǐ， ér yuán bù dé yǐ， líng tài yú wù yǐ wèi dào lǐ， yuē：「 zhī bù zhī， jiāng báo zhī ér hòu lín shāng zhī zhě yě。」 xǐ kē wú rèn ér xiào tiān xià zhī shàng xián yě， zòng tuō wú xíng ér fēi tiān xià zhī dà shèng， chuí pāi wàn duàn， yǔ wù wǎn zhuǎn， shě shì yǔ fēi， gǒu kě yǐ miǎn， bù shī zhī lǜ， bù zhī qián hòu， wèi rán ér yǐ yǐ。 tuī ér hòu xíng， yè ér hòu wǎng， ruò piāo fēng zhī hái， ruò yǔ zhī xuán， ruò mó shí zhī suì， quán ér wú fēi， dòng jìng wú guò， wèi cháng yǒu zuì。 shì hé gù？ fū wú zhī zhī wù， wú jiàn jǐ zhī huàn， wú yòng zhī zhī lèi， dòng jìng bù lí yú lǐ， shì yǐ zhōng shēn wú yù。 gù yuē：「 zhì yú ruò wú zhī zhī wù ér yǐ， wú yòng xián shèng， fū kuài bù shī dào。」 háo jié xiāng yǔ xiào zhī yuē：「 shèn dào zhī dào， fēi shēng rén zhī xíng ér zhì sǐ rén zhī lǐ， shì dé guài yān。」 tián pián yì rán， xué yú péng méng， dé bù jiào yān。 péng méng zhī shī yuē：「 gǔ zhī dào rén， zhì yú mò zhī shì、 mò zhī fēi ér yǐ yǐ。 qí fēng xū rán， è kě ér yán？」 cháng fǎn rén， bù jiàn guān， ér bù miǎn yú huàn duàn。 qí suǒ wèi dào fēi dào， ér suǒ yán zhī wěi bù miǎn yú fēi。 péng méng、 tián pián、 shèn dào bù zhī dào。 suī rán， gài hū jiē cháng yǒu wén zhě yě。

**EN:** Impartial without favoritism; even without private interest; resolute without a ruler; responsive to things without dividing them; without regard for care; without scheming in knowledge; without choosing among things — going along with them: of the methods of the Way among the ancients there was some in this. Peng Meng (彭蒙), Tian Pian (田駢), and Shen Dao (慎到), hearing of its way, delighted in it. Making the ten thousand things equal was their first principle. They said: "Heaven can cover them but cannot bear them; earth can bear them but cannot cover them; the great Way can embrace them but cannot distinguish them." Knowing that all things have their admissible and their inadmissible, they said: "To choose is to be partial; to teach is to fall short; the Way leaves nothing out." Therefore Shen Dao cast off knowledge and put away self, following along with the unavoidable; cool and drifting with things, he made that his way and pattern. He said: "To know not-knowing — that is to approach knowledge and then be neighborly-injured by it." Loose and careless, unbeholden, he laughed at the world's honoring the worthy; free and unrestrained, unmannered, he condemned the world's great sages. Hammering, grinding, cutting down, he turned and bent with things; casting off right and wrong, if only he could escape; not taking knowledge and care as teacher; not knowing before and after — towering, that was all. Pushed, he moved; dragged, he went; like a whirlwind's gyre, like a feather's swirl, like a grindstone's turn — whole and faultless; in motion and stillness without error; never once to blame. How is this? A thing without knowledge has no trouble of setting itself up, no burden of using knowledge; in motion and stillness it does not leave the pattern; therefore all its life it has no praise. Hence he said: "Come to be like a thing without knowledge — nothing more. There is no use for worthies and sages. A clod does not lose the Way." The bold and outstanding laughed at him together, saying: "Shen Dao's way is not the conduct of living men; it arrives at the pattern of dead men — it comes off as merely strange." Tian Pian was likewise: he studied under Peng Meng and got the untaught. Peng Meng's teacher said: "The men of the Way among the ancients came to nothing approved, nothing condemned — that was all. Their wind is gust and swirl — what could be said?" Always contrary to men, not one to be viewed — yet not free of smoothing and cutting down. What they call the Way is not the Way; what they affirm cannot avoid being condemned. Peng Meng, Tian Pian, and Shen Dao did not know the Way. Even so, in outline all had heard something of it.

## Section 11

**ZH:** 以本為精，以物為粗，以有積為不足，澹然獨與神明居，古之道術有在於是者。關尹、老聃聞其風而悅之。建之以常無有，主之以太一，以濡弱謙下為表，以空虛不毀萬物為實。
**PY:** yǐ běn wèi jīng， yǐ wù wèi cū， yǐ yǒu jī wèi bù zú， dàn rán dú yǔ shén míng jū， gǔ zhī dào shù yǒu zài yú shì zhě。 guān yǐn、 lǎo dān wén qí fēng ér yuè zhī。 jiàn zhī yǐ cháng wú yǒu， zhǔ zhī yǐ tài yī， yǐ rú ruò qiān xià wèi biǎo， yǐ kōng xū bù huǐ wàn wù wèi shí。

**EN:** Taking the root as the fine and things as the coarse; taking what is stored up as insufficiency; placidly dwelling alone with the spirit-bright: of the methods of the Way among the ancients there was some in this. Guanyin and Lao Dan, hearing of its way, delighted in it. They founded it on the constant not-having; they made the Great One its lord; they took softness, weakness, and lowly deference for its outward mark; and emptiness that does not destroy the ten thousand things for its substance.

## Section 12

**ZH:** 關尹曰：「在己無居，形物自著。其動若水，其靜若鏡，其應若響。芴乎若亡，寂乎若清，同焉者和，得焉者失。未嘗先人而常隨人。」
**PY:** guān yǐn yuē：「 zài jǐ wú jū， xíng wù zì zhù。 qí dòng ruò shuǐ， qí jìng ruò jìng， qí yīng ruò xiǎng。 wù hū ruò wáng， jì hū ruò qīng， tóng yān zhě hé， dé yān zhě shī。 wèi cháng xiān rén ér cháng suí rén。」

**EN:** Guanyin said: "In oneself there is no dwelling; forms of things reveal themselves of themselves. In motion it is like water; in stillness like a mirror; in response like the echo. Dim as though gone; still as though clear; one with them, harmonious; getting it, losing it. Never going before men, always following after men."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 老聃曰：「知其雄，守其雌，為天下谿；知其白，守其辱，為天下谷。」人皆取先，己獨取後，曰：「受天下之垢。」人皆取實，己獨取虛，無藏也故有餘，巋然而有餘。其行身也，徐而不費，無為也而笑巧。人皆求福，己獨曲全，曰：「苟免於咎。」以深為根，以約為紀，曰：「堅則毀矣，銳則拙矣。」常寬容於物，不削於人，可謂至極。關尹、老聃乎！古之博大真人哉！
**PY:** lǎo dān yuē：「 zhī qí xióng， shǒu qí cí， wèi tiān xià xī； zhī qí bái， shǒu qí rǔ， wèi tiān xià gǔ。」 rén jiē qǔ xiān， jǐ dú qǔ hòu， yuē：「 shòu tiān xià zhī gòu。」 rén jiē qǔ shí， jǐ dú qǔ xū， wú cáng yě gù yǒu yú， kuī rán ér yǒu yú。 qí xíng shēn yě， xú ér bù fèi， wú wèi yě ér xiào qiǎo。 rén jiē qiú fú， jǐ dú qū quán， yuē：「 gǒu miǎn yú jiù。」 yǐ shēn wèi gēn， yǐ yuē wèi jì， yuē：「 jiān zé huǐ yǐ， ruì zé zhuō yǐ。」 cháng kuān róng yú wù， bù xuē yú rén， kě wèi zhì jí。 guān yǐn、 lǎo dān hū！ gǔ zhī bó dà zhēn rén zāi！

**EN:** Lao Dan said: "Know the male, keep to the female — be the world's ravine. Know the white, keep to the soiled — be the world's valley." All men take the front; he alone took the back, saying: "I receive the world's grime." All men take the solid; he alone took the empty: storing nothing, therefore having more than enough — towering, having more than enough. His conduct of his person was easy and unwasted; without action, he laughed at craft. All men seek fortune; he alone was bent and whole, saying: "If only I escape fault." Taking depth for root and measure for thread, he said: "The hard will be destroyed; the sharp will be blunted." Always lenient toward things, never cutting men down — this may be called the utmost. Guanyin and Lao Dan! How great and broad were those true men of old!

## Section 14

**ZH:** 芴漠無形，變化無常，死與生與！天地並與！神明往與！芒乎何之？忽乎何適？萬物畢羅，莫足以歸，古之道術有在於是者。莊周聞其風而悅之。以謬悠之說，荒唐之言，無端崖之辭，時恣縱而不儻，不以觭見之也。以天下為沈濁，不可與莊語；以卮言為曼衍，以重言為真，以寓言為廣。獨與天地精神往來，而不敖倪於萬物，不譴是非，以與世俗處。其書雖瑰瑋而連犿無傷也，其辭雖參差而諔詭可觀。彼其充實不可以已，上與造物者遊，而下與外死生、無終始者為友。其於本也，宏大而辟，深閎而肆；其於宗也，可謂稠適而上遂矣。雖然，其應於化而解於物也，其理不竭，其來不蛻，芒乎昧乎，未之盡者。
**PY:** wù mò wú xíng， biàn huà wú cháng， sǐ yǔ shēng yǔ！ tiān dì bìng yǔ！ shén míng wǎng yǔ！ máng hū hé zhī？ hū hū hé shì？ wàn wù bì luó， mò zú yǐ guī， gǔ zhī dào shù yǒu zài yú shì zhě。 zhuāng zhōu wén qí fēng ér yuè zhī。 yǐ miù yōu zhī shuō， huāng táng zhī yán， wú duān yá zhī cí， shí zì zòng ér bù tǎng， bù yǐ jī jiàn zhī yě。 yǐ tiān xià wèi shěn zhuó， bù kě yǔ zhuāng yǔ； yǐ zhī yán wèi màn yǎn， yǐ zhòng yán wèi zhēn， yǐ yù yán wèi guǎng。 dú yǔ tiān dì jīng shén wǎng lái， ér bù áo ní yú wàn wù， bù qiǎn shì fēi， yǐ yǔ shì sú chù。 qí shū suī guī wěi ér lián huān wú shāng yě， qí cí suī cān chà ér chù guǐ kě guān。 bǐ qí chōng shí bù kě yǐ yǐ， shàng yǔ zào wù zhě yóu， ér xià yǔ wài sǐ shēng、 wú zhōng shǐ zhě wèi yǒu。 qí yú běn yě， hóng dà ér pì， shēn hóng ér sì； qí yú zōng yě， kě wèi chóu shì ér shàng suì yǐ。 suī rán， qí yīng yú huà ér jiě yú wù yě， qí lǐ bù jié， qí lái bù tuì， máng hū mèi hū， wèi zhī jǐn zhě。

**EN:** Dim and formless; changing without constancy; death with! life with! heaven and earth together with! the spirit-bright going with! Vague — whither? Sudden — where? The ten thousand things all gathered, with none sufficient to return to: of the methods of the Way among the ancients there was some in this. Zhuang Zhou, hearing of its way, delighted in it. With rambling discourses, wild and wide words, and phrases without boundary or shore, from time to time unbridled without bias — he did not show it one-sidedly. Holding the world sunk and turbid, he could not speak with it in solemn words: with goblet-words for flowing spread, with weighty words for truth, with lodging-words for breadth. Alone he went back and forth with the spirit of heaven and earth, yet never looked askance at the ten thousand things; he condemned no right and wrong, and so dwelt with the vulgar world. His book, though rare and strange, is seamless and unhurting; his phrases, though uneven, are wondrous and worth the viewing. He is full to bursting and cannot stop: above he roams with the Creator; below he is friend to those who set death and life outside and have no end or beginning. Toward the root he is great and open, deep and vast; toward the source he may be called rich and fitting and high-ascending. Even so, in responding to change and loosing things, his pattern never runs out, its coming never sheds its skin: vague and dim — it has never been exhausted.

## Section 15

**ZH:** 惠施多方，其書五車，其道舛駁，其言也不中。歷物之意，曰：「至大無外，謂之大一；至小無內，謂之小一。無厚不可積也，其大千里。天與地卑，山與澤平。日方中方睨，物方生方死。大同而與小同異，此之謂小同異；萬物畢同畢異，此之謂大同異。南方無窮而有窮，今日適越而昔來。連環可解也。我知天下之中央，燕之北，越之南是也。氾愛萬物，天地一體也。」
**PY:** huì shī duō fāng， qí shū wǔ chē， qí dào chuǎn bó， qí yán yě bù zhōng。 lì wù zhī yì， yuē：「 zhì dà wú wài， wèi zhī dà yī； zhì xiǎo wú nèi， wèi zhī xiǎo yī。 wú hòu bù kě jī yě， qí dà qiān lǐ。 tiān yǔ dì bēi， shān yǔ zé píng。 rì fāng zhōng fāng nì， wù fāng shēng fāng sǐ。 dà tóng ér yǔ xiǎo tóng yì， cǐ zhī wèi xiǎo tóng yì； wàn wù bì tóng bì yì， cǐ zhī wèi dà tóng yì。 nán fāng wú qióng ér yǒu qióng， jīn rì shì yuè ér xī lái。 lián huán kě jiě yě。 wǒ zhī tiān xià zhī zhōng yāng， yàn zhī běi， yuè zhī nán shì yě。 fàn ài wàn wù， tiān dì yī tǐ yě。」

**EN:** Hui Shi had many methods; his books filled five carts; his way was mixed and muddled, and his words never hit the center. Tracing the meaning of things, he said: "The utmost great has no outside — call it the great one; the utmost small has no inside — call it the small one. The thicknessless cannot be accumulated, yet its breadth is a thousand li. Heaven is as low as earth; mountains are level with marshes. The sun at its center is at its slant; things just born are just dying. Great sameness differs from small sameness — this is called small sameness-and-difference; the ten thousand things are wholly same and wholly different — this is called great sameness-and-difference. The south is endless and has an end. Going to Yue today and having come yesterday. Linked rings can be undone. I know the center of the world: it is north of Yan and south of Yue. Love all things universally: heaven and earth are one body."

## Section 16

**ZH:** 惠施以此為大觀於天下而曉辯者，天下之辯者相與樂之。卵有毛，雞三足，郢有天下，犬可以為羊，馬有卵，丁子有尾，火不熱，山出口，輪不蹍地，目不見，指不至，至不絕，龜長於蛇，矩不方，規不可以為圓，鑿不圍枘，飛鳥之景未嘗動也，鏃矢之疾而有不行不止之時，狗非犬，黃馬、驪牛三，白狗黑，孤駒未嘗有母，一尺之捶，日取其半，萬世不竭。辯者以此與惠施相應，終身無窮。
**PY:** huì shī yǐ cǐ wèi dà guān yú tiān xià ér xiǎo biàn zhě， tiān xià zhī biàn zhě xiāng yǔ lè zhī。 luǎn yǒu máo， jī sān zú， yǐng yǒu tiān xià， quǎn kě yǐ wèi yáng， mǎ yǒu luǎn， dīng zi yǒu wěi， huǒ bù rè， shān chū kǒu， lún bù niǎn dì， mù bù jiàn， zhǐ bù zhì， zhì bù jué， guī zhǎng yú shé， jǔ bù fāng， guī bù kě yǐ wèi yuán， záo bù wéi ruì， fēi niǎo zhī jǐng wèi cháng dòng yě， zú shǐ zhī jí ér yǒu bù xíng bù zhǐ zhī shí， gǒu fēi quǎn， huáng mǎ、 lí niú sān， bái gǒu hēi， gū jū wèi cháng yǒu mǔ， yī chǐ zhī chuí， rì qǔ qí bàn， wàn shì bù jié。 biàn zhě yǐ cǐ yǔ huì shī xiāng yīng， zhōng shēn wú qióng。

**EN:** Hui Shi took these for his great spectacle in the world and taught the disputers; the disputers of the world delighted in them together. The egg has hair. The chicken has three feet. Ying contains the world. The dog can be a sheep. The horse lays eggs. The tadpole has a tail. Fire is not hot. Mountains issue mouths. The wheel does not tread the ground. The eye does not see. The pointing does not reach; the reaching never ends. The tortoise is longer than the snake. The carpenter's square is not square; the compass cannot make a circle. The mortise does not enclose the tenon. The flying bird's shadow has never moved. The speeding arrow has a time of neither going nor not-going. The hound is not a dog. Yellow horse and black ox make three. The white dog is black. The orphan colt has never had a mother. A foot-long rod: take half each day, and ten thousand ages will not exhaust it. The disputers answered Hui Shi with these — never exhausted to the end of their lives.

## Section 17

**ZH:** 桓團、公孫龍辯者之徒，飾人之心，易人之意，能勝人之口，不能服人之心，辯者之囿也。惠施日以其知，與人之辯，特與天下之辯者為怪，此其柢也。
**PY:** huán tuán、 gōng sūn lóng biàn zhě zhī tú， shì rén zhī xīn， yì rén zhī yì， néng shèng rén zhī kǒu， bù néng fú rén zhī xīn， biàn zhě zhī yòu yě。 huì shī rì yǐ qí zhī， yǔ rén zhī biàn， tè yǔ tiān xià zhī biàn zhě wèi guài， cǐ qí dǐ yě。

**EN:** Huan Tuan (桓團) and Gongsun Long were followers of the disputers: they adorned men's hearts, altered men's meanings, could overcome men's mouths, but could not subdue men's hearts — such is the disputers' pen. Hui Shi, daily with his knowledge, disputed with men — he merely made marvels with the disputers of the world. That is the root of him.

## Section 18

**ZH:** 然惠施之口談，自以為最賢，曰：「天地其壯乎！」施存雄而無術。南方有倚人焉，曰黃繚，問天地所以不墜不陷，風雨雷霆之故。惠施不辭而應，不慮而對，遍為萬物說；說而不休，多而無已，猶以為寡，益之以怪。以反人為實，而欲以勝人為名，是以與眾不適也。弱於德，強於物，其塗隩矣。由天地之道觀惠施之能，其猶一蚉一虻之勞者也，其於物也何庸！夫充一尚可，曰愈貴，道幾矣！惠施不能以此自寧，散於萬物而不厭，卒以善辯為名。惜乎！惠施之才，駘蕩而不得，逐萬物而不反，是窮響以聲，形與影競走也。悲夫！
**PY:** rán huì shī zhī kǒu tán， zì yǐ wèi zuì xián， yuē：「 tiān dì qí zhuàng hū！」 shī cún xióng ér wú shù。 nán fāng yǒu yǐ rén yān， yuē huáng liáo， wèn tiān dì suǒ yǐ bù zhuì bù xiàn， fēng yǔ léi tíng zhī gù。 huì shī bù cí ér yīng， bù lǜ ér duì， biàn wèi wàn wù shuō； shuō ér bù xiū， duō ér wú yǐ， yóu yǐ wèi guǎ， yì zhī yǐ guài。 yǐ fǎn rén wèi shí， ér yù yǐ shèng rén wèi míng， shì yǐ yǔ zhòng bù shì yě。 ruò yú dé， qiáng yú wù， qí tú ào yǐ。 yóu tiān dì zhī dào guān huì shī zhī néng， qí yóu yī wén yī méng zhī láo zhě yě， qí yú wù yě hé yōng！ fū chōng yī shàng kě， yuē yù guì， dào jǐ yǐ！ huì shī bù néng yǐ cǐ zì níng， sàn yú wàn wù ér bù yàn， zú yǐ shàn biàn wèi míng。 xī hū！ huì shī zhī cái， tái dàng ér bù dé， zhú wàn wù ér bù fǎn， shì qióng xiǎng yǐ shēng， xíng yǔ yǐng jìng zǒu yě。 bēi fū！

**EN:** Yet Hui Shi's talk — he himself counted it the worthiest — said: "Heaven and earth, what grandeur!" Shi kept his strength but had no method. In the south there was an odd man named Huang Liao (黃繚), who asked why heaven and earth do not fall or sink, and the cause of wind, rain, thunder, and lightning. Hui Shi answered without decline, replied without reflection — discoursing on all the ten thousand things; discoursing without rest, more and more without end, yet still thinking it too little, and adding marvels. He took contradicting men as substance, and wished to win a name by overcoming men — therefore he did not fit with the crowd. Weak in virtue, strong on things, his road is a bend. Seen from the Way of heaven and earth, Hui Shi's ability is like the labor of one mosquito or one fly — what use is it for things! To fulfill the One might do — to say "further, and precious!" — the Way is nearly there! Hui Shi could not take this to settle himself: scattered among the ten thousand things without satiety, he ended with a name for fine disputation. Alas! Hui Shi's talent — racing and tossing and never attaining, chasing the ten thousand things and never returning — it is like exhausting an echo with sound, or form and shadow competing in a race. Sad indeed!
