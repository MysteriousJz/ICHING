# Kings who have wished to resign the Throne (讓王)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Miscellaneous Chapters _ Kings who have wished to resign the Throne - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (24 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 堯以天下讓許由，許由不受。又讓於子州支父，子州支父曰：「以為我天子，猶之可也。雖然，我適有幽憂之病，方且治之，未暇治天下也。」夫天下至重也，而不以害其生，又況他物乎！唯無以天下為者，可以託天下也。

**EN:** Yao offered the world to Xu You; Xu You did not accept it. He offered it again to Zizhou Zhifu (子州支父). Zizhou Zhifu said: "To make me Son of Heaven — that would be acceptable. However, I happen to have a deep-seated illness; I am just now treating it; I have no leisure to treat the world." The world is the heaviest thing — and he would not let it harm his life. How much less any other thing! Only one who will not act on the world can be entrusted with the world.

## Section 2

**ZH:** 舜讓天下於子州支伯，子州支伯曰：「予適有幽憂之病，方且治之，未暇治天下也。」故天下大器也，而不以易生，此有道者之所以異乎俗者也。

**EN:** Shun offered the world to Zizhou Zhibo (子州支伯). Zizhou Zhibo said: "I happen to have a deep-seated illness; I am just now treating it; I have no leisure to treat the world." The world is the greatest vessel — and he would not exchange it for life. This is wherein one who has the Way differs from the vulgar.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 舜以天下讓善卷，善卷曰：「余立於宇宙之中，冬日衣皮毛，夏日衣葛絺；春耕種，形足以勞動；秋收斂，身足以休息；日出而作，日入而息，逍遙於天地之間而心意自得。吾何以天下為哉？悲夫！子之不知余也！」遂不受。於是去而入深山，莫知其處。

**EN:** Shun offered the world to Shan Juan (善卷). Shan Juan said: "I stand in the midst of the universe: in winter I wear furs; in summer I wear grass-cloth. In spring I plough and sow — my body suffices for the labor; in autumn I gather in the harvest — my person suffices for the rest. I work at sunrise and rest at sunset, roaming free and easy between heaven and earth, and my heart is content. What use have I for the world! Alas — you do not know me!" Thereupon he did not accept. He departed into the deep mountains, and none knows where he dwells.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 舜以天下讓其友石戶之農，石戶之農曰：「捲捲乎后之為人，葆力之士也。」以舜之德為未至也，於是夫負妻戴，攜子以入於海，終身不反也。

**EN:** Shun offered the world to his friend, the farmer of Shihu (石戶). The farmer of Shihu said: "How diligent the sovereign is as a man — a strength-preserving scholar!" Considering Shun's virtue not yet complete, he thereupon, with his wife on his back and his children led by the hand, went into the sea and never returned all his days.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 大王亶父居邠，狄人攻之。事之以皮帛而不受，事之以犬馬而不受，事之以珠玉而不受，狄人之所求者土地也。大王亶父曰：「與人之兄居而殺其弟，與人之父居而殺其子，吾不忍也。子皆勉居矣！為吾臣與為狄人臣，奚以異？且吾聞之，不以所用養害所養。」因杖筴而去之。民相連而從之，遂成國於岐山之下。夫大王亶父可謂能尊生矣。能尊生者，雖貴富不以養傷身，雖貧賤不以利累形。今世之人，居高官尊爵者，皆重失之，見利輕亡其身，豈不惑哉！

**EN:** The Great King Danfu (亶父) dwelt at Bin (邠), and the Di people attacked him. He offered them skins and silk — not accepted; dogs and horses — not accepted; pearls and jades — not accepted. What the Di sought was his land. The Great King Danfu said: "To dwell with men's elder brothers and kill their younger; to dwell with men's fathers and kill their sons — that I cannot bear. Do your best to stay on, all of you! To be my subject or to be the Di's subject — wherein is that different? Moreover, I have heard it said: do not harm what is to be nourished for the sake of what nourishes it." Thereupon, leaning on his staff, he departed. The people, linked together, followed him, and they founded a state beneath Mount Qi. The Great King Danfu may be called one who could honor life. One who can honor life, though high and rich, does not let nourishment injure his body; though poor and low, does not let profit burden his form. The men of today who occupy high office and honored titles all hold them heavily, fearing loss; seeing profit, they lightly lose their persons. Is that not delusion!

## Section 6

**ZH:** 越人三世弒其君，王子搜患之，逃乎丹穴。而越國無君，求王子搜不得，從之丹穴。王子搜不肯出，越人薰之以艾，乘以王輿。王子搜援綏登車，仰天而呼曰：「君乎君乎！獨不可以舍我乎！」王子搜非惡為君也，惡為君之患也。若王子搜者，可謂不以國傷生矣，此固越人之所欲得為君也。

**EN:** The men of Yue killed their lords for three generations. Prince Sou (王子搜), fearing for it, fled into a cinnabar cave. Yue having no lord, they sought Prince Sou and, not finding him, pursued him to the cinnabar cave. Prince Sou would not come out. The men of Yue smoked him out with mugwort, then set the royal carriage for him. Prince Sou, taking hold of the strap, mounted, and cried to heaven: "A lord, a lord! Can you not leave me alone!" It was not that Prince Sou hated being lord; he hated the calamities of being lord. Such a one as Prince Sou may be called one who will not let a state injure his life — and that is just what the men of Yue wished to get for their lord.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 韓、魏相與爭侵地。子華子見昭僖侯，昭僖侯有憂色。子華子曰：「今使天下書銘於君之前，書之言曰：『左手攫之則右手廢，右手攫之則左手廢，然而攫之者必有天下。』君能攫之乎？」昭僖侯曰：「寡人不攫也。」子華子曰：「甚善！自是觀之，兩臂重於天下也，身亦重於兩臂。韓之輕於天下亦遠矣，今之所爭者，其輕於韓又遠。君固愁身傷生以憂戚不得也！」僖侯曰：「善哉！教寡人者眾矣，未嘗得聞此言也。」子華子可謂知輕重矣。

**EN:** Han and Wei were contending over encroached territory. Zihuazi (子華子) was received by Marquis Zhaoxi (昭僖侯). The marquis wore a worried look. Zihuazi said: "Suppose the whole world inscribed a covenant before you, reading: 'Seize it with the left hand and the right hand perishes; seize it with the right hand and the left hand perishes — but he who seizes it will surely possess the world.' Could you seize it, my lord?" Marquis Zhaoxi said: "I could not seize it." Zihuazi said: "Very good! Seen in this light, two arms weigh more than the world; and the person weighs more than two arms. Han weighs far less than the world; what you now contend for weighs far less than Han. Must you worry your body and injure your life, fretting over what cannot be gotten!" The marquis said: "Splendid! Many have instructed me; never have I heard such words." Zihuazi may be called one who knows the light and the heavy.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 魯君聞顏闔得道之人也，使人以幣先焉。顏闔守陋閭，苴布之衣而自飯牛。魯君之使者至，顏闔自對之。使者曰：「此顏闔之家與？」顏闔對曰：「此闔之家也。」使者致幣，顏闔曰：「恐聽者謬而遺使者罪，不若審之。」使者還，反審之，復來求之，則不得已。故若顏闔者，真惡富貴也。

**EN:** The lord of Lu, hearing that Yan He was a man who had gotten the Way, sent men with gift-silks ahead. Yan He kept a humble lane; dressed in coarse cloth, he was feeding the ox himself. When the lord's messenger arrived, Yan He answered him in person. The messenger said: "Is this Yan He's house?" Yan He answered: "This is He's house." The messenger presented the silks. Yan He said: "I fear your instructions are mistaken, and the fault would fall on you, messenger. Better to examine it." The messenger went back, examined it, came again to seek him — and could not get him. So a man like Yan He truly hated riches and rank.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 故曰：道之真以治身，其緒餘以為國家，其土苴以治天下。由此觀之，帝王之功，聖人之餘事也，非所以完身養生也。今世俗之君子，多為身棄生以殉物，豈不悲哉！凡聖人之動作也，必察其所以之，與其所以為。今且有人於此，以隨侯之珠彈千仞之雀，世必笑之。是何也？則其所用者重而所要者輕也。夫生者，豈特隨侯之重哉！

**EN:** Hence it is said: the Way's truth serves to order the person; its thread-ends serve to make the state and family; its dregs serve to order the world. Seen in this light, the works of emperors and kings are the sage's leftover business — not the means of keeping the person whole and nourishing life. Yet the gentlemen of the vulgar world today mostly cast away life for the body's sake and martyr themselves to things — is that not sad! Whenever the sage acts, he is sure to examine whereto it goes and wherefore it acts. Suppose there were a man here who took the marquis of Sui's pearl and used it as a pellet at a sparrow a thousand fathoms up — the world would surely laugh at him. Why? Because what he uses is heavy and what he demands is light. And life — is it not far more than the marquis of Sui's weight!

## Section 10

**ZH:** 子列子窮，容貌有飢色。客有言之於鄭子陽者曰：「列御寇，蓋有道之士也，居君之國而窮，君無乃為不好士乎？」鄭子陽即令官遺之粟。子列子見使者，再拜而辭。使者去，子列子入，其妻望之而拊心曰：「妾聞為有道者之妻子，皆得佚樂，今有飢色。君過而遺先生食，先生不受，豈不命邪！」子列子笑謂之曰：「君非自知我也。以人之言而遺我粟，至其罪我也，又且以人之言。此吾所以不受也。」其卒，民果作難而殺子陽。

**EN:** Master Liezi was in straits; his face had the look of hunger. A guest spoke to Ziyang of Zheng: "Lie Yikou is a scholar who has the Way. He dwells in your state in straits — is that not a sign you do not love scholars?" Ziyang at once ordered an officer to send him grain. Master Liezi saw the messenger, bowed twice, and declined. The messenger gone, Master Liezi went in; his wife looked at him, beat her breast, and said: "I have heard that the wife and children of one who has the Way all get ease and joy — and here is hunger on our faces! The lord sent you food, sir, and you would not accept it — is that not fate!" Master Liezi laughed and said: "The lord did not know me himself. He sent me grain on other men's words; when he comes to count me guilty, it will again be on other men's words. That is why I did not accept." In the end, the people did indeed rise in revolt and kill Ziyang.

## Section 11

**ZH:** 楚昭王失國，屠羊說走而從於昭王。昭王反國，將賞從者，及屠羊說。屠羊說曰：「大王失國，說失屠羊；大王反國，說亦反屠羊。臣之爵祿已復矣，又何賞之言？」王曰：「強之！」屠羊說曰：「大王失國，非臣之罪，故不敢伏其誅；大王反國，非臣之功，故不敢當其賞。」王曰：「見之！」屠羊說曰：「楚國之法，必有重賞大功而後得見。今臣之知不足以存國，而勇不足以死寇。吳軍入郢，說畏難而避寇，非故隨大王也。今大王欲廢法毀約而見說，此非臣之所以聞於天下也。」王謂司馬子綦曰：「屠羊說居處卑賤而陳義甚高，子綦為我延之以三旌之位。」屠羊說曰：「夫三旌之位，吾知其貴於屠羊之肆也；萬鍾之祿，吾知其富於屠羊之利也。然豈可以食爵祿而使吾君有妄施之名乎！說不敢當，願復反吾屠羊之肆。」遂不受也。

**EN:** When King Zhao of Chu lost his state, the sheep-butcher Yue (屠羊說) fled in his train. King Zhao returned to the state and was about to reward his followers; it reached the sheep-butcher Yue. Yue said: "When the great king lost the state, Yue lost his sheep-shop; when the great king returned to the state, Yue returned to his sheep-shop. Your servant's title and stipend are already restored — what more is there to say of reward!" The king said: "Insist!" Yue said: "The great king's losing the state was not your servant's fault — therefore I dared not lie under its punishment; the great king's returning to the state was not your servant's merit — therefore I dared not take its reward." The king said: "See him!" Yue said: "By the law of Chu, one must have great reward and great merit before one is seen. Now your servant's knowledge was not enough to preserve the state, and my courage not enough to die before the invader. When the army of Wu entered Ying, Yue feared the trouble and avoided the foe — I did not of purpose follow the great king. Now the great king wishes to break the law and void the covenant to see Yue — this is not what I would be heard of for in the world." The king said to Marshal Ziqi: "The sheep-butcher Yue dwells in meanness yet sets forth rightness most high. Ziqi, for me invite him to the rank of the Three Banners." Yue said: "The rank of the Three Banners — I know it is nobler than a sheep-shop; a stipend of ten thousand measures — I know it is richer than a sheep-shop's profit. But how could I feed on title and stipend and give my lord the name of reckless bestowal! Yue dares not accept; I beg to return to my sheep-shop." Thereupon he did not accept.

## Section 12

**ZH:** 原憲居魯，環堵之室，茨以生草，蓬戶不完，桑以為樞而甕牖，二室，褐以為塞，上漏下溼，匡坐而弦。子貢乘大馬，中紺而表素，軒車不容巷，往見原憲。原憲華冠縰履，杖藜而應門。子貢曰：「嘻！先生何病？」原憲應之曰：「憲聞之：『無財謂之貧，學而不能行謂之病。』今憲，貧也，非病也。」子貢逡巡而有愧色。原憲笑曰：「夫希世而行，比周而友，學以為人，教以為己，仁義之慝，輿馬之飾，憲不忍為也。」

**EN:** Yuan Xian (原憲) dwelt in Lu in a four-square chamber thatched with living grass, a tumbleweed door gaping, a mulberry branch for a hinge and a jar for a window; two rooms, stuffed with coarse cloth; it leaked above and was damp below — and there he sat upright, strumming. Zigong, drawn by great horses, purple within and white without, his carriage too tall for the lane, went to see Yuan Xian. Yuan Xian, in a flower-bark cap and heelless shoes, leaning on a goosefoot staff, answered the door. Zigong said: "Ah — how ill you are, sir!" Yuan Xian answered: "I have heard: 'To be without wealth is called poor; to study and be unable to practice is called ill.' Now Xian is poor — not ill." Zigong, backing off, wore an abashed look. Yuan Xian laughed: "To act watching the age, to make friends in factions, to study for others, to teach for oneself, the villainy of humaneness and rightness, the trappings of carriage and horse — Xian cannot bear to do them."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 曾子居衛，縕袍無表，顏色腫噲，手足胼胝。三日不舉火，十年不製衣，正冠而纓絕，捉衿而肘見，納履而踵決。曳縰而歌商頌，聲滿天地，若出金石。天子不得臣，諸侯不得友。故養志者忘形，養形者忘利，致道者忘心矣。

**EN:** Zengzi dwelt in Wei: his padded robe had no outer cover; his face was swollen and jaundiced; his hands and feet were callused. For three days he kindled no fire; for ten years he made no clothes. Straighten his cap, and the strings broke; gather his collar, and his elbows showed; put on his shoes, and his heels split. Dragging his heelless shoes, he sang the Hymns of Shang; his voice filled heaven and earth as though issued from metal and stone. The Son of Heaven could not make him a subject; the lords could not make him a friend. So: one who nourishes the will forgets the form; one who nourishes the form forgets profit; one who arrives at the Way forgets the heart.

## Section 14

**ZH:** 孔子謂顏回曰：「回來！家貧居卑，胡不仕乎？」顏回對曰：「不願仕。回有郭外之田五十畝，足以給饘粥；郭內之田十畝，足以為絲麻；鼓琴足以自娛；所學夫子之道者足以自樂也。回不願仕。」孔子愀然變容曰：「善哉回之意！丘聞之：『知足者不以利自累也，審自得者失之而不懼，行修於內者無位而不怍。』丘誦之久矣，今於回而後見之，是丘之得也。」

**EN:** Confucius said to Yan Hui: "Hui, come here! Your family is poor and your station low — why do you not take office?" Yan Hui answered: "I do not wish office. Hui has fifty acres outside the outer wall — enough for congee; ten acres inside the wall — enough for silk and hemp; my lute suffices to amuse myself; what I study of the Master's Way suffices to delight myself. Hui does not wish office." Confucius, moved, changed countenance: "How fine is Hui's meaning! I have heard: 'One who knows enough does not burden himself with profit; one who discerns his own attainment loses without fear; one whose conduct is cultivated within does not blush at having no position.' Qiu has recited it long; now in Hui I see it — that is Qiu's gain."

## Section 15

**ZH:** 中山公子牟謂瞻子曰：「身在江海之上，心居乎魏闕之下，奈何？」瞻子曰：「重生。重生則利輕。」中山公子牟曰：「雖知之，未能自勝也。」瞻子曰：「不能自勝則從，神無惡乎？不能自勝而強不從者，此之謂重傷。重傷之人，無壽類矣。」魏牟，萬乘之公子也，其隱巖穴也，難為於布衣之士，雖未至乎道，可謂有其意矣。

**EN:** Prince Mou of Zhongshan (中山) said to Zhanzi (瞻子): "My body is above the rivers and seas, but my heart dwells beneath the towers of Wei. What is to be done?" Zhanzi said: "Give weight to life. Give weight to life, and profit grows light." Prince Mou of Zhongshan said: "I know that, but I cannot master myself." Zhanzi said: "If you cannot master yourself, then follow it — can the spirit hate it? To be unable to master yourself and forcibly not follow — this is called double injury. One doubly injured is not of the long-lived kind." Wei Mou is a prince of ten thousand chariots; his seclusion in the rocky caves was harder for him than for a plain-clothed scholar. Though he had not arrived at the Way, he may be said to have had the idea of it.

## Section 16

**ZH:** 孔子窮於陳、蔡之間，七日不火食，藜羹不糝，顏色甚憊，而弦歌於室。顏回擇菜，子路、子貢相與言曰：「夫子再逐於魯，削迹於衛，伐樹於宋，窮於商、周，圍於陳、蔡，殺夫子者無罪，藉夫子者無禁。弦歌鼓琴，未嘗絕音，君子之無恥也若此乎？」顏回無以應，入告孔子。孔子推琴喟然而歎曰：「由與賜，細人也。召而來！吾語之。」

**EN:** When Confucius was in straits between Chen and Cai, seven days without a cooked meal, his goosefoot soup without grain, his face utterly worn, he went on strumming and singing in his chamber. Yan Hui was picking herbs. Zilu and Zigong said together: "The Master was twice driven out of Lu; his tracks were wiped out in Wei; a tree was cut down over him in Song; he came to a dead end in Shang and Zhou; he is besieged between Chen and Cai. Whoever kills him is guiltless; whoever insults him is unprohibited. Yet strumming and singing and playing the lute, he never lets the sound cease. Is the gentleman's shamelessness really like this?" Yan Hui had no answer; he went in and told Confucius. Confucius pushed the lute aside and sighed: "You and Ci are petty men. Call them in! I will tell them."

## Section 17

**ZH:** 子路、子貢入。子路曰：「如此者可謂窮矣。」孔子曰：「是何言也！君子通於道之謂通，窮於道之謂窮。今丘抱仁義之道，以遭亂世之患，其何窮之為？故內省而不窮於道，臨難而不失其德，天寒既至，霜露既降，吾是以知松柏之茂也。陳、蔡之隘，於丘其幸乎！」孔子削然反琴而弦歌，子路扢然執干而舞。子貢曰：「吾不知天之高也，地之下也。」

**EN:** Zilu and Zigong came in. Zilu said: "One like this may be called in straits." Confucius said: "What words are these! A gentleman penetrating to the Way is called penetrating; a gentleman blocked from the Way is called in straits. Now Qiu embraces the Way of humaneness and rightness and has met the calamity of a disordered age — wherein am I in straits? So, examining within, I am not blocked from the Way; facing difficulty, I do not lose my virtue. When the cold of heaven has come, when frost and dew have fallen — then I know the pine and cypress's luxuriance. This strait between Chen and Cai — for Qiu it is a fortune!" Confucius took up the lute again and strummed and sang; Zilu, roused, took up his shield and danced. Zigong said: "I did not know the height of heaven, the depth of earth."

## Section 18

**ZH:** 古之得道者，窮亦樂，通亦樂。所樂非窮通也，道德於此，則窮通為寒暑風雨之序矣。故許由娛於潁陽，而共伯得乎共首。

**EN:** The ancients who had gotten the Way were joyful in straits and joyful in success. What they joyed in was not straits or success: when the Way and its virtue are here, then straits and success are but the sequence of cold and heat, wind and rain. So Xu You was happy at Yingyang; and Earl Gong found his at Gongshou.

## Section 19

**ZH:** 舜以天下讓其友北人無擇，北人無擇曰：「異哉！后之為人也，居於甽畝之中，而遊堯之門。不若是而已，又欲以其辱行漫我。吾羞見之。」因自投清泠之淵。

**EN:** Shun offered the world to his friend Beiren Wuze (北人無擇). Beiren Wuze said: "Strange is the sovereign as a man! Dwelling in the furrows of the fields, he roams Yao's gate. Not content with that, he wishes to smear me with his shameful conduct. I am ashamed to see him." Thereupon he threw himself into the deep of Qingling.

## Section 20

**ZH:** 湯將伐桀，因卞隨而謀，卞隨曰：「非吾事也。」湯曰：「孰可？」曰：「吾不知也。」湯又因瞀光而謀，瞀光曰：「非吾事也。」湯曰：「孰可？」曰：「吾不知也。」湯曰：「伊尹何如？」曰：「強力忍垢，吾不知其他也。」湯遂與伊尹謀伐桀。

**EN:** Tang, about to attack Jie, took counsel with Bian Sui (卞隨). Bian Sui said: "That is no affair of mine." Tang said: "Who will do?" He said: "I do not know." Tang took counsel with Mao Guang (瞀光). Mao Guang said: "That is no affair of mine." Tang said: "Who will do?" He said: "I do not know." Tang said: "How about Yi Yin?" He said: "Strong of force, enduring of disgrace — beyond that I do not know him." Tang then took counsel with Yi Yin to attack Jie.

## Section 21

**ZH:** 剋之，以讓卞隨。卞隨辭曰：「后之伐桀也謀乎我，必以我為賊也；勝桀而讓我，必以我為貪也。吾生乎亂世，而無道之人再來漫我以其辱行，吾不忍數聞也。」乃自投稠水而死。

**EN:** Having conquered him, Tang offered it to Bian Sui. Bian Sui declined: "That the sovereign, attacking Jie, took counsel with me — he must have counted me a brigand; that having conquered Jie he offers it to me — he must count me greedy. I was born into a disordered age, and men without the Way twice come to smear me with shameful conduct. I cannot bear to hear it again." Then he threw himself into the Chou River and died.

## Section 22

**ZH:** 湯又讓瞀光曰：「知者謀之，武者遂之，仁者居之，古之道也。吾子胡不立乎？」瞀光辭曰：「廢上，非義也；殺民，非仁也；人犯其難，我享其利，非廉也。吾聞之曰：『非其義者，不受其祿；無道之世，不踐其土。』況尊我乎！吾不忍久見也。」乃負石而自沈於廬水。

**EN:** Tang offered it again to Mao Guang, saying: "The knowing planned it; the martial accomplished it; the humane should occupy it — that is the way of old. Why do you not take your stand, sir?" Mao Guang declined: "To depose the sovereign is not right; to kill the people is not humane; others brave the difficulty and I enjoy the profit — that is not integrity. I have heard it said: 'What is not right — I will not take its stipend; an age without the Way — I will not tread its soil.' How much less to be honored thus! I cannot bear the sight of it long." Then, bearing a stone, he sank himself in the Lu River.

## Section 23

**ZH:** 昔周之興，有士二人處於孤竹，曰伯夷、叔齊。二人相謂曰：「吾聞西方有人，似有道者，試往觀焉。」至於岐陽，武王聞之，使叔旦往見之，與盟曰：「加富二等，就官一列。」血牲而埋之。二人相視而笑曰：「嘻！異哉！此非吾所謂道也。昔者神農之有天下也，時祀盡敬而不祈喜；其於人也，忠信盡治而無求焉。樂與政為政，樂與治為治，不以人之壞自成也，不以人之卑自高也，不以遭時自利也。今周見殷之亂而遽為政，上謀而下行貨，阻兵而保威，割牲而盟以為信，揚行以說眾，殺伐以要利，是推亂以易暴也。吾聞古之士遭治世不避其任，遇亂世不為苟存。今天下闇，周德衰，其並乎周以塗吾身也，不如避之以絜吾行。」

**EN:** Of old, when Zhou rose, there were two scholars dwelling at Guzhu (孤竹), named Boyi and Shuqi. The two said to one another: "We have heard that in the west there is a man who seems to have the Way. Let us go and see." Coming to Qiyang, King Wu heard of them and sent his brother Dan (叔旦, the Duke of Zhou) to see them. He covenanted with them: "Wealth increased twofold; offices in the first rank." They smeared the blood of a victim and buried the text. The two looked at one another and laughed: "Ah — strange! This is not what we call the Way. Of old, when Shennong held the world, he sacrificed at the seasons with full reverence and prayed for no blessing; toward men he was loyal and true, ordering all, and asked nothing. He rejoiced to share the government with those who shared his joy; he rejoiced to share the order with those who shared his order; he did not complete himself by others' ruin; he did not exalt himself by others' lowliness; he did not profit himself by meeting the time. Now Zhou, seeing the disorder of Yin, suddenly takes the government; above it schemes, below it practices bribery; it keeps its arms to guard its might; it cuts the victim and covenants to make good faith; it parades conduct to please the crowd; it slaughters and attacks to demand profit — this is to push disorder to exchange for violence. We have heard that the scholars of old, meeting a well-ordered age, did not avoid its charge; meeting a disordered age, did not preserve themselves at any cost. Now the world is dark; Zhou's virtue decays. Rather than join Zhou and mire our persons, better to avoid it and keep our conduct clean."

## Section 24

**ZH:** 二子北至於首陽之山，遂餓而死焉。若伯夷、叔齊者，其於富貴也，苟可得已，則必不賴。高節戾行，獨樂其志，不事於世，此二士之節也。

**EN:** The two went north to Mount Shouyang, and there starved and died. Men like Boyi and Shuqi: as for riches and rank — if they could be gotten at a price, they would have none of them. Lofty in measure, upright in conduct, delighting alone in their own wills, serving nothing in the world — this was the measure of these two scholars.
