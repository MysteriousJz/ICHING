# The Adjustment of Controversies (齊物論)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Inner Chapters _ The Adjustment of Controversies - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (14 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 南郭子綦隱几而坐，仰天而噓，嗒焉似喪其耦。顏成子游立侍乎前，曰：「何居乎？形固可使如槁木，而心固可使如死灰乎？今之隱几者，非昔之隱几者也。」子綦曰：「偃，不亦善乎而問之也！今者吾喪我，汝知之乎？女聞人籟而未聞地籟，女聞地籟而未聞天籟夫！」子游曰：「敢問其方。」子綦曰：「夫大塊噫氣，其名為風。是唯无作，作則萬竅怒呺。而獨不聞之翏翏乎？山林之畏佳，大木百圍之竅穴，似鼻，似口，似耳，似枅，似圈，似臼，似洼者，似污者；激者，謞者，叱者，吸者，叫者，譹者，宎者，咬者，前者唱于而隨者唱喁。泠風則小和，飄風則大和，厲風濟則眾竅為虛。而獨不見之調調、之刁刁乎？」子游曰：「地籟則眾竅是已，人籟則比竹是已。敢問天籟。」子綦曰：「夫吹萬不同，而使其自已也，咸其自取，怒者其誰邪！」

**EN:** Nanguo Ziqi (南郭子綦) sat leaning on his armrest, looked up to Heaven and sighed — vacant and far away, as though he had lost his counterpart. Yancheng Ziyou (顏成子游) stood in attendance before him and said: "What is this? Can the body really be made like a withered tree, and the mind like dead ashes? The one leaning on the armrest now is not the one who leaned on it before." Ziqi said: "Yan — how good your question is! Just now I had lost myself; did you know that? You have heard the pipes of men but not the pipes of earth; you may have heard the pipes of earth, but not the pipes of Heaven!" Ziyou said: "May I ask their manner?" Ziqi said: "The Great Clod belches out its breath; its name is wind. If only it would not stir — but when it stirs, the ten thousand hollows howl in fury. Have you alone never heard that long drawn-out roar? The crags and crevices of the mountain forests, the hollows and holes of great trees a hundred spans around — like noses, like mouths, like ears, like beam-sockets, like mortars, like cups, like deep pools, like shallow puddles. They hiss, they whistle, they scold, they suck, they shout, they wail, they moan, they gnash; those in front sing out *yuu!* and those behind answer *yeee!* A light breeze brings a small harmony; a whirling gale, a great one. And when the fierce wind dies down, the myriad hollows are emptied. Have you alone never seen them swaying and tossing?" Ziyou said: "The pipes of earth, then, are these myriad hollows; the pipes of men are flutes of bamboo set side by side. May I ask about the pipes of Heaven?" Ziqi said: "That which blows the ten thousand differences and lets each be itself, each choosing for itself — who is it that sets them raging?"

## Section 2

**ZH:** 大知閑閑，小知閒閒；大言炎炎，小言詹詹。其寐也魂交，其覺也形開，與接為構，日以心鬭。縵者，窖者，密者。小恐惴惴，大恐縵縵。其發若機栝，其司是非之謂也；其留如詛盟，其守勝之謂也；其殺如秋冬，以言其日消也；其溺之所為之，不可使復之也；其厭也如緘，以言其老洫也；近死之心，莫使復陽也。喜怒哀樂，慮嘆變慹，姚佚啟態；樂出虛，蒸成菌。日夜相代乎前，而莫知其所萌。已乎已乎！旦暮得此，其所由以生乎！

**EN:** Great knowledge is broad and easy; small knowledge is cramped and fussy. Great speech blazes; small speech babbles on. In sleep their souls entangle; awake, their bodies open outward; whatever they come into contact with, they turn into contrivance, day after day battling with their minds. Some are slow-spreading, some deep-buried, some tight-knit. [uncertain: 縵者，窖者，密者 — a compressed triad describing types of scheming temperament; renderings of the three terms diverge widely.] Small fears are fretful and trembling; great fears are dull and blank. They shoot off like the bolt from a crossbow — this is called arbitrating right and wrong; they hold fast as though bound by oath and covenant — this is called clinging to victory. They wither like autumn and winter — this tells of their daily dissolution; they drown in what they do and cannot be made to return; they are stoppered up as though sealed — this tells of the dried-up moat of their old age; a mind near death — nothing can restore its light. Joy and anger, sorrow and delight; brooding, sighing, shifting, clinging; flightiness, indulgence, boldness, pose — music issuing from empty tubes, damp steam condensing into mushrooms. Day and night they alternate before us, and none knows where they sprout. Enough! Enough! From dawn to dusk we get these — is it from them that we are born?

## Section 3

**ZH:** 非彼無我，非我無所取。是亦近矣，而不知其所為使。若有真宰，而特不得其眹。可行已信，而不見其形，有情而無形。百骸、九竅、六藏，賅而存焉，吾誰與為親？汝皆說之乎？其有私焉？如是皆有，為臣妾乎，其臣妾不足以相治乎。其遞相為君臣乎，其有真君存焉。如求得其情與不得，無益損乎其真。一受其成形，不亡以待盡。與物相刃相靡，其行盡如馳，而莫之能止，不亦悲乎！終身役役而不見其成功，苶然疲役而不知其所歸，可不哀邪！人謂之不死，奚益？其形化，其心與之然，可不謂大哀乎？人之生也，固若是芒乎！其我獨芒，而人亦有不芒者乎！

**EN:** Without that there is no me; without me there is nothing to take hold of it. This comes near the truth — yet we do not know what it is that makes it so. It is as if there were a true lord, though we can find no trace of it. One may walk by it and find it trustworthy, yet never see its form: it has a genuine condition, but no form. The hundred bones, the nine openings, the six viscera are all present and complete in me — to which of them am I most akin? Do you delight in them all alike, or have you a favorite among them? If they are all there together, are they servants and concubines? Yet servants and concubines cannot govern one another. Do they take turns as ruler and subject? Or is there a true ruler present among them? Whether we succeed in seeking out its true condition or not neither adds to nor diminishes its truth. Once we receive this completed form, we do not lose it but simply wait out our end. Hacking and grinding against things, we gallop toward the finish and nothing can halt us — is it not pitiful! All our lives we toil and slave and never see it come to anything; worn out and weary with service, not knowing where we are bound — can we not be mourned! Men say, "He has not died" — what good is that? When the form transforms and the mind goes with it — can this not be called the greatest sorrow? Is the life of man really so beclouded? Or is it only I who am beclouded, and there are others who are not?

## Section 4

**ZH:** 夫隨其成心而師之，誰獨且無師乎？奚必知代而心自取者有之？愚者與有焉。未成乎心而有是非，是今日適越而昔至也。是以無有為有。無有為有，雖有神禹，且不能知，吾獨且柰何哉！夫言非吹也。言者有言，其所言者特未定也。果有言邪？其未嘗有言邪？其以為異於鷇音，亦有辯乎，其無辯乎？道惡乎隱而有真偽？言惡乎隱而有是非？道惡乎往而不存？言惡乎存而不可？道隱於小成，言隱於榮華。故有儒、墨之是非，以是其所非，而非其所是。欲是其所非而非其所是，則莫若以明。

**EN:** If a man follows his completed mind and takes it as his teacher, who is there that would be without a teacher? Must one understand the alternations of things for the mind to make its own choice? Even a fool has such a teacher. But to have right and wrong before the mind has been completed is like setting out for Yue today and arriving there yesterday. That is to make what-is-not into what-is. To make what-is-not into what-is — even the divine Yu (禹) could not make sense of it; what then can I do about it! Now speech is not mere blowing. The speaker says something, but what he says is not yet fixed. Has he really said something, or has he never said anything at all? If we take it to differ from the cheeping of fledglings, is there truly a distinction, or is there none? How is the Way obscured, that there should be true and false? How is speech obscured, that there should be right and wrong? Where could the Way go and fail to be present? Where could speech be present and be inadmissible? The Way is obscured by small completions; speech is obscured by glory and flowering. Hence we have the rights and wrongs of the Confucians and the Mohists: each affirms what the other denies, and denies what the other affirms. But if one would affirm what they deny and deny what they affirm, nothing compares with using clarity.

## Section 5

**ZH:** 物無非彼，物無非是。自彼則不見，自知則知之。故曰：彼出於是，是亦因彼。彼是，方生之說也。雖然，方生方死，方死方生；方可方不可，方不可方可；因是因非，因非因是。是以聖人不由，而照之于天，亦因是也。是亦彼也，彼亦是也。彼亦一是非，此亦一是非。果且有彼是乎哉？果且無彼是乎哉？彼是莫得其偶，謂之道樞。樞始得其環中，以應無窮。是亦一無窮，非亦一無窮也。故曰「莫若以明」。

**EN:** There is nothing that is not a "that"; there is nothing that is not a "this." Seen from "that," one does not see it; known from within, one knows it. Hence it is said: "that" arises out of "this," and "this" in turn depends on "that." This is the doctrine that "this" and "that" are born together. Even so: just as a thing is born, it is dying; just as it is dying, it is being born. Just as it is admissible, it is inadmissible; just as it is inadmissible, it is admissible. Affirming follows upon denying; denying follows upon affirming. Therefore the sage does not travel this road, but illuminates things by Heaven; and this too is a kind of "following this." "This" is also a "that"; "that" is also a "this." "That" has its own right and wrong; "this" also has its own right and wrong. Is there really a "that" and a "this"? Or is there really no "that" and no "this"? When "that" and "this" no longer find their counterpart, this is called the hinge of the Way. Only when the hinge finds the center of the ring can it respond without end. "Right" is one endlessness; "wrong" is another endlessness. Hence I say: nothing compares with using clarity.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 以指喻指之非指，不若以非指喻指之非指也；以馬喻馬之非馬，不若以非馬喻馬之非馬也。天地，一指也；萬物，一馬也。可乎可，不可乎不可。道行之而成，物謂之而然。惡乎然？然於然。惡乎不然？不然於不然。物固有所然，物固有所可。無物不然，無物不可。故為是舉莛與楹，厲與西施，恢恑憰怪，道通為一。其分也，成也；其成也，毀也。凡物無成與毀，復通為一。唯達者知通為一，為是不用而寓諸庸。庸也者，用也；用也者，通也；通也者，得也。適得而幾矣。因是已。已而不知其然，謂之道。勞神明為一，而不知其同也，謂之朝三。何謂朝三？曰狙公賦芧，曰：「朝三而莫四。」眾狙皆怒。曰：「然則朝四而莫三。」眾狙皆悅。名實未虧，而喜怒為用，亦因是也。是以聖人和之以是非，而休乎天鈞，是之謂兩行。

**EN:** To use a finger to show that a finger is not a finger is not as good as using a non-finger to show that a finger is not a finger; to use a horse to show that a horse is not a horse is not as good as using a non-horse to show that a horse is not a horse. [uncertain: an allusion to the sophists' treatises "On Pointings and Things" (指物論) and "A White Horse Is Not a Horse" (白馬論) of Gongsun Long (公孫龍); the exact dialectical target is debated.] Heaven and earth are one finger; the ten thousand things are one horse. The admissible we admit; the inadmissible we do not. A way is made by being walked; a thing is so by being called so. How is it so? It is so in being so. How is it not so? It is not so in being not so. Things inherently have that by which they are so; things inherently have that by which they are admissible. There is nothing that is not so; nothing that is not admissible. So for this purpose let us raise a stalk and a pillar, a leper and the beautiful Xishi (西施), things vast, slippery, deceitful, and bizarre — the Way threads them all into one. Their division is their completion; their completion is their dissolution. All things, without completion or dissolution, are again threaded into one. Only the man who sees through it knows the threading into one. For this reason he does not put things to use, but lodges them in the ordinary. The ordinary is the useful; the useful is the passable; the passable is the attained. Once he has attained, he is nearly there. He follows this and stops. Stopping without knowing that it is so — this is called the Way. But to weary the spirit-light trying to make things one, not knowing that they are the same, is called "three in the morning." What is "three in the morning"? A monkey keeper was handing out chestnuts. He said: "Three in the morning and four in the evening." The monkeys were all enraged. He said: "Then four in the morning and three in the evening." The monkeys were all delighted. Name and substance had not been diminished, yet joy and anger were put to use — this too is a "following this." Therefore the sage harmonizes things by their rights and wrongs and rests upon the potter's wheel of Heaven. This is called walking both ways.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 古之人，其知有所至矣。惡乎至？有以為未始有物者，至矣盡矣，不可以加矣。其次以為有物矣，而未始有封也。其次以為有封焉，而未始有是非也。是非之彰也，道之所以虧也。道之所以虧，愛之所以成。果且有成與虧乎哉？果且無成與虧乎哉？有成與虧，故昭氏之鼓琴也；無成與虧，故昭氏之不鼓琴也。昭文之鼓琴也，師曠之枝策也，惠子之據梧也，三子之知幾乎！皆其盛者也，故載之末年。唯其好之也，以異於彼，其好之也，欲以明之彼。非所明而明之，故以堅白之昧終。而其子又以文之綸終，終身無成。若是而可謂成乎，雖我亦成也。若是而不可謂成乎，物與我無成也。是故滑疑之耀，聖人之所圖也。為是不用而寓諸庸，此之謂以明。

**EN:** The knowledge of the men of old had somewhere it arrived. Where did it arrive? Some held that there had never begun to be things — that is the utmost, the exhaustion; nothing can be added to it. The next held that there are things, but that there had never begun to be boundaries among them. The next held that there are boundaries, but that there had never begun to be right and wrong. When right and wrong became manifest, the Way was diminished; and with the Way diminished, love was completed. Is there really completion and diminution? Or is there really no completion and no diminution? That there is completion and diminution is the Zhaos playing the zither; that there is no completion and no diminution is the Zhaos not playing the zither. Zhao Wen's (昭文) zither-playing, Music Master Kuang's (師曠) tapping of the rhythm-stick, Huizi's leaning against the sterculia tree — how nearly perfect was the knowledge of these three masters! Each was at his height in it, and so it was carried down to late years. Because they loved it, they set themselves apart from others; loving it, they wished to illuminate others with it. But illuminating others with what cannot be illuminated, they ended in the obscurity of "hard and white." And Zhao Wen's son ended with his father's string-craft — all his life, nothing was completed. If such as this can be called completion, then even I am complete. But if it cannot be called completion, then neither things nor I have any completion at all. Therefore the glittering dazzle of slippery doubt is what the sage charts away from. [uncertain: 滑疑之耀，聖人之所圖也 — 圖 is a crux: read here as "charts (and so shuns)"; another tradition reads 圖 as a graphic loan for 鄙, "the sage disdains."] For this reason he does not put things to use, but lodges them in the ordinary. This is called using clarity.

## Section 8

**ZH:** 今且有言於此，不知其與是類乎？其與是不類乎？類與不類，相與為類，則與彼無以異矣。雖然，請嘗言之。有始也者，有未始有始也者，有未始有夫未始有始也者。有有也者，有無也者，有未始有無也者，有未始有夫未始有無也者。俄而有無矣，而未知有無之果孰有孰無也。今我則已有謂矣，而未知吾所謂之其果有謂乎，其果無謂乎？

**EN:** Now let me venture a saying here. I do not know whether it belongs to the same kind as "this," or whether it does not belong to the same kind. But belonging to a kind and not belonging to a kind, taken together, form a kind of their own — and then it is no different from "that." Nevertheless, let me try to say it. There is a beginning. There is a not-yet-beginning-to-be a beginning. There is a not-yet-beginning-to-be a not-yet-beginning-to-be a beginning. There is being. There is nonbeing. There is a not-yet-beginning-to-be nonbeing. There is a not-yet-beginning-to-be a not-yet-beginning-to-be nonbeing. All of a sudden there is nonbeing — and we no longer know, of being and nonbeing, which really is and which really is not. Now I have already said something; but I do not know whether what I have said really says anything, or really says nothing at all.

## Section 9

**ZH:** 天下莫大於秋豪之末，而大山為小；莫壽乎殤子，而彭祖為夭。天地與我並生，而萬物與我為一。既已為一矣，且得有言乎？既已謂之一矣，且得無言乎？一與言為二，二與一為三。自此以往，巧歷不能得，而況其凡乎！故自無適有，以至於三，而況自有適有乎！無適焉，因是已。

**EN:** Under heaven nothing is greater than the tip of an autumn hair, and Mount Tai is small; none is longer-lived than a child who dies in the cradle, and Pengzu dies young. Heaven and earth were born together with me, and the ten thousand things are one with me. But since we are already one, can there be anything to say? Yet since I have already called it "one," can there be nothing said? One plus the saying makes two; two plus the one makes three. Go on from here, and even the cleverest reckoner could not keep count — how much less an ordinary man! So if in going from nonbeing to being we already arrive at three, how much further in going from being to being! No more going on — follow this and stop.

## Section 10

**ZH:** 夫道未始有封，言未始有常，為是而有畛也。請言其畛：有左，有右，有倫，有義，有分，有辯，有競，有爭，此之謂八德。六合之外，聖人存而不論；六合之內，聖人論而不議。春秋經世，先王之志，聖人議而不辯。故分也者，有不分也；辯也者，有不辯也。曰：何也？聖人懷之，眾人辯之以相示也。故曰：辯也者，有不見也。夫大道不稱，大辯不言，大仁不仁，大廉不嗛，大勇不忮。道昭而不道，言辯而不及，仁常而不成，廉清而不信，勇忮而不成。五者园而幾向方矣。故知止其所不知，至矣。孰知不言之辯，不道之道？若有能知，此之謂天府。注焉而不滿，酌焉而不竭，而不知其所由來，此之謂葆光。故昔者堯問於舜曰：「我欲伐宗、膾、胥敖，南面而不釋然。其故何也？」舜曰：「夫三子者，猶存乎蓬艾之間。若不釋然，何哉？昔者十日並出，萬物皆照，而況德之進乎日者乎！」

**EN:** The Way has never had boundaries; speech has never had any constancy. Because of a "this," borders came into being. Let me name the borders: there is left, there is right; there is graded relation, there is propriety; there is division, there is discrimination; there is rivalry, there is contention. These are called the eight powers. Beyond the six coordinates, the sage keeps things in being but does not discuss them; within the six coordinates, he discusses but does not pass judgment. On the Spring and Autumn annals, the ordering of the generations, the records of the former kings, the sage passes judgment but does not dispute. Therefore: that which divides has something in it that is not divided; that which disputes has something in it that is not disputed. How so? The sage embraces things within himself; ordinary men dispute them in order to show them off to one another. Hence it is said: in disputation there is something that is not seen. The great Way is not named; great disputation says nothing; great humaneness is not humane; great modesty makes no show of deference; great courage does not lash out. A way that is made bright is not the Way; speech that disputes falls short; humaneness made constant fails to complete; modesty made pure cannot be trusted; courage that lashes out fails to complete. These five are round, but they tend toward the square. Therefore to know to stop at what one does not know — that is the utmost. Who knows the disputation that does not speak, the way that is not a way? If there is one who can know it, this is called the storehouse of Heaven. Pour into it and it does not fill; ladle from it and it is not drained; and none knows where it comes from. This is called the hidden light. Thus long ago Yao asked Shun, saying: "I wish to attack Zong (宗), Kuai (膾), and Xu'ao (胥敖). Though I sit on the throne facing south, I am not at ease. Why is that?" Shun said: "Those three little rulers still dwell among the weeds and mugwort. If you are not at ease, why is that? In ancient times ten suns came out together and shone upon all things — how much more should one whose virtue surpasses the sun!"

## Section 11

**ZH:** 齧缺問乎王倪曰：「子知物之所同是乎？」曰：「吾惡乎知之！」「子知子之所不知邪？」曰：「吾惡乎知之！」「然則物無知邪？」曰：「吾惡乎知之！雖然，嘗試言之。庸詎知吾所謂知之非不知邪？庸詎知吾所謂不知之非知邪？且吾嘗試問乎女：民溼寢則腰疾偏死，鰌然乎哉？木處則惴慄恂懼，猨猴然乎哉？三者孰知正處？民食芻豢，麋鹿食薦，蝍且甘帶，鴟鴉耆鼠，四者孰知正味？猨，猵狙以為雌，麋與鹿交，鰌與魚游。毛嬙、麗姬，人之所美也，魚見之深入，鳥見之高飛，麋鹿見之決驟。四者孰知天下之正色哉？自我觀之，仁義之端，是非之塗，樊然殽亂，吾惡能知其辯！」齧缺曰：「子不知利害，則至人固不知利害乎？」王倪曰：「至人神矣：大澤焚而不能熱，河、漢沍而不能寒，疾雷破山、風振海而不能驚。若然者，乘雲氣，騎日月，而遊乎四海之外。死生无變於己，而況利害之端乎！」

**EN:** Nie Que (齧缺) asked Wang Ni (王倪): "Do you know what all things agree in approving?" He said: "How would I know that!" "Do you know what you do not know?" "How would I know that!" "Then does nothing know anything?" "How would I know that! Still, let me try to say it. How do I know that what I call knowing is not not-knowing? How do I know that what I call not-knowing is not knowing? Now let me ask you something. If a man sleeps in the damp, his back aches and he half-paralyzes — is it so for a loach? If he dwells in a tree, he trembles and quakes with fear — is it so for a gibbon? Of these three, which knows the right place to dwell? Men eat fodder-fed and grain-fed beasts; deer feed on tender grass; centipedes find snakes sweet; owls and crows relish mice. Of these four, which knows the right taste? Gibbons take baboons as mates; elk couple with deer; loaches swim with fishes. Mao Qiang (毛嬙) and Lady Li (麗姬) were beauties in the eyes of men — but fish seeing them dived deep, birds seeing them flew high, deer seeing them bolted away. Of these four, which knows the true beauty under heaven? From where I see it, the sprouts of humaneness and rightness, the paths of right and wrong, are tangled and confused beyond sorting — how could I know their distinctions!" Nie Que said: "If you do not know benefit and harm, then does the utmost man really not know benefit and harm?" Wang Ni said: "The utmost man is spirit-like. A great marsh in flames cannot heat him; the Yellow River and the Han frozen fast cannot chill him; swift thunder splitting the mountains and winds lashing the seas cannot startle him. Such a one rides the cloudy vapors, mounts the sun and moon, and roams beyond the four seas. Death and life make no change in him — how much less the tip-ends of benefit and harm!"

## Section 12

**ZH:** 瞿鵲子問乎長梧子曰：「吾聞諸夫子，聖人不從事於務，不就利，不違害，不喜求，不緣道，无謂有謂，有謂无謂，而遊乎塵垢之外。夫子以為孟浪之言，而我以為妙道之行也。吾子以為奚若？」長梧子曰：「是黃帝之所聽熒也，而丘也何足以知之！且女亦大早計，見卵而求時夜，見彈而求鴞炙。予嘗為女妄言之，女以妄聽之，奚？旁日月，挾宇宙，為其脗合，置其滑涽，以隸相尊。眾人役役，聖人愚芚，參萬歲而一成純。萬物盡然，而以是相蘊。予惡乎知說生之非惑邪！予惡乎知惡死之非弱喪而不知歸者邪！麗之姬，艾封人之子也。晉國之始得之也，涕泣沾襟；及其至於王所，與王同筐床，食芻豢，而後悔其泣也。予惡乎知夫死者不悔其始之蘄生乎！夢飲酒者，旦而哭泣；夢哭泣者，旦而田獵。方其夢也，不知其夢也。夢之中又占其夢焉，覺而後知其夢也。且有大覺而後知此其大夢也，而愚者自以為覺，竊竊然知之。君乎，牧乎，固哉！丘也，與女皆夢也；予謂女夢，亦夢也。是其言也，其名為弔詭。萬世之後，而一遇大聖知其解者，是旦暮遇之也。既使我與若辯矣，若勝我，我不若勝，若果是也？我果非也邪？我勝若，若不吾勝，我果是也？而果非也邪？其或是也，其或非也邪？其俱是也，其俱非也邪？我與若不能相知也，則人固受其黮闇。吾誰使正之？使同乎若者正之，既與若同矣，惡能正之！使同乎我者正之，既同乎我矣，惡能正之！使異乎我與若者正之，既異乎我與若矣，惡能正之！使同乎我與若者正之，既同乎我與若矣，惡能正之！然則我與若與人俱不能相知也，而待彼也邪？何化聲之相待，若其不相待。和之以天倪，因之以曼衍，所以窮年也。謂和之以天倪？曰：是不是，然不然。是若果是也，則是之異乎不是也亦無辯；然若果然也，則然之異乎不然也亦無辯。化聲之相待，若其不相待。和之以天倪，因之以曼衍，所以窮年也。忘年忘義，振於無竟，故寓諸無竟。」

**EN:** Qu Quezi (瞿鵲子) asked Chang Wuzi (長梧子): "I heard from the Master that the sage does not occupy himself with affairs, does not chase benefit, does not shun harm, does not delight in seeking, does not hug the Way; that in saying nothing he says something, and in saying something he says nothing; and that he roams beyond the dust and grime. The Master took these for wild and reckless words, but I take them for the working of the wondrous Way. What do you make of them?" Chang Wuzi said: "These are words that would have left even the Yellow Emperor dazzled — how could Confucius be fit to understand them! And you too are reckoning far too early: you see an egg and demand the cock that crows the dawn; you see a pellet and demand roast owl. Still, I will speak recklessly for you, and you listen recklessly — how would that be? The sage stands beside the sun and moon, tucks the universe under his arm, makes all things fit together as one, sets aside the slippery and confused, and honors the menial as his peer. Ordinary men toil and slave; the sage is dull and simple, blending ten thousand years into one pure whole. All things are so as they are, and so are gathered up in this. How do I know that delighting in life is not a delusion? How do I know that hating death is not like being orphaned young and losing the way home? Lady Li was the daughter of the border guard of Ai (艾). When the state of Jin first seized her, she wept until her tears soaked her collar; but once she had come to the palace, shared the king's square couch, and eaten fodder-fed and grain-fed meat, she regretted that she had ever wept. How do I know that the dead do not regret their former longing for life! One who dreams of drinking wine weeps in the morning; one who dreams of weeping goes hunting in the morning. While he is dreaming, he does not know it is a dream; within the dream he may even interpret a dream — only when he wakes does he know it was a dream. And there will come a great awakening, after which we know that all of this was a great dream. Yet the fool believes himself awake, knowingly, smugly certain: a lord here, a herdsman there — how set in his ways! Confucius and you are both dreaming; and I, who tell you that you are dreaming, am dreaming too. These words of mine are called a supreme paradox. If after ten thousand generations we once meet a great sage who knows the solution, it will be as though we met him morning and evening. Now suppose you and I have a dispute. If you win and I lose, does that make you really right and me really wrong? If I win and you lose, does that make me really right and you really wrong? Is one of us right and the other wrong? Are both of us right, or both of us wrong? If you and I cannot come to know, others are left in the dark with us. Whom shall we get to decide it? Get one who agrees with you to decide — but he already agrees with you; how could he decide it? Get one who agrees with me — but he already agrees with me; how could he decide it? Get one who differs from us both — but he already differs from us both; how could he decide it? Get one who agrees with us both — but he already agrees with us both; how could he decide it? So neither you, nor I, nor any other can come to know. Must we then wait upon yet another? What do the shifting voices depend upon? It is as if they depended on nothing. Harmonize them by the natural boundaries, follow them as they flow and spread — this is the way to live out your years. What is it to harmonize by the natural boundaries? It means: right what is not right; so what is not so. If right is really right, then right's difference from not-right needs no arguing; if so is really so, then so's difference from not-so needs no arguing. The shifting voices depend on one another as though depending on nothing. Harmonize them by the natural boundaries, follow them as they flow and spread — this is the way to live out your years. Forget the years, forget the standards, and be stirred up in the boundless — therefore lodge all things in the boundless."

## Section 13

**ZH:** 罔兩問景曰：「曩子行，今子止，曩子坐，今子起，何其無特操與？」景曰：「吾有待而然者邪！吾所待又有待而然者邪！吾待蛇蚹、蜩翼邪！惡識所以然？惡識所以不然？」

**EN:** Penumbra said to Shadow: "A moment ago you were moving, now you stop; a moment ago you were sitting, now you rise. Why do you have no constant course of your own?" Shadow said: "Am I as I am because I depend on something? And does that on which I depend in turn depend on something else? Do I depend on a snake's scales or a cicada's wings? How would I know why it is so? How would I know why it is not so?"

## Section 14

**ZH:** 昔者莊周夢為胡蝶，栩栩然胡蝶也，自喻適志與！不知周也。俄然覺，則蘧蘧然周也。不知周之夢為胡蝶與，胡蝶之夢為周與？周與胡蝶，則必有分矣。此之謂物化。

**EN:** Once Zhuang Zhou (莊周) dreamt he was a butterfly — a fluttering butterfly, well pleased with itself and free as it wished! It did not know it was Zhou. All at once he awoke — and there, solid and distinct, was Zhou. He did not know whether Zhou had dreamt he was a butterfly, or whether a butterfly was dreaming it was Zhou. Between Zhou and the butterfly there must indeed be a distinction. This is called the transformation of things.
