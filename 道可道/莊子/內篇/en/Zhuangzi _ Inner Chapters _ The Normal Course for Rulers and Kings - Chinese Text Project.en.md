# The Normal Course for Rulers and Kings (應帝王)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Inner Chapters _ The Normal Course for Rulers and Kings - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (7 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 齧缺問於王倪，四問而四不知。齧缺因躍而大喜，行以告蒲衣子。蒲衣子曰：「而乃今知之乎？有虞氏不及泰氏。有虞氏，其猶藏仁以要人，亦得人矣，而未始出於非人。泰氏，其臥徐徐，其覺于于，一以己為馬，一以己為牛，其知情信，其德甚真，而未始入於非人。」

**EN:** Nie Que questioned Wang Ni — four questions, and four times he did not know. Nie Que leapt up in great delight and went to tell Puyizi (蒲衣子). Puyizi said: "Do you know it only now? The house of Youyu (有虞氏, sc. Shun) cannot match the house of Tai (泰氏). The house of Youyu still stored up humaneness to bind men to itself; it did win men, but it never got out of the not-yet-human. [uncertain: 非人 — read here, with one commentarial line, as "the not-yet-(fully)-human," i.e., the realm of artifice and distinctions; another reads it as the "not-right," the sphere of approval and disapproval.] The house of Tai slept sound and easy, woke blank and free; now they took themselves for a horse, now for a cow. Their knowledge was genuine and sure; their virtue was utterly true — and they never entered into the not-yet-human."

## Section 2

**ZH:** 肩吾見狂接輿。狂接輿曰：「日中始何以語女？」肩吾曰：「告我：君人者，以己出經式義度，人孰敢不聽而化諸！」狂接輿曰：「是欺德也。其於治天下也，猶涉海鑿河，而使蚉負山也。夫聖人之治也，治外乎？正而後行，確乎能其事者而已矣。且鳥高飛以避矰弋之害，鼷鼠深穴乎神丘之下，以避熏鑿之患，而曾二蟲之無知！」

**EN:** Jianwu saw the madman Jieyu. Jieyu said: "What did Ri Zhongshi (日中始) tell you?" Jianwu said: "He told me: when a ruler of men frames rules, patterns, measures, and standards out of himself, who would dare not heed and be transformed!" Jieyu said: "That is fraudulent virtue. For governing the world, it is like wading into the sea to dig a river, or setting a mosquito to carry a mountain on its back. When the sage governs, does he govern what is outside? He rectifies and then acts, making sure that each can do his own task — that is all. Birds fly high to escape the harm of dart and arrow; tiny mice burrow deep beneath the sacred hill to escape the calamity of smoking and digging. And you — are you more witless than these two little creatures!"

## Section 3

**ZH:** 天根遊於殷陽，至蓼水之上，適遭無名人而問焉，曰：「請問為天下。」無名人曰：「去！汝鄙人也，何問之不豫也！予方將與造物者為人，厭則又乘夫莽眇之鳥，以出六極之外，而遊無何有之鄉，以處壙埌之野。汝又何帠以治天下感予之心為？」又復問。無名人曰：「汝遊心於淡，合氣於漠，順物自然，而無容私焉，而天下治矣。」

**EN:** Tian'gen (天根), roaming on the sunny side of Mount Yin, came to the banks of the Liao River (蓼水), where he happened upon a nameless man and asked him: "May I ask about the governing of the world?" The nameless man said: "Away with you! You boor — what a dismal question! I am on the point of becoming a companion of the Creator; and when I tire of that, I shall mount the vast and boundless bird, go out beyond the six extremities, roam in the land of Nothing-Whatever, and dwell in the wilds of open emptiness. Why do you come troubling my heart with the governing of the world?" He asked again. The nameless man said: "Let your mind roam in the bland; blend your breath with the featureless; follow things as they are of themselves, and make no room for the private — then the world will be governed."

## Section 4

**ZH:** 陽子居見老聃曰：「有人於此，嚮疾強梁，物徹疏明，學道不倦。如是者，可比明王乎？」老聃曰：「是於聖人也，胥易技係，勞形怵心者也。且也虎豹之文來田，猿狙之便、執嫠之狗來藉。如是者，可比明王乎？」陽子居蹴然曰：「敢問明王之治。」老聃曰：「明王之治，功蓋天下而似不自己，化貸萬物而民弗恃，有莫舉名，使物自喜，立乎不測，而遊於無有者也。」

**EN:** Yang Ziju (陽子居) saw Lao Dan and said: "Suppose there is a man here — swift and resolute, penetrating and clear-sighted in things, unwearied in learning the Way. Can such a one be compared with an enlightened king?" Lao Dan said: "Set beside a sage, he is a clerk trading his skills for bondage, wearing out his body and fretting his mind. Moreover, the patterned coats of tiger and leopard invite the hunter; the nimbleness of gibbon and monkey, and the rat-catching dog, invite the leash. [uncertain: 執嫠之狗 — 嫠 is variously read as 狸 ('the fox-catching dog') and as 'rat'; the general sense — talent invites snares — is unaffected.] Can such a one be compared with an enlightened king?" Yang Ziju started and said: "May I ask about the governing of an enlightened king?" Lao Dan said: "The governing of an enlightened king: his merit covers the world, yet seems not his own; his transforming bounty reaches the ten thousand things, yet the people do not lean on him; he has being, yet none can put a name to him; he lets each thing delight in itself; he stands upon the unfathomable and roams in what is not."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 鄭有神巫曰季咸，知人之生死存亡，禍福壽夭，期以歲月旬日，若神。鄭人見之，皆棄而走。列子見之而心醉，歸以告壺子，曰：「始吾以夫子之道為至矣，則又有至焉者矣。」壺子曰：「吾與汝既其文，未既其實，而固得道與？」眾雌而無雄，而又奚卵焉！而以道與世亢必信，夫故使人得而相女。嘗試與來，以予示之。」明日，列子與之見壺子。出而謂列子曰：「嘻！子之先生死矣，弗活矣，不以旬數矣！吾見怪焉，見溼灰焉。」列子入，泣涕沾襟，以告壺子。壺子曰：「鄉吾示之以地文，萌乎不震不正。是殆見吾杜德機也。嘗又與來。」明日，又與之見壺子。出而謂列子曰：「幸矣！子之先生遇我也。有瘳矣，全然有生矣。吾見其杜權矣。」列子入，以告壺子。壺子曰：「鄉吾示之以天壤，名實不入，而機發於踵。是殆見吾善者機也。嘗又與來。」明日，又與之見壺子。出而謂列子曰：「子之先生不齊，吾無得而相焉。試齊，且復相之。」列子入，以告壺子。壺子曰：「吾鄉示之以太沖莫勝。是殆見吾衡氣機也。鯢桓之審為淵，止水之審為淵，流水之審為淵。淵有九名，此處三焉。嘗又與來。」明日，又與之見壺子。立未定，自失而走。壺子曰：「追之！」列子追之不及，反以報壺子，曰：「已滅矣，已失矣，吾弗及也。」壺子曰：「鄉吾示之以未始出吾宗。吾與之虛而委蛇，不知其誰何，因以為弟靡，因以為波流，故逃也。」然後列子自以為未始學而歸，三年不出。為其妻爨，食豕如食人。於事無與親，彫琢復朴，塊然獨以其形立。紛而封哉，一以是終。

**EN:** In Zheng there was a spirit-shaman named Jixian (季咸), who knew men's death and life, survival and ruin, misfortune and fortune, long life and early death, fixing them to the year, the month, the decade, and the day — like a god. The people of Zheng, seeing him, would all turn and flee. Liezi saw him and was drunk with admiration. Returning, he told Huzi (壺子): "At first I took your Way, Master, for the utmost; but now there is something more utmost still." Huzi said: "I gave you the pattern, but not yet the substance — and do you suppose you have attained the Way? A flock of hens without a cock — what eggs can there be! You take the Way and pit it against the world, determined to be believed; that is why people can read your face. Try bringing him here, and let him be shown me." The next day Liezi brought him to see Huzi. Coming out, Jixian said to Liezi: "Ah! Your master is dying; he will not live; he has not ten days! I saw something strange in him — I saw wet ashes." Liezi went in, tears soaking his collar, and told Huzi. Huzi said: "Just now I showed him the pattern of earth — sprouting, yet unshaken and unrighted. He must have seen my blocked impulse of virtue. Bring him again." The next day they came again. Coming out, Jixian said to Liezi: "How lucky that your master met me! He will recover; he is wholly alive. I saw his blocked leverage." Liezi went in and told Huzi. Huzi said: "Just now I showed him heaven's soil, where name and substance cannot enter, and the impulse issues from the heels. He must have seen my impulse of goodness. Bring him again." The next day they came again. Coming out, Jixian said to Liezi: "Your master is not steady; I can get no reading of him. Let him steady himself, and I will read him again." Liezi went in and told Huzi. Huzi said: "Just now I showed him the great void where nothing prevails. He must have seen my impulse of balanced breath. Where the sea-monsters wheel is a deep; where the water is still is a deep; where the water flows is a deep. The deep has nine names; here were three of them. Bring him again." The next day they came again. Before Jixian had steadied on his feet, he lost his nerve and fled. Huzi said: "After him!" Liezi chased him but could not catch him, and came back to report: "He has vanished; he is lost; I could not reach him." Huzi said: "Just now I showed him what has never yet emerged from my source. I gave him emptiness and serpentine ease, so that he could not tell who or what I was — now I became bending grass, now flowing wave; therefore he fled." Then Liezi understood that he had never yet begun to learn, and went home. For three years he did not go out. He cooked for his wife; he fed the pigs as though feeding men. In affairs he showed no favoritism; he cut away the carving and returned to the uncut block; solitary as a clod, he stood in his form alone. Sealed off in the midst of the tangle — one with this to the very end.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 無為名尸，無為謀府，無為事任，無為知主。體盡無窮，而遊無朕，盡其所受於天，而無見得，亦虛而已。至人之用心若鏡，不將不迎，應而不藏，故能勝物而不傷。

**EN:** Do not be the corpse of fame; do not be the storehouse of schemes; do not be the bearer of affairs; do not be the master of knowledge. Embody the endless to the full, and roam where there is no trace; fulfill what you have received from Heaven, with no thought of gain — be empty, that is all. The utmost man's use of his mind is like a mirror: it does not escort things away nor run to welcome them; it responds but does not store. Therefore he can master things and not be injured.

## Section 7

**ZH:** 南海之帝為儵，北海之帝為忽，中央之帝為渾沌。儵與忽時相與遇於渾沌之地，渾沌待之甚善。儵與忽謀報渾沌之德，曰：「人皆有七竅，以視聽食息，此獨無有，嘗試鑿之。」日鑿一竅，七日而渾沌死。

**EN:** The lord of the southern sea was Swift (儵); the lord of the northern sea was Sudden (忽); the lord of the center was Chaos (渾沌). Swift and Sudden often met together in the land of Chaos, and Chaos treated them very well. Swift and Sudden planned how to repay Chaos's kindness, saying: "All men have seven openings, to see, hear, eat, and breathe; he alone has none. Let us try boring some for him." Each day they bored one opening — and on the seventh day Chaos died.

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 聖人 → "the sage"; 至人 → "the utmost man"; 德 → "virtue"; 道 → "the Way"; 知 → "knowledge"; 機 → "the impulse" (德機 "the impulse of virtue"); 朴 → "the uncut block"; 造物者 → "the Creator."
- Section 5's four "showings" (地文, 天壤, 太沖莫勝, 未始出吾宗) are rendered as concrete images with the technical vocabulary kept literal; the passage is deliberately oracular, and no rendering can remove that quality.
- `[uncertain]` flags: 2 (§1 非人; §4 執嫠之狗). A reviewer may also compare §5 萌乎不震不正 ("sprouting, yet unshaken and unrighted"), where some texts read 止 for 正.
