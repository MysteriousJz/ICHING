# The Seal of Virtue Complete (德充符)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Inner Chapters _ The Seal of Virtue Complete - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (6 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 魯有兀者王駘，從之遊者，與仲尼相若。常季問於仲尼曰：「王駘，兀者也，從之遊者，與夫子中分魯。立不教，坐不議，虛而往，實而歸。固有不言之教，無形而心成者邪？是何人也？」仲尼曰：「夫子，聖人也。丘也，直後而未往耳。丘將以為師，而況不如丘者乎！奚假魯國！丘將引天下而與從之。」常季曰：「彼兀者也，而王先生，其與庸亦遠矣。若然者，其用心也，獨若之何？」仲尼曰：「死生亦大矣，而不得與之變，雖天地覆墜，亦將不與之遺。審乎無假，而不與物遷，命物之化，而守其宗也。」常季曰：「何謂也？」仲尼曰：「自其異者視之，肝膽楚越也；自其同者視之，萬物皆一也。夫若然者，且不知耳目之所宜，而游心於德之和，物視其所一，而不見其所喪，視喪其足，猶遺土也。」常季曰：「彼為己，以其知得其心，以其心得其常心，物何為最之哉？」仲尼曰：「人莫鑑於流水，而鑑於止水，唯止能止眾止。受命於地，唯松柏獨也在，冬夏青青；受命於天，唯舜獨也正，幸能正生，以正眾生。夫保始之徵，不懼之實。勇士一人，雄入於九軍。將求名而能自要者，而猶若此，而況官天地，府萬物，直寓六骸，象耳目，一知之所知，而心未嘗死者乎！彼且擇日而登假，人則從是也。彼且何肯以物為事乎！」

**EN:** In Lu there was a footless man, Wang Tai (王駘), and those who followed him were as many as the followers of Confucius. Chang Ji (常季) asked Confucius: "Wang Tai is a footless man, yet those who follow him split Lu with you, Master. Standing, he teaches nothing; seated, he discusses nothing; yet they go to him empty and return full. Is there really such a thing as a teaching without words, a completion of the mind without form? What manner of man is he?" Confucius said: "The master is a sage; I have simply been slow to go to him. I shall take him as my teacher — how much more should those who are not my equals! And why stop at the state of Lu? I shall lead all under heaven to follow him." Chang Ji said: "He is a footless man, yet he kings it over you, Master; he must be far from the ordinary. If that is so, how does he use his mind?" Confucius said: "Death and life are great indeed, yet he does not change with them. Though heaven and earth were to topple and fall, he would not be lost along with them. Discerning the flawless, he does not shift with things; he lets things transform as they are fated, and holds fast to their source. [uncertain: 審乎無假 — rendered "discerning the flawless" (i.e., what is without falsity or defect); some take 假 as 'borrowed, dependent': 'discerning that he depends on nothing.']" Chang Ji said: "What does that mean?" Confucius said: "Seen from the standpoint of their difference, liver and gall are as far apart as Chu and Yue; seen from the standpoint of their sameness, the ten thousand things are all one. A man like that no longer knows what is fitting for ear or eye, but lets his mind roam in the harmony of virtue. He looks upon the oneness of things and does not see what anything has lost; he regards the loss of his foot as a clod of earth cast away." Chang Ji said: "He does it for himself. By his knowledge he gets at his mind; by his mind he gets at the constant mind. Why then do things gather around him?" Confucius said: "Men do not mirror themselves in flowing water; they mirror themselves in still water. Only the still can still the stillness of the multitude. Of those that received their lot from earth, only the pine and cypress stand evergreen in winter and summer; of those that received their lot from Heaven, only Shun stood upright — fortunate to be able to set his own life right, and thereby to set right the lives of the multitude. The proof of preserving the beginning is the reality of fearlessness. A single brave warrior will plunge boldly into nine armies. If one who seeks fame and can exact it of himself can still do that, how much more one who makes heaven and earth his office, the ten thousand things his storehouse, who merely lodges in his six limbs, who takes ears and eyes for images, who makes one of all that knowledge knows, and whose mind has never tasted death! Soon he will choose a day and ascend to the far-off; men follow after him, but how could he be willing to make things his task?"

## Section 2

**ZH:** 申徒嘉，兀者也，而與鄭子產同師於伯昏無人。子產謂申徒嘉曰：「我先出，則子止；子先出，則我止。」其明日，又與合堂同席而坐。子產謂申徒嘉曰：「我先出，則子止；子先出，則我止。今我將出，子可以止乎，其未邪？且子見執政而不違，子齊執政乎？」申徒嘉曰：「先生之門，固有執政焉如此哉？子而說子之執政而後人者也！聞之曰：『鑑明則塵垢不止，止則不明也。久與賢人處，則無過。』今子之所取大者，先生也，而猶出言若是，不亦過乎！」子產曰：「子既若是矣，猶與堯爭善，計子之德不足以自反邪？」申徒嘉曰：「自狀其過以不當亡者眾，不狀其過以不當存者寡。知不可奈何而安之若命，惟有德者能之。遊於羿之彀中，中央者，中地也，然而不中者，命也。人以其全足笑吾不全足者多矣。我怫然而怒，而適先生之所，則廢然而反。不知先生之洗我以善邪！吾與夫子遊十九年矣，而未嘗知吾兀者也。今子與我遊於形骸之內，而子索我於形骸之外，不亦過乎！」子產蹴然改容更貌曰：「子無乃稱！」

**EN:** Shentu Jia (申徒嘉), a footless man, studied together with Zichan (子產) of Zheng under Bahun Wuren (伯昏無人). Zichan said to Shentu Jia: "If I leave first, you stay behind; if you leave first, I stay behind." The next day they were again seated together in the same hall. Zichan said to Shentu Jia: "If I leave first, you stay behind; if you leave first, I stay behind. Now I am about to leave — will you stay, or will you not? Moreover, you see the chief minister and do not yield — do you make yourself the chief minister's equal?" Shentu Jia said: "Within our master's gate, is there really a chief minister such as this? You take delight in your chief-ministership and put others behind you! I have heard it said: 'When the mirror is bright, dust and grime do not settle; if they settle, it was not bright. Dwell long with worthy men, and you will be without fault.' Now it is our master whom you take as your greatness — yet you still utter words like these. Is that not a fault?" Zichan said: "Already as you are, you still vie with Yao in goodness. Take stock of your virtue — is it not enough to make you look within?" Shentu Jia said: "Many plead their fault away as undeserving of losing their feet; few own their fault as undeserving of keeping them. To know there is nothing to be done and to rest in it as destiny — only one of virtue can do that. To wander within the archer Yi's (羿) bow-range is to stand in the central place, the place where one is hit; and yet not to be hit — that is fate. Many have laughed at my incomplete feet for having feet of their own complete. I would bristle with anger; but going to the master's place, I would return calm and spent — without knowing it, the master washed me with goodness. I have wandered with him nineteen years, and he has never once let me know that I was footless. Now you and I wander within the frame of the body, yet you demand of me what lies outside the frame of the body. Is that not a fault?" Zichan started and changed countenance: "Say no more, sir!"

## Section 3

**ZH:** 魯有兀者叔山無趾，踵見仲尼。仲尼曰：「子不謹，前既犯患若是矣。雖今來，何及矣？」無趾曰：「吾唯不知務而輕用吾身，吾是以亡足。今吾來也，猶有尊足者存，吾是以務全之也。夫天無不覆，地無不載，吾以夫子為天地，安知夫子之猶若是也！」孔子曰：「丘則陋矣。夫子胡不入乎？請講以所聞！」無趾出。孔子曰：「弟子勉之！夫無趾，兀者也，猶務學以復補前行之惡，而況全德之人乎！」無趾語老聃曰：「孔丘之於至人，其未邪！彼何賓賓以學子為？彼且蘄以諔詭幻怪之名聞，不知至人之以是為己桎梏邪？」老聃曰：「胡不直使彼以死生為一條，以可不可為一貫者，解其桎梏，其可乎？」無趾曰：「天刑之，安可解？」

**EN:** In Lu there was a footless man, Shushan Wuzhi (叔山無趾), who came on his heels to see Confucius. Confucius said: "You were not careful; you already brought this calamity upon yourself. What good does it do to come now?" Wuzhi said: "It was only because I did not know what mattered and used my person recklessly that I lost my feet. I come now because there is still something more honorable than feet that survives, and I mean to keep it whole. Heaven covers all; earth bears all. I took you, Master, for heaven and earth — who would have thought you would be like this!" Confucius said: "I am indeed narrow. Will you not come in, sir, and speak of what you have heard?" Wuzhi went out. Confucius said: "Strive on, my disciples! Wuzhi is a footless man, yet still he works at learning to repair the evil of his former conduct — how much more should a man whose virtue is whole!" Wuzhi said to Lao Dan (老聃): "As for being an utmost man — Confucius has not got there, has he? Why else does he come studying with you so earnestly and so often? He longs to be known for a name that is strange and marvelous and fantastic — does he not know that the utmost man regards such things as his own fetters and shackles?" Lao Dan said: "Why not simply make him see death and life as a single thread, and admissible and inadmissible as a single strand, and so release his fetters? Would that not do?" Wuzhi said: "Heaven has punished him — how can he be released?"

## Section 4

**ZH:** 魯哀公問於仲尼曰：「衛有惡人焉，曰哀駘它。丈夫與之處者，思而不能去也。婦人見之，請於父母曰『與為人妻，寧為夫子妾』者，十數而未止也。未嘗有聞其唱者也，常和而已矣。無君人之位以濟乎人之死，無聚祿以望人之腹。又以惡駭天下，和而不唱，知不出乎四域，且而雌雄合乎前。是必有異乎人者也。寡人召而觀之，果以惡駭天下。與寡人處，不至以月數，而寡人有意乎其為人也；不至乎期年，而寡人信之。國無宰，寡人傳國焉。悶然而後應，氾而若辭。寡人醜乎，卒授之國。無幾何也，去寡人而行，寡人卹焉若有亡也，若無與樂是國也。是何人者也？」仲尼曰：「丘也，嘗使於楚矣，適見㹠子食於其死母者，少焉眴若，皆棄之而走。不見己焉爾，不得類焉爾。所愛其母者，非愛其形也，愛使其形者也。戰而死者，其人之葬也，不以翣資，刖者之屨，無為愛之，皆無其本矣。為天子之諸御，不爪翦，不穿耳；娶妻者止於外，不得復使。形全猶足以為爾，而況全德之人乎！今哀駘它未言而信，無功而親，使人授己國，唯恐其不受也，是必才全而德不形者也。」哀公曰：「何謂才全？」仲尼曰：「死生存亡，窮達貧富，賢與不肖，毀譽、饑渴、寒暑，是事之變，命之行也；日夜相代乎前，而知不能規乎其始者也。故不足以滑和，不可入於靈府。使之和豫通而不失於兌，使日夜無郤而與物為春，是接而生時於心者也。是之謂才全。」「何謂德不形？」曰：「平者，水停之盛也。其可以為法也，內保之而外不蕩也。德者，成和之修也。德不形者，物不能離也。」哀公異日以告閔子曰：「始也，吾以南面而君天下，執民之紀，而憂其死，吾自以為至通矣。今吾聞至人之言，恐吾無其實，輕用吾身而亡其國。吾與孔丘，非君臣也，德友而已矣。」

**EN:** Duke Ai of Lu (魯哀公) asked Confucius: "In Wei there is an ugly man named Ai Taituo (哀駘它). Men who keep company with him think of him and cannot tear themselves away; women who have seen him beg their parents, saying, 'Rather than be another man's wife, I would be his concubine' — a dozen and more, and still they come. No one has ever heard him take the lead; he only harmonizes with others. He has no ruler's station with which to save men from death, no store of grain with which to fill their bellies. His ugliness shocks the world; he harmonizes without leading; his knowledge never strays beyond the four quarters — and yet male and female gather before him. He must be something other than ordinary men. I summoned him and looked at him — indeed, his ugliness shocked the world. He dwelt with me not a month before I began to take interest in his person; not a year before I trusted him. The state had no chief minister, and I offered him the state. He answered dully and after a pause, vaguely, as though declining. I was abashed, but in the end I gave him the state. Before long he left me and went away, and I grieved as though I had lost something, as though I had no one with whom to enjoy the state. What manner of man is this?" Confucius said: "I once traveled to Chu, where I saw piglets nursing at their dead mother. After a little while, with a startled look, they all abandoned her and ran. They no longer saw themselves in her; she was no longer of their kind. What they loved in their mother was not her form, but that which moved her form. Men who die in battle are buried without their plumes; a man whose feet have been cut off no longer cherishes his shoes — both have lost the root. The attendants of the Son of Heaven do not trim their nails or pierce their ears; a newly married man is kept outside the court and sent on no more missions. If wholeness of form suffices for such treatment, how much more wholeness of virtue! Now Ai Taituo is trusted without having spoken, loved without having achieved anything; he made a man offer him his state and fear only that he would not take it. He must be one whose powers are whole while his virtue takes no form." Duke Ai said: "What do you mean by whole powers?" Confucius said: "Death and life, preservation and loss, failure and success, poverty and riches, worthiness and unworthiness, slander and praise, hunger and thirst, cold and heat — these are the changes of the world, the workings of destiny. Day and night they alternate before us, and knowledge cannot spy out their beginning. Therefore they are not worth the disturbance of harmony; they must not enter the spirit's storehouse. Keep it harmonious, easy, and free-flowing without losing joy; keep it day and night without a seam, in springtime with all things — this is what, meeting things, gives birth to the season in the mind. This is called whole powers." "And what is virtue taking no form?" Confucius said: "Levelness is the fullness of still water. It may serve as a model: keep it within, and it does not spill without. Virtue is the cultivation of completed harmony. When virtue takes no form, things cannot part from it." Another day Duke Ai told Min Zi (閔子): "At first, when I sat facing south and ruled the world, holding the thread of the people and grieving over their deaths, I thought myself utterly penetrating. Now that I have heard the words of an utmost man, I fear I lack the substance — that I have used my person recklessly and will lose the state. Confucius and I are not lord and subject; we are friends in virtue, that is all."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 闉跂支離無脤說衛靈公，靈公說之，而視全人，其脰肩肩。甕盎大癭說齊桓公，桓公說之，而視全人，其脰肩肩。故德有所長，而形有所忘，人不忘其所忘，而忘其所不忘，此謂誠忘。故聖人有所遊，而知為孽，約為膠，德為接，工為商。聖人不謀，惡用知？不斲，惡用膠？無喪，惡用德？不貨，惡用商？四者，天鬻也。天鬻者，天食也。既受食於天，又惡用人？有人之形，無人之情。有人之形，故群於人；無人之情，故是非不得於身。眇乎小哉！所以屬於人也。謷乎大哉！獨成其天。

**EN:** Clubfoot Splay-limb Lipless (闉跂支離無脤) persuaded Duke Ling of Wei. The duke was so delighted with him that when he looked at whole men, their necks seemed skinny and scrawny. Jug-neck Great-goiter (甕盎大癭) persuaded Duke Huan of Qi. The duke was so delighted with him that when he looked at whole men, their necks seemed skinny and scrawny. So when virtue grows tall, the form is forgotten. But men do not forget what ought to be forgotten, and forget what ought not to be forgotten — this is called true forgetting. Therefore the sage, in his roaming, regards knowledge as a monstrous growth, covenants as glue, favors as getting, and craft as trade. The sage does not scheme — what use has he for knowledge? He does not cut things apart — what use has he for glue? He has lost nothing — what use has he for favors? He does not trade — what use has he for wares? These four are Heaven's gruel; Heaven's gruel is Heaven's feeding. Since he receives his food from Heaven, what use has he for men? He has the form of a man, but not the feelings of a man. Having the form of a man, he flocks with men; lacking the feelings of a man, right and wrong do not reach his person. Minute and small! — that whereby he belongs to men. Lofty and great! — alone he completes his Heaven.

## Section 6

**ZH:** 惠子謂莊子曰：「人故無情乎？」莊子曰：「然。」惠子曰：「人而無情，何以謂之人？」莊子曰：「道與之貌，天與之形，惡得不謂之人？」惠子曰：「既謂之人，惡得無情？」莊子曰：「是非吾所謂情也。吾所謂無情者，言人之不以好惡內傷其身，常因自然而不益生也。」惠子曰：「不益生，何以有其身？」莊子曰：「道與之貌，天與之形，無以好惡內傷其身。今子外乎子之神，勞乎子之精，倚樹而吟，據槁梧而瞑。天選子之形，子以堅白鳴！」

**EN:** Huizi said to Zhuangzi: "Can a man really be without feelings?" Zhuangzi said: "He can." Huizi said: "If a man is without feelings, how can he be called a man?" Zhuangzi said: "The Way gave him a face; Heaven gave him a form. How can he not be called a man?" Huizi said: "If he is called a man, how can he be without feelings?" Zhuangzi said: "That is not what I mean by feelings. When I say he is without feelings, I mean that he does not let likes and dislikes wound him within; he always follows the natural and does not add to life." Huizi said: "If he does not add to life, how can he keep possession of his body?" Zhuangzi said: "The Way gave him a face; Heaven gave him a form; and he does not let likes and dislikes wound him within. But you, sir, wear your spirit out without and weary your essence; you lean against a tree and intone, doze propped on a withered sterculia. Heaven chose your form — and you clang away upon 'hard and white'!"

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 德 → "virtue"; 道 → "the Way"; 天 → "Heaven"; 命 → "destiny"/"fate"; 情 → "feelings" (§6) / "genuine condition" elsewhere; 聖人 → "the sage"; 至人 → "the utmost man"; 靈府 → "the spirit's storehouse"; 才全 → "whole powers"; 德不形 → "virtue taking no form."
- Mutilated men (兀者) are rendered "footless," following the standard reading of 兀 as amputation of the feet.
- The names in §5 are descriptive epithets; they are translated descriptively with the Chinese retained.
- `[uncertain]` flags: 1 (§1 審乎無假). Reviewers may also wish to compare §4's 悶然/氾若辭 ("dully and after a pause, vaguely, as though declining"), where the transmitted wording is odd.
