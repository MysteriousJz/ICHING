# Enjoyment in Untroubled Ease (逍遙遊)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Inner Chapters _ Enjoyment in Untroubled Ease - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete — contrary to the session brief, the body text is fully present in the file (7 sections).

## Section 1

**ZH:** 北冥有魚，其名為鯤。鯤之大，不知其幾千里也。化而為鳥，其名為鵬。鵬之背，不知其幾千里也；怒而飛，其翼若垂天之雲。是鳥也，海運則將徙於南冥。南冥者，天池也。齊諧者，志怪者也。諧之言曰：「鵬之徙於南冥也，水擊三千里，摶扶搖而上者九萬里，去以六月息者也。」野馬也，塵埃也，生物之以息相吹也。天之蒼蒼，其正色邪？其遠而無所至極邪？其視下也亦若是，則已矣。且夫水之積也不厚，則負大舟也無力。覆杯水於坳堂之上，則芥為之舟，置杯焉則膠，水淺而舟大也。風之積也不厚，則其負大翼也無力。故九萬里則風斯在下矣，而後乃今培風；背負青天而莫之夭閼者，而後乃今將圖南。蜩與學鳩笑之曰：「我決起而飛，槍榆、枋，時則不至而控於地而已矣，奚以之九萬里而南為？」適莽蒼者三湌而反，腹猶果然；適百里者宿舂糧；適千里者三月聚糧。之二蟲又何知！小知不及大知，小年不及大年。奚以知其然也？朝菌不知晦朔，蟪蛄不知春秋，此小年也。楚之南有冥靈者，以五百歲為春，五百歲為秋；上古有大椿者，以八千歲為春，八千歲為秋。而彭祖乃今以久特聞，眾人匹之，不亦悲乎！

**EN:** In the Northern Darkness there is a fish; its name is Kun (鯤). None knows how many thousand li Kun measures across. It transforms into a bird; its name is Peng (鵬). None knows how many thousand li Peng measures across the back. When it rouses itself and flies, its wings are like clouds hanging from the sky. When the seas heave, this bird sets out to migrate to the Southern Darkness. The Southern Darkness is the Pool of Heaven. The *Qixie* (齊諧) is a record of marvels, and in it we read: "When Peng journeys to the Southern Darkness, it lashes the waters for three thousand li, then spirals up on the whirlwind ninety thousand li, and departs borne on the breath of six months." [uncertain: 去以六月息者也 — read here as "riding the wind accumulated over six months"; an older reading takes it as "travels six months before it rests."] Heat-haze, drifting dust — these are living things blowing against one another with their breath. Is the deep blue of the sky its true color, or is it that the distance has no end? When Peng looks down, it too sees just the same, and that is all. Now if water is not heaped deep, it has no strength to bear a great boat. Tip a cup of water into a hollow in the hall and a mustard seed can serve as its boat; set the cup down in it and it runs aground, for the water is too shallow and the boat too big. If wind is not heaped deep, it has no strength to bear great wings. So at ninety thousand li the wind lies there beneath it; only then does it mount upon the wind; with the blue sky upon its back and nothing to thwart or bring it down, only then does it chart its course for the south. The cicada and the little dove laugh at it, saying: "I leap up and fly, strike for the elm or the sandalwood, and sometimes, failing to reach even that, tumble back to the ground — what is the use of rising ninety thousand li to go south?" One who travels to the green woods near at hand takes three meals and returns with his belly still full; one who travels a hundred li must grind grain the night before; one who travels a thousand li must gather provisions three months ahead. What do these two little creatures know! Small knowledge cannot match great knowledge; a short life cannot match a long life. How do we know it is so? The morning mushroom knows nothing of the month's waning and waxing; the summer cicada knows nothing of spring and autumn — such is a short life. South of Chu (楚) there is the Mysterious Spirit tree, which counts five hundred years as one spring and five hundred years as one autumn; in high antiquity there was the Great Chun tree, which counted eight thousand years as one spring and eight thousand years as one autumn. Yet today Pengzu (彭祖) alone is famed for long life, and everyone measures themselves against him — is it not pitiful!

## Section 2

**ZH:** 湯之問棘也是已。窮髮之北，有冥海者，天池也。有魚焉，其廣數千里，未有知其脩者，其名為鯤。有鳥焉，其名為鵬，背若泰山，翼若垂天之雲，摶扶搖羊角而上者九萬里，絕雲氣，負青天，然後圖南，且適南冥也。斥鴳笑之曰：「彼且奚適也？我騰躍而上，不過數仞而下，翱翔蓬蒿之間，此亦飛之至也。而彼且奚適也？」此小大之辯也。

**EN:** When Tang (湯) questioned Ji (棘), it was to the same effect. [uncertain: an allusive reference, now lost, to a dialogue of Tang and his teacher Ji retelling the Kun–Peng story; some editions attach the phrase to what precedes.] North of the Barren Thickets there is a Dark Sea — the Pool of Heaven. In it there is a fish several thousand li wide, whose length none can fathom; its name is Kun. There is also a bird; its name is Peng. Its back is like Mount Tai (泰山), its wings like clouds hanging from the sky; it spirals upward on the horn-twisting whirlwind ninety thousand li, passes beyond the cloudy vapors, bears the blue sky, and only then charts its course south, bound for the Southern Darkness. The marsh quail laughs at it, saying: "And where does it think it is going? I leap up and fly, come down again after a few fathoms, and soar among the reeds and wormwood — this too is the utmost of flying! So where does it think it is going?" This is the distinction between the small and the great.

## Section 3

**ZH:** 故夫知效一官，行比一鄉，德合一君而徵一國者，其自視也亦若此矣。而宋榮子猶然笑之。且舉世而譽之而不加勸，舉世而非之而不加沮，定乎內外之分，辯乎榮辱之竟，斯已矣。彼其於世，未數數然也。雖然，猶有未樹也。夫列子御風而行，泠然善也，旬有五日而後反。彼於致福者，未數數然也。此雖免乎行，猶有所待者也。若夫乘天地之正，而御六氣之辯，以遊無窮者，彼且惡乎待哉！故曰：至人無己，神人無功，聖人無名。

**EN:** So it is with those whose knowledge suffices to fill one office, whose conduct fits one village, whose virtue pleases one lord and wins credence in one state — they see themselves in just the same way. And yet Song Rongzi (宋榮子) would smile at them. Though the whole world praised him, he was not thereby spurred on; though the whole world condemned him, he was not thereby cast down. He was settled on the division between inner and outer; he discerned the boundary of honor and disgrace — and there he stopped. Toward the things of the world he did not go bustling and fretting. Even so, there was still something he had not planted. Liezi (列子) rode the wind as he went — how light and fine! — returning only after a fortnight. Toward the getting of blessings he did not go bustling and fretting. But although this freed him from walking, he still had something he depended on. As for one who rides the true course of heaven and earth, steers the shifting of the six breaths, and thus roams the boundless — what would such a one have to depend on? Therefore it is said: the utmost man has no self; the spirit-like man has no achievement; the sage has no name.

## Section 4

**ZH:** 堯讓天下於許由，曰：「日月出矣，而爝火不息，其於光也，不亦難乎！時雨降矣，而猶浸灌，其於澤也，不亦勞乎！夫子立而天下治，而我猶尸之，吾自視缺然，請致天下。」許由曰：「子治天下，天下既已治也。而我猶代子，吾將為名乎？名者，實之賓也，吾將為賓乎？鷦鷯巢於深林，不過一枝；偃鼠飲河，不過滿腹。歸休乎君！予無所用天下為。庖人雖不治庖，尸祝不越樽俎而代之矣。」

**EN:** Yao (堯) ceded the empire to Xu You (許由), saying: "When the sun and moon have come out, yet a torch-fire is not put out, how hard it must toil to add to the light! When the rains of the season have fallen, yet one still goes on soaking and watering, how laborious it is to add to the moisture! Were you, sir, to take your stand, the empire would be well ordered, yet I still occupy the seat. I look within and find myself deficient; I beg to hand the empire over to you." Xu You said: "You govern the empire, and the empire is already well governed. Were I nonetheless to take your place, would I be doing it for the name? The name is but the guest of the substance — would I consent to be the guest? The wren nesting in the deep forest needs no more than one branch; the mole drinking from the river needs no more than a bellyful. Go home and rest, my lord! I have no use for the empire. Even if the cook does not tend his kitchen, the officiating priest does not leap over the goblets and platters to take his place."

## Section 5

**ZH:** 肩吾問於連叔曰：「吾聞言於接輿，大而無當，往而不反。吾驚怖其言，猶河漢而無極也，大有逕庭，不近人情焉。」連叔曰：「其言謂何哉？」曰：「藐姑射之山，有神人居焉，肌膚若冰雪，淖約若處子，不食五穀，吸風飲露。乘雲氣，御飛龍，而遊乎四海之外。其神凝，使物不疵癘而年穀熟。吾以是狂而不信也。」連叔曰：「然，瞽者無以與乎文章之觀，聾者無以與乎鍾鼓之聲。豈唯形骸有聾盲哉？夫知亦有之。是其言也，猶時女也。之人也，之德也，將旁礡萬物，以為一世蘄乎亂，孰弊弊焉以天下為事！之人也，物莫之傷，大浸稽天而不溺，大旱、金石流、土山焦而不熱。是其塵垢粃糠，將猶陶鑄堯、舜者也，孰肯以物為事！宋人資章甫而適諸越，越人斷髮文身，無所用之。堯治天下之民，平海內之政，往見四子藐姑射之山，汾水之陽，窅然喪其天下焉。」

**EN:** Jian Wu (肩吾) asked Lian Shu (連叔), saying: "I heard words from Jieyu (接輿) — grandiose, with nothing to bear them out; they went on and on and never came back. I was astonished and frightened by them; boundless as the Milky Way, they were wildly wide of the mark, far from the genuine feelings of men." Lian Shu said: "What did he say?" Jian Wu replied: "'On the mountain of remote Gushe (姑射) there dwells a spirit-like man. His skin is like ice and snow; he is gentle and yielding as a maiden. He eats none of the five grains, but inhales the wind and drinks the dew. He rides the cloudy vapors, guides flying dragons, and roams beyond the four seas. His spirit concentrated, he keeps creatures free of blight and pestilence and makes the yearly grain ripen.' I took this for madness and did not believe it." Lian Shu said: "Just so. The blind have no share in the sight of patterned beauty; the deaf have no share in the sound of bells and drums. And is it only in the bodily frame that blindness and deafness exist? Knowledge has them too. Those words were spoken for the likes of you! [uncertain: 猶時女也 — a notorious crux; read here with the commentators as 時女 = 是汝, 'aimed at you'; other readings take 女 as 'maiden,' 'those words were like the girl he described.'] Such a man, such virtue as his, spreads wide over the ten thousand things and makes them one. The whole age longs for order — yet how could he weary and fret himself taking the world as his task? Such a man — nothing can injure him: a great flood reaching to the sky would not drown him; a great drought, though it melted metal and stone and scorched the hills of earth, would not heat him. From his very dust and dregs, his chaff and husks, one could still mold and cast a Yao or a Shun. How could he consent to make mere things his task! A man of Song (宋) laid in a stock of ceremonial caps and carried them to Yue (越), but the men of Yue cut their hair short and tattoo their bodies — they had no use for them. Yao brought order to the people of the empire and settled the governance of all within the seas; then he went to see the Four Masters on the mountain of remote Gushe, on the sunny side of the Fen River (汾水) — and there, in a daze, he lost his empire."

## Section 6

**ZH:** 惠子謂莊子曰：「魏王貽我大瓠之種，我樹之成而實五石，以盛水漿，其堅不能自舉也。剖之以為瓢，則瓠落無所容。非不呺然大也，吾為其無用而掊之。」莊子曰：「夫子固拙於用大矣。宋人有善為不龜手之藥者，世世以洴澼絖為事。客聞之，請買其方百金。聚族而謀曰：『我世世為洴澼絖，不過數金；今一朝而鬻技百金，請與之。』客得之，以說吳王。越有難，吳王使之將。冬，與越人水戰，大敗越人，裂地而封之。能不龜手一也，或以封，或不免於洴澼絖，則所用之異也。今子有五石之瓠，何不慮以為大樽而浮乎江湖，而憂其瓠落無所容？則夫子猶有蓬之心也夫！」

**EN:** Huizi (惠子) said to Zhuangzi: "The king of Wei (魏王) gave me the seed of a great gourd. I planted it, and when it matured it held five bushels; but if I filled it with water or broth, it was not solid enough to lift itself. I cut it in half to make a dipper, but it was so sprawling there was nothing it could hold. It is not that it was not wondrously large — but I smashed it to pieces for being useless." Zhuangzi said: "You, sir, are certainly clumsy when it comes to using the large. There was a man of Song who was skilled at making a salve that keeps the hands from chapping; generation after generation his family made their trade of bleaching silk floss. A traveler heard of it and asked to buy the formula for a hundred pieces of gold. The man gathered his clan and took counsel: 'For generations we have bleached silk floss and never earned more than a few pieces of gold; now in a single morning we can sell the craft for a hundred — let us give it to him.' The traveler obtained it and used it to persuade the king of Wu (吳王). When Yue brought war, the king of Wu made him a general; in winter he fought a water battle with the men of Yue and utterly defeated them, and was enfeoffed with a parcel of land. The power to keep hands from chapping was one and the same; with it one man won a fief, while the other could not escape bleaching floss — the difference lies in how a thing is used. Now you had a five-bushel gourd — why did you not think to lash it to yourself as a great float and drift upon the rivers and lakes, instead of worrying that it was too sprawling to hold anything? Then you, sir, still have weeds for a mind!"

## Section 7

**ZH:** 惠子謂莊子曰：「吾有大樹，人謂之樗。其大本擁腫而不中繩墨，其小枝卷曲而不中規矩，立之塗，匠者不顧。今子之言，大而無用，眾所同去也。」莊子曰：「子獨不見狸狌乎？卑身而伏，以候敖者；東西跳梁，不避高下；中於機辟，死於罔罟。今夫斄牛，其大若垂天之雲。此能為大矣，而不能執鼠。今子有大樹，患其無用，何不樹之於無何有之鄉，廣莫之野，彷徨乎無為其側，逍遙乎寢臥其下？不夭斤斧，物無害者，無所可用，安所困苦哉！」

**EN:** Huizi said to Zhuangzi: "I have a great tree; people call it the ailanthus. Its great trunk is so swollen and knotted it will not answer the inked line; its branches so curled and twisted they fit neither compass nor square. Set it up by the roadside, and carpenters do not so much as glance at it. Now your words, sir, are likewise grand but useless — a thing all the world casts off together." Zhuangzi said: "Have you alone never watched a wildcat? Crouching low, it lies in wait for its straying prey; it leaps about east and west, unhindered by high or low — then falls into the snare and dies in the net. Now consider the yak, big as clouds hanging from the sky: it can be great, all right, but it cannot catch mice. Now you have a great tree and fret that it is useless — why not plant it in the land of Nothing-Whatever, in the vast and trackless wilds, where you may wander idly in non-action by its side, and roam free and easy, lying down to sleep beneath it? No axe will shorten its days; nothing will harm it. Being of no use at all, from where could trouble or distress ever come?"

- Rendering choices: 道 → "the Way" (does not occur in this chapter); 知 → "knowledge" (noun) / "to know" (verb); 聖人 → "the sage"; 無為 → "non-action"; 逍遙 → "free and easy" in the body text (the chapter title keeps Legge's standard "Enjoyment in Untroubled Ease"); 天池 → "the Pool of Heaven"; 扶搖 → "the whirlwind"; 羊角 (§2) → "the horn-twisting whirlwind"; 六氣 → "the six breaths"; 至人/神人 → "the utmost man" / "the spirit-like man"; 心 → "mind" (蓬之心 "weeds for a mind").
