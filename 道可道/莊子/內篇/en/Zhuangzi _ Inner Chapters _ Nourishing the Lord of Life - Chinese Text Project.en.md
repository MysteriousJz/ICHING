# Nourishing the Lord of Life (養生主)
Source: Zhuangzi _ Inner Chapters _ Nourishing the Lord of Life - Chinese Text Project.html
Status: complete (6 sections)

## Section 1

**ZH:** 吾生也有涯，而知也无涯。以有涯隨无涯，殆已；已而為知者，殆而已矣。為善无近名，為惡无近刑。緣督以為經，可以保身，可以全生，可以養親，可以盡年。
**PY:** wú shēng yě yǒu yá， ér zhī yě wú yá。 yǐ yǒu yá suí wú yá， dài yǐ； yǐ ér wèi zhī zhě， dài ér yǐ yǐ。 wèi shàn wú jìn míng， wèi è wú jìn xíng。 yuán dū yǐ wèi jīng， kě yǐ bǎo shēn， kě yǐ quán shēng， kě yǐ yǎng qīn， kě yǐ jǐn nián。

**EN:** Our life has a limit, but knowledge has no limit. To take what is limited and chase after what is limitless is perilous; and for one who, even so, persists in the pursuit of knowledge, it is nothing but peril. [uncertain: 已而為知者 — read here as "knowing this, yet still pursuing knowledge"; others punctuate and read "to stop, and then act with knowledge."] Do good, but do not approach fame; do evil, but do not approach punishment. Follow the central channel and make it your constant; then you can preserve the body, keep life whole, nourish your kin, and live out your years. [uncertain: 緣督以為經 — 督 is read variously as the body's central "governing vessel" (督脈) and as simply "the middle"; "central channel" preserves both.]

## Section 2

**ZH:** 庖丁為文惠君解牛，手之所觸，肩之所倚，足之所履，膝之所踦，砉然嚮然，奏刀騞然，莫不中音。合於《桑林》之舞，乃中《經首》之會。文惠君曰：「譆！善哉！技蓋至此乎？」庖丁釋刀對曰：「臣之所好者道也，進乎技矣。始臣之解牛之時，所見无非牛者。三年之後，未嘗見全牛也。方今之時，臣以神遇，而不以目視，官知止而神欲行。依乎天理，批大郤，導大窾，因其固然。技經肯綮之未嘗，而況大軱乎！良庖歲更刀，割也；族庖月更刀，折也。今臣之刀十九年矣，所解數千牛矣，而刀刃若新發於硎。彼節者有間，而刀刃者无厚，以无厚入有間，恢恢乎其於遊刃必有餘地矣，是以十九年而刀刃若新發於硎。雖然，每至於族，吾見其難為，怵然為戒，視為止，行為遲。動刀甚微，謋然已解，如土委地。提刀而立，為之四顧，為之躊躇滿志，善刀而藏之。」文惠君曰：「善哉！吾聞庖丁之言，得養生焉。」
**PY:** páo dīng wèi wén huì jūn jiě niú， shǒu zhī suǒ chù， jiān zhī suǒ yǐ， zú zhī suǒ lǚ， xī zhī suǒ yǐ， huò rán xiàng rán， zòu dāo huō rán， mò bù zhōng yīn。 hé yú《 sāng lín》 zhī wǔ， nǎi zhōng《 jīng shǒu》 zhī huì。 wén huì jūn yuē：「 xī！ shàn zāi！ jì gài zhì cǐ hū？」 páo dīng shì dāo duì yuē：「 chén zhī suǒ hǎo zhě dào yě， jìn hū jì yǐ。 shǐ chén zhī jiě niú zhī shí， suǒ jiàn wú fēi niú zhě。 sān nián zhī hòu， wèi cháng jiàn quán niú yě。 fāng jīn zhī shí， chén yǐ shén yù， ér bù yǐ mù shì， guān zhī zhǐ ér shén yù xíng。 yī hū tiān lǐ， pī dà xì， dǎo dà kuǎn， yīn qí gù rán。 jì jīng kěn qìng zhī wèi cháng， ér kuàng dà gū hū！ liáng páo suì gèng dāo， gē yě； zú páo yuè gèng dāo， zhé yě。 jīn chén zhī dāo shí jiǔ nián yǐ， suǒ jiě shù qiān niú yǐ， ér dāo rèn ruò xīn fā yú xíng。 bǐ jié zhě yǒu jiān， ér dāo rèn zhě wú hòu， yǐ wú hòu rù yǒu jiān， huī huī hū qí yú yóu rèn bì yǒu yú dì yǐ， shì yǐ shí jiǔ nián ér dāo rèn ruò xīn fā yú xíng。 suī rán， měi zhì yú zú， wú jiàn qí nán wèi， chù rán wèi jiè， shì wèi zhǐ， xíng wèi chí。 dòng dāo shèn wēi， huò rán yǐ jiě， rú tǔ wěi dì。 tí dāo ér lì， wèi zhī sì gù， wèi zhī chóu chú mǎn zhì， shàn dāo ér cáng zhī。」 wén huì jūn yuē：「 shàn zāi！ wú wén páo dīng zhī yán， dé yǎng shēng yān。」

**EN:** Cook Ding (庖丁) was cutting up an ox for Lord Wenhui (文惠君). Wherever his hand touched, wherever his shoulder leaned, wherever his foot stepped, wherever his knee braced — zip! went the flesh, whoosh! went the knife — and nothing missed the note. It matched the dance of the *Mulberry Grove* (桑林); it chimed with the cadence of the *Jingshou* (經首). Lord Wenhui said: "Ah — splendid! Can skill really reach this far?" Cook Ding laid down his knife and replied: "What your servant loves is the Way, which goes beyond skill. When I first began cutting up oxen, all I saw was the ox itself. After three years, I no longer saw the ox as a whole. And now I meet it with the spirit rather than looking with the eyes: the senses know where to stop, and the spirit moves as it wills. I follow the natural grain, strike through the great seams, guide along the great hollows, and go by what is inherently so. My knife has never so much as touched a gristle-joint — how much less a great bone! A good cook changes his knife once a year, because he cuts; a common cook changes it once a month, because he hacks. This knife of mine has been in use for nineteen years and has cut up several thousand oxen, yet its blade is as though fresh from the whetstone. The joints have spaces in them, and the blade has no thickness; to enter what has space with what has no thickness — how vast the room! The blade roams with plenty to spare. That is why, after nineteen years, the blade is as though fresh from the whetstone. Even so, whenever I come upon a knotted cluster, I see the difficulty of it. I stand wary and on guard, my gaze settles, my movements slow. The knife moves ever so slightly — and flop! the joint comes apart, crumbling like a clod of earth. I stand with knife raised, look all about me, linger in deep satisfaction, then wipe the blade and put it away." Lord Wenhui said: "Splendid! From Cook Ding's words I have learned how to nourish life."

## Section 3

**ZH:** 公文軒見右師而驚曰：「是何人也？惡乎介也？天與，其人與？」曰：「天也，非人也。天之生是使獨也，人之貌有與也。以是知其天也，非人也。」
**PY:** gōng wén xuān jiàn yòu shī ér jīng yuē：「 shì hé rén yě？ è hū jiè yě？ tiān yǔ， qí rén yǔ？」 yuē：「 tiān yě， fēi rén yě。 tiān zhī shēng shì shǐ dú yě， rén zhī mào yǒu yǔ yě。 yǐ shì zhī qí tiān yě， fēi rén yě。」

**EN:** Gongwen Xuan (公文軒) saw the Right Commander (右師) and was astonished: "What manner of man is this? Why is he one-footed? Is it from Heaven, or from man?" The reply came: "It is from Heaven, not from man. In giving me life, Heaven made this man one of a kind; the outward form of man is given in pairs. By this I know it is from Heaven and not from man." [uncertain: the reply is unattributed (a bare 曰); most readers give it to the Right Commander, some to Gongwen Xuan answering his own question.]

## Section 4

**ZH:** 澤雉十步一啄，百步一飲，不蘄畜乎樊中。神雖王，不善也。
**PY:** zé zhì shí bù yī zhuó， bǎi bù yī yǐn， bù qí chù hū fán zhōng。 shén suī wáng， bù shàn yě。

**EN:** The marsh pheasant must go ten steps for one peck of food, a hundred steps for one drink — yet it does not long to be fattened in a cage. There its spirit might be a king, but it would not be good. [uncertain: 神雖王，不善也 — compressed; an alternative reading makes it the bird's own judgment: "though its spirit would thrive as a king, it knows the cage is not good."]

## Section 5

**ZH:** 老聃死，秦失弔之，三號而出。弟子曰：「非夫子之友邪？」曰：「然。」「然則弔焉若此，可乎？」曰：「然。始也，吾以為其人也，而今非也。向吾入而弔焉，有老者哭之，如哭其子；少者哭之，如哭其母。彼其所以會之，必有不蘄言而言，不蘄哭而哭者。是遁天倍情，忘其所受，古者謂之遁天之刑。適來，夫子時也；適去，夫子順也。安時而處順，哀樂不能入也，古者謂是帝之縣解。」
**PY:** lǎo dān sǐ， qín shī diào zhī， sān hào ér chū。 dì zǐ yuē：「 fēi fū zǐ zhī yǒu xié？」 yuē：「 rán。」「 rán zé diào yān ruò cǐ， kě hū？」 yuē：「 rán。 shǐ yě， wú yǐ wèi qí rén yě， ér jīn fēi yě。 xiàng wú rù ér diào yān， yǒu lǎo zhě kū zhī， rú kū qí zi； shǎo zhě kū zhī， rú kū qí mǔ。 bǐ qí suǒ yǐ huì zhī， bì yǒu bù qí yán ér yán， bù qí kū ér kū zhě。 shì dùn tiān bèi qíng， wàng qí suǒ shòu， gǔ zhě wèi zhī dùn tiān zhī xíng。 shì lái， fū zǐ shí yě； shì qù， fū zǐ shùn yě。 ān shí ér chù shùn， āi lè bù néng rù yě， gǔ zhě wèi shì dì zhī xiàn jiě。」

**EN:** When Lao Dan (老聃) died, Qin Shi (秦失) went to mourn him. He wailed three times and came out. A disciple said: "Were you not the master's friend?" He said: "I was." "Then is it acceptable to mourn him like that?" He said: "It is. At first I took those within for his kind of men, but now I see they are not. When I went in just now to mourn, there were old ones weeping for him as though for a son, and young ones weeping for him as though for a mother. For them to be gathered there like this, there must be some who speak though they do not wish to speak, and weep though they do not wish to weep. This is to flee Heaven and turn one's back on genuine feeling, forgetting what one has received; the ancients called it the punishment of fleeing Heaven. He came when it was time — that was the master's moment; he went along with the flow — that was the master's course. Rest at ease in the moment and dwell in the flow, and sorrow and joy cannot enter in. The ancients called this the Lord's release from the cords." [uncertain: 帝之縣解 — 縣 ("suspended," hung upside down) 解 ("released"); rendered "the Lord's release from the cords," i.e., liberation by the Lord (帝) from the suspended bonds of joy and sorrow.]

## Section 6

**ZH:** 指窮於為薪，火傳也，不知其盡也。
**PY:** zhǐ qióng yú wèi xīn， huǒ chuán yě， bù zhī qí jǐn yě。

**EN:** The grease is exhausted in feeding the fire, yet the flame passes on, and none knows its end. [uncertain: 指窮於為薪 — 指 is commonly read as a loan for 脂 ("grease, fat," sc. of a lamp or torch); read literally it would be "the fingers are exhausted in laying firewood."]

## Translator's Note

- Glossary consistency: 道 → "the Way" (§2); 神 → "spirit"; 天 → "Heaven"; 情 → "genuine feeling" (§5); 養生 → "nourishing life."
- Section 2 is the celebrated Cook Ding parable; onomatopoeia (砉然/騞然/謋然) rendered impressionistically ("zip," "whoosh," "flop") to keep the musical point of the passage.
- `[uncertain]` flags: 6 (§1 ×2, §3, §4, §5, §6) — each is a well-known crux in the commentarial tradition; none affects the chapter's overall argument.
